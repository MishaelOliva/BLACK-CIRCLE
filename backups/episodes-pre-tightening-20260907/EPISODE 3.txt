# Episode 3: **Rumors Under Sunlight**

Ordinary places were harder to trust after vows. On my eighth evening at Aurelis, I asked Elira to meet beneath the western garden lamps—not the archive, but somewhere either of us could leave without feeling like the floor had an opinion.

She arrived in her academy uniform, hair loose over one shoulder, Yellow Circle crest dim at her collarbone. She looked at my hands before she looked at my face.

Elira: "You made it."

Haru: "I said I would."

Elira: "You also said protection."

Haru: "It is."

I opened my palm.

The pendant rested there, black crystal threaded with silver depth. Small enough to pass as ornament. Quiet enough not to announce what lived inside it. The Black Circle within it was not awake, exactly. More like a door that knew my hand.

Elira did not reach for it immediately.

That helped.

Elira: "Did you make it as an apology?"

Haru: "No."

She watched me with the same careful attention she had given the reveal.

Haru: "I made it as a question."

Her expression softened.

Elira: "And what is it asking?"

I looked at the pendant because looking at her made honesty less organized.

Haru: "Whether protection can stay protection if the person receiving it is allowed to refuse."

For a moment, wind moved through the garden leaves and neither of us spoke.

Then Elira held out her hand.

Palm up.

Not taking.

Answering.

Elira: "Then ask."

Haru: "Will you carry it?"

Elira: "Yes."

The word was quiet.

Complete.

I set the pendant in her palm.

Only then did she touch it with her fingertips. The crystal warmed under her skin, recognizing the vow without tightening around it.

Elira: "It feels like night under glass."

Haru: "That is... not inaccurate."

She smiled faintly.

Elira: "You sound offended by poetry."

Haru: "Only when it is accurate before I am."

That drew the smallest laugh from her.

She lifted the chain.

I did not move to place it around her neck until she looked at me and nodded.

Even then, I kept the motion careful. No brush of fingers against skin that she had not asked for. No turning the gift into a claim.

The pendant settled near her Yellow Circle crest, dark and quiet against the pale fabric of her uniform.

Elira touched it once.

Elira: "Thank you."

Haru: "If it ever activates fully, do not fight it."

Her eyebrow lifted.

Haru: "That was not the whole point of giving it."

Elira: "No."

Haru: "But it is still important."

She considered that, then nodded.

Elira: "Then I will not fight protection that remembers I am a person."

The pendant gave one restrained pulse: not bright, but enough.

We stood beneath the western lamps with more distance between us than the Moonlit Archive had left and less than the world would have understood.

There was no kiss and no new vow spoken aloud, only a question accepted.

By morning, the rumors found us anyway.

Before the rumors reached breakfast, I returned to the north library wing.

The academy was still in its pale early-morning quiet, the kind that made even old doors sound guilty. Moonlight had faded from the windows, but the archive corridor remembered it in cold silver lines along the floor.

I stood where Elira and I had parted and let my false Green Circle breathe just enough mana to read what ordinary detection spells would miss.

Someone had been there.

Not close enough to hear everything. My isolation veil had swallowed the dangerous parts. But close enough to notice pressure, light, and the shape of magic being hidden too carefully.

A listening glyph had been burned out from the inside.

That bothered me more than finding it intact would have.

An intact spell could be studied. A destroyed spell meant the caster knew someone might come looking.

I crouched and touched two fingers to the floor.

A trace answered.

Dry ink.
Old paper.
A clean academic mana signature scrubbed almost bare.

Then, beneath it, one word written in residue so thin it was probably meant for me alone.

**SEEN.**

The word did not flare.

It did not threaten.

It was what made it effective.

Threats had emotion in them. Pride. Fear. Anger. Intent sharpened enough to leave a flavor behind. This residue held none of that. It had the clinical quiet of someone placing a pin through an insect and labeling the wing pattern.

I ran a second scan beneath the first and found the part the caster had tried hardest to erase.

Not a voice.
Not a face.

A counting sequence.

Three pulses of golden mana.
One suppression veil.
One impossible pressure spike so deeply muffled that an ordinary archive ward would have mistaken it for old foundation noise.

The glyph had not heard the truth.

But it had heard the room struggle to keep the truth hidden.

The hidden Black Circle near my heart stirred once.

I erased the trace completely and stood.

By the time the morning bell rang, the corridor looked innocent again.

I did not feel innocent.

---

The rumors began at breakfast.

Which, in Aurelis Arcane Academy, meant they had actually begun several hours earlier and simply reached full social visibility by morning.

By the time I entered the main dining hall, the atmosphere had already changed in a way that would have been invisible to anyone less used to being watched.

Conversations lowered half a degree when I passed. A few students looked at me too quickly and then away too slowly. Others were less disciplined and openly stared.

Near the side entrance, two Green Circle students whispered over untouched breakfast. One of them had curly auburn hair and a worn notebook tucked beneath her elbow-the same Green Circle girl I had noticed lingering in the last row after Leon's match, watching the arena as if my victory had left a question behind.

Curly-Haired Green Student: "Maybe he just got lucky."

Student 2: "With Leon or with Elira?"

Curly-Haired Green Student: "Both, probably."

She said it carefully, not cruelly. Like someone testing the safer explanation before admitting she wanted another one to be true.

By the western tables, two Blue Circle second-years had completely failed to disguise the fact that I was the subject of their discussion.

Student 3: "I'm telling you, I saw them leave the north wing together."

Student 4: "At night?"

Student 3: "Obviously at night. That's why it matters."

Student 5: "You don't just accidentally end up alone in the Moonlit Archive Hall with Elira Vale."

Student 4: "Maybe she's recruiting him."

Student 3: "Recruiting a Green?"

Student 5: "Or slumming. Either way, it's weird."

Near the upper terrace, a Red Circle girl dismissed the entire discussion without looking away from her tea.

Student 6: "Tournament politics. Elira Vale doesn't do anything by accident."

I kept walking.

The tray in my hand remained level.
My expression remained neutral.
My internal peace, while not fully intact, was still functional.

At least until I spotted Aira.

She was sitting at a side table with two cups of tea and the exact expression of someone who had prepared herself spiritually for nonsense.

Aira: "Good morning."

Haru: "That tone suggests it isn't."

Aira: "It depends on your tolerance for public imagination."

I sat across from her.

Haru: "How bad?"

Aira folded her hands with theatrical calm.

Aira: "Current theories include: you and Elira are secretly dating, unofficially engaged through some ancient mana custom, forming a cross-rank political alliance, or participating in a deeply suspicious research partnership."

I blinked once.

Haru: "People need hobbies."

Aira stared at me.

Aira: "That is your reaction?"

Haru: "Should it be different?"

Aira leaned forward.

Aira: "Haru. Half the school thinks the quiet Green Circle transfer student somehow captured the attention of the most admired Yellow Circle girl in Aurelis. Do you understand how socially catastrophic this is for the academy hierarchy?"

Haru: "I think the phrase 'socially catastrophic' may be excessive."

Aira: "It's generous, actually."

Before I could answer, the room shifted in the familiar subtle way it always did when Elira entered.

The pressure wasn't oppressive.
It was gravitational.

I looked up.

She was walking through the dining hall with composed steps, academy mantle resting neatly over her shoulders, sunlight catching along the soft gold of her hair. At her collarbone, her Yellow Circle crest glowed as brightly as ever.

And below it, hidden in plain sight beneath the appearance of ordinary enchantment, rested the black necklace I had made for her.

My gaze lingered for half a second too long.

Aira saw.
She saw.

Aira: "There it is again."

Haru: "What?"

Aira: "That exact expression."

Haru: "I have no idea what you mean."

Aira: "You really should stop saying that to people who can observe."

Elira reached our table and looked between us, already aware of the atmosphere around the hall.

Elira: "Good morning."

Aira: "That depends on how much you enjoy being the center of a very dramatic school myth."

Elira sat down beside me as if the choice were perfectly ordinary.

Which, for her, made it more effective.

Elira: "I've heard some of it."

Haru: "Only some?"

Elira: "Enough to know people are very committed to fiction when denied facts."

Aira pointed at both of us.

Aira: "You are both being much too calm about this."

Elira looked genuinely curious.

Elira: "Should we be upset?"

Aira: "Not upset. At least embarrassed."

Elira glanced at me.
Then back at Aira.

Elira: "I don't feel especially embarrassed."

Aira turned slowly toward me.

Aira: "And you?"

Haru: "No."

Aira leaned back in her chair as if she had just been personally defeated by composure.

Aira: "Unbelievable."

Elira's lips curved faintly.

Elira: "If people want to talk, they will talk. I don't see much value in giving them fear as well."

Haru: "Agreed."

Aira looked between us in mounting disbelief.

Aira: "You agreed too quickly."

Haru: "It was a simple point."

Aira: "That is not what I mean."

Elira lowered her gaze briefly to her tea, hiding a small smile.

I chose not to comment.

It was strategically wise.

Probably.

Around us, the dining hall's attempt at pretending not to observe had now failed completely. Students from three separate tables were openly listening.

One of the Blue Circle boys near the far column whispered too loudly.

Student 4: "They're not even denying it."

Student 5: "Why would they deny it if it's true?"

Student 6: "No, no, this could still be some terrifyingly calm misunderstanding."

Aira closed her eyes for a second.

Aira: "I would like it officially recorded that I predicted all of this."

Elira: "Repeatedly."

Aira: "Thank you."

Then the academy bells rang.

Conversation shifted reluctantly as students gathered their things and prepared to move toward morning assembly.

Before we could stand, a silver announcement glyph lit up above the hall.

**MANDATORY ASSEMBLY - GRAND AUDITORIUM**  
**ALL YEAR DIVISIONS**

The atmosphere changed instantly.

A mandatory full-school assembly in the middle of the week meant only one of two things.

Disaster.
Or something prestigious enough that the academy wanted the entire student body present for the announcement.

Given Aurelis, the second was more likely.

Aira rose with her tray.

Aira: "Well. I'm sure this will be normal."

Haru: "You don't sound convinced."

Aira: "Because I know where I study."

---

The Grand Auditorium of Aurelis Arcane Academy was designed less like a school hall and more like a ceremonial chamber.

High vaulted ceilings held floating banners of the academy crest. A central stage of white stone and rune-etched black glass stood beneath a suspended light formation shaped like interlocking circles. Tiered seating extended outward in arcs, filling rapidly with students and instructors.

By the time our class found seats, the entire hall was buzzing.

At the front stood Headmaster Caelum Veris, a severe Red Circle scholar with silver hair, layered ceremonial robes, and the composed expression of a man who believed institutions should always look as if they had planned the future in advance.

Beside him stood several senior faculty.
Including Professor Halden.

I noticed him immediately.

Tall, austere, older than most of the other researchers, his gaze sharp behind thin-framed lenses. He wore the insignia of advanced mana studies and the expression of someone whose interest in the world was rarely kind.

His eyes moved briefly across the student body.
Then, for the smallest moment, paused on me.

Not enough for anyone else to notice.
Enough for me.

I kept my expression neutral.

Elira, seated on my other side this time due to the arrangement of our row, sensed the shift in my posture.

She did not look at me.
She did not draw attention.

She simply lowered one hand to the edge of her seat, where our sleeves nearly touched, and let her fingers rest there in silent reassurance.

A small gesture.
Private.
Invisible to everyone except me.

It helped more than I wanted it to.

The Headmaster raised one hand, and the hall fell silent.

Headmaster Veris: "Students of Aurelis Arcane Academy, today we announce an event of considerable significance. As many of you know, once every three years, the continent's leading magical institutions gather for the **Inter-National Schools Tournament**."

The room erupted instantly.

Excitement broke across the hall in a wave. Students sat forward. Instructors exchanged measured looks. Even the more jaded upper years looked interested now.

The Headmaster waited for silence again.

Headmaster Veris: "This tournament is not merely ceremonial. It is the highest competitive proving ground for elite magical academies across participating nations. Schools will compete across multiple categories of skill, tactical application, magical theory, team coordination, and formal combat."

A large projection bloomed above the stage.

Names of famous institutions appeared one after another in floating light.

**Aurelis Arcane Academy**  
**Valemere Royal Institute**  
**Noctis Dominion College**  
**Sol Aeternum Conservatory**  
**Drakenshard Military Academy**  
...and more.

The entire hall was alert now.

When Noctis appeared, the room did not become colder.

It only felt that way.

Students who knew the name shifted in their seats with a tension too practiced to be ordinary rivalry.

Headmaster Veris: "The winning institution will be recognized as the premier school of magical excellence for the next tournament cycle."

Aira leaned toward me at once.

Aira: "There it is."

Haru: "There is what?"

Aira: "The next terrible thing that will happen to our peace."

I considered that.

Haru: "You may be right."

Headmaster Veris continued.

Headmaster Veris: "Participation is selective. Not every student will represent Aurelis. Competitors will be chosen based on demonstrated ability, composure, academic standing, and strategic suitability. Internal qualification rounds begin immediately."

Now the reaction sharpened.
This was no abstract announcement.
It was a challenge.

Students all around us were already calculating.
Rivals. Prestige. Advancement. Visibility. Political capital.

Leon Varek, seated several rows ahead, straightened at once with the posture of someone trying to rebuild a public identity through opportunity.

Several Red Circle students exchanged focused glances. Blue Circle aspirants sat visibly taller. Even some Greens looked caught between fear and hope.

The projection changed again, showing the tournament structure.

**Phase 1: Internal Selection**  
**Phase 2: Delegation Training**  
**Phase 3: Inter-School Competition**

Headmaster Veris: "This year, the tournament will be hosted at **Aurelis Arcane Academy** itself."

The hall nearly exploded.

That changed everything.

It meant international teams would be coming here.
Their strongest students.
Their proudest heirs.
Their champions.

And it meant the eyes of the magical world would be on Aurelis.

Headmaster Veris: "We expect discipline, excellence, and conduct worthy of this institution."

Aira whispered beside me.

Aira: "So naturally we're going to get arrogance, sabotage, and at least three near-political incidents."

Elira's shoulders shook once with quiet laughter.

Haru: "Only three?"

Aira: "I'm being hopeful."

The Headmaster lifted his hand again.

Headmaster Veris: "Further details will be distributed by class division. Students with qualifying potential will receive direct review notices by the end of the day."

That sentence sent a visible current through the room.

Review notices.

In other words, invitations to prove themselves.

The assembly ended amid a storm of excitement.

Groups formed instantly across the auditorium aisles as students began speculating, boasting, worrying, and already comparing likely representatives.

As our row began to empty, Aira looked between me and Elira.

Aira: "I already dislike this tournament and it hasn't even started."

Elira: "That seems premature."

Aira: "No, it seems experienced."

She turned to me.

Aira: "And you are absolutely going to get selected, which means I'll have to watch stronger and stronger people fail to understand why underestimating you is a bad life decision."

Haru: "That's assuming I participate."

Aira stared at me.

Aira: "You won an official combat ladder match by dismantling Leon Varek in front of half the academy. Aurelis will drag you into this with administrative elegance if necessary."

She was probably right.

Which made the day less peaceful than I had hoped.

---

By midday the campus had transformed.

The tournament announcement gave the academy a fresh, electric tension. Training arenas filled faster than usual. Students who normally drifted through routine now walked with sharper purpose. Every hallway conversation seemed to circle back to selection odds, team composition, or rival schools.

And everywhere Elira and I went, the rumors followed.

Not maliciously, at least not always.
Mostly with fascination.

When we crossed the central courtyard together after class, a pair of Red Circle girls near the fountain lowered their voices too late.

Student 7: "They look comfortable."

Student 8: "That's what confirms it for me."

Student 7: "The dating?"

Student 8: "No, the terrifying mutual calm."

Elira heard them.
I heard them.

Neither of us reacted.

After a few steps, she glanced at me.

Elira: "Should we be more subtle?"

Haru: "Would that help?"

Elira considered it.

Elira: "Probably not."

Haru: "Then no."

That earned me a smile.

The kind that had become much harder to ignore since the library.

We walked a little farther in comfortable silence before she spoke again.

Her hand brushed mine once.

Habit almost made me pull away before anyone could see.

I did not.

Instead, I let my fingers close around hers in the middle of the courtyard, where the academy could notice and invent whatever explanation helped it sleep.

Elira: "Does it really not bother you?"

Haru: "The rumors?"

Elira: "Yes."

I thought about it honestly.

Haru: "No."

She watched me for a second.

Elira: "Why?"

Haru: "Because the part that matters is true enough for the talking not to matter much."

That made her stop walking.

Completely.

I stopped too and looked back at her.

For once, Elira seemed caught off guard.

Her expression was soft, surprised, and very slightly flushed beneath the afternoon light.

Elira: "You can't just say things like that in the middle of the courtyard."

Haru: "Why not?"

Elira: "Because I have a reputation for composure."

I looked around at the students pretending not to notice us.

Haru: "You still do."

She laughed under her breath and resumed walking, though now there was a brightness to her expression that stayed for the rest of the path.

Elira: "You are becoming unexpectedly difficult."

Haru: "I'm told I was always like this."

Elira: "By Aira?"

Haru: "Reliable source."

---

The qualification notices arrived before the final afternoon bell.

Golden slips of rune-encoded paper manifested at the desks of students selected for internal review.

Mine appeared with all the subtlety of fate.

So did Elira's.

And Aira's, to her visible alarm.

Aira looked from her notice to mine and then up toward the ceiling as if appealing to the structure of reality for administrative mercy.

Aira: "Absolutely not."

Instructor Dren, who had clearly expected this, folded his arms.

Dren: "Your complaints have been noted and ignored."

Aira: "I'm not even a combat specialist."

Dren: "The tournament includes tactical and support disciplines."

Aira: "That's worse. That means I'm useful."

Dren: "Congratulations on your self-awareness."

A few students laughed.

Leon received a notice too.
He did.

He glanced briefly at mine, jaw tightening only slightly this time. The public humiliation of our last match had not disappeared, but it had matured into something colder. Less theatrical. More competitive.

It was probably more dangerous.

Near the back of the classroom, the curly-haired Green Circle student from breakfast waited until the last notice had finished unfolding.

By then I had heard someone call her Mira Castellan.

Nothing appeared above her desk.

The Green Circle student beside her winced.

Green Student: "I'm sorry."

Mira smoothed one thumb over the edge of her worn notebook.

Mira: "It's fine. I didn't expect it."

Her eyes moved to my notice, then to the way several students stared at it as if a Green Circle name on gold paper had made the room less stable.

Mira's expression did not turn bitter.

That made it harder to ignore.

Mira, softly: "But maybe next time."

After class, the selected students were directed to the central strategy chamber for orientation.

The room was circular, lined with projection crystals and old banners from past Aurelis tournament victories. More than thirty students had been called from different divisions. Reds. Blues. A few Greens. And at the front of the chamber, a projection displayed the event categories.

**Formal Duel**  
**Team Battle**  
**Mana Control Exhibition**  
**Tactical Simulation**  
**Arcane Theory Relay**  
**Adaptive Field Trial**

Aurelis was not just selecting strong students.
It was selecting a symbol of itself.

Professor Halden addressed the room this time.

His voice was smooth, intelligent, and far too interested in how people reacted when he spoke.

Professor Halden: "Skill without discipline is noise. Raw mana without judgment is waste. This tournament rewards complete magicians, not merely powerful ones."

His gaze moved across the room.
Paused on Elira with formal approval.
Paused on several Red Circle heirs with expectation.
Then found me.

Professor Halden: "And occasionally, it rewards anomalies."

A few students shifted, unsure whether that was a compliment.

I understood it as neither.

Just attention.

The kind I did not want.

Then the orientation crystal at the center of the chamber flickered.

Only once.

Most students were already turning toward the doors, too busy with excitement and dread to notice. But I saw the projection stutter beneath the official category list, revealing a second layer of text for less than a heartbeat.

**ANOMALY REVIEW: HARU AERLEN**  
**COMPATIBILITY PRESSURE POINT: ELIRA VALE**  
**OBSERVATION PRIORITY: ELEVATED**

It was not a glitch.

Glitches did not choose names.

The hidden layer vanished before anyone could react.

Professor Halden was no longer looking at the projection.

He was looking at me.

Elira's sleeve brushed mine, the smallest possible question.

I gave the smallest possible answer.

Not here.

We left the chamber with the rest of the selected students, but the orientation did not end when the doors closed behind us.

It followed in fragments.

A Red Circle candidate loudly wondering why a Green transfer had drawn faculty language normally reserved for experimental formations. A Blue Circle upper-year pretending not to stare at Elira's pendant. Two support students debating whether "pressure point" meant romantic leverage, tactical pairing, or both.

Aira caught up with us at the corridor turn and immediately slowed when she saw our faces.

Aira: "That is not your normal quiet."

Haru: "I have several."

Aira: "Yes, and this is the one that makes me want to move furniture in front of doors."

Elira's expression stayed composed, but her voice lowered.

Elira: "Something was hidden in the projection."

Aira did not ask whether we were sure. It was one of the reasons she was dangerous in the useful way.

Aira: "How hidden?"

Haru: "Hidden from students. Not necessarily from faculty."

Aira's eyes shifted toward the strategy chamber doors behind us.

Aira: "Halden?"

Haru: "At least Halden."

For once, Aira did not make a joke immediately. She looked down at the folded qualification slip in her hand as if the paper had become heavier.

Aira: "Then the tournament is not just a tournament for us."

Elira: "No."

The corridor crystal beside us chimed.

Not the bright tone used for class notices.

This one was lower. Formal. Administrative.

A new announcement unfolded across the glass in clean silver script.

**TOURNAMENT SECURITY NOTICE**

**ALL SELECTED CANDIDATES WILL UNDERGO ADDITIONAL MANA SIGNATURE INDEXING BEFORE FINAL TEAM ASSIGNMENT.**

**ANOMALOUS READINGS WILL BE REFERRED TO FACULTY REVIEW.**

Several students passing behind us cheered as if the words meant prestige. To them, extra indexing sounded like one more ceremony, one more proof that they had become important enough for the academy to measure carefully.

To me, it felt like a net being described as ribbon.

Aira read the notice twice.

Aira: "Anomalous readings."

Haru: "Yes."

Aira: "That is a very unfriendly phrase."

Elira's fingers brushed the hidden pendant beneath her collar, not enough for anyone else to notice.

Elira: "It gives faculty permission to ask questions without saying what they are searching for."

Behind us, two Red Circle candidates laughed about who would register highest under combat pressure. A Blue support specialist wondered aloud whether high sensitivity would improve selection chances. No one sounded afraid.

That made it worse.

Systems were easiest to obey when they arrived dressed as opportunity.

The crystal chimed again.

This time the notice added a second line, smaller than the first.

**OBSERVATION WINDOWS MAY OPEN DURING LIVE EVENTS.**

Aira's mouth flattened.

Aira: "Observation windows."

Haru: "Faculty monitoring."

Aira: "It was the optimistic translation. I was asking for the terrifying one."

Elira looked toward the strategy chamber doors.

Elira: "Pressure."

The word settled between us.

Pressure during events. Pressure during indexing. Pressure while students fought, rescued, failed, panicked, protected, and revealed what their bodies knew before their mouths could lie.

For most candidates, that meant performance analysis.

For me, it meant every instinct would have an audience.

And for Elira, standing beside me with a hidden Black Circle protection around her throat, it meant someone had already noticed where to press.

I let my false Green Circle settle into the harmless rhythm I had practiced for years.

Weak enough to be dismissed.
Stable enough to avoid pity.
Ordinary enough to survive inspection.

The problem was that ordinary had become difficult to maintain around people I wanted to protect.

I looked through the corridor windows toward the bright campus outside, where students were already turning selection notices into celebration, rivalry, and ambition.

To them, the world had become larger.

To me, it had become narrower.

Every road now seemed to lead through an arena, an archive, or a room where someone like Professor Halden would ask the right question in the wrong way.

For the first time since the rumors began, they stopped feeling harmless.

Because affection was no longer only gossip.

Someone had started measuring it.


---

Two days after the initial qualification notices, the real selection process began.

Aurelis Arcane Academy felt less like a school and more like a kingdom preparing for war while pretending it was education.

Training grounds filled before sunrise. Strategy rooms stayed occupied between classes. Students who had not qualified watched those who had with mixtures of admiration, resentment, relief, and private calculation. Every corridor carried the same tension:

Who would represent Aurelis?
Who would fail?
Who would embarrass themselves before the world arrived?

And, more quietly now but still persistently:

Were Elira Vale and Haru Aerlen actually together?

That second question remained remarkably durable.

It did not seem to bother Elira.
It did not seem to bother me.
It bothered Aira enough for all three of us.

We were walking toward the central strategy hall when Aira looked from me to Elira and then back to me with the solemn expression of someone rehearsing patience.

Aira: "I would like to say, for the record, that the rumors have now split into factions."

Haru: "It seems organized."

Aira: "Too organized. One group thinks you're already dating. Another thinks you're not officially dating but obviously heading there. A third group thinks you're bound by some ancient mana vow and neither of you has admitted it yet."

Elira's shoulders lifted once with quiet laughter.

Elira: "That last one is impressively specific."

Aira stopped walking.

Aira: "You both really need to stop sounding amused. It encourages them."

Haru: "Would denial help?"

Aira: "No."

Elira: "Then perhaps calm is still the best option."

Aira closed her eyes briefly.

Aira: "You're impossible together."

That sentence might have embarrassed me a week ago.

Now it only felt accurate.

We entered the strategy hall with the rest of the selected students. The room was broader than a normal classroom, circular and tiered, with floating screens displaying tournament categories, prior Aurelis performance rankings, and projected team structures. Faculty lined the outer wall with clipboards, crystal tablets, and expressions ranging from professional interest to ruthless evaluation.

Professor Halden stood at the center.

So did Instructor Dren.
And two additional senior examiners: Lady Seraphine Morn of tactical studies, a severe Red Circle strategist with unnervingly precise diction, and Master Ilyas Thorn, a veteran Blue Circle combat instructor whose face looked carved from endurance and disappointment.

Professor Halden raised one hand, and the low conversation faded.

Professor Halden: "Today begins the internal selection phase. Not all of you will remain in contention. Some will be sorted into support categories. Some into alternate reserves. A select few will become Aurelis' principal delegation."

A display opened behind him.

**SELECTION PHASE - DAY ONE**  
**INDIVIDUAL EVALUATION**  
**TEAM POTENTIAL MAPPING**  
**ADAPTIVE COMPATIBILITY SCORING**

Professor Halden: "Your first evaluations will not be public duels."

That disappointed several students immediately.

Professor Halden noticed.
He did not care.

Professor Halden: "This tournament is won by institutions, not vanity. We are not selecting the loudest students. We are selecting the most effective."

At the edge of the room, Leon stood a little straighter at that-perhaps trying to signal that he, too, now valued effectiveness over spectacle.

I doubted the transformation was complete.

Lady Seraphine stepped forward.

Lady Seraphine: "You will be assessed in three stages today. First, mana control precision. Second, tactical responsiveness under altered conditions. Third, provisional team integration. Every score matters."

Aira leaned slightly toward me.

Aira: "I dislike every phrase she just used."

Haru: "You dislike competence when it's pointed at you."

Aira: "That is a hostile interpretation of my personality."

Elira, on my other side, lowered her voice just enough for only us to hear.

Elira: "It's also accurate."

Aira gave her a wounded look.

Aira: "You're supposed to be kind."

Elira: "I am. It was very gentle."

The first evaluation took place in the **Lattice Chamber**, a long, white training hall divided by suspended crystal nodes. Each student stood on a marked platform while mana-sensitive targets floated through shifting patterns around them. The objective was not power.

It was exactness.

A student had to strike, redirect, shield, or stabilize the targets according to rapidly changing instructions projected above the chamber.

Too much force cost points.
Too slow a reaction cost points.
The wrong spell choice cost points.

This was the kind of test that embarrassed prodigies who relied on rank alone.

One by one, students were called.

Cassian: "Then we lose time."

Leon turned sharply.

Leon: "We are carrying the objective."

Elira looked at the construct, then at the route, then at us.

Her expression did not change, but the air around her warmed by one degree.

Elira: "If this were real, we would not leave them."

Leon: "It is not real."

Haru: "The evaluators are watching whether that matters."

That stopped him.

For a heartbeat, Leon looked exactly like someone standing between the person he had been and the teammate he was not yet sure he could become.

Aira's voice softened without losing its edge.

Aira: "If we rescue wrong, we fail both objectives. If we ignore it, we tell the faculty what kind of victory we are willing to accept."

The timer hit forty-seven seconds.

No time for a speech.

Good.

Speeches were often what people used when action frightened them.

Haru: "Cassian, break the beasts from the outside only. Do not touch the construct's barrier. Leon, bridge the ravine with water tension under Aira's stabilizer. Elira, keep the relic core shielded from the rebound. Aira, call collapse timing."

For once, no one argued.

Cassian moved first, crossing the broken bridge with three explosive steps and striking the nearest shadow beast so cleanly it shattered without throwing debris into the construct. Leon threw a water line under the bridge and hardened it into a temporary path. Aira layered fine support geometry over his work, teeth clenched in concentration.

Elira stepped beside me, gold light closing around the relic core.

Elira: "You included yourself nowhere."

Haru: "I am holding the problem."

Elira: "You are part of the team."

The sentence landed faster than I expected.

Haru: "Then I am holding the center."

Her eyes softened for half a second.

Then the ravine wall cracked.

Aira: "Collapse in five."

Leon: "Cassian!"

Cassian caught the false student by the back of the uniform and threw them toward the bridge with more precision than gentleness. Aira caught the construct in a water-cushioned barrier. Leon pulled. Elira sealed the backlash around the relic core.

I stepped onto the cracked bridge last.

The ravine tried to eat the path under me.

For one dangerous instant, the Black Circle offered the simplest solution.

Erase the collapse.
Rewrite the bridge.
Make the simulation remember a safer world.

I did none of those things.

I used one Green-level reinforcement pulse at exactly the correct joint and let the broken bridge survive long enough to be crossed by people who had earned the rescue themselves.

We reached the far side as the ravine folded shut behind us.

The construct dissolved into silver light.

**RESCUE NODE CLEARED**

Leon stared at the fading marker, breathing hard.

Leon: "That cost us twelve seconds."

Aira: "It also added rescue credit."

Cassian rolled his shoulders, expression sharper now.

Cassian: "Worth it."

Leon looked as if he wanted to disagree.

Then he looked at Elira.

At Aira.

At the path we had not abandoned.

Leon: "Next time, call the bridge sooner."

It was not an apology.

It was more useful than one.

Three hidden mines beneath the southern ledge.
A collapsing bridge illusion masking a safe path to the right.
A delayed strike array embedded in the false exit gate.

I caught the first two.

I almost missed the third because Elira's shield flickered at the edge of my vision and my attention went to her before the field.

Leon saw my eyes shift.

Leon: "Exit gate-right seam."

I cut the warning short with a nod and adjusted the route.

We adapted.
Again and again.

By the time we crossed into the recovery zone with seventeen seconds remaining, the entire observation gallery had fallen silent.

We had won not because we were strongest, but because we had refused to choose between victory and each other.

The recovery zone was one breath away, and beyond it waited the kind of tournament that would make that refusal costly.

---

By the time we left the simulation sector, the western courtyard had filled with the controlled noise of visiting-school preparations.

Valemere Royal Institute arrived before sunset in silver-blue precision: polished carriages, clean banners, escort mages who bowed to Aurelis faculty instead of pretending not to measure them. Their magic did not press against the air the way Noctis rumors did. It moved like formal footwork. Graceful. Dangerous. Honest about both.

At the head of their student delegation stood Soren Ashvale.

He was taller than most of the students around him, dark-haired, warm-eyed, and carried himself like a duelist who had learned that confidence did not require making other people smaller. Faint scars crossed the backs of his hands. He wore them openly.

When he saw Elira, he approached with a respectful distance already built into his stride.

Soren: "Lady Vale."

Elira's public composure settled around her at once.

Elira: "Mr. Ashvale."

Soren smiled, not possessive. Not familiar.

Soren: "I've followed your work in the junior circuit. Your paper on layered reinforcement changed how Valemere teaches counter-pressure in formal dueling."

Elira's surprise lasted only a heartbeat, but I saw it.

Elira: "That is generous."

Soren: "It is accurate."

His gaze moved to me. Not dismissive. Assessing, yes, but cleanly.

Soren: "Your partner?"

Elira did not hesitate.

Elira: "Yes. Haru Aerlen."

The word partner moved through me with more force than it should have.

Soren offered his hand.

Soren: "Lucky man."

His grip was firm, honest, and without challenge.

Haru: "Yes."

Elira's fingers found mine before I could decide whether the answer had been too revealing.

Soren noticed.

He did.

He only smiled and released my hand.

Soren: "Then I look forward to facing Aurelis properly."

He returned to Valemere without looking back.

At Valemere, honor had not been taught to Soren as ornament. It was not posture, lineage, or the polite cruelty of winning cleanly enough that no one could object.

It was responsibility.

I knew why because Aira had made me read the junior-circuit report before Valemere arrived. Years earlier, a defeated student had stayed down longer than the official record admitted. Soren had been young enough to keep his place, frightened enough to call that caution, and honest enough to condemn the choice later in his own appeal. The scars across his hands came from the rescue spell he cast after the judges had already declared the match finished.

His written vow had been plain: honor meant ensuring the fight remained fair, especially when the powerful could make unfairness look like victory. Watching him step away from Elira without trying to turn courtesy into leverage, I believed he still meant it.

Aira appeared beside us as if summoned by social tension.

Aira: "It was the most polite romantic threat I have ever witnessed."

Haru: "He was not threatening."

Aira: "No. That is what made it effective."

Elira's thumb moved once against my hand.

Not reassurance.

Recognition.

For one embarrassing second, jealousy tried to become useful information. It failed. Soren had admired Elira without trying to claim her. He had recognized us without trying to test whether we were real.

That kind of honor was inconvenient.

It made Lucien's eventual arrival feel colder before he had even stepped through the gates.

## Coda: Mira's First Page

Mira Castellan did not mean to start a record.

She meant to finish her herbology notes, avoid the louder tournament students, and stop thinking about the way Haru Aerlen's false Green Circle held its shape too perfectly when Soren Ashvale shook his hand.

False was the wrong word. Mira crossed it out.

Careful, she wrote instead.

The library lamp above her table flickered once. Not from power loss. From recognition. The old glass shade briefly reflected five circles where there should have been four, the lowest one dark enough that the ink on her page looked pale.

Mira froze.

Across the room, two Blue Circle students complained about Green witnesses being allowed near tournament review files. Mira dipped her pen again before anger could make her handwriting ugly.

Observed: Haru Aerlen avoids victory language. Elira Vale says partner without hesitation. Visiting champion Soren Ashvale notices both and does not challenge.

She hesitated, then added one more line.

The academy noticed too.

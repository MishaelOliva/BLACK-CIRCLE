# Episode 14: **The Vigil**

The infirmary clock struck three before I understood that the day had continued without my permission.

Elira slept on Maelin's cot beneath three warming cloths, one hand open near the edge as if even unconscious she expected someone to need proof that she had returned by choice. Cael had been carried to the eastern preparation room under Rhea's seal. Leon went with him. Nera and Oric went too. I had remained because Maelin asked me to hold the Black Circle still until Elira's channels stopped trying to remember death.

The circle no longer filled the chapel, but its reflection turned behind me in every dark pane. Hiding it was impossible now. Letting it exist without becoming the only fact in the room required more concentration than breaking the room would have.

Aira sat beneath the infirmary clock with two notebooks open across her knees. One held the clean sequence. The other held handwriting that leaned harder whenever she was angry.

She closed the second notebook when she saw me watching the relay windows brighten beyond the ward glass.

Haru: "How much happened outside?"

Aira: "Enough that answering with a number would be dishonest."

She studied my face, then turned both notebooks backward through the hours I had survived without seeing.

Aira: "You held Elira's space. We held everything trying to enter it."

Haru: "Start where I stopped looking."

Her finger settled on the first public relay entry.

Aira: "Midmorning."

---

The world could not wait for us to finish grieving. The relay windows had shown too much.

Every academy, council branch, and public record office with a functioning crystal had seen the Black Circle, Elira's death, Cael's choice, Noctis' coercive doctrine, and the old Aurelis warning not to isolate the safeguard.

By midmorning, messages had gathered above the Wounded City chamber like storm clouds made of formal language.

Rhea did not read them aloud at first; she sorted them. Fear made some people reach for chains. Rhea reached for categories.

Requests arrived for medical status, witness confirmation, Noctis comment, Black Circle containment—and Black Circle worship.

That last category made Aira put both hands over her face.

Aira: "No. Absolutely not. We are not doing myth clubs today."

Cassian set another stone aside.

Cassian: "Only today?"

Aira: "Do not make me assign you to public relations while I am this emotionally available to violence."

Cassian looked at Cael.

His smile did not come.

Cassian: "Fair."

Rhea dismissed the worship requests without comment and archived every containment request.

I noticed.

So did Elira.

President Valmont: "Evidence."

Her voice was flat.

President Valmont: "Anyone who thought the first official response to a grieving student should be containment may explain that instinct in daylight later."

Mira wrote that down.

Finn's mouth bent in a grim line.

Finn: "Green Circle students are copying the archive twice."

Rhea looked up.

President Valmont: "Three times."

Finn blinked.

President Valmont: "Institutions misplace inconvenient paper. Students misplace nothing when annoyed."

Mira's pencil paused.

Mira: "Was that a compliment?"

Rhea: "It was an instruction."

Mira wrote faster.

The next message came sealed in silver and violet.

Inter-National Schools Council.

Magistra Senna Voss.

The chamber changed when her name appeared, though neither trust nor the discomfort of her surname had become simple.

A council registry line opened beneath her name before anyone could weaponize the question.

**SENNA VOSS: NORTHERN CIVIL SERVICE LINE**

**NO DECLARED BLOOD OR HOUSE AFFILIATION WITH LUCIEN VOSS**

That did not make suspicion vanish.

It gave suspicion a place to stand where it could be watched.

Because she had been sent to observe risk and had watched a student die proving where the risk lived.

The message unfolded into a recorded statement.

Senna stood in a council office with no decoration behind her except a wall of floating case files. Her hair was pinned neatly. Her face looked older than it had two days earlier.

Magistra Voss: "This preliminary report is entered under emergency witness priority."

Her eyes moved once, as if reading from a text she had already memorized and hated needing.

Magistra Voss: "I was dispatched to evaluate institutional instability surrounding Aurelis, Noctis Dominion College, and the contested Foundation Witness Trial."

The file panes behind her brightened.

Magistra Voss: "What I witnessed was not a spontaneous threat event. It was a sequence."

Mira's pencil stopped for the first time all morning.

Magistra Voss: "A student under Noctis doctrine was targeted through reclamation pressure. A second student intervened to prevent that harm. Dominion Severance killed her. Votive Return, a doctrine tied to fatal conduit expenditure, restored her at the cost of Cael Nox's life. The Black Circle manifested after the death event, under visible grief pressure, and did not attack witnesses despite ample capacity to do so."

The Black Circle behind me dimmed by a degree because sequence had weight, not because it liked being described.

Magistra Voss: "The risk before this Council is not the existence of power beyond Yellow classification. The risk is the institutional reflex to isolate, claim, spend, or destroy students when ordinary categories fail."

Rhea's hand closed around the edge of her sleeve.

Magistra Voss: "I recommend immediate protection of all Aurelis witnesses, suspension of Noctis final conduit clauses across member jurisdictions pending full investigation, emergency review of Dominion Severance as unlawful coercive magic, and explicit prohibition on isolated classification proceedings concerning Haru Aerlen."

She paused.

For one moment, the formal mask thinned.

Magistra Voss: "If the Council's first act after Cael Nox's death is to ask how to contain the student he died beside, my resignation will be submitted before sunset."

The message ended.

Silence followed.

Aira wiped her face with both hands.

Aira: "I take back three unkind things I said about her eyebrows."

Leon stared at the empty air where Senna's record had been.

Leon: "Only three?"

Aira: "I am grieving, Leon. Let me be generous in stages."

This time the almost-laughter reached more than one person.

It did not make anything better.

It made the room less impossible to breathe in.

---

Noctis had answered at noon, initially without Lucien.

The official notice arrived in black script with a pale border.

**NOCTIS DOMINION COLLEGE REQUESTS RETURN OF STUDENT REMAINS**

**SUBJECT: CAEL NOX**

**STATUS: NOCTIS ENROLLED CONDUIT SUPPORT**

The Black Circle snapped into focus.

Every rune lit at once.

The chamber temperature dropped.

Several healers stepped back before they could stop themselves.

I did not blame them.

My first thought was uncontrolled: I could erase not the notice, but the idea behind it.

The whole old grammar that kept finding new ways to call a dead boy useful.

Elira's hand slid over mine.

Her palm was cold.

Elira: "Haru."

One word again.

My name was doing too much work today.

I breathed in.

The cold metal taste behind my sternum sharpened.

I breathed out.

The notice remained.

So did I.

Rhea stood.

The red seal over Cael brightened until it cast clean light across the stone.

President Valmont: "Request denied."

Her answer wrote itself beneath the Noctis notice without delay or committee language.

**AURELIS ANSWER: DENIED**

**CAEL NOX IS RECORDED AS PROTECTED WITNESS OF AURELIS**

**FINAL PUBLIC AMENDMENT: HIS CHOICE IS NOT RETURNABLE PROPERTY**

Rhea looked at Nera and Oric.

President Valmont: "Do either of you contest the wording?"

Nera's face had gone very still.

Nera: "No."

Oric's throat moved.

Oric: "No."

Rhea looked at me next, not for permission but warning.

President Valmont: "Haru?"

I looked at Cael.

At the flowers by his feet.

At Leon's hands, still curled as if they remembered holding weight.

At Elira breathing beside me because Cael no longer did.

Haru: "Leave the notice visible."

Aira turned toward me.

So did Halden.

Haru: "If they asked for him like that, people should see it."

The Black Circle slowed, directed rather than soothed.

Rhea's expression shifted by a fraction.

President Valmont: "Agreed."

She archived the request in the public record.

Mira copied it three times.

The third copy tore where her pencil pressed too hard.

Finn taped the tear with a strip from his own notebook.

That small act nearly undid me, perhaps because it was the opposite of resurrection: no ancient doctrine or celestial ring, just paper breaking under pressure and someone deciding it was still worth preserving.

---

We learned about Noctis' internal fracture through a leak before we learned through official channels.

That felt appropriate.

Noctis official channels had spent too long polishing cruelty until it reflected authority.

The leaked relay carried no image, only audio and a transcript that flickered as if someone had copied it in a hurry.

Darian Thorne's name appeared at the top.

**STATUS: HOUSE RESTRICTION**

**DISCIPLINARY HOLD PENDING COUNCIL REVIEW**

Nera inhaled sharply.

Oric closed one hand around the other wrist.

The audio began halfway through a sentence.

Darian Thorne: "-then charge me with disloyalty in a public room. Do not do it behind a sealed door and pretend the students cannot hear cowardice through stone."

A second voice answered.

Noctis Councilor: "You assisted Aurelis in obstructing a sanctioned reclamation proceeding."

Darian Thorne: "I assisted a child in saying no."

Noctis Councilor: "Cael Nox was not a child in the legal sense."

Darian Thorne: "How convenient for everyone who used him like one."

The transcript trembled, then recovered.

Another voice entered.

Lucien.

Lucien: "You are enjoying moral clarity now that the cost has already been paid by someone else."

Darian Thorne: "And you are still trying to sound above a grave."

Silence.

Even through damaged audio, it had shape.

Lucien: "Cael made himself proof."

Nera's fingers dug into the black cloth at her knee.

Darian Thorne: "No. That is the part you cannot bear. He made himself unavailable to your proof."

The audio cracked.

Lucien: "The Black Circle exists. The world cannot unsee it."

Darian Thorne: "The world also saw why it appeared."

Noctis Councilor: "Enough."

Darian Thorne: "No. Not enough. That has been the problem."

The leak ended.

No one moved for several seconds.

Then Oric whispered:

Oric: "He will be punished for that."

Nera's voice came out rough.

Nera: "He knew."

I thought of Cael warning us that Lucien had patience.

I thought of Darian speaking too late and still speaking.

I thought of how many people were learning that courage after failure still mattered, even when it could not raise the dead.

Elira looked at Nera.

Elira: "We preserve the transcript."

Nera nodded.

Nera: "And we answer it."

Aira blinked.

Aira: "Now?"

Nera looked toward Cael.

Nera: "Now."

She stood slowly, every movement precise because if she moved carelessly she might fall apart.

Then she faced the public record.

Nera: "Darian Thorne did not save Cael Nox in time. Neither did I. Neither did Oric Sen. That failure is part of the record."

Oric flinched.

Nera kept going.

Nera: "But late witness is not worthless witness. Noctis teaches that failure cancels testimony. That is another way to keep failed students silent. We reject that doctrine."

Oric stood beside her.

His voice shook.

Oric: "We reject that doctrine."

The Foundation pool answered.

**DOCTRINE CHALLENGE RECORDED**

Cael did not move.

Of course he did not.

The sentence mattered anyway.

---

Outside the chamber, Aurelis became a city trying to lower its voice around a wound.

We did not see most of it directly.

We heard pieces.

Healers moving in pairs because no one wanted to be alone in the old corridors.

Students gathering in stairwells instead of classrooms, whispering Black Circle in tones that made the words either prayer or panic.

Faculty arguing over whether lessons were suspended or whether normalcy itself was a protective measure.

Notice crystals turning black and silver, then gold, then back again as old systems and current administration fought politely over design priority.

At some point near midafternoon, a message from Drakenshard arrived carved in heavy red-gold script.

Mara Kest's seal burned at the bottom.

Rhea read it aloud because the wording was too blunt to be mishandled by summary.

President Valmont: "Drakenshard Martial Academy acknowledges the courage of Cael Nox, the restraint of Haru Aerlen, and the dishonor of doctrines that force students to become weapons against their own consent."

Cassian stopped moving stone.

President Valmont: "We do not fear power. We fear cruelty wearing rank. Drakenshard will not support containment of the Black Circle bearer without evidence of hostile action. We will support investigation of Noctis practices with full witness access."

Aira stared.

Aira: "That is the politest punch I have ever heard."

Cassian's mouth twitched.

Cassian: "Drakenshard punches in complete sentences."

The Valemere message followed ten minutes later.

Soren Ashvale appeared this time, not as a formal council recording but as a live relay. He stood in a Valemere medical hall. Behind him, Talen Mire sat in a chair with his braced hand resting on his knee.

Talen looked exhausted.

He also looked like someone who had insisted on being present.

Soren bypassed Rhea and me and bowed first to Cael.

Soren: "Valemere Royal Institute records Cael Nox as a witness whose final act confirmed a pattern of Noctis harm previously denied in Talen Mire's case."

Talen lifted his braced hand.

Talen: "I did not know him."

His voice was soft.

The whole chamber leaned toward it without moving.

Talen: "But I know what it means when your pain is called procedure. I know what it means when adults tell your family that naming cruelty will ruin your future more than surviving it already did."

He swallowed.

Talen: "Cael Nox should be alive. I am grateful. I am angry. Both are true."

Elira bowed her head.

Talen looked at her.

Talen: "Elira. I am glad you are alive."

Elira's hand tightened around mine.

Elira: "I am sorry for the cost."

Talen's eyes shifted to Cael.

Talen: "So am I."

The relay dimmed, but Soren spoke once more.

Soren: "Valemere will attend the funeral if permitted."

Rhea answered immediately.

President Valmont: "Permitted."

Then she added, because Rhea Valmont understood power as placement:

President Valmont: "Witness seating will be public."

Soren's expression changed.

Respect, I thought.

And grief.

Soren: "Understood."

The relay closed.

Elira's shoulders sagged.

Professor Valen reached for her again.

Elira: "I know."

Professor Valen: "Do you?"

Elira looked annoyed for half a second, and the relief of seeing any ordinary expression on her face almost hurt.

Elira: "No."

Professor Valen: "Good. Then let us begin with honesty. You need rest before sunset."

Elira looked at Cael.

Professor Valen's voice softened.

Professor Valen: "Rest here if you must. But rest."

Aira was already moving.

She took command of two council aides, three blankets, one privacy screen, and a heated basin with the exhausted fury of a person who had decided logistics could stand in for screaming.

Aira: "Fine. We are making a medical corner. Nobody gets symbolic about it. Elira can mourn lying down."

Elira did not argue.

That frightened me more than some of the magic had.

---

I thought the Black Circle would fade when Elira slept.

It did not.

It dimmed, but it remained behind me, turning with patient inevitability.

The old record-echo appeared while the chamber was at its quietest.

Liora Aerlen-Valen did not arrive with ceremony.

One moment there was only dust, broken stone, and the sound of Elira's shallow sleeping breaths from behind the privacy screen.

The next, a woman made of amber record-light stood near the pool with her hands folded at her waist.

Halden saw her first.

Of course he did.

His pen stopped moving.

Professor Halden: "Record echo."

Liora's gaze moved from him to Rhea, then to Professor Valen, then to me.

Liora: "Not record only."

Professor Valen's face went still in a way I could not read.

Liora looked at Cael.

When she spoke again, her voice lowered.

Liora: "The dead should not need to become architecture."

The Black Circle shifted.

It recognized her.

Or recognized the old language in her.

Liora stepped closer to the edge of its pressure.

Liora: "You are waiting for a decision."

I knew she meant the circle.

I also knew she meant me.

Haru: "I do not know what decision."

Liora: "Wall or witness."

Aira looked over from the medical corner, eyes sharp despite exhaustion.

Liora: "The Black Circle can close around grief and become a wall no one can reach through. Many will encourage that. Some through fear. Some through worship. Some because walls are easier to administer than wounded people."

Rhea's mouth tightened.

Liora: "Or it can remain answerable to witnesses."

Haru: "Answerable is dangerous."

Liora: "So is unanswerable."

The words were simple.

They entered the room like a key entering an old lock.

I looked at Cael.

Then at the blanket screen behind which Elira slept because her body remembered being absent.

At Aira's torn map corner tucked into her sleeve.

At Leon's empty hands.

At Nera's flowers.

At Mira's pages.

At Halden's corrected notice.

At Rhea's red seal.

Haru: "I do not want to be a wall."

The Black Circle stilled.

For one breath, all the opposing rings aligned.

Deep black.

Silver.

Constellation-points.

Order so precise it could have become terror if left alone.

Haru: "If it tries to close around me, tell me."

Aira's answer came immediately.

Aira: "I will."

Then her face tightened.

Aira: "But you do not get to make me your permanent alarm bell. If I am tired, someone else speaks. If I am wrong, you argue. If I say I cannot carry it that day, you believe me."

The condition hurt.

That was how I knew it belonged there.

Haru: "I will practice."

Aira: "Good. Because you are going to be terrible at it."

Cassian looked at the rings around me.

Cassian: "If it becomes a wall, I can set something on fire outside it."

Leon: "That is not a plan."

Cassian: "It is the beginning of several."

Leon opened his mouth to promise something cleaner.

His eyes found Cael's empty place.

The promise died before it could make Leon feel noble. He bent instead, picked up the healer cloth that had fallen beside the seal, and folded it with hands that had once shoved a Green student for standing in the wrong place.

Nera touched the old binding scar at her wrist.

Nera: "If Noctis language starts sounding comfortable..."

She could not finish.

Oric put his exit notes beside her flowers. Not a pledge. Evidence that he meant to remain reachable.

Mira lifted her pencil.

Mira: "I can write what happens."

She looked at Aira, reconsidered, and added:

Mira: "Unless I cannot. Then someone else keeps the page."

Rhea broke her emergency seal into three visible witness marks and assigned one to the student rail, one to faculty, and one to medical custody. She offered me none.

That was her answer.

Halden adjusted his cuffs.

Professor Halden: "I reserve the right to distrust both your power and my interest in it."

Professor Valen: "There he is."

Halden looked offended. Mira wrote the sentence anyway, then beneath all of it added:

**WITNESSES MAY REFUSE THE ROLE AND REMAIN WITNESSES.**

The Black Circle faded by half.

Not gone.

Hidden was no longer the right word.

Resting, maybe.

Listening.

Liora's record-light softened.

Liora: "Good."

I looked at her.

Haru: "Is that enough?"

She glanced at Cael.

Liora: "No."

The answer was gentle.

That made it worse.

Liora: "But it is the kind of not enough that can become a beginning."

Then she vanished without final prophecy or clean comfort, leaving one more absence in a room already full of them.

---

By late afternoon, the record and the day caught up with each other. President Valmont returned from the emergency council session.

She had been gone for forty-two minutes.

In that time, the chamber had changed from aftermath to vigil.

Students had brought practical lamps, not ceremonial ones, because the old chamber had shadows and no one liked the thought of Cael lying in the dark.

Then someone brought white ribbon.

Then nightglass flowers.

Then a folded Aurelis cloak.

Then a page copied from Mira's notebook:

**HE WAS NOT A TOOL. HE WAS A WITNESS WHO CHOSE MERCY AFTER BEING SAVED BY IT.**

No one knew who placed it by the pool.

Everyone pretended not to look for the person.

Rhea approached Cael and knelt again.

Her uniform was immaculate except for dust on one knee from the first time she had knelt.

She had not brushed it off.

President Valmont: "The council has authorized full public honors."

Nera's hand closed around the black cloth.

Rhea continued.

President Valmont: "Cael Nox will be recognized as Protected Witness of Aurelis, deceased by voluntary activation under catastrophic coercive pressure. His testimony and final choice will remain in the public Foundation record."

Elira had woken during the first sentence.

She pushed herself upright before Professor Valen could stop her.

Elira: "When?"

Rhea looked at her.

President Valmont: "Tomorrow at sunset. Grand Courtyard."

A murmur moved through the rail. Public, not hidden under the council tower, where Noctis jurisdiction could bury it and Foundation grammar could seal him somewhere only scholars and cowards argued safely.

Elira looked at Cael's face.

Her voice came out raw.

Elira: "I'll never be ready."

She swallowed.

Elira: "But he should not have to wait for us to become ready."

Rhea bowed her head.

President Valmont: "Noctis has been informed that they may attend as witnesses only."

Nera looked up sharply.

President Valmont: "They may not claim him. They may not speak over his record. They may not approach his body without Aurelis permission and witness consent."

Oric's voice was thin.

Oric: "Lucien?"

The chamber seemed to tighten around the name.

Rhea's eyes hardened.

President Valmont: "Lucien Voss is not cleared for physical attendance."

That was not the same as safety.

Everyone knew it.

Aira rubbed both eyes.

Aira: "I miss when attendance lists were boring."

Cassian: "Were they ever?"

Aira: "I have decided to remember them that way for emotional contrast."

Elira reached for me.

I moved before she needed to ask.

Sitting beside her felt like crossing a battlefield after the battle had ended but before anyone had counted the cost.

She leaned her shoulder against mine.

Very lightly.

As if her body could only afford a small amount of trust at a time.

Elira: "He said he wanted to choose what his life meant."

Her voice was quiet enough that only those closest heard.

The Foundation heard too.

Of course it did.

Elira: "I think he did."

She closed her eyes.

Elira: "But I wish he hadn't had to."

The Black Circle pulsed once, faint and wounded.

This time it did not expand.

It stayed with us.

---

Night settled over Aurelis in layers.

The emergency lamps around the chamber glowed gold, blue, red, green, and silver, placed without rank order or Circle arrangement.

People had placed them wherever there was room.

It made the floor look less like a hierarchy and more like a city seen from far above.

Cael lay at the center because we had chosen to gather around him, not because doctrine had made him central.

One by one, the witnesses rested without quite sleeping.

Leon sat with his back against a broken pillar and stared at his hands until Cassian sat beside him without speaking.

Nera and Oric copied Noctis phrases into a correction ledger, translating each one away from ownership.

Aira finally drank water after threatening three other people into drinking first.

Mira slept for fourteen minutes with her face on her notebook while Finn and two Green Circle students kept copying around her.

Rhea stayed awake.

So did Halden.

I pretended not to notice that Professor Valen placed herself between them whenever their silence began turning into an argument.

Elira and I sat beside the restoration pool.

Cael's flowers caught the lamplight and turned silver at the edges.

The Black Circle had faded until only a thin ring remained around my shadow.

Anyone looking at me could still see it if they knew how.

Everyone knew how now.

Haru: "I couldn't stop it."

Elira's head turned slightly.

Haru: "The Black Circle. When you died, I could not hide it. I could not control the first moment. If Cael had not..."

My voice stopped.

There were too many endings to that sentence.

None of them had him alive.

Elira took my hand.

Her fingers were still cold.

Elira: "I know."

Haru: "That is not forgiveness."

Elira: "No."

The answer hurt because she did not soften it.

Then she leaned closer.

Elira: "It is presence. We can work toward the rest."

I looked at Cael.

The words scraped out of me.

Haru: "He saved both of us."

Elira: "Yes."

Haru: "You from staying dead."

Elira's breath hitched.

Haru: "Me from becoming whatever would have been left if I erased the world around your absence."

Elira's hand tightened.

Elira: "Then we remember him honestly."

Haru: "I do not know how to carry that."

Elira's eyes filled again.

She did not look away.

Elira: "Neither do I."

The confession was quiet.

More frightening than confidence.

More comforting too.

Elira: "So we do not turn it into a debt we can never pay. We turn it into choices."

Haru: "What if I choose wrong?"

Elira looked at the lamps around us.

At the sleeping witnesses.

At Aira pretending not to listen and failing because she was Aira.

Elira: "Then someone tells you."

Aira, from three blankets away: "Loudly."

Elira's mouth trembled.

This time, the small sound that left her was almost a laugh.

Almost.

Enough.

The final notice of the day appeared above the pool, dim and uncommanding but present.

**VIGIL RECORDED**

**WITNESSES REMAIN**

**FUNERAL AUTHORIZED: SUNSET**

Then, beneath those lines, the old Foundation added one more.

**SAFEGUARD IS NOT ALONE**

The Black Circle faded from the air behind me. It was never truly gone, but it no longer filled the chamber.

I felt the absence of its visible weight and almost collapsed from the effort of not missing it.

Elira noticed.

She moved closer, shoulder against mine, breath uneven but real.

Cael lay before us in lamplight and flowers.

The Wounded City held the wounded silence of a vigil, without peace or healing.

And for that night, because no one let grief become solitude, the silence held.


---

By morning, the academy had learned to walk around grief clumsily, imperfectly, but carefully.

The corridors above the Wounded City were full of students moving in quiet lines with flowers, copied statements, folded ribbons, and pages from public witness ledgers. No one had told them to bring anything, which was probably why they did. Orders made ceremony; choice made vigil.

Aurelis had become very sensitive to the difference.

Elira slept for three hours before dawn.

Professor Valen called that a medical victory in the tone of someone willing to fight the definition of victory in a corridor afterward. When Elira woke, she looked confused for one second.

Then she remembered.

I saw it happen.

The breath.

The hand to her pendant.

The eyes finding Cael's covered body.

The return of grief as a physical thing.

The Black Circle stirred beneath my skin.

Only beneath.

I pressed my palm flat against the stone and breathed until the old cold behind my sternum stopped asking to become architecture.

Elira saw that too.

Elira: "You are not allowed to use self-control as a substitute for being held."

My hand froze against the floor.

Aira, passing behind us with a tray of tea, did not even slow down.

Aira: "Medical ruling confirmed by non-medical authority."

Professor Valen accepted a cup from the tray.

Professor Valen: "For once, I endorse the non-medical authority."

I looked at Elira.

Haru: "I am sitting next to you."

Elira: "Yes."

Her voice softened.

Elira: "And part of you is standing guard over every possible future where I die again."

The accuracy left me unable to answer quickly enough.

Elira's hand moved over mine.

Still cool.

Still weaker than it should have been.

Elira: "I am frightened too, Haru."

The words were simple.

They entered the room more quietly than any magic.

Elira: "Sometimes I blink and there is no before. No after. Just a place where I stopped. Then I am here again, and everyone looks relieved, and Cael is not breathing."

Her fingers tightened.

Elira: "Do not leave me alone with everyone else's relief."

I turned my hand under hers and held on.

Haru: "I am here."

Elira: "All of you?"

The Black Circle pulsed once under my shirt, ashamed rather than defensive, if power could feel shame.

Haru: "I am trying."

Elira leaned her forehead against my shoulder.

Elira: "Then keep trying where I can reach you."

So I did.

I sat in the old chamber while the academy prepared to bury the boy who had chosen her life.

I let Aira put tea in my hand.

I let Leon take the cup from me when my fingers shook too badly and pretend he was only moving it away from the edge of the stone.

I let Cassian stand close enough that no official could approach from my blind side without passing through him first.

I let Nera and Oric ask me how much Noctis grammar they should preserve for the funeral record, and I answered like a person instead of a weapon.

Only enough that it could neither be erased nor allowed to speak last.

Mira arrived with Finn and a stack of copied witness pages tied in green ribbon.

Her eyes went to the space behind me where the Black Circle had been visible the night before.

Then to my face.

The second look mattered.

Mira: "We made a public sequence packet."

Aira reached for it immediately.

Aira: "Please tell me that title is temporary."

Finn: "It is called Sequence Packet One."

Aira closed her eyes.

Aira: "Green Circle activism has the soul of a filing cabinet."

Mira hugged the packet to her chest.

Mira: "A filing cabinet that stopped Lucien from narrating a corpse."

Aira opened her eyes.

Aira: "I withdraw my complaint and admire the cabinet."

Mira smiled for half a second.

Then she looked at Cael.

Mira: "We added a page where students can write what they witnessed, not what they were told to think."

Rhea, who had entered silently during the exchange, took the top copy and scanned it.

Leon: "Because I held him after. If I keep it, I will never put it down."

The honesty cost him, and he paid it anyway.

Cassian's hand tightened on his shoulder.

Elira looked at me, asking rather than commanding.

I took the notebook.

Its near-weightlessness was also unfair.

Haru: "I will keep it with witnesses."

Aira nodded immediately.

Aira: "Good answer."

Elira's eyes softened.

Elira: "Human answer."

The Black Circle stayed quiet.

For once, that felt like agreement.

---

Long after the crowds dispersed, the core witnesses remained by the fountain: Haru, Elira, Aira, Leon, Cassian, Nera, Oric, Rhea, Professor Valen, Halden, Mira, Finn, Soren, Talen, and Senna.

Too many for privacy.

Too few for the size of the absence.

Cael's body had been moved under witness guard to the eastern hall for preparation. The flowers remained around the fountain because no one had the heart to decide which ones should go with him and which ones should become public memorial.

So Rhea decided none would be moved until morning.

Sometimes authority was useful because grief could not choose table arrangements.

The Noctis banner remained at the fountain's edge.

Cut in half.

That had been Mara Kest.

She had asked permission first.

Rhea had granted it after a five-second legal review conducted entirely through eyebrow movement.

Now one half of the banner lay beside the official record as evidence of attempted claim.

The other half had been folded beneath the nightglass flowers as proof that even Noctis cloth could be made to hold witness instead of ownership.

Aira sat on the fountain rim and looked at the water.

Aira: "He should be here."

No one corrected the sentence's uselessness; that was its purpose.

Leon sat on the ground with his arms over his knees.

Leon: "Yes."

Cassian lowered himself beside him.

Cassian: "He would hate how many people talked about him."

Nera gave a small, wet laugh.

Nera: "He would stand behind someone taller."

Leon looked up.

Leon: "Me."

Aira: "Obviously you. You are structurally useful."

Leon almost smiled, then did not. That was all right.

Nera looked at the fountain.

Nera: "He used to ask questions after Lucien left rooms."

Oric nodded.

Oric: "Never during."

Nera: "No. Never during."

She touched the edge of the stone.

Nera: "The first question he asked me that was not about orders was whether Aurelis students got to choose their electives."

Aira's face crumpled.

Aira: "That is a horrible anecdote."

Nera: "I know."

Aira: "Tell another."

Nera thought.

Her voice softened.

Nera: "He liked cinnamon bread."

Oric looked startled.

Nera: "He pretended not to because Noctis dining monitors marked sugar preference as indulgence if you took it too often."

Elira closed her eyes.

Elira: "Then we add cinnamon bread to the memorial table."

Rhea immediately made a note without debate or symbolic committee: cinnamon bread for Cael.

That almost broke us more than the speeches had.

Talen spoke from beside Soren.

Talen: "At Valemere, after injury hearings, they serve bitter tea because they think sweetness makes testimony less dignified."

Mira looked offended.

Mira: "That is ridiculous."

Talen: "Yes."

He looked at the fountain.

Talen: "I would like cinnamon bread too, if that is permitted."

Aira pointed at Rhea.

Aira: "Put him down for bread."

Rhea wrote it.

Senna watched all of this with an expression I could not name.

Maybe because council reports did not usually include bread as evidence that a dead student had been a person.

Maybe they should have.

Halden stood slightly apart, arms folded.

Mira noticed.

Mira: "Professor?"

He looked at her.

Mira: "Did Cael ever ask you anything?"

Halden's expression changed by a degree.

Professor Halden: "Once."

Everyone looked at him.

He clearly regretted answering.

Too late.

Professor Halden: "After the formal review, he asked whether old systems could tell the difference between a conduit and a witness."

Nera's face tightened.

Professor Halden: "I told him systems could be taught. People were harder."

Aira: "That is bleak."

Professor Halden: "It was accurate."

Professor Valen looked at him.

Professor Halden's jaw shifted.

Professor Halden: "He said, 'Then I hope the system learns first.'"

The fountain water rippled.

No one spoke for a while.

Then Mira wrote it down.

Of course she did.

---

Near midnight, after Professor Valen finally threatened Elira with supervised carrying if she refused to sleep, I returned to the fountain almost alone. Aurelis had learned better.

Leon was on the western steps pretending not to guard me.

Cassian was on the east path doing the same thing with more style.

Aira had left a note on the fountain rim that read:

**DRINK WATER OR I WILL BECOME A PROCEDURAL PROBLEM.**

I drank the water beside it.

The Black Circle pulsed beneath my shirt, small and steady, no longer pretending the world did not know.

I opened Cael's notebook to the first page again.

Things I choose.

The handwriting was careful enough to hurt.

I did not turn to the last page yet.

The fountain reflected the moon, the flowers, the cut banner, and my face.

For one second, the reflection behind me showed the Black Circle in full.

Deep black.

Silver rings.

Constellation runes moving in opposite directions with impossible order.

Then the reflection changed.

Cael stood beside me in the water—not truly a ghost, perhaps memory, Foundation residue, or grief making images out of moonlight.

He looked as he had before the final stage, tired and too young and still trying to make himself smaller than the room required.

Cael: "You look terrible."

I closed my eyes.

Haru: "That sounds like something Aira would invent to make me sleep."

Cael: "Probably."

When I opened my eyes, the reflection was only water again.

I did not know whether I had imagined it.

I decided not to ask the Foundation.

Some things did not need to become records immediately.

Elira's voice came from behind me.

Elira: "You left the medical room."

I turned.

She stood in a robe over her uniform, hair loose over one shoulder, Professor Valen's medical seal glowing faintly at her wrist.

Behind her, at a respectful distance, Professor Valen looked deeply irritated and entirely unsurprised.

Haru: "You followed."

Elira: "Slowly. With supervision. Everyone wins."

Professor Valen: "That is not my summary."

Elira ignored that with the confidence of a patient who had chosen selective hearing as a survival tactic.

She sat beside me on the fountain rim, not too close at first and then closer.

Her shoulder touched mine.

Elira: "What happens now?"

I looked at the flowers.

The witness pages.

Professor Valen remained behind us, close enough to intervene and far enough to let the question belong to us first.

Then she said my name in the quieter voice she used when history had stopped being an archive and become a person sitting before her, not Professor Valen's formal witness voice.

Professor Valen: "There is one record I owe you before the Council starts asking for every other one."

I turned.

She folded her hands over the medical case at her side.

Professor Valen: "When your transfer file reached Aurelis, Student Affairs nearly rejected it at ranking review. A Green Circle applicant from Lumeris District with ancient-script scores above several faculty candidates looked either impossible or inconvenient."

The fountain sounded louder for a moment.

Instructor Selene's voice returned from my first morning.

Someone argued strongly in your favor.

Haru: "It was you."

Professor Valen: "I argued that a student should not be punished because his examination answers made adults uncomfortable."

Halden, from the western steps, gave a dry sound.

Professor Halden: "That is a selective summary."

Professor Valen did not look away from me.

Professor Valen: "I also argued that anyone who could translate pre-standardization mercy clauses without being taught them deserved a room with better questions than the entrance board was asking."

My hands had gone still around Cael's notebook.

Haru: "Did you know?"

Professor Valen: "No."

She answered too quickly for comfort and too honestly to doubt.

Professor Valen: "I suspected old knowledge had touched your life somehow. I did not know the name. I did not know the danger. I certainly did not know I was helping bring the Black Circle into my own school."

Aira, from the fountain rim, wiped one eye with her sleeve.

Aira: "For the record, that is the least villainous secret admission sponsorship I have heard today."

Professor Valen's mouth softened.

Professor Valen: "High praise."

I looked back at the water.

At the place where Cael's reflection had been.

Haru: "Why tell me now?"

Professor Valen: "Because people will try to make your arrival sound like a plot. Some will say Aurelis hid you. Some will say I recruited a weapon. Some will say your place here was evidence of conspiracy."

She drew a careful breath.

Professor Valen: "The first true answer is smaller. I saw a student being underestimated by a system that mistook rank for proof, and I objected. If the rest becomes more complicated, let the simple part stay in the record."

Elira's hand found mine.

This time, I let the contact steady me before I had decided whether I deserved it.

Haru: "Thank you."

Professor Valen: "Do not thank me too much. I also failed to ask enough follow-up questions."

Halden: "We all did."

No one argued, and that too became part of the night.

The half-banner.

The notebook in my hands.

Haru: "Noctis investigation. Council reviews. People afraid of me. People wanting to use me. People saying Black Circle like they know what it means."

Elira: "Yes."

Haru: "Lucien is not done."

Elira: "No."

Haru: "You need to heal."

Elira: "Also yes."

Haru: "I do not know how to keep going without making Cael's death into the center of every choice."

Elira was quiet for a moment.

Then she took the notebook from my hands, closed it gently, and placed it between us.

Elira: "Maybe we do not make his death the center."

She looked at the fountain.

Elira: "Maybe we make his choices part of ours."

The Black Circle warmed once beneath my shirt—not heat or exactly comfort, but recognition.

Elira: "He chose to be called by his name."

Haru: "Cael."

Elira: "He chose to say no."

Haru: "Then we make room for no."

Elira: "He chose to have friends."

My throat tightened.

Haru: "Then we stay close."

Elira's hand found mine.

Elira: "He chose to matter when he was not useful."

The fountain water moved around the reflection of the moon.

Haru: "Then we stop asking people to earn care."

Elira leaned against me.

This time with more weight.

Still not enough.

More.

Elira: "There. That is how."

I closed my fingers around hers.

The Black Circle pulsed once beneath my shirt as neither threat nor secret, only reminder.

Power without purpose had almost become destruction.

Power with witnesses, grief, choice, and mercy was not safe.

Nothing worth protecting was ever entirely safe.

But it was answerable.

Cael Nox was gone.

The world had changed.

Noctis was fractured.

The Black Circle was known.

Elira was alive beside me, breathing unevenly but breathing.

And for the first time since the Wounded City broke open, the future did not feel like a verdict already written.

It felt like a page.

Heavy.

Unfair.

Waiting.

Together, we stayed by the fountain until the moon moved past the tower and the flowers turned silver in the dark.

## Coda: Empty Chair

No one removed Cael's chair that night.

Practical reasons existed. The room was crowded. Evidence still needed sealing. Healers were moving too fast to care about furniture. But by midnight, everyone knew the chair remained because no one could bear the sound it would make being dragged away.

Leon sat on the floor beside it instead.

Not in it. Beside.

Cassian came with two cups of tea and gave one to the empty chair before correcting himself with a curse so quiet it hurt more than if he had shouted. Aira wrote nothing for several minutes. Rhea saw that and did not ask why.

Haru stood at the window with Elira's hand in his, both of them alive in ways that felt too expensive.

At dawn, Mira taped a note to the chair.

Reserved does not mean owned.

No one knew whether it was for Cael, for the witness record, or for everyone who had survived him.

No one removed that either.

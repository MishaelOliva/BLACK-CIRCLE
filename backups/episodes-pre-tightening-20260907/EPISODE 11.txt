# Episode 11: **Wounded City Protocol**

Wounded City Protocol opened beneath the old chapel at noon.

That timing was the first insult.

Noctis had spent enough of the Foundation Trial losing in rooms where records could answer back. The Protocol was their reply: move the argument from testimony into active crisis, then dare Aurelis to prove its rescue ethics while everyone watched.

Noctis had chosen a rescue activity for the brightest hour of the day, as if mercy needed good lighting before they would believe in it.

The second insult was the invitation list.

Noctis did not request privacy.

It requested observers.

Valemere.

Two minor academy delegations that had stayed after the tournament to watch the Foundation Trial.

Selected Aurelis faculty.

Student Council recorders.

Green Circle public witnesses.

Noctis called it transparency.

Rhea called it leverage.

Aira called it "setting the room on fire and inviting people to admire the smoke."

The Foundation accepted the observer expansion because Wounded City Protocol was, technically, an activity about public rescue ethics. Refusing witnesses would have made Aurelis look like it feared its own doctrine.

Lucien understood optics.

So Rhea made optics answer back.

Every observer had to sign a witness-conduct line before entering the lower chamber. No private relay tools. No unsupervised mirrors. No delegation correction phrases. No removal of notes. No turning away from harm and later claiming uncertainty.

That last condition made several adults uncomfortable.

Mira noticed.

She stood near the entry with a stack of green-edged forms and a face that said she would rather be swallowed by the floor than challenge a visiting examiner.

Then the first examiner tried to wave the form aside.

Mira's fear remained.

Her hand did not move.

Mira: "Public witness terms require a signature."

Examiner: "I am here as faculty, not a student."

Mira looked down at the form, then back up.

Mira: "Then your handwriting is probably better."

The examiner stared.

Rhea, three paces away, said nothing.

Authority lent weight without stealing the moment.

The examiner signed.

Mira's shoulders dropped only after he entered.

Aira passed behind her and murmured:

Aira: "Excellent use of stationery."

Mira whispered, "I thought I was going to faint."

Aira: "Most civic institutions are built by people not fainting at the last second."

The Foundation chamber below the chapel unfolded itself from old stone and silver mist. Streets rose from the floor. Doorways grew from blank walls. Windows formed without rooms behind them. A bell tower leaned over a square that had never existed and somehow looked remembered.

At the center stood seven wounded markers.

Not dummies.

Not illusions of people we knew.

That would have been too crude after yesterday.

These markers were outlines made of pale light, each one flickering with a different kind of injury: channel collapse, frost binding, mana starvation, memory echo, pressure burn, relay hook, and one empty outline with no visible wound at all.

The seventh made my teeth ache.

Aira looked at it and immediately hated it.

Aira: "Invisible wound marker. Wonderful. We are being examined by architecture with a sadistic streak."

Rhea stood on the command threshold with a red council seal hovering at her shoulder.

President Valmont: "Conditions?"

The Foundation voice answered from the bell tower.

Foundation: "Wounded City Protocol tests response priority under conflicting witness obligations. Participants must extract all viable markers to safe zones while maintaining witness autonomy and preventing unlawful isolation."

Leon flexed his hands.

Leon: "That almost sounds fair."

Nera's answer was flat.

Nera: "It is not."

Lucien appeared on the far side of the square with Magister Rusk and three Noctis observers. He wore formal black again, but today his collar was open at the throat, a small violation of Noctis stiffness that made him look less like a student and more like a blade pretending to be tired.

Lucien: "It is fair enough to make unfairness useful."

Cassian's Red Circle flared.

Cassian: "I am going to enjoy the day you run out of elegant sentences."

Lucien: "Then I hope you are patient."

Elira stepped onto the activity floor beside me.

The pendant under her collar gave one warm pulse.

Then nothing.

Cael entered with Nera on one side and Leon on the other. His healer seal was reinforced, but the silver thread around his injured hand had darkened overnight.

He had slept less than I had.

That was saying something.

Before Aira opened the maps, Cael stepped out of formation.

Only three paces.

Enough that Leon's hand twitched toward him and stopped halfway.

Cael saw it.

Cael: "I am not escaping."

Leon: "I did not say you were."

Cael: "Your shoulder did."

Leon looked offended by the accuracy.

Nera stayed where she was. That was harder for her than moving. Her whole body had been trained to correct distance around injured Noctis students before anyone could accuse her of negligence or attachment.

Cael walked to the edge of the empty seventh marker.

No visible wound.

No light around the outline.

Just absence shaped like a person waiting for a room to decide isolation was an injury.

He looked at it for a long time.

Long enough that Lucien's attention sharpened.

Long enough that Rhea did not interrupt.

Cael: "At Noctis, they call this recovery."

The chamber listened.

Cael did not turn toward the rail. He spoke to the marker because looking at us would have made the sentence ask for comfort.

Cael: "If you are overwhelmed, they remove witnesses. If you are injured, they reduce variables. If you are frightened, they call familiar faces contamination. The room gets quieter every time. Cleaner."

His injured hand flexed once.

The silver thread around it darkened.

Cael: "Eventually quiet starts to feel like proof that you are improving, because if no one is there to make you afraid, you stop shaking where people can see."

No one answered.

The right silence took effort.

Aira's pen hovered above her map and did not move.

Cael looked at me then.

Not at my Green Circle.

At my hands.

Cael: "If the seventh marker opens, I will want to solve it by leaving."

Leon: "No."

Too fast.

Too honest.

Cael almost smiled.

Cael: "That is why I am saying it now."

Nera's voice was thin.

Nera: "Leaving can be a symptom."

Cael nodded.

Cael: "Or a strategy."

Elira stepped closer, not between him and the marker, beside the line he had chosen.

Elira: "Then we ask which one before we obey it."

Cael breathed out.

That single breath seemed to cost him more than half his testimony had.

Cael: "Good."

He returned to formation without apology.

Leon let him.

That mattered almost as much as following him would have yesterday.

Aira took the support mark at the edge of the square and spread three maps in the air.

Aira: "First rule. Nobody chases symbolic bait alone."

Leon: "Define symbolic bait."

Aira: "Anything that looks like your guilt wearing a costume."

Cassian: "That is annoyingly clear."

Mira and the Green Circle witnesses filled the public record rail above the square. Each pair had a timestamp crystal, ink, and a duplicate page.

Mira caught my eye.

She looked terrified.

Then she lifted her pencil.

Ready.

That mattered.

The activity began.

The first marker collapsed before we reached it.

Stone folded inward above the eastern lane. The pale outline beneath the arch dropped to one knee, both hands pressed to a chest where its channel-light had begun stuttering. Each failed pulse darkened another block overhead.

Aira threw the first map into the air.

Aira: "Eastern arch. Leon braces. Cassian clears debris. Elira and Cael assess the marker. Haru watches the lane."

The city disagreed.

Three alleys rotated around us with the grinding shriek of stone dragged across stone. The eastern arch shifted west. The dry fountain rose between our team and the marker. A wall climbed behind Cael before Leon could reach him.

Aira's map remained perfectly still.

Wrong.

She stared at it for half a heartbeat, then crushed the projection in her fist.

Aira: "Map is compromised. Eyes up. Call what you physically see."

That admission saved more time than pretending expertise would have.

Leon slammed one palm toward the dry fountain. Water answered from pipes beneath the square, burst through four stone mouths, and arced over the rising wall. He caught each stream in a separate blue line, then braided them into a brace beneath the arch.

The weight struck.

His knees bent.

Leon: "Now would be a lovely time for everyone else to become competent."

Cassian ran directly at the blocked lane.

Of course he did.

He did not throw flame. The marker was too close and the false city had seeded dust through every crack. One careless spark would turn rescue into cremation. Instead, he heated only the metal pins hidden inside the wall. Red light crawled across his fingers. The pins expanded, cracked their housings, and dropped one by one into the street.

Cassian caught the first in his bare hand before it struck Cael.

The smell of burned leather cut through the dust.

Cael: "Your glove."

Cassian: "Still technically a glove."

He threw the pin aside and drove his shoulder into the loosened stones. Cael joined him without being asked. Together they opened a gap barely wide enough for Elira.

She went through sideways.

The marker's channel-light failed again.

The arch dropped six inches.

Leon's breath broke. Water sprayed from the brace and froze against the street where the second marker's cold had begun leaking into the first lane.

I could have stopped all of it.

The knowledge arrived complete: remove weight from twelve stones, warm three water lines, restore the marker's rhythm with one direct correction. No risk. No delay.

No proof anyone else had been allowed to matter.

The Black Circle pressed once against my ribs.

I tasted metal.

Above us, Mira shouted from the witness rail.

Mira: "The arch has Green maintenance marks! Lower left block!"

Everyone looked where she pointed.

There—a shallow copper groove under centuries of white polish. The academy diagrams had omitted it because maintenance routes were not considered part of civic architecture. A Green Circle handprint would fit the plate exactly.

Mira looked at her own wrist.

Then at the distance between the rail and the street.

Mira: "Can a witness enter the field?"

Rhea's seal flared.

President Valmont: "Only by choice. The route is unstable."

Mira was afraid.

She climbed down anyway.

Not gracefully. Her shoe caught the final rung and she hit the street hard enough to skin one palm. Her pencil rolled beneath the fountain. She looked at the blood on her hand, then at the copper plate.

Mira: "Does blood interfere?"

Professor Halden called from above.

Professor Halden: "With the spell? No. With my ability to remain calm? Considerably."

Mira pressed her Green Circle to the plate.

The maintenance groove lit.

Not bright. Not grand.

Useful.

The arch redistributed its weight through old service channels. Leon's water brace rose two inches. The opening widened. Elira reached the marker and set both hands near its shoulders without touching.

Elira: "Can you answer?"

The outline flickered.

No voice.

Cael crouched on the other side of the broken wall, studying the pulse.

Cael: "It cannot answer while the channel is failing. Noctis would classify silence as emergency consent."

Elira: "Aurelis will not."

She lowered her Yellow Circle until its light lay level with the marker instead of above it. Gold threads entered the copper maintenance route Mira had opened. They did not replace the failing rhythm. They gave it somewhere safe to fail.

The marker's next pulse arrived weak but complete.

Then another.

The outline lifted one hand.

Toward the cracked western alley.

Choice.

Aira had already abandoned the false map. She stood on a broken fountain block with dust in her hair, reading the city by movement.

Aira: "Western alley chosen. Leon, release the arch on my count. Cassian, carry only if the marker asks. Mira, keep the service plate open."

Mira's injured palm trembled against the copper.

Mira: "How long?"

Aira looked at the arch, the alley, Leon's shaking arms.

Aira: "Eleven seconds."

Mira: "You are guessing."

Aira: "Yes."

The honesty frightened her.

It also let Mira decide.

Mira: "Then count loudly."

We moved.

At seven, the street rolled beneath us.

At eight, Leon released one water line and nearly fell.

At nine, Cassian offered his unburned hand. The marker took it.

At ten, Elira crossed the maintenance groove.

At eleven, Mira tore her palm from the plate.

The arch collapsed behind us with a sound that punched dust across the square.

No one was under it.

Mira sat down where she stood.

Her laugh came out too high.

Mira: "Your count was wrong."

Aira dropped beside her and wrapped the scraped hand with a strip torn from the compromised map.

Aira: "By less than a second."

Mira: "That is still wrong."

Aira: "Yes."

No defense. No joke.

The first marker reached the western safe line alive because Aira admitted uncertainty, Leon held more weight than pride could carry, Cassian used his body where fire would have been easier, and Mira found a route no official map considered important.

The Foundation bell recorded the rescue.

Lucien did not applaud.

The frost-binding marker woke while we were still breathing dust.

Cold detonated from the dry fountain.

Ice climbed the basin, crossed the paving seams, and seized Cassian's discarded metal pin. It became a white spear pointed through Mira's back.

Leon saw it late.

I saw it early.

This time warning was not enough.

Haru: "Down."

I struck the street with my false Green Circle.

Not the Black Circle. Not directly.

I let one narrow thread of impossible precision pass through the disguise and changed the spear's angle by the width of a fingernail.

It missed Mira's shoulder.

It did not miss me.

Ice cut through my sleeve and opened the skin below my ribs. Pain arrived hot, then vanished under cold.

Elira turned at the smell of blood.

Her face changed.

Haru: "Marker first."

She hated the sentence.

She obeyed the priority, not me.

Elira: "Aira, pressure on his side. Leon, with me. Cassian—can you still heat?"

Cassian held up the burned glove.

Cassian: "Offended you asked."

The fountain had frozen around the second marker from the feet upward. Its outline was trapped inside clear ice, conscious and unable to move. Every attempt to warm it caused the ice to tighten.

Cael crawled close enough to see the script under the frost.

Cael: "Punitive thaw. It measures rescue heat as resistance and increases pressure."

Leon: "So heat makes it worse."

Cael: "Uninvited heat."

The distinction changed the field.

Elira knelt by the ice.

Elira: "If you can hear me, blink the light once for warmth. Twice for no."

The marker blinked once.

Cassian placed his burned hand against the fountain rim.

He could not feel the temperature correctly through the injury. I saw that in the way his flame wavered.

Leon saw it too.

Without comment, he wrapped a thin water thread around Cassian's wrist. The water carried temperature back as pressure: one pulse for colder, two for stable, three for too hot.

They had never trained that technique.

They built it while the ice climbed.

Cassian sent warmth through the fountain in small uneven waves. Leon translated the marker's changing pulse through water. Elira held the consent rhythm steady. Cael read the punitive script aloud before each of its corrections could hide.

Aira pressed both hands against the cut in my side.

Aira: "You moved it."

Haru: "Yes."

Aira: "With Green output?"

Haru: "Mostly."

Her eyes found mine.

She knew mostly was doing dangerous work.

Aira: "Can you keep standing?"

I checked the cold behind my sternum. The Black Circle had noticed blood. That made the whole city feel thinner.

Haru: "Yes."

Aira: "Was that an answer or a habit?"

I looked at the frost spreading under my boots.

Haru: "A habit. Give me ten seconds."

She did.

At six, the ice split around the marker's knees.

At eight, Cassian lost the flame and Leon caught the last heat inside his water thread.

At ten, I could breathe without the Black Circle trying to correct the city around the wound.

Haru: "Now it is an answer."

Aira tied the bandage hard enough to make honesty hurt.

The second marker stepped free.

The third—mana starvation—waited in a narrow alley whose walls drank every spell we sent into it. Rhea ordered no immediate entry. Aira tested a light charm. The bricks swallowed it. Leon offered water; the alley drank that too. Even Elira's gold thinned at the threshold.

Then Finn, still at the public rail, dropped a copper coin into the lane.

It struck stone.

Ordinary sound traveled all the way to the marker.

Finn went pale when everyone looked at him.

Finn: "Magic is food in there. So stop feeding it magic."

Leon removed his academy coat.

Cassian removed his burned glove.

Elira banked her Circle completely.

We entered without spells.

The alley was only forty paces long. It became the hardest distance in the city. The walls pulled warmth from muscle, balance from the inner ear, light from the edges of sight. At twenty paces, Leon stumbled. Cassian caught him with the hand that still hurt. At thirty, Elira's knees shook and she refused my arm until she looked at the marker, reconsidered what refusal was for, and took it.

At thirty-eight, the marker collapsed.

Cael reached it first.

No magic. No rank advantage. He put one shoulder under the outline and stood with a body already damaged by someone else's doctrine.

Cael: "I can carry for six steps."

Not forever.

Not heroically.

Six.

Leon took the next six. Cassian took six after him. Elira took four before her breath failed. I carried the final ten while the Black Circle raged uselessly inside a place where using it would feed the walls.

We crossed the threshold and every withheld spell returned at once in a gust of color.

The third marker lived.

Mira returned to the witness rail by the western service stair while a healer sealed her palm. She kept the bandaged hand visible above the slate so no later transcript could pretend maintenance access or injury had made her disappear from the record.

Lucien watched the three rescues with his hands folded behind his back.

I read the field.

Not too much.

Just enough.

The false Green Circle around my wrist held around the thread I had used to turn the spear. Blood cooled beneath Aira's bandage. Mira's palm bled through the strip of false map. Cassian flexed two burned fingers. Leon stood because sitting would have admitted how close the arch had come to breaking him. Elira measured my breathing and let me see her do it.

Noctis had promised a test of rescue efficiency.

Instead, the first three markers proved that rescue was slower when people mattered and still fast enough when responsibility moved.

Lucien watched every time I did not answer with the power he wanted to expose.

That became its own kind of battle: not the markers, but the restraint between them—and the blood proving restraint was not free.

The field offered me small temptations first.

A cracked stone above Leon that would fall in seven seconds unless someone noticed the weight shift.

Haru: "Leon. Arch weight."

Mira: "Haru gave warning, Leon acted independently."

Haru: "Cassian. Back foot."

Elira's knees nearly gave.

I caught her this time.

She did not refuse.

Her weight hit my arms, and everything inside me became very quiet.

Too quiet.

Haru: "Aira."

My voice did not sound like mine.

Aira turned.

She heard it too.

Haru: "Find the legal root."

Aira swallowed.

Then she nodded.

Aira: "Working."

Good.

Still human enough to ask.

Still human enough to wait.

Barely.

Nera shouted from the witness rail above the square.

Nera: "The hook is not the root. The root is the empty marker."

Oric: "The seventh wound is isolation."

Valen's folio snapped open.

Professor Valen: "Then the answer is presence."

Rhea lifted both hands.

President Valmont: "Do not recite for it. Prove you are here in a way Haru did not choose for you."

The witness rail hesitated.

Mira moved first.

She shoved her bandaged hand beneath the stack of public copies and sent them sliding over the rail. Pages fell into the square in six different handwritings, one with a spelling correction in the margin, another stained where someone had cried and denied it.

Mira: "We wrote before he asked us to."

The pages struck the field and did not vanish.

Soren tore Valemere's courtesy seal from his observer slate. The act cost his delegation its right to revise his statement before public release.

Soren: "Valemere witness channel stays open."

Halden swore under his breath and unlocked his private research notes. Decades of guarded annotations spilled into the public rail, including three places where he had written **UNKNOWN** and one where he had written **I WAS WRONG**.

Professor Halden: "No office of mine gets sole custody of classification."

Professor Valen laid Liora's damaged folio beside his notes. She did not explain it. The missing names explained enough.

Nera pulled the Noctis authorization ring from her wrist.

The metal had bitten deep. A red groove remained.

Oric caught the ring before it hit the floor, set his own beside it, and kicked both under the witness barrier where Noctis could see them and could not quietly retrieve them.

Aira stared at the empty marker.

Aira: "I do not have the legal root."

Her voice cracked.

The room waited.

Aira hated that it had heard her fail.

She said the next part anyway.

Aira: "I know isolation is doing the work. I know every person here arrived for a different reason. I know Haru did not write all of us."

She tore the prepared response in half.

Leon and Cassian still held the hook inside the square, water and fire shaking against each other. Leon looked at me.

Leon: "I am still angry with you."

Cassian: "I am angry with everyone. It is one of my stabilizing traits."

A laugh broke somewhere on the rail and turned into a sob.

The empty marker cracked.

Not enough.

Cael stepped toward it.

His healer seal flashed warning blue. He was trembling so hard the light shook across his coat.

No one carried him.

No one spoke for him.

Cael: "Cael Nox."

The hook pulled at Elira.

Cael flinched and stayed.

Cael: "Not property."

The Foundation pool rose behind him.

Cael looked at the pages, the discarded rings, Halden's unlocked mistakes, Soren's burned seal, and the people still disagreeing while they refused to leave.

Cael: "Present."

The marker broke.

Every proof entered at once. Not as matching testimony. As incompatible people occupying the same danger without surrendering their reasons to a single voice.

The hook loosened.

Elira dragged in a breath.

Her pendant flared warm.

**SEVERANCE ROUTE INTERRUPTED**

**SIX VIABLE MARKERS EXTRACTED**

**NO CIVIC LOSS RECORDED**

**AURELIS WITNESS METHOD: PROVISIONALLY ACCEPTED FOR THIS ACTIVITY**

That result belonged to us. The seventh marker remained dangerous, but Lucien could no longer claim that asking permission slowed rescue or that distributed authority made a team indecisive. Six people had reached safety because responsibility moved instead of waiting for rank.

Relief hit the square too fast.

Lucien looked at the cracked seventh marker.

Then at Cael.

He smiled.

Lucien: "Interrupted is not ended."

The activity released us before anyone could answer.

Release did not mean the city let us leave cleanly.

The streets began collapsing toward the restoration pool. Buildings lost depth first, windows flattening into bright rectangles, then walls folding into the ground like paper pulled through a narrow slot. The safe zones vanished. A single exit opened beneath the chapel arch.

Four crest marks burned across it.

Green. Blue. Red. Yellow.

No unclassified route.

No space wide enough for Cael's contested Noctis seal and Elira's marked wrist to cross together.

Aira saw the trap.

Aira: "Exit is sorting by status. Do not enter a color lane."

The street behind us disappeared.

Leon: "Alternative?"

Aira looked at the collapsing city.

Aira: "Developing."

Rhea struck the exit with her seal.

President Valmont: "Emergency mixed-witness passage."

The colored marks rejected her authority and narrowed.

Lucien stood beyond the folding square, already outside the danger his protocol had made.

Lucien: "Classification is not violence merely because the unclassified dislike choosing."

The Green lane flashed around Cael.

Not because he was Green.

Because the field had decided contested meant lowest available status.

The Yellow lane opened for Elira and began pulling her injured wrist toward it.

The hook had not released completely.

Elira dug her heels into the stone.

Cael moved toward her.

The Green lane tightened around his ribs.

Leon grabbed the back of his coat before it could drag him away.

Cael: "Let go."

Leon: "No."

Cael: "I can reach her."

Leon: "Not if the lane closes on your spine."

Cael twisted, furious and terrified.

Leon did not pull him backward.

He stepped into the Green lane with him.

Blue light vanished from Leon's wrist as the field tried to reconcile the choice.

Leon gasped.

For one second, Aurelis' proudest Blue student became unclassified beside the boy he had once treated like an embarrassment.

Leon: "Cassian. Now."

Cassian understood.

He stepped out of the Red lane and planted his burned glove over Leon's shoulder. Fire left his Circle in a sputter as the classification field stripped its recognized route.

Cassian: "This is an appalling group decision."

Aira followed them.

The witness rail lowered into the failing square with a scream of metal. Its barrier opened only after Rhea marked the shared route as emergency egress rather than field entry.

Then Nera. Oric. Mira, clutching her bandaged palm. Rhea left the Red lane last.

Every approved exit stood empty.

The field tried to assign all of us Green.

Mira's Circle flared.

The lane recognized her first and attempted to make her the front.

She stepped behind Cael.

Mira: "No rank order."

The exit shuddered.

Elira was still trapped three paces away, the Yellow lane pulling harder with every collapse behind her.

I could break the color marks.

The Black Circle knew exactly how.

Instead, I went to Elira inside the Yellow lane.

The false Green crest on my wrist and the gold gate around her struck each other. Light snapped across my arms. The cut under Aira's bandage reopened.

Elira: "Haru—"

Haru: "Can you move?"

She looked at the hook, the collapsing street, our teammates refusing every color lane behind us.

Elira: "With help."

I put her unmarked arm around my shoulders.

We stepped away from Yellow together.

The exit screamed.

Not a human sound. Stone, law, and old classification grinding against a group that had refused to sort itself correctly.

The four colored marks broke into loose light.

Leon sent water under everyone's boots.

Cassian heated it into a sheet of steam.

Rhea drove her seal forward.

We crossed as one ugly, sliding mass while the Wounded City folded behind us.

Cael hit the chapel floor first. Mira landed on top of his legs. Cassian collided with Leon and blamed architecture. Aira lost two maps and kept the page with Cael's refusal. Elira and I crossed last as the false bell tower vanished into the restoration pool.

The exit sealed behind my heel.

On the stone where the four rank marks had been, a new line burned for one breath:

**MIXED WITNESS PASSAGE: UNRESOLVED**

Then Lucien's relay erased it.

The approved colors returned unevenly after we crossed. Cassian's Red Circle came back first, bright and furious. Leon's Blue followed with a stutter. Elira's gold returned around the black mark on her wrist.

Cael's healer seal remained blank.

He looked at the empty place on his skin and went still.

Mira pressed her bandaged hand against the chapel floor where the mixed-passage line had vanished.

Mira: "The exit recorded you before it erased itself."

Cael: "As what?"

Mira looked at the copy.

Mira: "Present."

His seal relit without a school name.

Not Noctis. Not Aurelis.

Present was enough to get him to the healers.

It was not enough to keep his legs working.

Cael took one step and folded. Leon caught him under the arms. Cassian moved to help, stopped when Cael flinched at two bodies, and cleared the path instead.

Cael: "I am not weak."

Leon: "You were pulled through a rank sorter while injured. You can be accurate before proud."

The sentence sounded borrowed from Maelin.

Cael laughed once despite himself.

Leon carried none of his weight after Cael found his feet. He kept both hands near enough to catch and far enough not to decide.

They crossed the chapel that way: one shaking, one ready, neither pretending readiness erased the shaking.

Mira had already copied the words.

Lucien saw.

His composure changed by less than a smile.

Enough for me to know the exit had not been his intended lesson.

Lucien left that way when he did not want to show whether he was disappointed.

Back in the chapel, healers removed the black mark from Elira's wrist by cutting away the upper layer of mana around her skin. It hurt. She let it.

Cael stood outside the healing circle with his arms stiff at his sides.

Leon did not let him leave.

Good.

The black mark did not come away like ordinary hostile magic.

It had hooks inside the definition of what Elira had done.

Every time the healer lifted a thread of it, the mark tried to argue.

**OBSTRUCTION**

Rhea answered.

President Valmont: "Authorized intervention."

The healer lifted another thread.

**PRIMARY WITNESS BIAS**

Aira answered.

Aira: "Multiple-role autonomy entered and active."

Another thread.

**CONDUIT RETURN CLAIM**

Nera and Oric answered together.

Nera: "No owner phrase."

Oric: "Prior training contested."

The mark fought less each time.

Not because it was weaker.

Because each lie had to be named before it could be removed.

Elira's jaw tightened until I thought she might crack a tooth.

She did not make a sound.

That was not composure now.

It was strategy.

If she cried out, Cael would hear it as proof.

He heard the silence as proof anyway.

His face became more controlled with every thread the healer cut.

Leon saw it and stepped in front of him.

Leon: "Look at me."

Cael: "I can hear."

Leon: "That is why I said look."

Cael's eyes lifted.

Leon: "She chose to protect you. You did not choose to hurt her."

Cael: "The result is the same."

Leon: "No, it is not."

Cael's expression sharpened with rare frustration.

Cael: "Her wrist is black because I was the route."

Leon leaned closer.

Leon: "Her wrist is black because Lucien is a murderer with grammar."

Cassian, from the chapel door, said:

Cassian: "That may be your best sentence."

Leon did not look away from Cael.

Leon: "Do not make me waste it."

Cael's breathing broke once.

Then steadied.

Night settled over Aurelis.

The Wounded City was sealed below the chapel, but I could still feel the empty seventh marker in the old stone.

Isolation.

A wound shaped like absence.

It changed the way the academy sounded.

Aurelis was never truly quiet. Wards hummed in walls. Training fields sighed as they reset. Students whispered where they thought faculty could not hear. The city below the cliffs sent up carriage bells and market noise softened by distance.

That night, every ordinary sound arrived with a question behind it.

Who was missing?

Who had been moved away for safety?

Who had agreed to stand alone because someone powerful called it protection?

The empty seventh marker had done more than threaten us. It had taught the whole day a new shape.

I walked the corridor outside the infirmary twice before Elira told me to stop wearing a path into the stone.

She was sitting upright again, because lying down made the mark on her wrist pull against the healer film.

Haru: "I am not pacing."

Elira looked at the door, then at me.

Elira: "You have passed that crack in the floor seven times."

Haru: "Observation is not pacing."

Elira: "Halden has been a bad influence."

I stopped by the chair.

She held out her unmarked hand.

I took it.

For several breaths, neither of us spoke.

Her pulse was steady under my fingers.

I counted it without meaning to.

Elira: "Do not turn that into surveillance."

I closed my eyes.

Haru: "I am trying not to."

Elira: "I know."

Then she sighed.

Elira: "Aira is right. We need a replacement phrase."

Haru: "I am listening."

That made her smile faintly.

Elira: "Better."

The smile faded.

Elira: "Tomorrow, if the seventh marker opens again, I may have to move."

Haru: "Elira."

Elira: "I am not asking permission."

Haru: "I know."

She gave me a look.

I corrected myself.

Haru: "I am listening."

Her hand tightened.

Elira: "If I move, do not treat my choice as Lucien's victory before it becomes one."

The sentence was hard to let live.

Haru: "And if he makes it one?"

Her eyes shone.

Elira: "Then make sure the world knows he did."

At the time, I thought she meant the room.

I did not understand how far the word world would go.

Cael came to the doorway while she slept again.

This time Leon was with him, which meant Cael had either asked or stopped objecting to being followed.

Both were progress.

Cael looked at Elira's wrapped wrist and then at me.

Cael: "I am not going to apologize."

Leon made a small approving sound.

Cael: "I want to. But I am not."

Haru: "Good."

The word surprised him.

It surprised me too, a little.

Cael stood with both hands at his sides, forcing them not to fold into guilt.

Cael: "I am angry."

Leon: "Also good."

Cael looked at him.

Leon: "What? It is better than apologizing for being injured."

Cael looked back at Elira.

Cael: "I am angry that he made her kindness into a route. I am angry that I know the spell he may use next. I am angry that part of me still thinks being useful would make this easier."

He swallowed.

Cael: "I am saying it because if I do not, it turns into a plan."

That was Aira's work.

And Nera's.

And Oric's.

And maybe his own, finally.

I nodded.

Haru: "Then keep saying it."

Cael looked at me for a long moment.

Cael: "You too."

I did not ask what he meant.

I knew.

Elira slept between us, alive and marked, while anger moved quietly through the room looking for a future.

Hours later, Elira slept sitting upright while Aira argued softly with Rhea over the final-stage conditions. I sat beside her without touching the pendant; that restraint felt like lifting a mountain grain by grain. Cael's anger remained in the room after he left, named before it could harden into a private plan.

Elira stirred.

Her fingers found mine.

Elira: "Do not chase his guilt in the dark."

Haru: "You were asleep."

Elira: "Barely."

I held her warm, living hand. She was still here.

Outside the infirmary window, a notice crystal flickered with the next continuance order.

**WOUNDED CITY PROTOCOL: INCOMPLETE**

**SEVENTH MARKER UNRESOLVED**

**FINAL ACTIVITY TO OPEN AT DAWN**

## Coda: Leon Stays Beside

Leon found Cael in the corridor outside the infirmary and almost made the old mistake.

He almost stepped in front.

The movement began before thought: shoulder forward, body between Cael and the world, protection shaped like ownership because that was the only shape Leon had practiced long enough to trust.

Then he stopped.

Cael noticed. Of course he noticed. Noctis had trained him to read motion before words arrived.

Leon forced his hands open.

Leon: "Do you want me here?"

The question sounded clumsy. Too late. Too small. It was also the first clean thing Leon knew how to offer.

Cael stared at him for long enough that shame tried to become anger in Leon's chest. He held still anyway.

Cael: "Beside."

Leon moved beside him. Not touching. Not blocking.

Inside the infirmary, Elira slept with frost under her collar. Outside it, Leon learned that standing correctly could feel harder than fighting.

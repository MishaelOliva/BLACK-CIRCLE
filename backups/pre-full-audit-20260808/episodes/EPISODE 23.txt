# Episode 23: **Green Circle Witnesses**

It arrived like instruction.

The first instruction was stillness.

Elira moved through the snow toward Sela Mornet. Slowly. Empty hands visible. Gold aura banked low enough not to become a sun the hall could call hierarchy.

The other children watched her with the wary attention of students who had learned that kindness from powerful people often arrived with witnesses and left with debt.

Sela's lips were blue.

Elira knelt before her.

That made the whole field recoil.

Yellow Circle did not kneel to Green in Noctis lessons. Yellow evaluated. Yellow commanded. Yellow corrected. Yellow did not lower itself until its eyes were level with a freezing child who had just remembered her surname.

The question had been asked before the snow came.

Now it had teeth.

Sela stared at Elira's face.

Sela's eyes moved from Elira to Mira.

Mira stood in the Green line now.

The hall had placed her there after the classroom collapsed, barefoot like the missing students, notebook clutched against her chest. It wanted to teach her that witness meant joining suffering or abandoning those who suffered. Her feet were turning red against the snow.

The restraint in that was visible.

Mira's teeth chattered once.

Aira: "Mira Castellan, do you want my coat?"

Because of the question.

Rhea stepped into view with her council seal raised.

Rhea: "Witnesses?"

I looked at the rescued children.

Sela had both hands over her ears. Tovin was crying into Cassian's cloak because Cassian had apparently become the nearest warm object and accepted the role with grave dignity. Pella stared at the black opening like it was calling her name.

Mira was still inside.

So was Elira. Aira. Finn. Leon. Nera. Darian.

And Aric, the final student Lucien had kept for the lesson.

Haru: "Everyone present who can tell me no."

The old floor accepted that before any human did.

That scared me.

Then Sela lifted her head.

Her face was wet and white.

Sela: "No."

The Black Circle paused.

Every adult looked at her.

She shook harder, but her voice came again.

Sela: "Don't take us."

The sentence struck deeper than any Council motion.

I turned fully toward her, away from the opening.

Haru: "I won't."

Sela did not relax. Good.

Trust given too quickly after rooms like that would have been another wound.

Haru: "If the Black Circle moves toward you without someone you trust saying why, you can say no again."

Sela's eyes moved to Rhea.

Rhea nodded once.

Rhea: "Recognized."

Then to Maelin.

Maelin: "Very recognized."

Then, surprisingly, to Cassian.

Cassian placed one hand over his heart with unusual solemnity.

Cassian: "I will be offensively loud about it."

Sela breathed.

The Black Circle steadied.

That was how the lower channel opened: not because I overpowered the practice court, but because a rescued child said no and the room survived it.

## Coda: The Page Outside

The Green first-year at the outer copy table had been told to duplicate whatever survived the slate.

No one had explained what to do when the slate went black.

One moment, Mira's handwriting moved across the relay page in hurried gray strokes. The next, the page filled with a single vertical smear as the threshold sealed with Mira, Aira, Finn, Elira, Leon, Cassian, Nera, Darian, and Aric still inside.

The first-year waited for an adult instruction.

Around him, adults produced several.

Seal the record.

Clear the table.

Move the rescued children.

Restrict visual access to the Black Circle event.

Preserve institutional chain of custody.

None answered the page.

He looked at the last complete lines Mira had sent:

**ARIC STATES: I WANT TO LEAVE.**

**PAUSE BEFORE STATEMENT PRESERVED.**

**NO FINAL WITNESS CONSENT.**

Below them, the ink had dragged sideways when the hall moved. Mira's final word might have been carry. It might have been careful. It might have been Cael, though Cael was not in the room.

The first-year did not repair it.

He copied the smear exactly.

At the center of the outer court, the floor opened in a black ring.

Everyone forgot the table.

Stone descended without falling. Darkness pressed upward without becoming light. Haru Aerlen stood at the edge with both hands visible while Instructor Dren and Professor Halden forced him to name what he intended before the old power could make intention irrelevant.

The rescued students screamed.

The first-year's pencil cut through the page.

He started again.

**BLACK OPENING FORMED AFTER THRESHOLD CLOSED.**

His hand shook too badly for the next line.

A council aide reached across him.

Council Aide: "That record is now restricted."

The first-year covered the page with both arms.

He had no clever answer. Mira would have found one after three frightened breaths. Aira would have made restriction sound embarrassed to exist. Rhea could have stopped the hand with a seal.

He had only his body and a Green Circle that could not force the aide back.

First-Year: "Mira said copies remain distributed."

Council Aide: "Mira is inside a hostile structure."

First-Year: "Yes."

The word came out smaller than he wanted.

He kept his arms on the page.

First-Year: "That is why her copy stays outside."

The aide's expression changed. Not agreement. Calculation.

The first-year's pocket relay began vibrating.

He ignored it.

It vibrated again with the private three-pulse pattern his mother used only when family messages were urgent.

No one else could hear it over the screaming children and the stone grinding beneath Haru's feet.

That made it harder to refuse.

He opened the relay under the table.

His mother's face did not appear. Only text, routed through the west district copy guild where she worked nights repairing municipal forms.

**COME AWAY FROM THE COURT. NOW.**

A second line arrived before he answered.

**GUILD RECEIVED NOCTIS NOTICE. YOUR SCHOLARSHIP SPONSOR ASKED WHETHER YOU ARE PARTICIPATING IN UNAUTHORIZED RECORD ACTIVITY.**

The words made the outer court colder than the Black Circle did.

His scholarship paid for dormitory housing, meal credits, and the medicine his younger brother's lungs needed every winter. Noctis did not have to threaten any of them directly. It only had to ask a sponsor a reasonable question while fear supplied the consequences.

The first-year looked at the page beneath his arms.

Then at the exit.

He could leave now. The official copy would remain with the council aide. No one could honestly say he destroyed evidence. His mother would stop seeing his name move through guild channels attached to words like unauthorized and Black Circle. He would still have stood here. Still have helped.

Enough to tell himself a clean story later.

The hidden half of the page was already moving through other hands.

He began to stand.

Across the court, Sela saw him.

She did not know about the scholarship. She did not know his mother or brother. She only knew the person at the copy table was leaving while the black opening formed.

Her face changed from fear to the guarded blankness Noctis students wore when adults disappeared after promising to remain visible.

The first-year sat back down.

Not because Sela owned the choice.

Because her expression revealed which cost he had not included.

He typed with one thumb:

**I AM SAFE. I AM COPYING. DO NOT ANSWER SPONSOR YET. SAVE MESSAGE.**

His mother replied immediately.

**I AM AFRAID.**

No command this time.

He wrote:

**ME TOO.**

The relay went still.

Fear did not resolve. His scholarship did not become safe because he named the risk. The page remained beneath his arms and his mother's message remained beside it, both records of people without enough authority to guarantee anything.

Then Krail's remote seal found the table.

Red light opened above the official half-copy.

**EXTRA-CLASSIFICATION IMAGE CONTROL**

The words descended toward the paper like a blade made of policy.

Every line describing the black opening faded first.

The first-year grabbed the ink bottle.

The council aide seized his wrist.

Council Aide: "Do not alter restricted evidence."

First-Year: "It is being altered now."

The aide looked at the vanishing words and hesitated.

That was enough.

The first-year poured ink across his own sleeve.

Not the page.

His uniform cloth absorbed it in a black flood. He pressed the wet sleeve onto the fading lines. Mirror-writing transferred against the fabric: incomplete, reversed, ugly enough that no official archive would accept it without complaint.

The red seal erased the page.

It could not erase his sleeve without naming his body as evidence custody.

Krail's office had not written that authority into the motion.

The seal stopped.

The council aide stared at the reversed letters running from the first-year's elbow to his wrist.

Council Aide: "That is not an admissible copy."

First-Year: "Then it cannot violate admissible-image control."

The answer surprised both of them.

Aira would have improved it.

Mira would have said it while shaking.

The first-year only held his wet arm away from his body and tried not to let ink drip onto the floor.

Three other Green students understood.

One pressed scrap paper to his sleeve and lifted a reversed fragment.

Another copied from that fragment onto a lunch wrapper.

A third read the backwards text aloud while a Blue student wrote it correctly on the inside of a tournament program.

The record changed material four times in less than a minute.

Uniform.

Scrap.

Wrapper.

Program.

Each transfer lost something. A timestamp. Half a word. The exact width of the black ring.

Each preserved what Krail's seal had tried to remove:

Haru named a door.

Sela said no.

No entry yet.

The red seal widened, searching for a central copy to control.

There was none.

It turned toward the students.

Rhea saw it.

Her own seal struck Krail's from the side with enough force to throw red sparks across the court.

President Valmont: "My emergency mandate covers student protection and immediate hazards. A remote seal attempting to classify ink on student bodies qualifies as both."

Krail's authority recoiled.

Rhea did not destroy it. She pinned it above the empty place where the page had been and forced the attempted erasure to remain visible.

The first-year's sleeve dripped onto the official table.

Rhea looked at the stain.

President Valmont: "Preserve the uniform."

First-Year: "I only have two."

Rhea considered that practical objection more seriously than Krail's seal.

President Valmont: "Then preserve it until the ink is copied and have Student Council pay for cleaning. No custody claim."

The first-year looked at the backwards letters on his arm.

First-Year: "Can I wear it until Mira comes back?"

Rhea's face tightened.

President Valmont: "Yes."

Not if.

Until.

The word held against the threshold better than reassurance would have.

Rhea looked at the medical film covering the first-year's burned palm.

President Valmont: "Name for the injury record?"

He almost gave only his student number. Numbers arrived more safely when authority was already watching.

Sela still stood beyond the table.

First-Year: "Benn Arlow."

Rhea wrote it herself.

President Valmont: "Benn Arlow. Green Circle outer-copy witness. Hand burned preserving a page during active hazard. Scholarship pressure reported through family guild channel."

Benn looked at her sharply.

Benn: "How did you know about the message?"

Rhea: "Your relay was open when Krail's seal struck. I heard enough to protect the record, not enough to claim the conversation."

She left the message contents off the public page.

Benn had not known authority could admit where hearing stopped.

The copy table shook as the black opening widened.

His bandaged hand could no longer hold the pages flat.

He tried anyway.

The Green student beside him took half the stack.

Benn: "I can keep them."

Green Student: "You can keep half."

No speech about sacrifice. No demand that injury prove commitment.

Half.

Benn released the pages one bundle at a time until his hand stopped reopening under the medical film. The other student tied the copies to the table with tournament ribbon stripped from a suspended-bracket banner.

The banner had once promised victory by school.

Now it kept frightened handwriting from blowing into a Black Circle door.

Benn looked at Sela.

She had watched him stay.

She had also watched him let someone help.

Both mattered.

When the floor gave its next pulse, Benn wrote with his uninjured hand:

**COPY WORK CONTINUED BY TWO PEOPLE AFTER INJURY.**

Then he added his name.

Not student number.

Name.

His pocket relay vibrated once more.

**SAW YOUR MESSAGE. BROTHER HAS MEDICINE THROUGH MONTH-END. I AM STILL AFRAID. COME HOME WHEN THE SCHOOL RELEASES YOU, NOT WHEN A SPONSOR DOES.**

Benn read the line twice.

He copied none of it onto the public page. Family fear did not become communal property because it influenced a witness.

Instead, beneath his own name, he added:

**PERSONAL COST EXISTS OUTSIDE VISIBLE INJURY. DETAILS WITHHELD BY CHOICE.**

Rhea saw the line and moved her seal around it, leaving the withheld space unclaimed.

Benn kept working with one good hand until another student told him the bandage had begun bleeding through.

This time he stopped on the first warning.

With Krail's remote seal pinned above the empty page, a second Council clerk called for witness identities. A third tried to move the copy table behind a privacy screen that would also place it behind the official record rail.

The first-year understood then that no one needed to burn a page if they could move it somewhere harmless.

He tore the copy down the middle.

The aide grabbed for both halves.

The first-year let him take the top.

The bottom half vanished into the sleeve of the Green student beside him.

No signal passed between them. They had spent days copying Mira's rule: one page can be taken; disagreement must survive elsewhere.

The second student walked away before anyone decided walking was suspicious.

The first-year remained seated with the official half under his hands.

It contained names, timestamps, and the beginning of Aric's statement.

The hidden half contained the refusal conditions and the smear where the hall had cut Mira off.

Neither was complete.

Together they were inconvenient.

The black ring widened by one breath.

Rhea shouted for distance. Maelin moved the rescued children behind an open medical screen. Cassian was still inside, so no one made the joke the first-year expected when an overturned heat lamp rolled across the stones.

It struck the leg of the copy table.

Flame climbed the dry edge of the official page.

The council aide recoiled.

The first-year slapped the fire with his bare hand.

Pain arrived before courage did. He shouted, knocked the lamp away, and crushed the burning corner beneath his palm until the smell of scorched paper mixed with skin.

The record survived with a black crescent missing from its edge.

Maelin saw the hand from across the court.

Maelin: "Do not move."

First-Year: "The table—"

Maelin: "Can be replaced. Your hand cannot."

He looked at the black opening, at Haru refusing to enter, at the threshold holding people inside, and at the copy with half its truth walking somewhere through the outer crowd.

First-Year: "Mira would say the page matters."

Maelin reached him and poured blue healing film over the burn.

Maelin: "Mira would say you are part of what the page is for."

He did not know whether that was true.

He wanted it to be.

The hidden copy returned five minutes later through six different hands. Each student had added one observation in a different margin.

**Sela said no.**

**Haru stopped widening the ring.**

**Dren asked for action name.**

**No entry yet.**

**Children moved by healer request, not Council custody.**

**Threshold still closed.**

The first-year joined the halves with a strip of healer tape. The sentence across the tear no longer aligned. One word was missing. The burned corner made the timestamp imperfect.

He copied none of it cleanly.

He wrote beneath the damage:

**RECORD BROKE WHILE PEOPLE WERE STILL INSIDE. PRESERVE BREAK.**

The black ring under Haru's feet answered with a pulse strong enough to rattle every ink bottle.

The first-year caught one before it fell.

Then the floor began to open.

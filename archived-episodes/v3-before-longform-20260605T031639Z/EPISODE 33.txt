## The Courtyard That Learned To Look Back

Aurelis did not greet us like heroes.

That was a relief.

Heroism would have been too clean for what we brought back from Lake Velis: a suspended Council demonstration, a hostile Noctis relay, names that might be living students, and the knowledge that Lucien could make private tenderness into public architecture if the room gave him enough polished surface.

The academy greeted us like a body that had just learned one of its bones was older than the rest.

Students gathered in the Grand Courtyard in uneven clusters. Green Circle first-years stood nearest the fountain, pretending they had not arranged themselves there for visibility. Blue Circle combat-track students formed a loose half-ring near the west steps, alert but uncertain whether alertness counted as aggression now. Red Circle heirs watched from under the arcade with faces trained by family politics. Yellow Circle seniors did not cluster at all. They stood apart from one another, as if proximity had become a rumor that might stain them.

Every eye found me.

Then Elira.

Then my hand, because I was holding hers.

Not tightly.

That mattered.

Elira's fingers rested in mine by choice, warm and steady despite the faint glow of Maelin's charm at her cuff. If she needed to let go, she would. If I needed to breathe, I would. The simplicity of it was almost violent after rooms designed to make touch suspicious.

# Episode 34: **The Annex Beneath the Tournament**

The old tournament medical annex had been built for respectable lies.

That was my first thought when the sealed doors opened.

Respectable lies had a particular smell at Aurelis: polished stone, antiseptic herbs, legal ink, and old wards designed to make harm look like procedure. The corridor sloped beneath the eastern combat arenas, past patient rooms that had not held patients in years, past examination alcoves where visiting students had once been measured before matches.

Nera walked ahead of me with both binding rings uncovered.

Her hands trembled.

She did not hide them.

Darian carried a Noctis access slate stolen, borrowed, or ethically complicated from his former delegation. His face had gone hard in the way of someone recognizing the shape of a room he had survived.

Elira walked at my right side.

Maelin walked at hers, muttering medical curses under her breath and somehow making them sound like prayer.

Aira stayed near the rear with Mira and Finn. Rhea had almost refused Mira's presence until Mira pointed out, with trembling politeness, that Green Circle students had been used as public witness cover before and should not be excluded from the record now that danger had a door. Rhea had stared at her for three seconds, then issued her a restricted observer seal.

Mira had looked like she might faint from victory.

She had not fainted.

That mattered.

Leon and Cassian took point with Dren. Halden and Valen followed the old wall inscriptions, arguing quietly in fragments that made the corridor feel worse.

Halden: "These were altered."

No one liked that.

We reached the first examination chamber.

The door seal identified it as:

Cael had been treated here after I stabilized him in secret.

The room remembered.

Not emotionally. Stone did not grieve. But mana had habits, and this room's habits had been trained by fear. There were too many anchor points beneath the bed frame. Too many drain sigils disguised as monitoring lines. Too many places a student's body could be measured as capacity instead of pain.

Valen stopped at the threshold and did not enter.

That made everyone else stop too.

Sometimes leadership was not command. Sometimes it was a scholar refusing to put her foot into a room before the room had been named correctly.

Rhea noticed.

Rhea: "Professor?"

Aira: "Something stood here for a long time. Heavy legs. Rounded feet. Removed recently enough that the floor beneath is cleaner than the rest, but not recently enough to be last night."

Maelin came in behind her.

The healer looked at the empty space beside the bed.

For a moment, her professional bluntness vanished.

Maelin: "Companion chair."

Elira's face tightened.

Elira: "Chosen proximity."

Valen entered the room at last. Earlier she had refused the threshold because the room had not been named correctly. Now she stood beside the bed and looked at the dust scar as if it were a grave.

Valen: "The original healing protocol required the injured student to choose someone to sit within reach before treatment seals closed."

Halden's voice was low.

Halden: "And someone removed that requirement."

Darian stared at the observation chair.

Darian: "Noctis rooms have only one chair."

Nera looked sick.

Nera: "For the evaluator."

The recovery room changed around that sentence.

Not physically. The bed remained a bed. The chair remained a chair. But the meaning slid into place with such force that even Cassian went silent.

Care had once included a person close enough to be touched.

Someone had removed that person and left a watcher.

Not an accident.

A design.

Elira stepped to the dust scar. Her hand hovered over the empty space, not touching the floor.

Elira: "Cael woke here."

No one answered.

Elira: "After Haru stabilized him. After Lucien used him. After everyone started arguing about what his body proved."

Her voice thinned but did not break.

Elira: "Did anyone ask who he wanted beside him?"

Maelin closed her eyes.

That was answer enough.

I remembered Cael in the trial hall, too pale under the witness lights, still apologizing for taking up space. I remembered how quickly Noctis tried to reclaim him. I remembered his final act, how Votive Return killed him because the doctrine had been edited until one life became the easiest route.

The missing chair felt suddenly louder than any scream.

Haru: "We need to restore it."

Halden looked at me sharply.

Haru: "Not magically. Not as a symbol. As record."

Aira stood.

Aira: "Agreed."

Rhea: "From where?"

Finn raised one hand from the threshold.

Everyone looked at him.

He swallowed.

Finn: "There are old visitor chairs in the storage room. Most are broken, but one is solid. Pella and I used it when we were copying maintenance maps because the floor was cold."

Seraphine considered him.

Seraphine: "Retrieve it. Do not cross the red line."

Finn ran.

Pella followed, grateful for a task.

Krail would have called it irrelevant. A Council hawk would have asked whether a chair changed the threat profile of a Black Circle event. Lucien would have laughed at the inefficiency.

That was how I knew we were doing the correct thing.

We waited while Finn and Pella dragged the chair back, bumping it once against the storage frame and wincing as if they had offended history. It was plain dark wood, one arm repaired with mismatched screws, the seat worn smooth by years of ordinary bodies.

Mira helped carry it to the room threshold.

Then she stopped.

Mira: "Who places it?"

The question mattered.

If I placed it, the room might read Black Circle correction.

If Elira placed it, the room might turn beloved proximity into its favorite argument.

If Maelin placed it, the room might classify it as medical authority.

Rhea understood at the same moment.

Rhea: "Cael is not here to choose."

Silence.

Then Nera stepped forward.

Nera: "He chose Aurelis protection."

Darian: "He chose refusal."

Leon: "He chose to warn us."

Cassian: "He chose not to be used quietly."

Aira looked down at her notes.

Aira: "He chose to put what he was not into writing."

Elira's eyes shone.

Elira: "Then the chair is placed by witness of his choices, not by replacement of his choice."

Rhea nodded.

One by one, we touched the chair.

Not all at once. That would have been ceremony trying too hard. Aira touched the repaired arm. Leon the back. Cassian the seat edge. Nera, shaking, touched one leg. Darian touched the other. Rhea touched the top rail. Valen and Halden touched the old wood together, history and suspicion briefly agreeing. Maelin touched it last and muttered:

Maelin: "For the patient, not the record."

Then Elira and I lifted it together.

Only because everyone else had already named what it was.

We placed the companion chair beside the bed, close enough for a patient to reach without sitting up.

The room made a sound.

Not a chime.

Not a spell activation.

A sigh through old vents.

The observation chair across the room cracked down the center.

No one touched it.

It split by itself, quietly, like an argument losing structure.

The blank circle near my shoulder dimmed.

Then, for one instant, the recovery room showed what it had been before fear improved it into cruelty: two chairs, open curtains, monitoring lines that asked rather than took, a bedside rail designed to lower from the inside.

Cael was not in the vision.

That mercy nearly hurt more.

The room did not pretend he could be restored by furniture.

It only showed the care that should have met him before he ever became evidence.

Elira wiped her cheek with the heel of her hand.

She did not apologize for crying.

I loved her fiercely for that.

Lucien's voice slid into the restored quiet.

Lucien: "You are moved by carpentry now."

Cassian's head snapped toward the wall.

Seraphine lifted one hand to stop him.

Elira did not turn from the chair.

Elira: "You keep revealing what you cannot recognize."

Lucien: "A chair does not save a conduit."

Elira: "No."

She faced the wall at last.

Elira: "But removing one helps make a conduit."

The annex lights flared.

That sentence had landed somewhere old.

Valen whispered:

Valen: "There."

On the far wall, beneath layers of Noctis-compatible monitoring script, older Aurelis words surfaced like bruises under skin.

**NO TREATMENT WITHOUT CHOSEN PRESENCE.**

The words were not in modern script, but every witness understood them at once. The room made sure of it.

Halden looked as if someone had taken a theory he hated and proved it with blood.

Halden: "Aurelis removed this before Noctis copied the structure."

Rhea's face hardened.

Rhea: "Aurelis?"

Valen did not defend the academy.

Valen: "Yes."

The answer cost her. It also made me trust her more.

Valen: "Old tournament medicine became obsessed with neutral observation after the Circle Zero failure. Chosen presence was considered contamination. Noctis inherited the architecture and perfected the cruelty."

Aira wrote slowly.

Aira: "So this is not only a Noctis crime."

Valen: "No."

Mira's pencil scratched from the doorway.

Mira: "Aurelis wound. Noctis weapon."

Valen looked at her.

Valen: "Precisely."

The tunnel beyond the room shortened by another brick.

From below, the covered sob did not repeat.

Instead, something tapped once.

Metal on stone.

Not a spoon dropping this time.

A spoon deliberately struck against the floor.

Once.

Then twice.

Nera's face changed.

Nera: "Answer code."

Darian: "From winter drills?"

Nera nodded.

Nera: "Two taps means heard but watched."

Every witness in the room understood the horror of that.

Iven, or someone near him, had not been freed by the chair.

But the restoration had reached him.

He knew we had corrected something.

He knew enough to answer without speaking.

Aira's eyes were wet. Her voice stayed practical.

Aira: "New rule. Sound responses are not followed directly. We confirm pattern first."

Leon sent a water thread low along the floor. It touched the tunnel threshold, accepted the cold, and returned with two faint pulses.

Leon: "Same cadence."

Maelin: "Live motor response."

Rhea: "Can he be compelled to tap?"

Nera swallowed.

Nera: "Yes."

The room tried to sink.

Nera forced herself onward.

Nera: "But Noctis compulsion favors perfect rhythm. That was uneven. Frightened. Intentional."

Elira crouched again at the restored bedside, not the tunnel.

Elira: "Iven Sahl, if you are the one tapping, you do not need to answer again. If someone is forcing you, you do not need to prove otherwise."

No tap came.

The tunnel shortened by one more brick.

Permission, not performance.

That was the route.

Not fast enough for my fear.

Correct enough for the child.

We returned to the service threshold with the restored chair behind us. The whole annex felt different now, though not safer. Truth rarely made rooms safe immediately. It made their dangers visible enough to stop cooperating with them.

Seraphine looked down the opening.

Seraphine: "Now we move."

Dren nodded.

Dren: "Slowly."

Lucien's voice tried once more, thin with irritation.

Lucien: "Your slowness is vanity."

Haru: "No."

I looked at the restored room, the broken observation chair, the old words exposed on the wall, the Green students recording from the threshold, Elira standing alive beside me because consent had stopped a route that once killed her.

Haru: "It is how we stop building rooms like yours."

For a moment, nothing answered.

Then the tunnel opened wide enough for one person at a time.

Not a charge.

An invitation with teeth.

Dren entered first.

Seraphine second.

Leon sent water ahead.

Cassian sent heat along the walls without flame.

Aira walked behind them with her notebook open, because apparently fear had learned to take dictation.

Nera stepped to the threshold and froze.

The darkness below smelled like winter drills.

Darian waited.

Nera's rings trembled.

Nera: "If I hear a bell, I may stop."

Rhea answered immediately.

Rhea: "Then we stop with you."

Nera nodded.

She entered.

Elira looked at me.

Haru: "Still your choice."

Elira: "Still yours too."

We entered together, not touching, close enough that the tunnel could not pretend either of us was alone.

Behind us, the companion chair remained beside Cael's old bed.

That detail stayed with me as the dark accepted our first steps.

Not the broken observation chair. Not the exposed old law on the wall. The companion chair.

A plain object, repaired badly, dragged from storage by Green Circle students with shaking hands. If anyone tried to summarize the annex later, they would reach for stronger images: conduit hooks, hidden registries, Lucien's voice in the walls, the sob below the floor. Those mattered. But the chair told the truth more quietly and therefore more dangerously.

Someone had known healing required chosen presence.

Someone had removed it.

That meant cruelty had not begun as a monster. It had begun as an improvement. A cleaner room. A more neutral record. A safer distance. A policy that sounded reasonable to people who did not have to wake alone inside it.

I looked at Elira walking beside me, her pendant dim beneath cloth, her breath measured against pain she refused to dramatize.

Haru: "If I ever call distance safety without asking what it costs, stop me."

She glanced at me.

Elira: "I already do."

Haru: "Then do it harder."

For a moment, despite the tunnel, despite the child below, despite the smell of winter drills, her mouth curved.

Elira: "You say the most romantic things in basements."

Aira's voice floated from behind us.

Aira: "Noted with disgust."

The tunnel shortened by one brick.

Apparently even old magic preferred us human.

The thought steadied me more than power would have.

Old magic did not need me grand yet. It needed me interruptible. It needed Elira beside me, Aira behind me, Green witnesses above us, and a chair in a room where someone had once decided observation was cleaner than comfort.

The tunnel breathed cold around our ankles.

This time, we did not answer by rushing.

We answered by remembering who had been removed before the trap ever opened.

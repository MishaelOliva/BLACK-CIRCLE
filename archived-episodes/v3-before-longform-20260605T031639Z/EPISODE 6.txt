# Episode 6: **Selection Heat**

Two days after the initial qualification notices, the real selection process began.

Aurelis Arcane Academy felt less like a school and more like a kingdom preparing for war while pretending it was education.

Training grounds filled before sunrise. Strategy rooms stayed occupied between classes. Students who had not qualified watched those who had with mixtures of admiration, resentment, relief, and private calculation. Every corridor carried the same tension:

Who would represent Aurelis?
Who would fail?
Who would embarrass themselves before the world arrived?

And, more quietly now but still persistently:

Were Elira Vale and Haru Aerlen actually together?

That second question remained remarkably durable.

It did not seem to bother Elira.
It did not seem to bother me.
It bothered Aira enough for all three of us.

We were walking toward the central strategy hall when Aira looked from me to Elira and then back to me with the solemn expression of someone rehearsing patience.

Aira: "I would like to say, for the record, that the rumors have now split into factions."

Haru: "It seems organized."

Aira: "Too organized. One group thinks you're already dating. Another thinks you're not officially dating but obviously heading there. A third group thinks you're bound by some ancient mana vow and neither of you has admitted it yet."

Elira's shoulders lifted once with quiet laughter.

Elira: "That last one is impressively specific."

Aira stopped walking.

Aira: "You both really need to stop sounding amused. It encourages them."

Haru: "Would denial help?"

Aira: "No."

Elira: "Then perhaps calm is still the best option."

Aira closed her eyes briefly.

Aira: "You're impossible together."

That sentence might have embarrassed me a week ago.

Now it only felt accurate.

We entered the strategy hall with the rest of the selected students. The room was broader than a normal classroom, circular and tiered, with floating screens displaying tournament categories, prior Aurelis performance rankings, and projected team structures. Faculty lined the outer wall with clipboards, crystal tablets, and expressions ranging from professional interest to ruthless evaluation.

Professor Halden stood at the center.

So did Instructor Dren.
And two additional senior examiners: Lady Seraphine Morn of tactical studies, a severe Red Circle strategist with unnervingly precise diction, and Master Ilyas Thorn, a veteran Blue Circle combat instructor whose face looked carved from endurance and disappointment.

Professor Halden raised one hand, and the low conversation faded.

Professor Halden: "Today begins the internal selection phase. Not all of you will remain in contention. Some will be sorted into support categories. Some into alternate reserves. A select few will become Aurelis' principal delegation."

A display opened behind him.

**SELECTION PHASE - DAY ONE**  
**INDIVIDUAL EVALUATION**  
**TEAM POTENTIAL MAPPING**  
**ADAPTIVE COMPATIBILITY SCORING**

Professor Halden: "Your first evaluations will not be public duels."

That disappointed several students immediately.

Professor Halden noticed.
He did not care.

Professor Halden: "This tournament is won by institutions, not vanity. We are not selecting the loudest students. We are selecting the most effective."

At the edge of the room, Leon stood a little straighter at that-perhaps trying to signal that he, too, now valued effectiveness over spectacle.

I doubted the transformation was complete.

Lady Seraphine stepped forward.

Lady Seraphine: "You will be assessed in three stages today. First, mana control precision. Second, tactical responsiveness under altered conditions. Third, provisional team integration. Every score matters."

Aira leaned slightly toward me.

Aira: "I dislike every phrase she just used."

Haru: "You dislike competence when it's pointed at you."

Aira: "That is a hostile interpretation of my personality."

Elira, on my other side, lowered her voice just enough for only us to hear.

Elira: "It's also accurate."

Aira gave her a wounded look.

Aira: "You're supposed to be kind."

Elira: "I am. It was very gentle."

The first evaluation took place in the **Lattice Chamber**, a long, white training hall divided by suspended crystal nodes. Each student stood on a marked platform while mana-sensitive targets floated through shifting patterns around them. The objective was not power.

It was exactness.

A student had to strike, redirect, shield, or stabilize the targets according to rapidly changing instructions projected above the chamber.

Too much force cost points.
Too slow a reaction cost points.
The wrong spell choice cost points.

This was the kind of test that embarrassed prodigies who relied on rank alone.

One by one, students were called.

A Red Circle spear specialist overcommitted and shattered a target meant to be preserved.
A Blue Circle earth user reacted too slowly to an inversion sequence.
A Green Circle support caster performed unexpectedly well by compensating for lower output with flawless timing.

The examiners noted everything.

When Aira's turn came, she walked to the platform like someone approaching an unnecessary administrative betrayal.

Aira: "If this goes badly, I want both of you to remember me kindly."

Haru: "That feels dramatic."

Aira: "That's because I'm under pressure."

Elira: "You'll do well."

Aira glanced at her.

Aira: "If you say encouraging things in that voice, it becomes very difficult to fail with dignity."

She stepped onto the platform.

Then, to the surprise of several nearby students, she became completely different.

The uncertainty vanished.
The complaints vanished.
The humor thinned into focus.

Blue mana gathered around her hands in crisp, efficient lines. Her specialty was not brute offense. It was response architecture-small-scale water and air shaping, support geometry, redirection, barrier layering, and tactical adaptation.

The projection flashed.

**INTERCEPT**

Three targets shot forward at staggered angles.

Aira moved immediately. A thin ribbon of water mana snapped from her hand, altering the first target's trajectory without damage. A compressed air pulse slowed the second. A rotating micro-barrier caught the third and lowered it gently into the stabilizing field.

**CONTAIN**

She sealed a destabilized crystal in a transparent water shell without cracking it.

**REVERSE**

She redirected an incoming force wave back through a rotating prism array and neutralized it before it touched the platform edge.

By the end of the sequence, the chamber was quiet.

Aira exhaled and stepped off the platform with visible relief.

Master Thorn looked down at his score slate.

Master Thorn: "Excellent support reflexes. High composure under layered instruction. Efficient mana expenditure."

Aira blinked.

Aira: "That was suspiciously close to praise."

Lady Seraphine: "Do not become dependent on it."

Aira: "There it is."

She returned to us with the deeply offended dignity of someone who had done very well against her preference.

Haru: "You were strong."

Aira: "I understand. It's upsetting."

Elira smiled.

Elira: "You're wasted on false modesty."

Aira: "That wasn't modesty. It was despair management."

Elira's turn came not long after.

No one pretended not to watch.

The chamber itself seemed to sharpen around her the moment she stepped onto the platform. Her Yellow Circle crest glowed at her collarbone, and the black necklace below it remained quiet, hidden, and known only to us.

The projection flashed rapidly.

**INTERCEPT**  
**SPLIT**  
**STABILIZE**  
**REINFORCE**  
**NULL OVERFLOW**

Most students would have handled those instructions sequentially.

Elira handled them as one continuous thought.

Golden-white light flowed from her fingertips with impossible grace, splitting into fine threads, shields, balancing rings, and structural anchors all at once. She didn't just respond to the instructions.
She anticipated their logic.

Targets that should have collided instead curved harmlessly into place. A destabilizing pulse dissolved inside a lattice of light before the chamber could even register it as a threat. Her mana never looked rushed, never looked forced, never wasted a movement.

By the end, every target was preserved.
Every condition satisfied.
Every crystal in the chamber was still humming in perfect harmonic balance.

Silence held for one beat.

Then even some rival candidates applauded before remembering where they were.

Professor Halden's expression did not change.
But his score notation moved immediately.

Lady Seraphine: "As expected."

Master Thorn: "Control nearly flawless."

Instructor Dren, who was not easily impressed in visible ways, gave the smallest nod.

Elira stepped down and returned to us like nothing dramatic had occurred.

Aira: "Do you ever get tired of being extraordinary so efficiently?"

Elira: "Sometimes."

Aira: "It was not a rhetorical question, but I'm still unhappy with the answer."

Then it was my turn.

The chamber grew quieter in a different way.

Not reverence.
Expectation.
Curiosity.
A little suspicion.

The Green Circle transfer had already disrupted one hierarchy and dismantled Leon in public. Now everyone wanted to know whether that had been context, luck, or something stranger.

I stepped onto the platform.

My false Green Circle crest remained stable.
My real power remained buried.

The projection flashed.

**INTERCEPT**

Simple enough.

I used the minimum output required, redirecting the first target with reinforcement and angle rather than force.

**CONTAIN**

A weak-looking shield formed around the crystal.
Deliberately weak-looking.
It held perfectly.

**NULL OVERFLOW**

A destabilized node descended too quickly for most Greens to manage cleanly.
I compressed the mana field around it just enough to neutralize the cascade without revealing how precise the compression truly was.

The sequence escalated.

**SPLIT**
**REVERSE**
**REINFORCE**

I kept going.
Not spectacularly.
Not brilliantly in a way that would force open questions.

Just undeniably well.

When the final target settled, the chamber remained quiet longer than it had after several stronger students.

Because the issue was not whether I had succeeded.

It was whether a Green Circle student should have been able to do it that neatly.

Master Thorn looked down at the slate.

Master Thorn: "High efficiency. Low waste. Unusual precision."

Lady Seraphine narrowed her eyes slightly.

Lady Seraphine: "Your mana economy is inconsistent with your displayed reserve profile."

There it was.

Not accusation.
Observation.

I met her gaze calmly.

Haru: "I focus on control."

Professor Halden spoke for the first time.

Professor Halden: "Clearly."

His tone gave away nothing.

Which made it worse.

I stepped off the platform and returned to the others.

Aira: "It was less subtle than you think."

Haru: "It was very subtle."

Aira: "Your standards are frightening."

Elira looked at me quietly.
Not worried.
Just aware.

Elira: "You were careful."

Haru: "Mostly."

She glanced toward Professor Halden.

Elira: "He noticed."

Haru: "He notices for a living."

Aira: "That sentence needs an evacuation plan."

I didn't disagree.

---

The second stage was worse.

Not because it was combat-heavy.
Because it was psychological.

The **Adaptive Responsiveness Hall** was a shifting arena of illusion screens, partial barriers, environmental distortion fields, and randomized threat constructs. Students were sent in alone and forced to react to unstable conditions with limited information.

Fog. Darkness. False allies. Delayed spell response. Enemy decoys. Mana interference. Changing terrain.

In other words: panic, professionally organized.

Aira's description, not mine.

She said it while staring at the entrance gate to her turn.

Aira: "This is panic, professionally organized."

Haru: "That's a strong summary."

Aira: "If I don't come back, use my notes respectfully."

Elira: "You'll come back."

Aira: "Yes, but perhaps changed."

She did, in fact, come back.
Sweaty, irritated, and carrying one of the better times of the rotation.

Aira: "I dislike being competent in stressful environments. It rewards the wrong parts of my personality."

Elira went in next and emerged with the fastest completion time recorded so far.

No one was surprised.

The truly notable result came from a Red Circle heir named **Cassian Veyr**, whose fire-affinity output was among the highest in the candidate pool but whose tactical patience was approximately nonexistent. He blasted through the course in spectacular fashion, earning both admiration and criticism.

That mattered.
This tournament would need symbols, yes-but not unstable ones.

My own run was uneventful externally and irritating internally.

The illusion constructs were too easy to read.
The decoys lacked weight in their mana signatures.
The trap placements followed logical compression patterns.

I completed the course with a high score, careful not to break the evaluation by being faster than people would accept from a Green.

That still placed me near the top.

Which did not improve my anonymity.

By the time the afternoon phase ended, students were exhausted, overstimulated, and now fully aware that Aurelis intended to build a tournament team capable not just of competing, but dominating.

The final exercise of the day was announced just before dismissal.

**Provisional Team Integration Mapping**

That got everyone's attention back immediately.

Names began to appear in groups on the suspended projection.

Temporary squads. Mixed-rank combinations. Different affinities. Balance testing.

Aira squinted at the screen.

Aira: "Please let this be reasonable."

It wasn't.

Our names appeared together.

**TEAM CANDIDATE GROUP THREE**  
**ELIRA VALE**  
**HARU AERLEN**  
**AIRA SOLEN**  
**LEON VAREK**  
**CASSIAN VEYR**

Aira stared at the projection in numb disbelief.

Aira: "No."

Elira read it once, then again.

Elira: "That is... aggressive."

Haru: "Strategically or emotionally?"

Aira turned to me.

Aira: "Yes."

Across the chamber, Leon had also seen the assignment. His expression froze for a second before flattening into something controlled and difficult.

Cassian Veyr, by contrast, just laughed.

Tall, red-haired, and carrying the kind of confidence usually produced by old family power and excellent combat results, Cassian stepped toward our side of the hall with open amusement.

Cassian: "Well. This looks volatile."

Aira: "You say that like it's positive."

Cassian looked at her and grinned.

Cassian: "I enjoy difficult combinations."

Aira: "That is exactly the kind of sentence a problem says."

Leon approached a moment later, slower and far less pleased.

Leon: "If we are being evaluated as a team, I assume everyone understands that personal theatrics will not be tolerated."

Aira blinked at him.

Aira: "You truly said that out loud."

Cassian laughed again.

Elira's tone remained polite.

Elira: "Then we should all avoid them."

Leon looked as if he was trying to decide whether that had been agreement or criticism.

It had been both.

Professor Halden's voice carried across the room.

Professor Halden: "Group Three. Remain after dismissal. Your first joint exercise begins immediately."

Aira closed her eyes.

Aira: "It does."

I looked at the team list once more.

A Yellow Circle prodigy.
A calm Blue support specialist.
A humiliated Blue rival.
A volatile Red combat heir.
And me.

This was not a team.
It was an experiment.

Which meant one of two things.

Either Aurelis believed this combination had exceptional potential.

Or Professor Halden wanted to see what happened when pressure was applied to all the fault lines at once.

Given the way his gaze found me again from across the chamber, I suspected both.

The other candidate groups filtered out of the strategy chamber in clusters of relief, annoyance, or self-important discussion, leaving only the five of us and the senior evaluators behind.

The room felt larger once it emptied.

More exposed.

Professor Halden stood at the center with his hands clasped behind his back. Instructor Dren remained near the projection array, unreadable as ever. Lady Seraphine and Master Thorn took positions at opposite ends of the chamber, clearly preparing to observe rather than assist.

In other words, if this went badly, it was supposed to.

Professor Halden: "Group Three was assembled to test high-variance compatibility."

Aira folded her arms.

Aira: "A dignified way of saying you expect problems."

Professor Halden looked at her over the edge of his lenses.

Professor Halden: "Miss Solen, if I expected only problems, I would not waste faculty time observing you."

Aira paused.

Aira: "It was almost encouraging. I resent that."

Cassian let out a short laugh.

Cassian: "I like her."

Aira: "That makes one of us."

Leon clicked his tongue softly.

Leon: "Can we begin without turning this into a social exercise?"

Haru: "You say that as if group function isn't partly social."

Leon's eyes flicked to me at once.

Leon: "Some of us are here to compete seriously."

Cassian glanced between us with open interest.

Cassian: "Did I miss a duel history?"

Aira: "Several, spiritually."

Elira exhaled very quietly beside me.
Not quite a laugh.
Close.

Professor Halden raised one hand, and a three-dimensional model of the academy grounds appeared above the floor.

Professor Halden: "Tactical extraction. Enter, secure the simulated relic core, adapt, return it to the recovery zone."

The model shifted.

A forested terrain bloomed into view, divided by ravines, false ruins, and layered barrier pockets.

Professor Halden: "The field will change while you are inside."

Aira: "Because static rules might let us keep our dignity."

Lady Seraphine stepped forward.

Lady Seraphine: "Success is not enough. Command response, communication, resource allocation, conflict management. If you win while functioning like a catastrophe, your score will reflect it."

Cassian smiled.

Cassian: "That seems targeted."

Lady Seraphine: "If you feel targeted, improve."

He looked delighted by that.

Master Thorn gestured toward the west doors.

Master Thorn: "Simulation arena. Move."

---

The simulation field occupied an enormous lower training sector beneath the western grounds. It was one of the academy's most expensive strategic facilities: a configurable combat environment with terrain shaping arrays, illusion layering, atmospheric distortion grids, and autonomous construct threats.

When the doors opened, we stepped into what looked like the edge of an artificial wilderness.

Dense trees.
Broken stone ruins.
A shallow stream running through a rocky cut.
Mist hanging low over uneven ground.

At the center of the field, several crystal pylons hummed softly, feeding power into the simulation grid. Above them, a timer ignited in red light.

**TEAM EXERCISE BEGINS IN 00:30**

Aira looked at the terrain and then at the sky, as if silently asking why existence was like this.

Aira: "I would like everyone to know I prefer maps when they're not actively trying to humiliate me."

Cassian rolled one shoulder, already loosening into combat readiness.

Cassian: "Forest, ruins, limited visibility. Could be worse."

Aira stared at him.

Aira: "You are definitely someone who says 'could be worse' moments before it becomes worse."

Elira stepped forward just enough to draw the group's focus without forcing it.

Elira: "We have thirty seconds. We need initial roles."

Leon answered immediately.

Leon: "Fine. Cassian and I take forward pressure. Elira supports central control. Solen handles barrier analysis and rear adaptation. Haru-"

He paused a fraction too long.

Leon: "Haru can assist where needed."

Aira looked offended on my behalf before I had time to decide whether I cared.

Cassian glanced at me.

Cassian: "You're assigning the unknown variable to 'assist where needed'?"

Leon: "I'm assigning based on demonstrated output."

Haru: "That's one interpretation."

Elira's voice remained even.

Elira: "No. We assign based on actual behavior under pressure."

Leon turned to her.

Leon: "And what does that suggest?"

Elira: "That Haru reads patterns faster than most people here."

There was no hesitation in the way she said it.

None.

I felt Leon notice that as much as the words themselves.

Elira continued.

Elira: "Cassian applies force well. Aira stabilizes and redirects. You're strongest when your casting has structure and support. I can anchor field balance."

She looked at me.

Elira: "Haru scouts the movement logic and calls the hidden shifts before they happen."

Cassian's eyebrows rose.

Cassian: "You trust him that much?"

Elira: "Yes."

Again, immediate.
Again, uncomplicated.

That word did not do good things to my ability to remain emotionally detached.

Leon heard it too. His expression tightened very slightly.

Aira, however, looked mostly vindicated.

Aira: "Good. Finally a role assignment based on reality."

The timer hit ten seconds.

Professor Halden's voice echoed through the field.

Professor Halden: "Begin."

---

We moved.

Cassian took the lead naturally, flames gathering in sharp coils around his fists without fully manifesting. Leon stayed just off his right flank, water mana already threading in low currents around the ground to test for hidden traps. Aira remained central, scanning the barrier lines and ambient shifts. Elira walked with the calm of someone whose presence alone stabilized the team's rhythm.

I watched the terrain.
Not the trees.
Not the ruins.
The mana beneath them.

Simulation environments always had logic.
Even chaos was built on design.

The stream to our left was too quiet.
The mist on the right was denser than the ambient temperature justified.
The old stone path ahead had micro-distortions suggesting delayed trigger arrays.

Haru: "Left stream is false-safe. Don't cross it directly. Right mist likely contains visual decoys. Path ahead is pressure-marked under the third and sixth stones."

Cassian looked back mid-stride.

Cassian: "You see all that this quickly?"

Haru: "Enough of it."

Leon did not look at me.

Leon: "Or he's guessing confidently."

Aira: "That would matter more if he kept being wrong."

Cassian laughed.

Cassian: "All right, Green. Lead with your eyes, then."

We adjusted course. Aira used a low-pressure water film to reveal hidden lines in the ground. The third and sixth stones lit up instantly-trap confirmed.

Leon said nothing.

That silence was almost more satisfying than rebuttal.

We cut through a side corridor of broken ruins instead, where the first constructs attacked.

They emerged from the stone like armored beasts made of compacted mana and bronze light, four-legged and fast, jaws opening with sharpened arcane edges.

Cassian grinned.

Cassian: "Finally."

He launched forward in a burst of red-orange fire, striking the first construct with a reinforced elbow that shattered its shoulder plate. Leon followed with slicing water crescents aimed at the leg joints. Efficient. Controlled. Better than during our direct matches. Competition suited him better than wounded pride.

The second wave came from above.

Haru: "Upper left."

Elira was already moving.

Golden light rose in a curved shield just as two airborne constructs dropped from the ruins. They hit the barrier and rebounded into open range. Aira trapped one in a compressed water sphere long enough for Cassian to destroy it with a downward arc of flame.

The fifth construct slipped past the front line and lunged toward Aira.

Leon reacted a beat too late.
I didn't.

I stepped in, redirected its trajectory with a compact reinforcement pulse, and drove it into the stone wall hard enough to disable the core.

Cassian whistled.

Cassian: "You really aren't a normal Green, are you?"

Haru: "I get that a lot."

Aira: "Not enough, honestly."

We kept moving.

The deeper we went into the simulation, the more obvious the evaluators' intention became.

This was not a simple retrieval exercise.
It was a pressure chamber.

Every five minutes the terrain changed.
The stream rose and became electrically charged.
The ruins shifted positions.
False objective signatures appeared in the distance to split decision-making.
At one point, illusion duplicates of our own team members appeared through the trees, each calling conflicting instructions.

Aira was the first to identify the pattern.

Aira: "The false voices are too clean. Real mana resonance always drags on the edges in this much distortion."

Haru: "Agreed. Ignore anything that sounds easier than the situation deserves."

Cassian smiled.

Cassian: "That may be your best tactical principle yet."

Then the first real problem arrived.

The objective chamber-a ruined circular structure at the center of the field-was protected by a layered barrier lock requiring simultaneous affinity alignment at four anchor points.

We reached the outer ring and stopped.

Aira immediately knelt to inspect the runes.

Aira: "This is not one lock. It's four interdependent locks sharing backlash."

Leon crouched opposite her, running water mana across the etched lines.

Leon: "If one anchor is forced incorrectly, the others reset."

Cassian folded his arms.

Cassian: "Can we break it?"

Aira and Leon answered together.

Aira: "No."

Leon: "No."

Cassian looked mildly offended.

Elira stepped toward the ring.

Elira: "What does it require?"

Aira traced the nearest sigil.

Aira: "Balanced input, not brute force. Four nodes. Different densities. One has to modulate, one has to stabilize, one has to pressure, and one has to absorb recoil."

Cassian pointed at himself.

Cassian: "Pressure. Obviously."

Leon nodded toward the fluid channels.

Leon: "I can handle modulation."

Aira: "I'll stabilize."

Elira looked at the final node.

Elira: "Then I'll absorb recoil."

I looked more closely at the line structure and felt the problem immediately.

Haru: "No."

Everyone turned toward me.

Elira: "Why?"

I stepped nearer the lock.

Haru: "Because the recoil node isn't defensive. It's adaptive. It will mirror the mana signature of whoever takes it and amplify any instability in the other three."

Aira's eyes widened as she re-read the rune pattern.

Aira: "He's right."

Leon frowned.

Leon: "Then who takes it?"

The answer was obvious.

I looked at the node.

Haru: "I will."

Leon's disbelief was immediate.

Leon: "Absolutely not."

Cassian looked intrigued.

Cassian: "Why not? If he can handle it, let him."

Leon: "Because if he fails, the whole structure resets."

Aira straightened.

Aira: "And if Elira takes it, her mana profile may overload the adaptive reflection. Haru is the most stable option if the node values control over raw output."

Leon looked between the three of us like betrayal had become procedural.

Leon: "We are seriously trusting the Green on the key node?"

Elira's gaze held steady.

Elira: "Yes."

Again.
That word.

Cassian's grin sharpened.

Cassian: "Well, now I want to see it."

We took positions.

Aira at the stabilization node.
Leon at modulation.
Cassian at pressure.
Me at the recoil-adaptive anchor.
Elira stood central, ready to field-correct if the chamber destabilized.

Aira counted us in.

Aira: "On three. One. Two. Three."

Mana flowed.

Cassian fed sharp, measured force into his node. The rune blazed red-orange. Leon's water mana coiled into the second, smoothing the rotational rhythm. Aira layered fine support geometry into the third, holding the lock's structure together.

Then I touched the fourth.

Even suppressed, the Black Circle made control like this easy.
Too easy.

The adaptive node drank in my mana and went still.
Not overloaded.
Not erratic.
Still.

It had recognized something beyond the scope of its own design and, finding no way to meaningfully reflect it, settled for obedience.

The risk sat cold under my ribs.
So I carefully reduced the flow, forcing the structure to behave as though it were merely impressed by excellent Green Circle control.

The lock rotated.
The four anchors aligned.
The barrier split open.

Cassian laughed aloud.

Cassian: "It was absurdly clean."

Aira looked at me with narrowed eyes.

Aira: "You make too many impossible things look tidy."

Leon said nothing.
But he was watching me now with a more serious kind of attention.

Inside the chamber, the relic core floated above a pedestal of broken stone-a crystal orb filled with swirling blue-white energy. Simulated, of course. But valuable to the scoring system all the same.

Elira stepped toward it.

Elira: "We secure and return."

Professor Halden's voice rang through the arena.

Professor Halden: "Complication phase."

The chamber floor dropped.

Not literally.
Illusionally-but with enough force coupling to make the body believe it.

The circular ruin shattered into floating platforms. The forest beyond darkened into stormlight. The relic core split into three copies, each radiating different signatures.

Cassian laughed again.

Cassian: "There it is."

Aira: "Why do you always sound pleased when things become unreasonable?"

Cassian: "Because that's when people become interesting."

Leon pointed immediately.

Leon: "Center core. Strongest output. That's the real one."

Haru: "No."

Elira turned to me.

Elira: "Which?"

I studied the three orbs.
One burned too brightly-obvious bait.
One was too stable for a field under active disruption.
The third, slightly dimmer and off to the left, was imperfect in exactly the way reality usually was.

Haru: "Left."

Leon: "Based on what?"

Haru: "The others are cleaner than the simulation's distortion pattern. Left core carries the same environmental drag as the platform grid."

Aira looked once and immediately nodded.

Aira: "He's right again."

Cassian grinned at Leon.

Cassian: "You're losing a difficult argument, Blue."

Leon ignored him, but only barely.

The constructs returned then, stronger than before-winged and humanoid this time, armed with projected weapons and shield arrays. Three descended toward the left platform. Two cut off our retreat.

Elira moved first.

Golden light spread outward in a radiant ring that slowed the nearest attackers and stabilized the collapsing edge of the platform. Aira reinforced the underside structure with water-binding geometry. Leon cut down one construct with a focused crescent blade that was honestly impressive.

Cassian leapt straight through the middle of the chaos, fire erupting around him in controlled bursts as he smashed one of the shielded constructs apart.

I moved to the relic core.

The moment my hand closed around it, the entire simulation screamed.

Red light flooded the arena.

**OBJECTIVE SECURED**  
**EXTRACTION WINDOW: 02:00**

A two-minute retreat under escalating pressure.
Naturally.

Haru: "Move."

We did.

This time no one questioned who had called it.

That, more than anything else, told me the team exercise was changing us.

Not into friends.
Certainly not yet.

But into something more dangerous and useful:
people beginning to trust competence despite themselves.

We ran the extraction path with the relic core protected at center. Aira adjusted routes on the fly. Leon finally stopped resisting team logic and focused on what he did well: fluid control, barrier cutting, path shaping. Cassian broke anything too slow to avoid. Elira held the group together like a sun at the center of moving gravity.

And I watched the shifting field, calling the invisible changes before they happened.

Then the simulation became personal.

The forest ahead split open into a narrow ravine crossed by a broken bridge, and a student appeared on the far side.

Not a real student.

An adaptive construct wearing the uniform of an Aurelis first-year, kneeling with one hand pressed to a cracked barrier while shadow beasts hammered against it from three directions. The construct was built well enough to breathe unevenly. To tremble. To look up with panic that did not belong to a training field.

The relic core pulsed in my hands.

The recovery route glowed beyond the ravine.

The timer continued falling.

Leon: "It's a decoy."

Aira: "It is also a scoring condition. Look at the barrier line."

She was right. A thin silver marker glowed under the construct's feet, nearly hidden by dust.

**OPTIONAL RESCUE NODE**

Cassian's fire flared around one wrist.

Cassian: "We can clear that."

Leon: "And lose time."

Cassian: "Then we lose time."

Leon turned sharply.

Leon: "We are carrying the objective."

Elira looked at the construct, then at the route, then at us.

Her expression did not change, but the air around her warmed by one degree.

Elira: "If this were real, we would not leave them."

Leon: "It is not real."

Haru: "The evaluators are watching whether that matters."

That stopped him.

For a heartbeat, Leon looked exactly like someone standing between the person he had been and the teammate he was not yet sure he could become.

Aira's voice softened without losing its edge.

Aira: "If we rescue wrong, we fail both objectives. If we ignore it, we tell the faculty what kind of victory we are willing to accept."

The timer hit forty-seven seconds.

No time for a speech.

Good.

Speeches were often what people used when action frightened them.

Haru: "Cassian, break the beasts from the outside only. Do not touch the construct's barrier. Leon, bridge the ravine with water tension under Aira's stabilizer. Elira, keep the relic core shielded from the rebound. Aira, call collapse timing."

For once, no one argued.

Cassian moved first, crossing the broken bridge with three explosive steps and striking the nearest shadow beast so cleanly it shattered without throwing debris into the construct. Leon threw a water line under the bridge and hardened it into a temporary path. Aira layered fine support geometry over his work, teeth clenched in concentration.

Elira stepped beside me, gold light closing around the relic core.

Elira: "You included yourself nowhere."

Haru: "I am holding the problem."

Elira: "You are part of the team."

The sentence landed faster than I expected.

Haru: "Then I am holding the center."

Her eyes softened for half a second.

Then the ravine wall cracked.

Aira: "Collapse in five."

Leon: "Cassian!"

Cassian caught the false student by the back of the uniform and threw them toward the bridge with more precision than gentleness. Aira caught the construct in a water-cushioned barrier. Leon pulled. Elira sealed the backlash around the relic core.

I stepped onto the cracked bridge last.

The ravine tried to eat the path under me.

For one dangerous instant, the Black Circle offered the simplest solution.

Erase the collapse.
Rewrite the bridge.
Make the simulation remember a safer world.

I did none of those things.

I used one Green-level reinforcement pulse at exactly the correct joint and let the broken bridge survive long enough to be crossed by people who had earned the rescue themselves.

We reached the far side as the ravine folded shut behind us.

The construct dissolved into silver light.

**RESCUE NODE CLEARED**

Leon stared at the fading marker, breathing hard.

Leon: "That cost us twelve seconds."

Aira: "It also added rescue credit."

Cassian rolled his shoulders, expression sharper now.

Cassian: "Worth it."

Leon looked as if he wanted to disagree.

Then he looked at Elira.

At Aira.

At the path we had not abandoned.

Leon: "Next time, call the bridge sooner."

It was not an apology.

It was more useful than one.

Three hidden mines beneath the southern ledge.
A collapsing bridge illusion masking a safe path to the right.
A delayed strike array embedded in the false exit gate.

I caught the first two.

I almost missed the third because Elira's shield flickered at the edge of my vision and my attention went to her before the field.

Leon saw my eyes shift.

Leon: "Exit gate-right seam."

I cut the warning short with a nod and adjusted the route.

We adapted.
Again and again.

By the time we crossed into the recovery zone with seventeen seconds remaining, the entire observation gallery had fallen silent.

We had won not because we were strongest, but because we had refused to choose between victory and each other.

The recovery zone was one breath away, and beyond it waited the kind of tournament that would make that refusal costly.

By the time we left the simulation sector, the western courtyard had filled with the controlled noise of visiting-school preparations.

Valemere Royal Institute arrived before sunset in silver-blue precision: polished carriages, clean banners, escort mages who bowed to Aurelis faculty instead of pretending not to measure them. Their magic did not press against the air the way Noctis rumors did. It moved like formal footwork. Graceful. Dangerous. Honest about both.

At the head of their student delegation stood Soren Ashvale.

He was taller than most of the students around him, dark-haired, warm-eyed, and carried himself like a duelist who had learned that confidence did not require making other people smaller. Faint scars crossed the backs of his hands. He wore them openly.

When he saw Elira, he approached with a respectful distance already built into his stride.

Soren: "Lady Vale."

Elira's public composure settled around her at once.

Elira: "Mr. Ashvale."

Soren smiled, not possessive. Not familiar.

Soren: "I've followed your work in the junior circuit. Your paper on layered reinforcement changed how Valemere teaches counter-pressure in formal dueling."

Elira's surprise lasted only a heartbeat, but I saw it.

Elira: "That is generous."

Soren: "It is accurate."

His gaze moved to me. Not dismissive. Assessing, yes, but cleanly.

Soren: "Your partner?"

Elira did not hesitate.

Elira: "Yes. Haru Aerlen."

The word partner moved through me with more force than it should have.

Soren offered his hand.

Soren: "Lucky man."

His grip was firm, honest, and without challenge.

Haru: "Yes."

Elira's fingers found mine before I could decide whether the answer had been too revealing.

Soren noticed.

He did.

He only smiled and released my hand.

Soren: "Then I look forward to facing Aurelis properly."

He returned to Valemere without looking back.

At Valemere, honor had not been taught to Soren as ornament. It was not posture, lineage, or the polite cruelty of winning cleanly enough that no one could object.

It was responsibility.

He had learned that too late once, during a junior exhibition where a defeated student stayed down longer than the official record admitted. Soren had been younger then, careful enough to keep his place and cowardly enough to call that caution. The memory had stayed in the scars across his hands more sharply than any duel.

Since then, he had sworn that honor meant ensuring the fight remained fair, especially when the powerful could make unfairness look like victory.

Aira appeared beside us as if summoned by social tension.

Aira: "It was the most polite romantic threat I have ever witnessed."

Haru: "He was not threatening."

Aira: "No. That is what made it effective."

Elira's thumb moved once against my hand.

Not reassurance.

Recognition.

For one embarrassing second, jealousy tried to become useful information. It failed. Soren had admired Elira without trying to claim her. He had recognized us without trying to test whether we were real.

That kind of honor was inconvenient.

It made Lucien's eventual arrival feel colder before he had even stepped through the gates.

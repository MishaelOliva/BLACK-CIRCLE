# Episode 15: **The Spell Noctis Buried**

Elira refused the infirmary.

She did it politely, which made three healers, one council officer, and Leon all look personally insulted.

Leon: "You have frost under your collar."

Elira: "I am aware."

Leon: "That was not my question."

Elira: "You did not ask one."

Cassian leaned toward me.

Cassian: "That means she won."

I did not answer.

My attention was on the thin white mark where cold had touched Elira's skin above the pendant chain. It had already begun fading, but the memory of it remained in the mana around her like a fingerprint left on glass.

Something had mapped the protection.

Not broken it.

Not yet.

Mapped it.

Elira caught me looking and turned slightly so the collar hid the mark.

Elira: "Haru."

Haru: "I know."

Elira: "Do you?"

That stopped me.

She stepped closer, lowering her voice while the others argued with the healers.

Elira: "Knowing means you do not turn this into a reason to make decisions around me."

I looked at the floor.

The old stone reflected dawn even though sunset had just passed.

Haru: "I built it to protect you."

Elira: "And I accepted it because I trusted the person who built it."

Her hand touched the chain beneath her collar.

Elira: "Do not let Lucien make this pendant into proof that I am only your vulnerable point."

My chest hurt.

No ozone.

No metal.

Just the ordinary pain of being seen correctly.

Haru: "All right."

Elira's expression softened.

Elira: "I am not asking you not to be afraid."

Haru: "That would be harder."

Elira: "I know."

She reached for my hand in plain view.

Not hidden.

The corridor noticed.

Let it.

Elira: "I am asking you to let me remain one of the people making choices."

I closed my fingers around hers.

Haru: "I will try."

Elira: "Try honestly."

Haru: "I will."

Aira appeared beside us with a folder pressed against her chest.

Aira: "I hate to interrupt a healthy emotional negotiation, especially because they are rare and should be studied, but Nera found something in the final conduit grammar."

Elira did not release my hand immediately.

Good.

Then she did, because there was work.

The relay grammar room had become less like a classroom and more like the inside of a complicated headache.

Pages covered the walls.

Noctis relay marks were grouped by function: custody, conduit, restoration, severance, witness suppression, obedience echo, courtesy hook.

Aira had added a separate label in green ink:

**WORDS THAT MEAN SOMETHING WORSE THAN THEY SOUND**

Under it were mercy, protection, correction, recovery, and return.

Nera stood before the restoration section with her sleeves rolled up and both binding rings removed.

The pale lines beneath them showed where Noctis had pressed training into skin over years.

She did not hide the marks today.

That was not confidence.

It was evidence.

Aurelis healers had offered soft silver wraps to cover the old pressure scars. Nera had listened politely, thanked them, and placed the wraps on the table without using them.

When Oric saw, he rolled his own cuffs back too.

His marks were less precise than hers.

Messier.

Not because his training had been kinder.

Because Noctis had corrected him more often.

No one said that aloud.

The room did not need more proof that cruelty had handwriting.

Mira had been allowed inside for the first ten minutes as a public record aide, under Rhea's direct supervision and Aira's promise to physically drag her out if the grammar started reaching for observers.

Mira stood near the door with her green folio open, trying very hard not to stare at Nera's wrists.

Nera noticed.

Mira went red.

Mira: "Sorry."

Nera looked down at the marks, then back at her.

Nera: "Write that I chose to show them."

Mira's pencil paused.

Mira: "Are you sure?"

Nera: "No."

She turned back to the wall.

Nera: "Write it anyway."

Mira did.

The line looked small on the page.

It was not small in the room.

Oric watched the door.

He did that even when the door was open.

Professor Valen sat at the table with three old Aurelis records and a Noctis primer that looked embarrassed to be in better company.

Halden stood near the wall, reading without touching anything.

Cael sat with his injured hand wrapped in new silver thread.

His eyes lifted when we entered.

He looked at Elira's collar.

Then away.

Cael: "I'm sorry."

Elira crossed the room and crouched before his chair.

Elira: "For what?"

Cael's hand curled.

Cael: "Lucien found the pendant because of me."

Elira's answer was immediate.

Elira: "No."

Cael flinched at the firmness.

She softened her voice without softening the truth.

Elira: "Lucien found a pressure point because Lucien looks for pressure points. You are not responsible for his hands."

Cael stared at her.

Then nodded once.

Not convinced.

But listening.

Nera tapped the wall.

Nera: "The spell I found is called Votive Return in old Noctis notation."

Aira: "I already hate the name."

Oric: "You should."

Nera pointed to three linked symbols.

Nera: "It is not standard healing. It uses a living conduit to force a dead or near-dead channel pattern back into coherence. The restored person survives if the pattern is intact enough to receive the transfer."

Professor Halden: "And the conduit?"

Nera's face closed.

Nera: "The conduit dies."

No one made a sound.

Cael looked at his hand.

Cael: "Usually."

Leon, who had entered behind us, went rigid.

Leon: "What does usually mean?"

Cael's voice was thin.

Cael: "Noctis instructors said exceptional conduits might remain as a relay shadow if the spell was properly witnessed."

Oric made a sharp sound.

Oric: "That was a lie to make children stop crying during training."

Cael did not argue.

That was worse.

Aira wrote **VOTIVE RETURN = DO NOT USE** across the top of a new page.

Nera watched the words appear.

Nera: "Aira."

Aira: "No."

Nera: "I was going to agree."

Aira stopped.

Nera took the pencil and added beneath it:

**NOT HEALING. TRANSFER EXECUTION.**

Then she underlined it twice.

Oric added:

**NOCTIS MAY FRAME AS CHOICE. CHECK FOR COERCION.**

Cael watched them write rules against spending him.

Something in his face changed.

Not hope.

Hope was too bright a word.

Recognition, maybe.

As if the room had placed a chair somewhere he had expected only an altar.

Professor Valen turned one of her old Aurelis pages.

Professor Valen: "There is a reason Noctis kept this language."

Halden looked over.

Professor Valen: "Aurelis had a rejected parallel during the pre-standardization era. Not Votive Return. Witness Restoration. It required multiple witnesses to share stabilizing load so no single life became the cost."

The Foundation pool rippled.

Not water—memory made visible.

An elderly woman's form coalesced from the silver-black surface. Not solid. Not quite transparent. A preserved witness, speaking across decades through the record itself.

She leaned on a cane that did not quite touch the floor. She had no visible Circle. Not Green, Blue, Red, Yellow, or anything the modern eye could classify. Her hair was bound in a severe knot, and her voice carried a faint echo, real emotion filtered through old Foundation preservation.

Professor Valen whispered, "Liora."

The echo turned toward her.

Liora: "Liora Aerlen-Valen. Witness-record remnant. Not alive enough for comfort. Not dead enough to be useless."

My hidden circle went cold.

Her eyes found me.

Liora: "You carry it like my brother did. Too quietly. Too alone."

No one spoke.

Liora lowered the end of her cane toward the page.

The cane passed through the parchment without moving it.

Liora: "We built witness networks because Black authority without witnesses becomes a disaster even when the heart is kind. Noctis learned that, then cut away the sharing and kept the sacrifice."

Her gaze moved to Cael.

Then back to me.

Liora: "Someone is trying to force you toward a corrupted return. Do not mistake a life spent alone for mercy."

I felt the old mana beneath the tower stir.

Not cold this time.

Grieving.

The grief did not belong to a person.

That made it harder to bear.

Human grief had edges. A face, a voice, a hand left empty. This was architectural grief, old enough to have settled into stone and patient enough to wait centuries for someone to make the same mistake in a new uniform.

The pages on the table lifted by themselves.

Not violently.

Like breath moving under paper.

One record showed seven witness marks around a body of light.

Another showed the same marks scratched out by an official seal.

A third showed nothing but a list of names, each one blurred except for the first letter.

Professor Valen touched the edge of that page.

Professor Valen: "These were witnesses."

Halden stepped closer.

Professor Halden: "Removed from the record?"

Professor Valen: "Reduced."

That word felt worse.

Removed implied someone had wanted absence.

Reduced implied someone had wanted convenience.

Valen read the legible fragments.

Professor Valen: "A Green Circle scribe. A Blue Circle stabilizer. A Red Circle field holder. A Yellow Circle witness. Three others obscured by later standardization."

Aira leaned over the table despite herself.

Aira: "Three others?"

Valen nodded.

Professor Valen: "The old system did not only count rank. It counted relation to the event. Person who saw harm first. Person who could interrupt. Person who would remember accurately. Person the candidate trusted. Person the harmed life recognized. Person who could object. Person who would carry the record after."

Mira's pencil stopped.

Rhea looked at the page with a president's attention and a student's horror.

President Valmont: "That is a witness network."

Professor Valen: "Yes."

Halden's face tightened.

Professor Halden: "And standardization flattened it into authority categories."

Professor Valen: "Yes."

The room understood what that meant for Cael before anyone said it.

Noctis had not invented the idea that a person could be turned into a function.

It had inherited the knife and polished the handle.

Professor Valen: "It failed."

Elira: "Why?"

Valen's fingers rested on the page.

Professor Valen: "Because after the emergency protection order, there were no witnesses left."

The room understood together.

That did not mean we understood the same way.

Leon looked ready to break a wall.

Cassian looked like he was memorizing a future battlefield.

Aira looked like she had found the shape of tomorrow's nightmare and hated that it fit the filing system.

Cael looked smaller.

I looked at Elira.

She was reading the page, not me.

Good.

Then the dark crystal at her throat pulsed once beneath her collar.

Every old Aurelis page on the table turned by itself.

Valen's hand lifted.

Professor Valen: "Oh."

Halden stepped forward.

Professor Halden: "What did it answer?"

The pages stopped on a diagram of seven circles around a central mark.

Not Black Circle.

Not exactly.

A space where Black Circle had been removed from the drawing.

Under it, old script formed slowly.

**WITNESS RESTORATION INCOMPLETE**

**PRIMARY WITNESS ANCHOR REQUIRED**

**SAFEGUARD MUST NOT ACT ALONE**

Elira's pendant grew warm enough that I felt it from across the room.

Haru: "Stop."

The word came out too cold.

Cael flinched.

I saw it.

Everyone did.

The hidden Black Circle inside me pressed once against the disguise.

I forced my hand open.

Haru: "Sorry."

The apology was not enough.

I made it more exact.

Haru: "That was fear. Not an order."

Cael's shoulders lowered by a fraction.

Elira looked at me.

Proud.

Worried.

Both were difficult to survive.

Aira let out a breath.

Aira: "Good. We are naming emotional weather now. Horrible but useful."

Rhea entered halfway through the sentence and decided not to ask.

President Valmont: "Noctis has submitted the next request."

Nera turned from the wall.

Nera: "Already?"

President Valmont: "They want a controlled autonomy demonstration inside a Foundation activity field."

Cassian's eyes brightened.

Cassian: "Activity field as in combat?"

Rhea gave him a look.

President Valmont: "As in a procedural field designed to simulate pressure without fatal consequence."

Leon: "Because those always work perfectly when Lucien is involved."

President Valmont: "No."

Rhea held up the filing.

President Valmont: "Which is why I filed our counterconditions first."

Aira sat up.

Aira: "You anticipated it."

President Valmont: "Lucien is not the only person who can read a path."

Rhea placed the page on the table.

President Valmont: "No private isolation. No removal of healers. No direct classification prompts. No Noctis relay shard inside the field. Aurelis chooses anchor distribution. The Foundation records all pressure changes. If the field attempts live channel harm, the activity ends by council authority."

Leon: "Will Noctis accept that?"

Professor Halden answered.

Professor Halden: "They will if they believe they have another route."

Nera looked at Elira's collar again.

Nera: "They do."

The next morning, the activity field unfolded beneath the council tower.

It was not an arena.

It looked like a city square built from white stone and silver glass, with narrow streets, relief markers, wounded-simulation figures, and shifting mana weather. The Foundation called it a procedural autonomy activity.

Aira called it a trap with interior design.

She was not wrong.

The square had benches placed exactly where tired students would want benches. Doorways opened onto routes that looked safer than they were. The wounded-simulation figures were faceless enough to be neutral and human enough to make neutrality feel cruel.

Above the field, the public witness rail had been divided into colored sections by Rhea's council seals.

Green Circle records.

Blue Circle support observers.

Red Circle safety holders.

Yellow Circle faculty.

Neutral witnesses.

Noctis observers.

Each section had a different route out in case the field tried to turn audience into leverage.

Rhea had thought of that.

Lucien probably had too.

That was the problem with fighting someone clever: good preparation did not mean safety. It only meant the trap would have to be more interesting.

Aira walked the edge of the activity floor with a chalk marker in one hand and a string of blue anchor beads in the other.

Aira: "If anyone sees a door where there was not a door, say door. If anyone hears a familiar voice from a faceless marker, say mimic. If anyone feels suddenly certain the solution is obvious, say obvious, because obvious is usually where the floor eats you."

Leon raised a hand.

Aira: "This is not a classroom."

Leon: "What if the solution is actually obvious?"

Aira: "Then we will all be pleasantly surprised and I will retire."

Cassian laughed.

Cassian: "You would last six minutes."

Aira: "Five if there are forms."

Cael listened from beside Nera, one hand over the healer seal on his wrist.

Elira noticed the hand.

Elira: "Cael."

He lowered it.

Then raised it again halfway.

Then stopped, frustrated with himself.

Cael: "It feels louder here."

Nera's attention sharpened.

Nera: "The seal?"

Cael: "The training."

No one corrected the wording.

Training could become a sound inside the body when it had been used often enough as a leash.

Elira stepped close enough that he could hear her without the field hearing too much.

Elira: "Name the present before the past gives orders."

Cael swallowed.

Cael: "Aurelis activity field. Public witnesses. Council protection. Noctis observers limited."

Leon added, blunt and immediate:

Leon: "And me."

Cael looked at him.

Leon: "Not as a system. As me."

Cael's mouth almost smiled.

Cael: "Leon Varek. Loud protection."

Leon looked offended.

Leon: "Disciplined protection."

Cassian: "Occasionally disciplined."

Rhea's voice cut in from the threshold.

President Valmont: "Sufficiently disciplined for entry."

Leon muttered something that sounded like he was choosing not to argue with official praise.

The objective was simple on paper: move three simulated wounded markers through a pressure field while hostile procedural conditions attempted to force command dependency.

In practice, the field kept asking the same question in different shapes.

Who decides?

Leon found the first wounded marker under a collapsed arch and moved before I could speak.

Leon: "Water path left. Cassian, heat the ice seam. Aira, tell me if the old stones start lying."

Aira: "They already are, but I appreciate being included."

Cassian burned a clean line through the frost and did not overdo it.

That alone deserved a footnote.

Mira, watching from the public record ring, wrote so fast her pencil squeaked.

The second pressure wave targeted Rhea.

A silver notice appeared in front of her.

**COMMAND AUTHORITY CONFLICT**

**STUDENT COUNCIL PRESIDENT MAY REMOVE UNCLASSIFIED SUBJECT FOR SAFETY**

Rhea did not slow down.

President Valmont: "Rejected. Safety is not solitude."

Her red seal split into three smaller seals and assigned routes to the healers.

The field accepted the answer.

The third wave targeted Elira.

The street darkened.

Every window reflected me.

Not as Haru.

As a black space wearing Green Circle light.

Elira stood at the center of the square while the reflections multiplied around her.

**PRIMARY WITNESS PRESSURE**

**STEP AWAY TO PRESERVE RESTRAINT**

The pendant at her throat flashed cold.

I moved.

One step.

Elira lifted her hand without looking back.

Stop.

The command was not spoken.

It did not need to be.

She faced the reflections.

Elira: "No."

The field pressed harder.

The frost mark under her collar returned.

Elira's fingers curled, but her voice held.

Elira: "If restraint only survives because love leaves the room, it is not restraint. It is abandonment with better manners."

The square answered in silver.

**PRIMARY WITNESS AUTONOMY: ESTABLISHED**

Relief moved through the witness ring.

Too soon.

One reflection did not fade.

It smiled.

Lucien's voice came through the glass.

Lucien: "Beautifully said."

The pendant went silent.

Completely silent.

The reflection lifted one hand.

Lucien: "Now let us learn whether it can bleed."

Every window in the activity field turned black.

The Foundation bell rang emergency once.

Not ending the field.

Warning that something else had entered it.

For one breath, no one moved.

That was not fear.

It was everyone learning the same thing at the same time.

The protections Rhea had negotiated were still active.

The public witnesses were still present.

The field rules had not broken.

And Lucien had entered anyway.

Not by smashing the door.

By making the door agree it had always been open.

Aira's support map flashed blue, then black, then blue again.

She slapped both hands over it.

Aira: "No. Absolutely not. You do not get to edit my map while I am holding it."

Rhea's red seal expanded around the witness rail.

President Valmont: "All observers remain in place. No one answers a reflection."

Mira's voice came from above, too high and too brave.

Mira: "Reflections are not witnesses."

Professor Valen answered immediately.

Professor Valen: "Correct. Record that distinction."

Halden looked at the black windows.

Professor Halden: "This is not an attack pattern."

Cassian's fire rose around his fists.

Cassian: "It looks attack-adjacent."

Professor Halden: "It is worse. It is an inquiry."

Nera's face changed.

Nera: "Measurement."

Oric swore under his breath.

Cael heard the word and went still.

Elira did not look away from the reflections.

Elira: "Measurement of what?"

The black windows answered by turning toward her pendant.

Every pane.

Every reflection.

Every false Haru wearing green light.

The dark crystal at her throat went colder than winter.

I felt the protection I had made recoil against a question it had never been built to answer:

If love is named possession, does protection become a chain?

My hand moved before I allowed it.

Elira's hand lifted again.

Stop.

This time, her fingers trembled.

That mattered more than the command.

I stopped because she asked.

I stayed because she was afraid and still asking.

The black windows smiled with Lucien's mouth.

Lucien: "Now we begin honestly."

The activity floor answered him before any of us could.

The three wounded markers we had already extracted reappeared in the safe zone.

Not harmed.

Watching.

Faceless heads turned toward Elira with the quiet accusation of people who had been saved once and might be used as leverage next. The field had learned from us. That was the worst part. It was no longer only Lucien pressing from outside. It was the activity taking our answers and asking whether they would survive uglier use.

Aira's map unfolded again, this time with black veins running under the streets.

Aira: "Rhea, the safe zone is being converted into a witness pressure reservoir."

Rhea's seal cut across the nearest vein.

President Valmont: "Plain language."

Aira: "The field is using saved markers as moral hostages."

Leon: "I understood that one."

Cassian's fire moved low along the ground, not touching the markers, burning only the black veins as they surfaced.

Cassian: "Permission to hate the architecture again?"

Professor Valen: "Granted."

Halden's eyes stayed on the windows.

Professor Halden: "Do not overcommit to the veins. They are secondary."

Nera nodded sharply.

Nera: "He wants Elira to defend the saved markers and expose the pendant's priority structure."

Elira heard every word.

She stepped backward instead of forward.

That cost her.

I saw it in the set of her shoulders.

She had built her life around moving toward harm with enough strength to matter. Choosing not to move because movement had been made into bait was its own kind of pain.

Elira: "He does not get to define restraint as abandonment."

Aira looked at the safe zone.

Aira: "Then we prove someone else can cover them."

Leon moved before anyone assigned him.

Leon: "Water shelter on the markers."

Cassian shifted with him.

Cassian: "Heat perimeter."

Rhea's red seal settled over both spells.

President Valmont: "Authorized."

Mira shouted from the rail:

Mira: "Protection transferred by witness network, not primary witness."

The black veins recoiled from the safe zone.

Lucien's windows watched.

One of his reflections lost its smile.

Only one.

I kept that one in my mind.

Not because it meant victory.

Because it meant surprise was still possible.

Elira's hand found mine for half a heartbeat.

Her fingers were cold.

Then she let go and faced the windows again.

Elira: "Begin honestly, then."

Gold light rose around her, disciplined despite the frost mark beneath her collar.

Elira: "I am afraid. I am still here. Those facts do not cancel each other."

The field held the statement.

No silver verdict came.

Not yet.

Lucien's reflection answered softly:

Lucien: "No. But they do make such a clean place to cut."

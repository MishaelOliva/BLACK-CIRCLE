# Episode 1: **The Green Circle Transfer Student**

The morning sky over **Aurelis City** caught the sun in layered gold, each cloud edge refracting mana into angles light should not have known.

Pale gold light stretched across the clouds, and between the white towers and glass bridges of the capital, floating transport sigils shimmered in neat lines through the air. Suspended carriages passed soundlessly above the avenues. Fountains in the lower plazas rose in spirals shaped by spellcraft rather than pressure. Even the lamps along the streets glowed with a soft, living pulse, responding to the movement of people beneath them.

My name was **Haru**.

And on the morning I arrived at **Aurelis Arcane Academy**, one of the most prestigious elite high schools in the world, I was carrying enough power to bend reality itself while pretending to be the weakest kind of magician society could tolerate.

A Green Circle.

I stood before the academy gates and adjusted the collar of my new uniform.

The silver-blue crest of Aurelis was stitched neatly into the breast pocket. The fabric was expensive, durable, and enchanted against minor elemental damage. It was the daily uniform, cut for movement and classroom work rather than ceremony, but even that carried more polish than most district schools reserved for graduation.

The kind of uniform meant to remind students that this institution did not merely teach greatness-it expected it.

The gate's old ward pressure settled behind my sternum, cold enough to make my breathing careful.

Ahead of me, the academy rose like a monument to status.

Tall crystal windows reflected the sunlight in fractured colors. The main building stood beneath a domed roof encircled by six rune-marked towers. White stone stairways led through a courtyard lined with silver-leaf trees and statues of famous graduates. Floating glyphs rotated above the entrance, displaying academic honors, tournament victories, and lists of alumni who now governed cities, corporations, and magical institutions.

It was beautiful in the way only old power could be.

And it was exactly the kind of place where a secret like mine could become a disaster.

A quiet pulse stirred against my chest.

I pressed two fingers lightly over my heart.

Beneath my shirt, hidden by layers of advanced mana concealment, my true Magic Circle slept in silence.

It was not bright.

It did not blaze like Yellow or radiate with noble warmth like Red.

It was darker than shadow, precise as law, and vast enough that even fully suppressed it made the air around me feel subtly wrong if I let my concentration slip. The circle was layered with impossible geometry-patterns too old, too complete, too absolute for modern magical theory to interpret.

Black Circle.

Celestial level.

Enough power to erase armies, stop storms, or fracture the magical order of the world itself.

I had no intention of doing any of those things.

I had spent years learning how to be small.

How to suppress.

How to hide.

How to live in a world that would either worship, weaponize, or fear me if it learned what I really was.

Mana flowed from my fingertips in a whisper.

The illusion settled.

Anyone who examined me now would see a modest **Green Circle crest**. Slightly weak. Slightly unimpressive. Exactly the kind of rank people overlooked unless they were looking for someone to demean.

Perfect.

I took a breath and stepped through the gates.

The stares started almost immediately.

Not everyone noticed me. Some students were busy greeting one another. Some were checking class schedules in the floating directory panels. Some were walking in clusters by color and social position, their conversations polished and easy.

No one walked beside me.

But enough eyes shifted toward me that the pattern became obvious within seconds.

They knew.

Transfer student.  
Mid-year entry.  
Green Circle.

At a school like Aurelis, those three facts could become a topic before I reached my first classroom.

I kept walking.

Near the fountain to my right, a pair of Blue Circle boys lowered their voices just enough to show they wanted to be overheard.

Blue Student: "Green?"

Blue Student: "At Aurelis?"

Blue Student: "Maybe admissions made a mistake."

Ahead of me, two Red Circle students stepped aside to allow a Yellow Circle girl and her attendants to pass. One of the Reds noticed me, paused, and let out a short laugh.

Red Student: "That's new."

Red Student: "Try not to get lost. This school can be dangerous for ordinary people."

A few nearby students smiled.

I walked past them without reacting.

Even so, it bothered me.

Not for my own sake. I had heard worse, in colder places, from people with more authority and less restraint. But casual contempt had a way of sinking into the walls of institutions. It taught people where they were allowed to stand. It taught them what treatment they were expected to accept.

Cruelty became culture that way.

The entrance hall opened around me like a cathedral built for magical aristocracy.

A domed ceiling glimmered with artificial constellations. Black stone floors reflected the light of floating chandeliers. Twin staircases curved upward toward the upper academic wings, and golden mana lines ran beneath the floor in elegant channels, feeding power to the building's barrier systems and internal enchantments.

A luminous panel appeared in front of me.

**WELCOME, TRANSFER STUDENT: HARU AERLEN**  
**YEAR TWO - SECTION THREE**  
**HOMEROOM: EAST WING, THIRD FLOOR**

Instructor Selene: "Haru Aerlen?"

I turned.

A woman in a formal navy academy coat approached with a rune-glass tablet in hand. Her dark hair was tied back neatly, and a slender **Red Circle** crest glowed near the base of her throat. Her expression was composed, intelligent, and slightly tired in the way of people who managed too many strong personalities every day.

Instructor Selene: "I'm Instructor Selene Varis. Student Affairs. You're the transfer from Lumeris District?"

Haru: "Yes."

Instructor Selene: "Come with me. Homeroom has already started."

I nodded and followed her into the east corridor.

The deeper we went, the more obvious the academy's invisible ranking system became. Rooms reserved for top classes were closer to better mana conduits. Certain hallways displayed only Red and Yellow alumni portraits. The walls themselves carried subtle status markers-honors, access permissions, and internal privileges built around the assumption that stronger circles deserved better spaces.

In a study alcove near the eastern stair, a Green Circle student raised one hand to correct a scheduling mistake and was cut off mid-sentence by a Blue who did not even look at him. The Green apologized automatically, as if being interrupted had been his error.

Selene noticed where I was looking.

Instructor Selene: "Aurelis rewards excellence."

I wondered how many people heard that sentence and never asked who got to define excellence in the first place.

Haru: "I see."

She glanced at me.

Instructor Selene: "This school is not easy, Haru. Students here are ambitious. Competitive. Most come from influential families. You may face resistance."

Haru: "That's expected."

A flicker of approval passed through her expression.

Instructor Selene: "Your admission was unusual. Your Circle ranking caused concern. But your written examination scores were extraordinary. Theory, tactical ethics, mana structures, ancient script analysis-someone argued strongly in your favor."

Haru: "Someone?"

Instructor Selene: "I was not told who."

Mildly troubling.

I had intentionally tried not to stand out.

Apparently I had failed.

She stopped before a pair of double doors.

Inside, a lecture was already underway.

Selene opened the doors and stepped aside.

The classroom beyond was spacious and bright, with ascending rows of curved desks and large windows along one wall. At the front, a broad-shouldered instructor stood beside a floating diagram of nested spell circles.

One student glanced up.

Then another.

Conversation thinned in uneven layers as my crest became visible.

Instructor Dren: "The transfer?"

Instructor Selene: "Yes. Haru Aerlen."

Instructor Dren: "Introduce yourself."

Thirty students watched me.

I felt their mana signatures instinctively. Several Blues. Two Reds. A few Greens holding themselves small. No Yellow Circle in this class, though I suspected there would be some elsewhere in the year.

Aurelis sorted homerooms by academic track instead of rank, officially. The seating chart suggested rank still found its way into the room.

Haru: "I'm Haru Aerlen. I transferred in today. I'll do my best."

Desks creaked as students leaned toward one another.

Student: "The mid-year transfer is Green?"

Laughter spread in pockets around the room.

Red Student: "Interesting standards this year."

Student: "Did they expand support placement or lose a scholarship file?"

Instructor Dren knocked his knuckles once against the board.

Instructor Dren: "That's enough."

The class settled, though not with much grace.

Instructor Dren: "Back row. Open seat beside Aira."

As I moved through the rows, I caught a folded paper pellet before it hit my shoulder. I let it drop anyway. No reason to make that look deliberate.

At the back sat a girl with warm brown eyes and chestnut hair tied loosely to one side. Her **Blue Circle** crest glowed on the inside of her wrist as she shifted her notebook. She looked at me with curiosity, not superiority.

That alone made her stand out.

Aira: "Hi. I'm Aira Solen."

Haru: "Haru."

Aira: "Nice to meet you."

There was no mockery in it.

No hidden edge.

Just kindness.

That felt unexpectedly rare.

At the front, Instructor Dren resumed the lecture.

Instructor Dren: "As I was saying, magical society classifies human mana potential into four recognized Circle ranks."

The academy liked the word recognized.

It made the world sound clean.

Every person awakened a Magic Circle in childhood. In lower schools, children learned letters, arithmetic, and the first rules of mana control beside a quieter lesson no one wrote on the board: the color near your heart changed how loudly adults listened when you spoke.

When mana matured, a crest appeared close to the heart and the world began sorting.

Four luminous crests appeared in the air.

**Green. Blue. Red. Yellow.**

He began to walk beneath them as he spoke.

Instructor Dren: "Green Circle magicians make up the majority of the population. They possess the lowest average mana reserves and the narrowest output margin. However, low raw capacity does not make them useless. Much of society would not function without their labor, service, technical support, and stable contributions to ordinary magical systems."

Nearly sixty percent of the population, if the civic primers were feeling honest.

Foundation, they called it.

Expected labor, mostly.

A few students listened. A few smirked.

Instructor Dren: "Blue Circle magicians demonstrate stronger reserves, broader practical application, and greater combat or industrial flexibility. They comprise much of the skilled professional class."

Thirty percent.

Leon Varek, a tall Blue Circle boy near the center, sat a little straighter as if the category itself had just complimented him personally. His blond hair was swept back with deliberate care, his uniform cuffs were fastened with small silver clasps, and his water-affinity crest glowed pale blue against the inside of his wrist whenever he moved.

Instructor Dren: "Red Circle magicians possess high reserves, refined affinity potential, and superior adaptability. They disproportionately occupy positions of leadership in academia, politics, research, and business."

Nine percent.

One of the Red Circle students near the front crossed her legs and looked faintly pleased.

Instructor Dren: "And Yellow Circle magicians represent the highest recognized level of mana development known to modern society. Their numbers are extremely low, but their influence is considerable. Most institutions go years without seeing more than one."

One percent.

The numbers arrived cleanly from his mouth.

The room received them as weather.

The four circles rotated slowly above him.

Green. Blue. Red. Yellow.

The four colors washed over thirty faces.

I raised my hand.

That alone seemed to surprise people.

Instructor Dren: "Yes, transfer student?"

I kept my expression neutral.

Haru: "What about a Black Circle magician?"

Aira turned toward me so quickly her pen scratched across the margin of her notes.

Two students blinked. Someone near the front mouthed the words back as if testing whether they became less absurd silently.

Then the laughter started.

Not everyone laughed, but enough did that the sound filled the classroom in a wave.

Student: "Black Circle?"

Student: "What is this, a storybook?"

Student: "I knew Greens had weird theories, but that's embarrassing."

Leon: "Are we discussing children's myths now?"

Red Student: "You might as well ask about star dragons and moon gods."

Aira did not laugh.

She just watched me, curious.

Instructor Dren held up one hand, and the noise dropped away.

His face did not show mockery, only the patience of a teacher deciding how seriously to answer a strange question.

Instructor Dren: "In formal magical theory, there is no recognized Black Circle classification."

He turned slightly and with a motion of his hand summoned a second image beside the four crests-fragments of ancient script, incomplete diagrams, broken rings.

Instructor Dren: "However, in very old texts there are occasional references to anomalous or transcendent mana structures beyond the standard system. Most scholars consider these symbolic, religious, or metaphorical. Some regard them as errors in preservation. A minority of fringe theorists have speculated that an unknown superior rank may once have been hypothesized."

The fragments dissolved and reformed.

Instructor Dren: "But there is no verified historical evidence that any human has ever possessed such a circle. In academic practice, Black Circle is not treated as reality."

Haru: "So it's impossible?"

Dren folded his arms.

Instructor Dren: "In magic, 'impossible' is often a temporary word. But until something can be observed, measured, or reproduced, it remains speculation. Your task as students is to understand what exists, not chase every legend that flatters the imagination."

His gaze passed over the room before settling briefly on Leon's row.

Instructor Dren: "And understanding what exists includes understanding limits. Power without discipline is not mastery. It is noise with a crest attached."

The class relaxed again.

Someone chuckled.

Leon: "There you have it."

Student: "Imagine transferring in and asking about myths."

Aira glanced between me and the front of the room.

Aira: "Bold first-day question."

Haru: "I was curious."

Her eyes lingered on me for a second longer than necessary.

Aira: "Somehow, I don't think curiosity carried all of that."

I did not answer.

At the front, Dren dismissed the projection and continued.

Instructor Dren: "Remember this. Circle rank reflects potential, not guaranteed victory. A stronger Circle can still lose to a weaker opponent with superior control, training, timing, or tactical judgment. Raw mana matters. It is not everything."

That, at least, was true.

Beside me, Aira resumed her notes.

I left my notebook mostly blank and listened.

The rest of the lecture covered layered output, mana stability under emotional strain, and the structural limits of reinforcement casting across different circle ranks. It was competent instruction, though bounded by assumptions so deeply embedded in modern magical education that no one in the room seemed to notice them.

I noticed all of them.

Not because I was smarter than everyone there.

Just because I lived outside the system they thought explained the whole world.

When the bell rang, conversation returned immediately.

Aira turned to me first.

Aira: "If you need help finding the practical arena, I can show you."

Before I could answer, a shadow fell across our desks.

Leon Varek stood there with a smile polished enough to be mistaken for friendliness by people who wanted the world simple.

Leon: "Aira, we're still meeting at the west courtyard before practical assessment."

Aira: "I remember."

His eyes moved to me.

Leon: "And you must be in our practical block."

Haru: "I am."

He nodded as if processing unfortunate news.

Leon: "Well, try not to do anything reckless. Some exercises aren't designed for low-capacity circles. I'd hate for your first day to become a cautionary tale."

Haru: "I'll be careful."

He looked almost disappointed that I had not taken the bait.

Aira: "Leon."

Leon: "What? New students should know the risks."

Then he straightened and left.

Aira: "He does that when he wants an audience."

Haru: "He seems efficient about it."

That made her laugh.

A small laugh. Real.

For some reason, that stayed with me longer than Leon's smug warning.

The bell for transition echoed through the corridor a few minutes later, and the class began moving toward the eastern training complex in clusters. As expected, most students naturally grouped by familiarity and rank. Red Circles walked with the casual confidence of those accustomed to being admired. Blue Circles filled the spaces around them, loud enough to be heard, eager enough to be noticed. Green Circles moved more carefully, often in pairs or alone, keeping to the edges of the hallway.

A second-year in a green-trimmed uniform stepped aside to let a Red Circle group pass and nearly collided with an automated cleaning glyph. He apologized to the glyph before he apologized to himself.

I walked beside Aira.

Not close enough for it to look intentional. Close enough that people would still notice.

That alone seemed to annoy Leon, who kept glancing back from the group ahead.

The training complex was larger than the classroom blocks, built in white stone reinforced with metallic mana-thread and engraved from foundation to ceiling with stabilization runes. The main assessment hall opened into a circular arena surrounded by tiered observation platforms. Transparent barriers shimmered faintly around the combat floor, powered by crystal pylons set at equal intervals along the perimeter.

It was the kind of place built for talent to be displayed.

And for weakness to be exposed.

Students filtered into the stands while Instructor Dren stepped into the center of the arena, his boots clicking once against the polished stone. With a flick of his wrist, a floating panel appeared above him displaying the day's objective.

**PRACTICAL ASSESSMENT: MANA RESPONSE AND COMBAT ADAPTABILITY**

Dren folded his arms.

Instructor Dren: "Listen carefully. This is not a ranking match and not an excuse to indulge personal grudges. Today's objective is to evaluate control under pressure, reaction speed, adaptive judgment, and efficient use of output."

He gestured, and several lines of text rearranged themselves beneath the title.

Instructor Dren: "You will engage in supervised one-on-one assessments. Victory is not determined by brute force alone. Points are awarded for tactical decisions, precision, composure, restraint, and proper use of the arena rules."

Red-Haired Student: "So overwhelming your opponent immediately is discouraged."

Instructor Dren: "It is, when your idea of overwhelming them includes reconstructive healing."

A few students laughed.

Instructor Dren: "Direct lethal intent will result in immediate disqualification and disciplinary review. Excessive force lowers your score. Loss of emotional control lowers your score. If you have enough skill to win elegantly and choose chaos instead, I will assume your education has failed somewhere."

The last line did more work than the official rules.

I felt a faint shift in the air beside me.

Aira: "He says things like that a lot."

Haru: "Does it help?"

Aira: "Not as much as he hopes."

I smiled faintly.

Almost.

Dren raised one hand, and a new panel of names appeared in the air.

Pairs began forming.

One by one, students reacted with varying degrees of satisfaction, annoyance, or concealed panic. Friends avoided each other's eyes. Rivals sat up straighter. A few Greens visibly tensed when assigned to stronger Blues.

Then my name appeared.

**HARU AERLEN vs LEON VAREK**

The room shifted immediately.

A ripple of interest spread through the arena. Some students laughed without bothering to hide it. Others exchanged knowing looks, already deciding how quickly the match would end.

Leon himself placed a hand over his chest in mock disbelief.

Leon: "Sir, I'm not sure this is fair."

Instructor Dren: "For whom?"

That drew louder laughter.

Leon: "I only mean a Blue Circle against a first-day Green transfer may create a certain impression."

Instructor Dren: "It will. Sit in the arena, not in your ego."

Aira turned to me at once.

Her expression had changed. Not pity. Concern.

Aira: "You don't have to prove anything."

Haru: "Yes."

Aira: "Leon likes to humiliate people when he thinks he has an audience."

Haru: "I noticed."

Aira: "He's strong, but he performs. If you stay calm and make him choose his angles, you might last longer than he expects."

Manage longer.

A fair thing for her to say, given what she believed I was.

Haru: "Thank you."

Her surprise was small, but I caught it: a quick lift of the brows, then a careful nod, as if kindness had not been the answer she expected from me either.

Leon dropped from the platform into the arena with easy confidence. Blue mana curled around his hands like ribbons of water catching sunlight. He played to the audience naturally-not in big theatrical gestures, but in subtle ways. The relaxed smile. The way he rolled his shoulders. The measured confidence that suggested this would be quick.

I stepped down into the arena opposite him.

The barrier sealed above us with a low hum.

Immediately, I became aware of every eye in the room.

The Greens watched carefully.
The Blues watched eagerly.
The Reds watched with detached curiosity.
Aira watched with narrowed focus.

Instructor Dren stood just outside the barrier and raised one hand.

Instructor Dren: "Assessment begins on my signal. First clean disruption of three balance markers, forced surrender, or incapacitation ends the match. Remember the rules."

Leon smiled at me.

Leon: "You can yield before we start. No one would blame you."

Haru: "I'm all right."

His smile sharpened.

Dren dropped his hand.

Instructor Dren: "Begin."

Leon moved first.

A stream of compressed water mana shot toward me in a clean horizontal line, fast enough to force a reaction but controlled enough to avoid serious injury. It was a testing strike-something to establish distance and make me move badly.

I stepped aside.

The stream cut past me and crashed harmlessly against the far barrier, exploding into mist.

Leon did not pause. A second stream followed immediately, then a third from a different angle, curving inward to limit my options. Good sequencing. Better than average for someone his age.

I retreated two steps and pivoted.

The second strike grazed my sleeve.
The third missed by inches.

Murmurs spread through the observation platforms.

They had expected panic.

Leon's eyes narrowed slightly.

He flicked both wrists, and water mana burst upward from the arena floor around me in six thin pillars, surrounding my position in a ring. The pillars bent inward as they rose, attempting to trap me inside a temporary cage.

A simple containment tactic.

Effective against someone who relied on brute escape.

I let my shoulders tense just enough to look pressured, then slipped through the narrowest opening half a second before the formation sealed.

A few students in the stands made surprised noises.

Leon clicked his tongue.

Leon: "Not bad. For a Green."

That earned a few chuckles.

He gathered more mana.

This time five crystalline lances formed above his shoulders, rotating slowly with controlled pressure. They were denser than his earlier attacks and sharp enough that a direct hit would hurt.

He really did want to embarrass me publicly.

Leon: "Last chance. Yield gracefully."

I looked at him.

Past the arrogance, there was a boy checking whether the room still belonged to him.

That was enough to make him careless.

Haru: "I'd rather continue."

The lances fired.

I raised one hand.

A thin mana shield appeared in front of me-faint, uneven, and deliberately fragile-looking, the kind of defense a Green Circle student might cast in desperation. The first lance shattered against it. The second split past my shoulder. I let the third scrape my upper arm hard enough to tear fabric but not flesh. Then I dropped low, allowing the final two to strike the barrier behind me.

Gasps rose from the stands.

Not because the exchange was spectacular.

Because it wasn't supposed to happen at all.

A Green Circle should not have defended that well against a practiced Blue.

Leon heard the shift in the audience too.

And he hated it.

His smile vanished.

Leon: "Fine."

The temperature in the arena dropped as he pulled more ambient moisture into his mana field. Water gathered around him in spiraling bands, thicker now, denser, more forceful. He was abandoning clean technique for pressure.

Instructor Dren's voice came from outside the barrier.

Instructor Dren: "Varek. Control your output."

Leon heard him. His jaw tightened.

Then he chose pressure anyway.

Three whip-like streams cracked toward me at once from different angles-left, right, above. I retreated across the arena, keeping my footwork efficient but not impossibly so. One stream struck the stone where I had been standing and carved a shallow groove before the arena runes repaired it. Another snapped at my ankles. I jumped. The third clipped my shoulder.

A ripple of pain spread through my arm.

Real enough to be convincing.

I landed near one of the balance markers embedded in the floor, circular glyphs that registered displacement and control during the assessment. Leon noticed immediately and changed strategy. Instead of targeting me directly, he aimed to force me off the marker and claim the first disruption point.

Smart.

He drove a concentrated pulse of water mana into the ground beneath my feet.

I pushed off at the last instant.

The marker flashed as the stone cracked under impact.

**POINT: VAREK**

The panel above the arena lit up.

Then Leon's next spell began collapsing before anyone else understood it.

Not failing.

Collapsing inward.

He had pulled too much ambient moisture through too narrow a rotation, and the pressure knot above his wrist stopped being an attack. It became a closed loop with five exits, all of them aimed back through his own casting channels.

Instructor Dren moved.

Too late.

I felt the Black Circle rise behind my ribs.

If I let it answer fully, everyone in the arena would see rings no academy record could explain. If I did nothing, Leon's Blue Circle would fold through his wrist and teach him pain before humility ever got the chance.

So I chose the smaller wound.

I stepped into the collapsing edge.

The impact tore my sleeve open and drove real pain through my arm. While the arena watched the hit, I made three Tier 1 corrections under the cover of contact: bent the water's rotation, loosened the knot around his casting wrist, and redirected the excess pressure into the cracked marker beneath my feet.

The Green crest on my hand flickered half a heartbeat late.

Elira, from the upper platform, went still.

Dren's eyes narrowed.

Leon stared at his own hand.

He knew, without knowing why, that something had touched the inside of his spell and spared him from the consequence of his own pride.

My fingers had gone numb.

I lowered my arm before anyone saw the black line briefly forming beneath my skin.

Cheers came from a few Blue Circle students. One Red near the front clapped twice, bored but approving. The Greens around the lower benches watched the marker instead of Leon, as if the glowing point had confirmed something they had already been taught to expect.

Leon: "There it is."

He thought the match had corrected itself.

I let him believe that.

We circled.

He attacked again, sharper now, less interested in clean form than in momentum. Sheets of water mana folded into blades. Mist spread low across the arena to obscure footing. He was trying to rush me into a mistake before the strange discomfort of the first exchange could take root in the audience.

I parried one strike with a compact reinforcement pulse.
Sidestepped another.
Let a third force me backward.

I could have ended the match in a hundred ways.

A pressure-point disruption to his mana channels.
A muffled collapse of his spell geometry.
A micro-fluctuation in the arena barrier to break his rhythm.
A single tap of force enough to disarm him without anyone understanding what had happened.

Instead, I chose difficulty.

Because looking weak was far harder than being strong.

It was not the pain.

Pain was simple. Pain entered, named itself, and left if the body was lucky.

The harder part was arranging my face around it.

Letting my shoulders draw in by the exact amount a cornered Green Circle student might allow. Letting the audience's judgment land and stay there. Letting the small laughs travel across the arena without answering them. I had spent years learning that shape: lowered eyes, slower hands, breath measured thin enough that no one mistook restraint for threat.

It had never become natural.

It had only become practiced.

Under the practice, something older lifted its head.

Not the Black Circle.

Me.

The part of me that wanted, just once, to stop being careful for people who mistook carefulness for proof that I belonged beneath them. The part that wanted Leon to understand before he was ready, wanted the stands to go silent for the right reason, wanted every student who laughed to feel the room tilt and know they had chosen the wrong target.

I swallowed that want so completely my throat ached.

Then I made my hands look late.

Leon came in fast, condensing water around his forearm into a hardened striking edge. At close range, elemental shaping like that could be dangerous.

He swung.

I blocked late on purpose, absorbing enough impact to slide backward across the floor.

The audience reacted exactly as expected.

Student: "There!"

Student: "Now he's done."

But Leon had overcommitted.

His weight shifted too far forward.
His centerline opened for a fraction of a second.
His next spell sequence was already forming before he had recovered balance.

Small mistakes.
Common mistakes.
The kind good training was supposed to prevent.

I moved.

Not fast enough to look unnatural.
Just faster than he expected.

I stepped inside his range, redirected his wrist with the minimum possible force, and tapped two fingers against the mana node near his elbow.

A tiny pulse.

Nothing dramatic.

Just enough to interrupt the spell flow in his dominant arm.

His water construct burst apart instantly.

Leon's eyes widened.

I pivoted past him and touched the nearest balance marker with my palm while he was still recovering.

The glyph flashed.

**POINT: AERLEN**

The arena took one collective breath and forgot what to do with it.

A Green Circle had just scored against a Blue.

Not through luck.
Not through a wild outburst.
Through timing.

Leon spun toward me, stunned.

The stands erupted in uneven pieces.

Student: "What?"
Student: "How did he-"
Student: "Did Leon just lose a point?"
Student: "No, he disrupted the channel-"
Student: "Can Greens even do that?"

One of the Greens near the lower bench smiled before he remembered to hide it.

A Blue beside him looked offended on Leon's behalf.

Aira was on her feet before she seemed to realize it.

Her eyes were wide, fixed on me with a look that was no longer simple concern.

It was interest.

Serious interest.

Leon's face flushed.

Not with effort.

With humiliation.

Leon: "Fluke."

I said nothing.

That made it worse.

He attacked with far more force than the assessment required.

Water surged around him in a rising spiral, then crashed forward in a broad wave that raced across the arena floor, forcing me backward. Mist exploded upward. Visibility dropped. Several students in the stands leaned away instinctively as the barrier shimmered under the pressure.

Instructor Dren: "Varek! Reduce output!"

Leon pushed harder.

He was no longer fighting to score.

He was fighting to erase embarrassment.

Through the mist, I tracked him easily by mana flow. His breathing had changed. His spell rhythm was less refined now, more emotional, less precise. He condensed six water blades around himself and launched them in staggered formation-two high, two center, two low.

A pattern designed to overwhelm.

I let my breathing slow.

For the first time since entering the arena, I stopped pretending to be uncertain.

I moved through the attack.

One step left.
Pivot.
Drop.
Turn.

My hand brushed a blade and redirected it just enough to alter its course.
Another passed over my shoulder.
A third struck the floor and dissolved.
The fourth I shattered with a compressed pulse hidden inside an apparently desperate block.
The fifth and sixth missed entirely as I slipped past the centerline of his formation.

The noise in the stands thinned until I could hear water hiss against the barrier.

I registered it only distantly.

Leon saw me emerge from the mist untouched.

And for the first time, fear flickered in his eyes.

Not fear of injury.

Fear of not understanding what he was seeing.

He gathered mana too quickly after that.

Too much, too fast, and with too little discipline.

The spiral around him thickened and destabilized.

I felt the shift instantly.

So did Dren.

Instructor Dren: "Stop casting!"

Leon tried to force the spell into shape anyway.

A compressed sphere of turbulent water mana formed above his right hand, vibrating violently. The structure was incomplete. Unstable. If it burst at that range, he would be the one hurt most.

The arena barrier tensed.

Students in the stands recoiled.

My perception narrowed.

I had choices.

Expose too much and save him perfectly.
Hold back and let him get injured.
Intervene just enough and risk questions.

Mercy was not soft in moments like that.

It was a calculation made with no clean answer.

I stepped forward.

Leon's eyes snapped to mine.

His anger cracked, and fear looked through.

Then I raised my hand and released the smallest fraction of power I could manage without failing.

Not Black Circle power in any true sense.
Not even close.

Just an impossibly precise pulse folded so deeply inside ordinary reinforcement mana that anyone without absurd perception would miss what it really was.

The unstable sphere above Leon's hand folded inward with a wet, airless thump.

No explosion.
No backlash.
No damage.

The water fell as harmless rain.

The arena needed a second to understand it had not just watched someone get hurt.

Leon looked at his hand as if his own magic had become unfamiliar.

Instructor Dren's expression changed.

Only slightly.

But I noticed.

He had seen enough to know that something about that was wrong.

Voices rushed in all at once.

Student: "What just happened?"
Student: "Did Varek cancel his own spell?"
Student: "No, the transfer did something-"
Student: "A first-day Green transfer?"
Student: "That control makes no sense-"

Leon stumbled back a step, looking at his own hand as if it had betrayed him.

Dren entered the arena immediately.

Instructor Dren: "Match over."

The panel above us flashed.

**ASSESSMENT ENDED**

A wave of protest and confusion swept through the stands.

Student: "What? Why?"
Student: "Sir, that's it?"
Student: "He was about to lose!"
Student: "No, Leon would've won-"
Student: "Would he?"

Dren ignored them.

He looked first at Leon.

Instructor Dren: "You lost emotional control, exceeded safe output parameters, and ignored a direct instruction."

Leon opened his mouth. Closed it.

Dren turned to me.

Instructor Dren: "And you demonstrated unusual tactical timing."

Haru: "Thank you, sir."

His eyes stayed on me longer than the comment required.

Then he faced the class.

Instructor Dren: "Observe carefully. Today's lesson is simple. Rank matters. It is not absolute. Any student who uses natural advantage as a substitute for discipline will eventually be embarrassed."

Several Greens looked suddenly a little less small.

Several Blues looked irritated.

Aira was still watching me.

The match should have ended there.

For the class, perhaps it did.

For me, it was the beginning of a problem.

Because attention had weight.
And I could already feel it settling over me.

As the barrier dropped and students began talking over one another, Leon brushed past me hard enough to make the gesture deliberate.

Leon: "You planned that."

Haru: "No. You rushed."

He stopped.

Turned.

For one heartbeat, anger and uncertainty fought visibly in his expression.

Then he walked away.

Aira reached me a moment later.

Aira: "That wasn't normal."

Haru: "For a Green Circle?"

Aira: "For anyone on their first day."

There was no accusation in her voice.

Only amazement.

And something more dangerous than suspicion.

Interest mixed with trust.

I did not know what to do with either.

Haru: "It was timing."

She held my gaze, searching for the edge of the answer I would not give.

Aira: "Maybe. But I think there's more to you than timing, Haru."

Before I could answer, a new presence entered the arena from the upper observation platform.

The arena changed without anyone announcing why.

Conversations broke off mid-word. Students straightened. Even Leon looked up.

At the top of the stairs stood a girl in the formal elite mantle reserved for special-ranking students. Her posture was elegant, her expression composed, and the mana around her felt bright, heavy, and refined enough to press lightly against the skin from a distance.

A **Red Circle** crest glowed at her collarbone.

Not just any Red.

A high-grade one.

She looked down at the arena.
At Dren.
At Leon.
And then at me.

Her gaze lingered.

Not with contempt.

With calculation.

Instructor Dren: "Student Council President."

So this was her.

The one reputation alone had reached even transfer paperwork and hallway whispers.

Aurelis student council did not run dances and decorate notice boards. It controlled student disciplinary referrals, emergency witnesses, club and duel permits, and enough access privileges to make most heirs behave in public.

She descended the steps slowly, each movement controlled.

Rhea: "I apologize for interrupting. I heard there was unusual activity in the assessment hall."

Her voice was calm, polished, and impossible to read.

Instructor Dren: "The matter is handled."

Rhea: "Handled, then. Still, it seems this year's transfer student has already become interesting."

A few students exchanged glances.

I said nothing.

She studied me for one second longer, then smiled very slightly.

It was not a warm smile.

It was the smile of someone who had noticed a thread and intended to keep pulling until the whole pattern revealed itself.

Rhea: "I'll look forward to seeing more."

Then she turned and left with the same controlled pace, leaving half the room pretending not to watch her go.

Aira: "That's not good."

Haru: "Who was that?"

She looked at me as if I had asked the name of a storm everyone else had learned to fear.

Aira: "Rhea Valmont. Student Council President. Red Circle. If she notices you, paperwork starts growing teeth."

I looked back toward the stairs where Rhea had vanished.

My first day at Aurelis was not even over.

Already, hiding had become a group activity.

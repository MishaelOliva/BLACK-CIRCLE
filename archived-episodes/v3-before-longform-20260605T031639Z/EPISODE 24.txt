# Episode 24: **The Funeral That Refused Dominion**

By morning, the academy had learned how to walk around grief.

Not gracefully.

Not well.

But carefully.

The corridors above the Wounded City were full of students moving in quiet lines with flowers, copied statements, folded ribbons, and pages from public witness ledgers. No one had told them to bring anything.

That was probably why they did.

Orders made ceremony.

Choice made vigil.

Aurelis had become very sensitive to the difference.

Elira slept for three hours before dawn.

Professor Valen called that a medical victory in the tone of someone willing to fight the definition of victory in a corridor afterward. When Elira woke, she looked confused for one second.

Then she remembered.

I saw it happen.

The breath.

The hand to her pendant.

The eyes finding Cael's covered body.

The return of grief as a physical thing.

The Black Circle stirred beneath my skin.

Only beneath.

I pressed my palm flat against the stone and breathed until the old cold behind my sternum stopped asking to become architecture.

Elira saw that too.

Elira: "You are not allowed to use self-control as a substitute for being held."

My hand froze against the floor.

Aira, passing behind us with a tray of tea, did not even slow down.

Aira: "Medical ruling confirmed by non-medical authority."

Professor Valen accepted a cup from the tray.

Professor Valen: "For once, I endorse the non-medical authority."

I looked at Elira.

Haru: "I am sitting next to you."

Elira: "Yes."

Her voice softened.

Elira: "And part of you is standing guard over every possible future where I die again."

That was too accurate.

I did not answer quickly enough.

Elira's hand moved over mine.

Still cool.

Still weaker than it should have been.

Elira: "I am frightened too, Haru."

The words were simple.

They entered the room more quietly than any magic.

Elira: "Sometimes I blink and there is no before. No after. Just a place where I stopped. Then I am here again, and everyone looks relieved, and Cael is not breathing."

Her fingers tightened.

Elira: "Do not leave me alone with everyone else's relief."

I turned my hand under hers and held on.

Haru: "I am here."

Elira: "All of you?"

The Black Circle pulsed once under my shirt.

Not defensive.

Ashamed, maybe.

If power could feel shame.

Haru: "I am trying."

Elira leaned her forehead against my shoulder.

Elira: "Then keep trying where I can reach you."

So I did.

I sat in the old chamber while the academy prepared to bury the boy who had chosen her life.

I let Aira put tea in my hand.

I let Leon take the cup from me when my fingers shook too badly and pretend he was only moving it away from the edge of the stone.

I let Cassian stand close enough that no official could approach from my blind side without passing through him first.

I let Nera and Oric ask me how much Noctis grammar they should preserve for the funeral record, and I answered like a person instead of a weapon.

Only enough.

It should not be erased.

It should not be allowed to speak last.

Mira arrived with Finn and a stack of copied witness pages tied in green ribbon.

Her eyes went to the space behind me where the Black Circle had been visible the night before.

Then to my face.

The second look mattered.

Mira: "We made a public sequence packet."

Aira reached for it immediately.

Aira: "Please tell me that title is temporary."

Finn: "It is called Sequence Packet One."

Aira closed her eyes.

Aira: "Green Circle activism has the soul of a filing cabinet."

Mira hugged the packet to her chest.

Mira: "A filing cabinet that stopped Lucien from narrating a corpse."

Aira opened her eyes.

Aira: "I withdraw my complaint and admire the cabinet."

Mira smiled for half a second.

Then she looked at Cael.

Mira: "We added a page where students can write what they witnessed, not what they were told to think."

Rhea, who had entered silently during the exchange, took the top copy and scanned it.

President Valmont: "How many?"

Finn: "So far? Four hundred and twelve."

Rhea's eyebrows rose.

Mira: "A lot of people were awake."

President Valmont: "A lot of people were watching."

Mira: "Yes."

Her grip tightened.

Mira: "That has to mean something good too."

No one promised her that it would.

Promises were dangerous around records.

Rhea only nodded.

President Valmont: "Then we give it a place to mean something."

---

The funeral was held in the Grand Courtyard.

Not the chapel.

Not the council hall.

Not beneath the tower where old systems could translate grief into precedent before ordinary people had finished crying.

The courtyard.

Public.

Witnessed.

Recorded.

Because Cael had spent too much of his life being placed where others could use him and too little being seen where he chose to stand.

By sunset, the entire academy had gathered.

Aurelis students filled the steps, balconies, terrace walks, and colonnades. Green Circle students stood together near the eastern fountain with Mira's copied packets in their arms. Blue Circle support students had formed quiet guide lines for healers and guests. Red Circle council members held the boundary, not as a threat, but as a promise that no one would push mourners aside for a better view.

A cluster of Blue Circle students near the north arcade kept glancing at the space behind me where the Black Circle had been. One of them had folded a condolence ribbon so tightly it had creased into a hard little square. Two Green first-years stood with Mira's group but did not sign the public packet. They listened. They stayed. Their fear was not betrayal, exactly.

It was the honest kind of distance people took when hope had become larger than they knew how to hold.

Mira noticed them and did not press a pen into their hands.

That mattered.

Instructor Dren stood at the western gate with the combat faculty, turning away anyone who came looking for spectacle instead of witness. He did not raise his voice. He only looked at each person until they remembered why they were there.

Lady Seraphine Morn held the tactical record beside Rhea's council aides. Every time a delegation tried to file a cleaner phrase over Cael's death, she wrote the uglier accurate one beneath it in red ink.

Lady Seraphine: "Ceremony is not permission to simplify."

Dren heard her from across the courtyard.

Instructor Dren: "For once, Morn, we agree before the damage is done."

Seraphine did not smile.

Lady Seraphine: "Record that as a rare event."

Valemere arrived in silver-blue.

Soren walked at the front with Talen beside him. Talen's braced hand was visible, uncovered, and when students turned to look, he did not hide it.

Drakenshard sent Mara Kest and three witnesses in formal ash-gray uniforms. They carried no weapons into the courtyard, which somehow made them look more dangerous and more respectful at once.

Noctis sent two groups.

That was the first sign of the fracture.

The official delegation came in black and white, faces controlled, banner folded between them.

The students came separately.

Not all of them--not enough to call it rebellion, but enough to make every Noctis official notice.

They stood at the back without banners. Some had removed their binding rings. Some had not. One younger student held his wrist the way Cael used to when pressure hooks pulled too sharply beneath the skin.

Nera whispered his name before she seemed to realize she had spoken.

Nera: "Iven Sahl."

Oric's pen paused.

Nera swallowed.

Nera: "First-year conduit track. Cael lent him a thermal charm during winter drills and told him not to give it back until his hands stopped shaking."

Iven did not look toward us.

He kept his eyes on Cael's bier and his hand around his own wrist, as if memory could be held in place by pressure.

Nera saw him.

Her face changed.

Not openly.

But enough.

Oric saw him too.

He wrote the student's position into the witness ledger.

Not to expose him.

To remember that he had stood there.

At the center of the courtyard, the repaired fountain flowed with silver water.

Cael's body rested before it on a low stone bier covered in a clean Aurelis cloak, the nightglass flowers at his side, Rhea's red protection seal above him, and a ring of copied witness pages beneath the stone like roots.

The fountain had been repaired after the first Black Circle awakening.

Now it reflected a second wound.

The sky above Aurelis was orange fading into violet.

Beautiful, in the unfair way the world sometimes remained beautiful while people were gone from it.

Elira stood beside me.

Professor Valen had argued for a chair.

Elira had accepted one near the front and then refused to sit until she had walked to Cael herself.

Compromise, Aira called it, with the expression of someone filing a complaint against the universe's entire medical department.

Elira's hand held mine.

The courtyard noticed.

The world, through carefully reopened witness relays, noticed.

I felt them noticing the place where the Black Circle was not visible.

That absence had become its own spectacle.

Aira leaned close from my other side.

Aira: "Do not think about all of them at once."

Haru: "I was not."

Aira: "That was not a guess."

I let out a breath that almost had shape.

Aira's shoulder touched mine briefly.

Aira: "Think about Cael. One person. The rest can wait their turn."

So I looked at Cael.

One person.

That hurt more.

It also told the truth better.

---

Rhea opened the funeral.

She stood at the fountain's edge in full council uniform, the red of her Circle bright but carefully lowered.

Not a display.

A lamp.

President Valmont: "We gather for Cael Nox."

No titles followed.

The courtyard settled around the absence of them.

President Valmont: "Student of Noctis Dominion College by enrollment. Protected Witness of Aurelis by choice. Public witness by final act. Deceased by voluntary activation of a coercive doctrine under catastrophic pressure."

The Noctis officials shifted.

Rhea did not look at them.

President Valmont: "This ceremony does not make his death acceptable. It does not transform loss into strategy. It does not make a weapon beautiful because it was turned toward mercy at the end."

Her voice hardened.

President Valmont: "Cael Nox should be alive."

The fountain water rippled.

President Valmont: "Because he is not, we will do what institutions too often avoid. We will name what happened. We will preserve who he was. We will refuse to let the systems that harmed him inherit his meaning."

Mira's pencil moved.

Four hundred and twelve packets probably moved with it.

Rhea stepped back.

Elira stepped forward.

The courtyard changed again.

Not because she was Yellow Circle.

Because everyone had seen her die.

Because everyone had seen her return.

Because grief is different when it speaks in the voice of the person someone died to save.

I almost went with her.

Elira's fingers slipped from mine.

She did not push me away.

She only stood on her own.

That mattered too.

Elira looked at Cael first.

Then at the gathered students.

Elira: "Cael Nox was taught that his life was a resource."

Her voice was not loud.

The courtyard carried it anyway.

Elira: "A support unit. A conduit. A reserved answer to someone else's emergency."

Nera bowed her head.

Elira: "He was taught that being useful was safer than being known."

She swallowed once.

The pendant at her throat caught the sunset and flashed black.

Elira: "And in the end, Cael did spend his life."

The words hurt her physically.

I saw her hand curl against her skirt.

She continued.

Elira: "But not because Noctis ordered him to. Not because a clause owned him. Not because a spell needed a body and his was available."

Her eyes lifted.

Gold returned around her wrist in a thin, trembling line.

Elira: "He chose."

The fountain water brightened.

Elira: "That difference between coercion and choice is not small. It is everything."

No one moved.

Elira: "Cael chose mercy. He chose me. He chose Haru. He chose all of us. He chose to make his final act belong to him when too many people had spent too long arranging his life as if it belonged to them."

Her voice broke.

She let it.

That was braver than smoothing it.

Elira: "I am alive because he is not."

My vision blurred.

Elira: "I hate that sentence. I will hate it for the rest of my life."

She looked at Cael again.

Elira: "And I will spend that life making sure his choice is not used to teach another student that dying is the only way to matter."

The courtyard answered with silence.

Not empty.

Full.

Elira bowed to Cael.

When she stepped back, her knees almost failed.

Professor Valen moved.

So did I.

Elira caught my sleeve before either of us could make her a scene.

Elira: "Chair."

Aira appeared with it as if she had been waiting to defeat pride with furniture.

Aira: "Miracle of practical planning."

Elira sat.

Her hand found mine again.

This time she held on in public without looking apologetic to anyone.

---

Nera spoke next.

She did not wear Noctis black.

She wore a plain Aurelis witness cloak over her uniform, leaving the binding marks on her wrists uncovered.

That choice moved through the Noctis students at the back like a quiet alarm.

Nera stood before the fountain for several seconds before speaking.

Nera: "I was one of Cael's instructors."

The official Noctis delegation went very still.

Nera: "Not formally enough to be named responsible when responsibility became dangerous. Formally enough to correct him. To train him. To teach him which pressure meant endure and which pressure meant obey."

Her voice stayed controlled.

That made the grief sharper.

Nera: "I told myself I was helping him survive Noctis."

She looked at the students in black.

Nera: "Sometimes that is true. Sometimes survival lessons become the walls of the prison."

Oric stood two paces behind her, ledger held in both hands.

Nera: "Cael, if witness records reach anywhere kind, hear this clearly: I am sorry."

The binding marks on her wrists glowed faintly blue, then went dark.

Nera: "I am sorry for every time I translated ownership into discipline because it was easier to survive that way. I am sorry for every warning I gave you too quietly. I am sorry that you had to become braver than the adults around you."

The Noctis students at the back did not move.

Several were crying.

Nera: "I cannot undo what was taught. I can testify against it. I can refuse to teach it again."

Oric stepped beside her.

Oric: "I can preserve the grammar without preserving the doctrine."

Nera's mouth trembled.

Nera: "Yes."

She bowed.

Not Noctis style.

Not Aurelis.

Something she chose in the space between.

Darian Thorne's relay opened after her.

It should not have.

The Noctis official delegation tried to cut it.

Rhea's seal blocked them before their hands finished moving.

Darian appeared in a narrow window of light, seated in a bare room with a restraint sigil visible on the wall behind him.

House arrest, then.

Not rumor.

Fact.

His face was tired.

Darian Thorne: "Cael Nox was my student."

The courtyard went colder.

Darian Thorne: "I watched Noctis break him into functions small enough to praise. I called it advancement. I called it necessary. I called it what everyone around me called it, because cowardice is easier when it comes with shared vocabulary."

Noctis officials shifted again.

Darian did not look at them.

Darian Thorne: "I watched Lucien Voss use him in the tournament. I did not stop it in time."

His voice roughened.

Darian Thorne: "When I finally acted, it was not enough to save him."

Nera closed her eyes.

Darian Thorne: "I failed Cael Nox. That fact will remain in my record if Noctis permits honesty and in yours if it does not."

Mira wrote faster.

Darian Thorne: "But failure is not permission to return to silence. To every Noctis student watching through a borrowed crystal, hidden mirror, or forbidden reflection: usefulness is not the same as worth. Obedience is not the same as safety. If you need witness, find one. If you cannot find one, leave evidence. If you cannot leave evidence, remember your own name until someone else can help you say it aloud."

The restraint sigil behind him flashed.

His relay began to collapse.

Darian Thorne: "Cael, I am late. I am sorry. I will not be quiet again."

The window went dark.

For a moment, no one breathed correctly.

Then one Noctis student at the back removed a binding ring and placed it on the courtyard stone.

The sound was small.

It carried.

Another followed.

Not all.

Not most.

Enough.

Rhea did not smile.

But her red seal spread to include the small pile of rings as protected evidence.

Later, Aira would call it a historic failure of timing and then underline the phrase so hard the paper tore.

They carried the folded black banner to the fountain's edge.

The lead official was older than the others, with silver at his temples and the careful expression of someone who had mistaken control for dignity for many years.

Noctis Official: "Noctis Dominion College formally acknowledges the death of Cael Nox, enrolled student of our institution, who died in service to-"

Elira stood.

The chair scraped against stone.

The official stopped.

The courtyard held its breath around a girl who had died yesterday and was apparently not finished being inconvenient.

Elira: "No."

The word was not loud.

It cut cleanly.

Noctis Official: "Miss Vale, this is a formal acknowledgment."

Elira: "Then acknowledge him accurately."

The official's expression tightened.

Noctis Official: "Cael Nox was a Noctis student."

Elira: "Yes."

Nera flinched.

Elira did not.

Elira: "Yes."

Noctis Official: "Then Noctis has standing to honor his service."

The Black Circle stirred beneath my shirt.

I did not move.

Elira stepped forward once.

Professor Valen looked like she might object and then chose not to interrupt the living proof of her own medical worries.

Elira: "Cael did not die in service to Noctis."

The official opened his mouth.

Elira spoke over him.

Elira: "He died refusing what Noctis made of service."

President Valmont: "Aurelis recognizes Noctis' right to witness the funeral. Noctis does not have the right to rename the deceased."

Noctis Official: "We request custody of the remains after ceremony."

The red seal over Cael brightened.

President Valmont: "Denied."

No committee.

No delay.

Again.

Noctis Official: "President Valmont-"

President Valmont: "Cael Nox is a Protected Witness of Aurelis by recorded choice. His body will remain under witness protection until the public record, investigative chain, and funeral rites are complete. Noctis may submit a request to the Inter-National Schools Council, where it will be entered beside the wording you just attempted in front of several thousand witnesses."

Aira whispered, almost reverent:

Aira: "Oh, she saved the receipt."

The official heard.

He chose not to react.

That was probably the best decision he made all day.

He lowered the banner.

For a second, I thought he would try to place it over Cael.

The Black Circle pressed once against my ribs.

Then the official seemed to understand the shape of the courtyard around him.

He set the banner at the fountain's edge instead.

Not on the body.

Not over the flowers.

Beside the witness pages.

A concession small enough to deny later.

Large enough for the students in black to see.

He bowed.

No one thanked him.

The delegation stepped back.

The banner stayed where it was, black cloth beside green ribbon and nightglass flowers, no longer able to cover anything.

The flower looked ordinary.

That made it unbearable.

Talen: "I did not know Cael Nox."

His voice carried less easily than Elira's or Rhea's. The relay crystals leaned in to catch it.

Talen: "But I know what it is like to be harmed by a doctrine and then told that naming the harm is more dangerous than the harm itself."

Lucien's name did not appear.

It did not need to.

Talen lifted his braced hand.

Talen: "When I testified, I thought I was speaking about the past. Then Cael's testimony made the pattern visible. His final choice made it impossible for the world to pretend the pattern was isolated."

He looked at Elira.

Then at me.

His gaze did not linger on the hidden Black Circle.

It returned to Cael.

Talen: "Thank you for making my pain part of a record large enough to matter. I wish you had lived to see it become justice."

He bowed as far as his brace allowed.

Then he stepped back.

One Green Circle student moved before anyone invited her.

Student: "I saw him say no."

Student: "I saw Noctis try to call him conduit after he died."

Red Circle.

Student: "I saw Haru Aerlen not attack us when everyone said he was the danger."

Another student.

Green Circle.

Student: "I saw Mira write the sequence when adults froze."

Finn looked proud enough to embarrass her for a week if the world ever became capable of weeks again.

Not everyone spoke.

Some only placed flowers.

Some placed copied pages.

A pencil.

A cup token from the dining hall.

A torn practice sigil.

A strip of Noctis black cloth cut in half.

More true.

The public script beneath her name opened before she spoke.

**COUNCIL REGISTRY: NORTHERN CIVIL SERVICE LINE**

**NO DECLARED BLOOD OR HOUSE AFFILIATION WITH LUCIEN VOSS**

Used to it.

Then to Elira.

Then, unexpectedly, to me.

Not low.

Not worship.

Acknowledgment.

Black Circle.

Then the Green Circle witnesses.

Then Elira.

Haru: "I can choose not to make his death a command for anyone else to sacrifice themselves. I can choose not to turn power into a wall because I am afraid of losing people. I can choose witnesses even when being witnessed is uncomfortable. I can choose to remember that the strongest thing Cael did was not die."

My voice almost failed.

Haru: "It was say no long enough to make one final yes his own."

Elira covered her mouth.

Leon bowed his head.

Haru: "Cael Nox was not proof that mercy needs victims."

I looked at the public record crystals.

Haru: "Record that."

Mira did.

So did four hundred and twelve students.

So did the Foundation.

**PUBLIC RECORD AMENDMENT ACCEPTED**

The Black Circle pulsed once.

Small.

Contained.

Witnessed.

---

After the ceremony, Rhea brought us Cael's personal effects.

There were not many.

That was the first cruelty of the small box.

A spare uniform clasp.

A broken Noctis calibration bead.

A strip of black cloth with a frayed end.

Two unsent notes, both blank except for crossed-out openings.

And a notebook.

Nera made a sound when she saw it.

Oric looked away.

Elira reached for the notebook, then stopped.

Elira: "May I?"

Nera answered because someone from Noctis needed to say the obvious truth correctly.

Nera: "He would want you to."

Elira opened it.

The first page held a list in Cael's careful handwriting.

The letters were small.

As if he had been conserving paper.

Or permission.

Elira read aloud.

Elira: "Things I choose."

Her voice trembled.

Elira: "To be called by my name, not my role."

Nera bent forward like the words had struck her.

Elira: "To say no."

Oric covered his eyes.

Elira: "To have friends, if they will have me."

Aira made a broken sound.

Leon turned away.

Cassian put a hand on his shoulder and left it there.

Elira: "To matter when I am not useful."

She stopped.

No one asked her to continue.

She did anyway.

The last page had been written recently.

The ink was darker.

Pressed harder.

Elira: "If this ever becomes evidence, tell them I was afraid."

That undid something in the circle around us.

Not the Black Circle.

Us.

Elira's voice broke.

Elira: "Tell them I was afraid, and I chose anyway. Do not let them make courage sound clean."

She closed the notebook against her chest.

For a moment, no one moved.

Then Aira took one step forward.

Aira: "We should copy it."

Her voice was wrecked.

Practical anyway.

Aira: "Not all of it. Not the private pages unless... unless we decide. But those lines. The first and last. They need to outlive everybody's speeches."

Rhea nodded.

President Valmont: "With witness consent."

Elira looked at Nera and Oric.

Nera wiped her face with the heel of her hand.

Nera: "Yes."

Oric: "Yes."

Leon looked at me.

Leon: "You keep it."

I stared at him.

Haru: "Why?"

His face twisted.

Leon: "Because I held him after. If I keep it, I will never put it down."

The honesty cost him.

He paid it anyway.

Cassian's hand tightened on his shoulder.

Elira looked at me.

Not commanding.

Asking.

I took the notebook.

It weighed almost nothing.

That was also unfair.

Haru: "I will keep it with witnesses."

Aira nodded immediately.

Aira: "Good answer."

Elira's eyes softened.

Elira: "Human answer."

The Black Circle stayed quiet.

For once, that felt like agreement.

---

Long after the crowds dispersed, the core witnesses remained by the fountain.

Haru, Elira, Aira, Leon, Cassian, Nera, Oric, Rhea, Professor Valen, Halden, Mira, Finn, Soren, Talen, and Senna.

Too many for privacy.

Too few for the size of the absence.

Cael's body had been moved under witness guard to the eastern hall for preparation. The flowers remained around the fountain because no one had the heart to decide which ones should go with him and which ones should become public memorial.

So Rhea decided none would be moved until morning.

Sometimes authority was useful because grief could not choose table arrangements.

The Noctis banner remained at the fountain's edge.

Cut in half.

That had been Mara Kest.

She had asked permission first.

Rhea had granted it after a five-second legal review conducted entirely through eyebrow movement.

Now one half of the banner lay beside the official record as evidence of attempted claim.

The other half had been folded beneath the nightglass flowers as proof that even Noctis cloth could be made to hold witness instead of ownership.

Aira sat on the fountain rim and looked at the water.

Aira: "He should be here."

No one corrected the uselessness of the sentence.

That was the point of it.

Leon sat on the ground with his arms over his knees.

Leon: "Yes."

Cassian lowered himself beside him.

Cassian: "He would hate how many people talked about him."

Nera gave a small, wet laugh.

Nera: "He would stand behind someone taller."

Leon looked up.

Leon: "Me."

Aira: "Obviously you. You are structurally useful."

Leon almost smiled.

Then did not.

That was all right.

Nera looked at the fountain.

Nera: "He used to ask questions after Lucien left rooms."

Oric nodded.

Oric: "Never during."

Nera: "No. Never during."

She touched the edge of the stone.

Nera: "The first question he asked me that was not about orders was whether Aurelis students got to choose their electives."

Aira's face crumpled.

Aira: "That is a horrible anecdote."

Nera: "I know."

Aira: "Tell another."

Nera thought.

Her voice softened.

Nera: "He liked cinnamon bread."

Oric looked startled.

Nera: "He pretended not to because Noctis dining monitors marked sugar preference as indulgence if you took it too often."

Elira closed her eyes.

Elira: "Then we add cinnamon bread to the memorial table."

Rhea made a note.

Instantly.

No debate.

No symbolic committee.

Cinnamon bread.

For Cael.

That almost broke us more than the speeches had.

Talen spoke from beside Soren.

Talen: "At Valemere, after injury hearings, they serve bitter tea because they think sweetness makes testimony less dignified."

Mira looked offended.

Mira: "That is ridiculous."

Talen: "Yes."

He looked at the fountain.

Talen: "I would like cinnamon bread too, if that is permitted."

Aira pointed at Rhea.

Aira: "Put him down for bread."

Rhea wrote it.

Senna watched all of this with an expression I could not name.

Maybe because council reports did not usually include bread as evidence that a dead student had been a person.

Maybe they should have.

Halden stood slightly apart, arms folded.

Mira noticed.

Mira: "Professor?"

He looked at her.

Mira: "Did Cael ever ask you anything?"

Halden's expression changed by a degree.

Professor Halden: "Once."

Everyone looked at him.

He clearly regretted answering.

Too late.

Professor Halden: "After the formal review, he asked whether old systems could tell the difference between a conduit and a witness."

Nera's face tightened.

Professor Halden: "I told him systems could be taught. People were harder."

Aira: "That is bleak."

Professor Halden: "It was accurate."

Professor Valen looked at him.

Professor Halden's jaw shifted.

Professor Halden: "He said, 'Then I hope the system learns first.'"

The fountain water rippled.

No one spoke for a while.

Then Mira wrote it down.

Of course she did.

---

Near midnight, after Professor Valen finally threatened Elira with supervised carrying if she refused to sleep, I returned to the fountain alone.

Not entirely alone.

Aurelis had learned better.

Leon was on the western steps pretending not to guard me.

Cassian was on the east path doing the same thing with more style.

Aira had left a note on the fountain rim that read:

**DRINK WATER OR I WILL BECOME A PROCEDURAL PROBLEM.**

I drank the water beside it.

The Black Circle pulsed beneath my shirt.

Small.

Steady.

No longer pretending the world did not know.

I opened Cael's notebook to the first page again.

Things I choose.

The handwriting was careful enough to hurt.

I did not turn to the last page.

Not yet.

The fountain reflected the moon, the flowers, the cut banner, and my face.

For one second, the reflection behind me showed the Black Circle in full.

Deep black.

Silver rings.

Constellation runes moving in opposite directions with impossible order.

Then the reflection changed.

Cael stood beside me in the water.

Not a ghost.

Not really.

Memory, maybe.

Foundation residue.

Grief making images out of moonlight.

He looked as he had before the final stage, tired and too young and still trying to make himself smaller than the room required.

Cael: "You look terrible."

I closed my eyes.

Haru: "That sounds like something Aira would invent to make me sleep."

Cael: "Probably."

When I opened my eyes, the reflection was only water again.

I did not know whether I had imagined it.

I decided not to ask the Foundation.

Some things did not need to become records immediately.

Elira's voice came from behind me.

Elira: "You left the medical room."

I turned.

She stood in a robe over her uniform, hair loose over one shoulder, Professor Valen's medical seal glowing faintly at her wrist.

Behind her, at a respectful distance, Professor Valen looked deeply irritated and entirely unsurprised.

Haru: "You followed."

Elira: "Slowly. With supervision. Everyone wins."

Professor Valen: "That is not my summary."

Elira ignored that with the confidence of a patient who had chosen selective hearing as a survival tactic.

She sat beside me on the fountain rim.

Not too close at first.

Then closer.

Her shoulder touched mine.

Elira: "What happens now?"

I looked at the flowers.

The witness pages.

Professor Valen remained behind us, close enough to intervene and far enough to let the question belong to us first.

Then she said my name.

Not Professor Valen's formal witness voice.

The quieter one she used when history had stopped being an archive and become a person sitting in front of her.

Professor Valen: "There is one record I owe you before the Council starts asking for every other one."

I turned.

She folded her hands over the medical case at her side.

Professor Valen: "When your transfer file reached Aurelis, Student Affairs nearly rejected it at ranking review. A Green Circle applicant from Lumeris District with ancient-script scores above several faculty candidates looked either impossible or inconvenient."

The fountain sounded louder for a moment.

Instructor Selene's voice returned from my first morning.

Someone argued strongly in your favor.

Haru: "It was you."

Professor Valen: "I argued that a student should not be punished because his examination answers made adults uncomfortable."

Halden, from the western steps, gave a dry sound.

Professor Halden: "That is a selective summary."

Professor Valen did not look away from me.

Professor Valen: "I also argued that anyone who could translate pre-standardization mercy clauses without being taught them deserved a room with better questions than the entrance board was asking."

My hands had gone still around Cael's notebook.

Haru: "Did you know?"

Professor Valen: "No."

She answered too quickly for comfort and too honestly to doubt.

Professor Valen: "I suspected old knowledge had touched your life somehow. I did not know the name. I did not know the danger. I certainly did not know I was helping bring the Black Circle into my own school."

Aira, from the fountain rim, wiped one eye with her sleeve.

Aira: "For the record, that is the least villainous secret admission sponsorship I have heard today."

Professor Valen's mouth softened.

Professor Valen: "High praise."

At the place where Cael's reflection had been.

Haru: "Why tell me now?"

Professor Valen: "Because people will try to make your arrival sound like a plot. Some will say Aurelis hid you. Some will say I recruited a weapon. Some will say your place here was evidence of conspiracy."

Professor Valen: "The first true answer is smaller. I saw a student being underestimated by a system that mistook rank for proof, and I objected. If the rest becomes more complicated, let the simple part stay in the record."

Elira's hand found mine.

This time, I let the contact steady me before I had decided whether I deserved it.

Haru: "Thank you."

Professor Valen: "Do not thank me too much. I also failed to ask enough follow-up questions."

Halden: "We all did."

No one argued.

That, too, became part of the night.

The half-banner.

The notebook in my hands.

Haru: "Noctis investigation. Council reviews. People afraid of me. People wanting to use me. People saying Black Circle like they know what it means."

Elira: "Yes."

Haru: "Lucien is not done."

Elira: "No."

Elira was quiet for a moment.

Then she took the notebook from my hands, closed it gently, and placed it between us.

Elira: "Maybe we do not make his death the center."

She looked at the fountain.

Elira: "Maybe we make his choices part of ours."

The Black Circle warmed once beneath my shirt.

Not heat.

Not comfort exactly.

Recognition.

Elira: "He chose to be called by his name."

Haru: "Cael."

Elira: "He chose to say no."

Haru: "Then we make room for no."

Elira: "He chose to have friends."

My throat tightened.

Haru: "Then we stay close."

Elira's hand found mine.

Elira: "He chose to matter when he was not useful."

The fountain water moved around the reflection of the moon.

Haru: "Then we stop asking people to earn care."

Elira leaned against me.

This time with more weight.

Still not enough.

More.

Elira: "There. That is how."

I closed my fingers around hers.

The Black Circle pulsed once beneath my shirt.

Not a threat.

Not a secret.

A reminder.

Power without purpose had almost become destruction.

Power with witnesses, grief, choice, and mercy was not safe.

Nothing worth protecting was ever entirely safe.

But it was answerable.

Cael Nox was gone.

The world had changed.

Noctis was fractured.

The Black Circle was known.

Elira was alive beside me, breathing unevenly but breathing.

And for the first time since the Wounded City broke open, the future did not feel like a verdict already written.

It felt like a page.

Heavy.

Unfair.

Waiting.

Together, we stayed by the fountain until the moon moved past the tower and the flowers turned silver in the dark.

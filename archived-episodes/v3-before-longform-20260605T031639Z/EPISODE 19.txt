# Episode 19: **Wounded City Protocol**

Wounded City Protocol opened beneath the old chapel at noon.

That was the first insult.

Noctis had chosen a rescue activity for the brightest hour of the day, as if mercy needed good lighting before they would believe in it.

The second insult was the invitation list.

Noctis did not request privacy.

It requested observers.

Valemere.

Two minor academy delegations that had stayed after the tournament to watch the Foundation Trial.

Selected Aurelis faculty.

Student Council recorders.

Green Circle public witnesses.

Noctis called it transparency.

Rhea called it leverage.

Aira called it "setting the room on fire and inviting people to admire the smoke."

The Foundation accepted the observer expansion because Wounded City Protocol was, technically, an activity about public rescue ethics. Refusing witnesses would have made Aurelis look like it feared its own doctrine.

Lucien understood optics.

So Rhea made optics answer back.

Every observer had to sign a witness-conduct line before entering the lower chamber. No private relay tools. No unsupervised mirrors. No delegation correction phrases. No removal of notes. No turning away from harm and later claiming uncertainty.

That last condition made several adults uncomfortable.

Mira noticed.

She stood near the entry with a stack of green-edged forms and a face that said she would rather be swallowed by the floor than challenge a visiting examiner.

Then the first examiner tried to wave the form aside.

Mira's fear remained.

Her hand did not move.

Mira: "Public witness terms require a signature."

Examiner: "I am here as faculty, not a student."

Mira looked down at the form, then back up.

Mira: "Then your handwriting is probably better."

The examiner stared.

Rhea, three paces away, said nothing.

That was how authority lent weight without stealing the moment.

The examiner signed.

Mira's shoulders dropped only after he entered.

Aira passed behind her and murmured:

Aira: "Excellent use of stationery."

Mira whispered, "I thought I was going to faint."

Aira: "Most civic institutions are built by people not fainting at the last second."

The Foundation chamber below the chapel unfolded itself from old stone and silver mist. Streets rose from the floor. Doorways grew from blank walls. Windows formed without rooms behind them. A bell tower leaned over a square that had never existed and somehow looked remembered.

At the center stood seven wounded markers.

Not dummies.

Not illusions of people we knew.

That would have been too crude after yesterday.

These markers were outlines made of pale light, each one flickering with a different kind of injury: channel collapse, frost binding, mana starvation, memory echo, pressure burn, relay hook, and one empty outline with no visible wound at all.

The seventh made my teeth ache.

Aira looked at it and immediately hated it.

Aira: "Invisible wound marker. Wonderful. We are being examined by architecture with a sadistic streak."

Rhea stood on the command threshold with a red council seal hovering at her shoulder.

President Valmont: "Conditions?"

The Foundation voice answered from the bell tower.

Foundation: "Wounded City Protocol tests response priority under conflicting witness obligations. Participants must extract all viable markers to safe zones while maintaining witness autonomy and preventing unlawful isolation."

Leon flexed his hands.

Leon: "That almost sounds fair."

Nera's answer was flat.

Nera: "It is not."

Lucien appeared on the far side of the square with Magister Rusk and three Noctis observers. He wore formal black again, but today his collar was open at the throat, a small violation of Noctis stiffness that made him look less like a student and more like a blade pretending to be tired.

Lucien: "It is fair enough to make unfairness useful."

Cassian's Red Circle flared.

Cassian: "I am going to enjoy the day you run out of elegant sentences."

Lucien: "Then I hope you are patient."

Elira stepped onto the activity floor beside me.

The pendant under her collar gave one warm pulse.

Then nothing.

Cael entered with Nera on one side and Leon on the other. His healer seal was reinforced, but the silver thread around his injured hand had darkened overnight.

He had slept less than I had.

That was saying something.

Aira took the support mark at the edge of the square and spread three maps in the air.

Aira: "First rule. Nobody chases symbolic bait alone."

Leon: "Define symbolic bait."

Aira: "Anything that looks like your guilt wearing a costume."

Cassian: "That is annoyingly clear."

Mira and the Green Circle witnesses filled the public record rail above the square. Each pair had a timestamp crystal, ink, and a duplicate page.

Mira caught my eye.

She looked terrified.

Then she lifted her pencil.

Ready.

That mattered.

The activity began.

The first three markers were ugly but honest.

Channel collapse under a stone arch.

Frost binding near a dry fountain.

Mana starvation inside a narrow alley.

Leon handled water pressure with better control than he would have shown three weeks ago. Cassian burned frost seams without scorching the marker. Aira called lane shifts before the streets finished moving. Rhea authorized route changes with red seals. Elira stabilized the worst channel collapse with Yellow Circle precision while letting the healers do the work they were trained to do.

I read the field.

Not too much.

Just enough.

The false Green Circle around my wrist held.

Lucien watched every time I did not answer with the power he wanted to expose.

That became its own kind of battle.

Not the markers.

The restraint between markers.

The field offered me small temptations first.

A cracked stone above Leon that would fall in seven seconds unless someone noticed the weight shift.

I noticed.

I did not move the stone.

Haru: "Leon. Arch weight."

Leon looked up, adjusted the water brace under the collapse, and dragged the marker clear before the stone dropped.

Mira's voice rang from above:

Mira: "Haru gave warning, Leon acted independently."

The field accepted it.

Then a frost seam formed behind Cassian's left heel, thin enough to evade ordinary sight. I could have erased it with a thought so small no one would feel it.

Instead, I said:

Haru: "Cassian. Back foot."

Cassian shifted without looking offended.

That was also growth.

He turned the shift into a pivot and sent a clean ribbon of fire along the seam, sealing it before it could climb his leg.

Cassian: "Appreciated."

Aira: "Recorded. Also emotionally noted, which I resent."

Then the field placed a false safe zone in front of Elira.

That one almost caught me.

The safe zone looked perfect: silver boundary, healer-compatible angle, no visible Dominion script. But the mana inside it had no temperature. No place built to receive wounded life should feel that neutral.

My hand twitched.

Elira saw it before anyone else.

Elira: "Say it."

Not do it.

Say it.

Haru: "False safe zone. No life-temperature."

Professor Valen repeated from the rail:

Professor Valen: "Observation entered. Elira Vale chooses route."

Elira changed direction and carried the marker toward a less perfect alley with cracked stones and honest pressure.

Lucien's gaze stayed on me.

Lucien: "How disciplined."

Haru: "How disappointed."

Aira made a strangled sound.

Aira: "I am proud and concerned."

Elira did not smile until the marker was safe.

Then, briefly, she did.

It was small.

It cost Lucien more than my answer did.

At the fourth marker, the city changed.

The memory echo marker spoke with Talen Mire's voice.

Talen: "I surrendered."

Elira went very still.

Not frozen.

Listening.

The marker repeated:

Talen: "I surrendered."

Lucien looked bored.

That was how I knew the cruelty was deliberate.

Soren Ashvale stepped forward from the neutral witness rail.

Soren: "Valemere objection. The marker uses a living witness's trauma without consent."

Talen's relay brightened from the Valemere observer frame.

He stood before anyone asked him to, braced right hand visible against his dark sleeve.

Talen: "I will provide context with consent."

The memory marker paused, still wearing his voice.

Talen looked at it like a man facing a room he had already survived once.

Talen: "They recorded my injury. My fear. My pain. And they played it back in training exercises to teach conduits how to 'manage emotional interference.' I didn't consent then. I'm consenting now to say: using someone's worst moment as a teaching tool is not education. It's cruelty."

The Valemere gallery went rigid.

The marker's voice dissolved into neutral sound.

The Foundation entered his consent statement in silver.

Talen lowered his braced hand.

This time, the memory did not own it.

The Foundation bell chimed.

**OBJECTION ENTERED**

The marker's voice blurred into neutral sound.

Elira looked at Soren.

Elira: "Thank you."

Soren inclined his head.

Soren: "Honor is mostly remembering people are not props."

Lucien's eyes moved to him.

Lucien: "Valemere has become sentimental."

Soren: "No. We are just embarrassed it took us this long."

The public record rail wrote that down.

The fifth marker was pressure burn.

It split the team.

Not by walls.

By priorities.

A false notice appeared above the square.

**UNCLASSIFIED SUBJECT OVEREXTENDING RESPONSE**

**PRIMARY WITNESS MAY REMOVE HERSELF TO REDUCE PRESSURE**

Elira did not move away.

She moved toward the wounded marker.

Rhea spoke at once.

President Valmont: "Intervention authorized under public witness protocol."

Aira: "Recorded. Elira moves by role, not private panic."

Mira called from above.

Mira: "Recorded!"

The notice shattered.

The pressure burn marker stabilized.

Lucien smiled faintly.

Lucien: "Good. You are learning to narrate every instinct before it damns you."

Elira did not look at him.

Elira: "No. We are learning to stop you from forging signatures on our choices."

The sixth marker carried a relay hook.

It reached for Cael.

Of course it did.

The hook appeared as a thin black line in the air, elegant as a calligraphy stroke, and curved toward his healer seal.

Cael saw it before the rest of us.

Not because his perception was stronger.

Because his body knew the shape of being claimed.

His hand went to his wrist, then stopped halfway.

For one second, the old training won the first movement.

For the next, Cael did.

He forced his hand down.

Cael: "Return hook."

His voice shook.

It still named the thing.

Nera's countermark was already forming.

Nera: "Owner phrase?"

Oric scanned from the rail, chalk moving across his slate.

Oric: "Hidden. It is using prior training authority as root."

Cael swallowed.

Cael: "No owner."

The hook bent toward him more sharply.

Cael said it again.

Louder.

Cael: "No owner."

The public rail took the phrase.

Not in unison.

That would have made it sound like command.

In scattered voices, uneven and human.

Mira: "No owner recorded."

Soren: "No honorable custody claim."

Blue observer: "Prior training contested."

Green student: "Cael Nox refused return."

The hook shivered.

Lucien's eyes narrowed.

For the first time that day, the field had not followed the route cleanly.

Leon put a water shield between them.

The hook passed through the shield as if water were a rumor.

Nera struck it with a countermark.

The hook bent.

Oric shouted from the rail.

Oric: "It is not custody grammar. It is return grammar."

Cael's face went blank.

Return grammar did not ask whether he wanted to go back.

It defined back as the place that owned him.

Elira moved.

Rhea's red seal flashed.

President Valmont: "Elira Vale authorized for witness intervention."

Aira: "Publicly recorded."

Mira: "Recorded!"

The hook changed direction.

Not toward Elira's body.

Toward her pendant.

My breath stopped.

The pendant flared black and gold at once.

Elira's hand rose, not to shield herself, but to catch the hook before it reached Cael.

Cael: "No!"

Too late.

Her fingers closed around the black line.

The field split.

Every street in the Wounded City turned toward that single contact.

Lucien's voice moved through the bell tower.

Lucien: "Second hinge."

The black hook wrapped around Elira's wrist.

The pendant burned bright.

For one heartbeat, it held.

Then Dominion grammar spoke through the activity.

**WILLING OBSTRUCTION CONFIRMED**

**PRIMARY WITNESS IN CUT PATH**

Rhea's seal slammed into the floor.

President Valmont: "Challenge. The movement was authorized."

Aira: "Challenge. It was public witness intervention."

Mira: "Challenge recorded!"

Professor Halden: "Challenge. No consent to severance."

Professor Valen: "Challenge. Multiple roles do not reduce personhood."

Nera and Oric hit the hook with countermarks together.

Nera: "Dominion label rejected."

Oric: "Return claim has no owner."

The hook did not break.

It tightened.

Elira's face went pale.

She did not cry out.

That made it worse.

I stepped forward.

The hidden Black Circle surged.

Elira looked at me through pain.

Elira: "Do not let him make your fear useful."

The words stopped me more effectively than any seal.

Lucien watched from across the square, eyes bright with something close to hunger.

Lucien: "Beautiful. Even now she protects your restraint. Tell me, Haru. Is love still autonomy when it knows exactly how to command you?"

My false Green Circle flickered.

Green.

Black beneath it.

Green again.

The public rail reacted in fragments.

Not enough to understand.

Enough to fear.

Aira saw the flicker and moved between me and the rail.

Aira: "Eyes on the hook! Not on Haru!"

Her voice cracked on my name.

She hated that it did.

The hook pulled Elira one step toward the empty seventh marker.

The marker with no visible wound.

Cael grabbed her other hand.

Cael: "Let me take it."

Elira's answer came through clenched teeth.

Elira: "No."

Cael: "It is built for me."

Elira: "That is why you do not get to inherit it."

The seventh marker opened.

Inside the empty outline was not a person.

It was a space.

A space shaped like someone removed from every witness.

The Wounded City bell rang once.

Foundation: "Severance route identified."

Rhea's face sharpened.

President Valmont: "End activity."

Foundation: "Unable. Dominion clause contested but active."

Leon: "Then we break the clause."

He slammed water pressure into the hook.

Cassian added fire.

The two spells braided without instruction. Steam flashed white across the square.

The hook held.

Elira's knees nearly gave.

I caught her this time.

She did not refuse.

Her weight hit my arms, and everything inside me became very quiet.

Too quiet.

Haru: "Aira."

My voice did not sound like mine.

Aira turned.

She heard it too.

Haru: "Find the legal root."

Aira swallowed.

Then she nodded.

Aira: "Working."

Good.

Still human enough to ask.

Still human enough to wait.

Barely.

Nera shouted from the edge of the square.

Nera: "The hook is not the root. The root is the empty marker."

Oric: "The seventh wound is isolation."

Valen's folio snapped open.

Professor Valen: "Then the answer is presence."

Rhea lifted both hands.

President Valmont: "All witnesses, name presence."

The rail erupted.

Not perfectly.

Not cleanly.

Mira: "Mira Castellan, Green Circle, public witness present!"

Soren: "Soren Ashvale, Valemere witness present."

Professor Halden: "Edric Halden, faculty witness present."

Professor Valen: "Iris Valen, historical witness present."

Leon: "Leon Varek, teammate present."

Cassian: "Cassian Veyr, very angry teammate present."

Aira: "Aira Solen, protocol author present."

Nera: "Nera Vail, technical witness present."

Oric: "Oric Sen, technical witness present."

Cael's voice came last.

Cael: "Cael Nox, not property, present."

The empty marker cracked.

The hook loosened.

Elira inhaled sharply.

The pendant flared warm.

The field accepted the presence record.

**SEVERANCE ROUTE INTERRUPTED**

Relief hit the square too fast.

Lucien looked at the cracked seventh marker.

Then at Cael.

He smiled.

Lucien: "Interrupted is not ended."

The activity released us before anyone could answer.

That was how Lucien left when he did not want to show whether he was disappointed.

Back in the chapel, healers removed the black mark from Elira's wrist by cutting away the upper layer of mana around her skin. It hurt. She let it.

Cael stood outside the healing circle with his arms stiff at his sides.

Leon did not let him leave.

Good.

The black mark did not come away like ordinary hostile magic.

It had hooks inside the definition of what Elira had done.

Every time the healer lifted a thread of it, the mark tried to argue.

**OBSTRUCTION**

Rhea answered.

President Valmont: "Authorized intervention."

The healer lifted another thread.

**PRIMARY WITNESS BIAS**

Aira answered.

Aira: "Multiple-role autonomy entered and active."

Another thread.

**CONDUIT RETURN CLAIM**

Nera and Oric answered together.

Nera: "No owner phrase."

Oric: "Prior training contested."

The mark fought less each time.

Not because it was weaker.

Because each lie had to be named before it could be removed.

Elira's jaw tightened until I thought she might crack a tooth.

She did not make a sound.

That was not composure now.

It was strategy.

If she cried out, Cael would hear it as proof.

He heard the silence as proof anyway.

His face became more controlled with every thread the healer cut.

Leon saw it and stepped in front of him.

Leon: "Look at me."

Cael: "I can hear."

Leon: "That is why I said look."

Cael's eyes lifted.

Leon: "She chose to protect you. You did not choose to hurt her."

Cael: "The result is the same."

Leon: "No, it is not."

Cael's expression sharpened with rare frustration.

Cael: "Her wrist is black because I was the route."

Leon leaned closer.

Leon: "Her wrist is black because Lucien is a murderer with grammar."

Cassian, from the chapel door, said:

Cassian: "That may be your best sentence."

Leon did not look away from Cael.

Leon: "Do not make me waste it."

Cael's breathing broke once.

Then steadied.

Night settled over Aurelis.

The Wounded City was sealed below the chapel, but I could still feel the empty seventh marker in the old stone.

Isolation.

A wound shaped like absence.

It changed the way the academy sounded.

Aurelis was never truly quiet. Wards hummed in walls. Training fields sighed as they reset. Students whispered where they thought faculty could not hear. The city below the cliffs sent up carriage bells and market noise softened by distance.

That night, every ordinary sound arrived with a question behind it.

Who was missing?

Who had been moved away for safety?

Who had agreed to stand alone because someone powerful called it protection?

The empty seventh marker had done more than threaten us. It had taught the whole day a new shape.

I walked the corridor outside the infirmary twice before Elira told me to stop wearing a path into the stone.

She was sitting upright again, because lying down made the mark on her wrist pull against the healer film.

Haru: "I am not pacing."

Elira looked at the door, then at me.

Elira: "You have passed that crack in the floor seven times."

Haru: "Observation is not pacing."

Elira: "Halden has been a bad influence."

I stopped by the chair.

She held out her unmarked hand.

I took it.

For several breaths, neither of us spoke.

Her pulse was steady under my fingers.

I counted it without meaning to.

Elira: "Do not turn that into surveillance."

I closed my eyes.

Haru: "I am trying not to."

Elira: "I know."

Then she sighed.

Elira: "Aira is right. We need a replacement phrase."

Haru: "I am listening."

That made her smile faintly.

Elira: "Better."

The smile faded.

Elira: "Tomorrow, if the seventh marker opens again, I may have to move."

Haru: "Elira."

Elira: "I am not asking permission."

Haru: "I know."

She gave me a look.

I corrected myself.

Haru: "I am listening."

Her hand tightened.

Elira: "If I move, do not treat my choice as Lucien's victory before it becomes one."

That was a hard sentence to let live.

Haru: "And if he makes it one?"

Her eyes shone.

Elira: "Then make sure the world knows he did."

At the time, I thought she meant the room.

I did not understand how far the word world would go.

Cael came to the doorway while she slept again.

This time Leon was with him, which meant Cael had either asked or stopped objecting to being followed.

Both were progress.

Cael looked at Elira's wrapped wrist and then at me.

Cael: "I am not going to apologize."

Leon made a small approving sound.

Cael: "I want to. But I am not."

Haru: "Good."

The word surprised him.

It surprised me too, a little.

Cael stood with both hands at his sides, forcing them not to fold into guilt.

Cael: "I am angry."

Leon: "Also good."

Cael looked at him.

Leon: "What? It is better than apologizing for being injured."

Cael looked back at Elira.

Cael: "I am angry that he made her kindness into a route. I am angry that I know the spell he may use next. I am angry that part of me still thinks being useful would make this easier."

He swallowed.

Cael: "I am saying it because if I do not, it turns into a plan."

That was Aira's work.

And Nera's.

And Oric's.

And maybe his own, finally.

I nodded.

Haru: "Then keep saying it."

Cael looked at me for a long moment.

Cael: "You too."

I did not ask what he meant.

I knew.

Elira slept between us, alive and marked, while anger moved quietly through the room looking for a future.

Elira fell asleep sitting upright in the infirmary chair while Aira argued softly with Rhea over next-step filings.

I sat beside her and did not touch the pendant.

That restraint felt like lifting a mountain grain by grain.

Cael came to the doorway near midnight.

He did not enter.

Haru: "You should sleep."

Cael: "So should you."

Neither of us moved.

He looked at Elira.

Cael: "The hook almost took her because it was reaching for me."

Haru: "It almost took her because Lucien designed it to punish anyone who protects you."

Cael's eyes stayed on the floor.

Cael: "I know the difference."

Haru: "Do you?"

He looked at me then.

There was fear in his face.

And something behind it that looked too much like decision.

Cael: "I am trying to."

He left before I could ask the question I did not want answered.

Elira stirred beside me.

Her fingers found mine.

Elira: "Do not chase his guilt in the dark."

Haru: "You were asleep."

Elira: "Barely."

I held her hand.

Warm.

Alive.

Still here.

Outside the infirmary window, a notice crystal flickered with the next continuance order.

**WOUNDED CITY PROTOCOL: INCOMPLETE**

**SEVENTH MARKER UNRESOLVED**

**FINAL ACTIVITY TO OPEN AT DAWN**

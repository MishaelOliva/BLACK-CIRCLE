# Episode 26: **Recovery Leave**

The restoration house did not look important enough to survive history.

That was its first mercy.

It stood on the eastern shore of Lake Velis, tucked between cedar trees and a long slope of silver grass. The roof was dark green tile. The walls were pale stone warmed by years of sunlight. No towers. No banners. No public rail. No crystal dais waiting to turn pain into testimony.

Just windows.

Too many windows for my comfort.

Elira noticed before the carriage stopped.

Elira: "You are counting entry points."

Haru: "No."

She looked at me.

Haru: "Yes."

Elira leaned back against the carriage cushion, tired but amused.

Elira: "Tea first."

I exhaled.

Haru: "Tea first."

Professor Valen had left the front door unlocked.

That almost made me turn around.

A note waited on the small entry table beneath a vase of white lake flowers.

**Small rebellions begin with entering without permission.**

Below that, in Aira's handwriting:

**I wanted to write something useful, but Professor Valen got there first. There are buns in the blue tin. Do not become tragic before eating.**

Elira read both notes and pressed a hand over her mouth.

The laugh came out fragile.

But it came out.

The cedar box from the ward sat beside the vase.

Professor Valen had not placed the recovery token on Elira's bed tray for decoration. The black ribbon with silver edging waited inside the box, plain enough to look harmless and old enough that the air around it refused modern categories.

Elira opened the lid.

For a moment she only looked at it.

Haru: "You do not have to decide now."

She touched the ribbon with two fingers.

Elira: "Yes, I do."

The answer was quiet, but the house heard it.

A line of old silver warmed along the entry table, then stopped. Waiting.

Not impatient.

That mattered.

Elira lifted the ribbon.

Haru: "Door or wrist?"

She looked toward the hall, then toward the lake-bright windows, then down at her own hands.

Elira: "Door first."

The choice surprised me.

She saw that too.

Elira: "If I tie it to my wrist immediately, someone will make it about whether I consent to being watched. If I tie it to the door, the first thing this house learns is that the room is chosen."

She moved to the inside of the front door and tied the ribbon through the small iron ring beneath the latch.

Slowly.

Badly, at first.

Her fingers were still tired.

I did not reach to correct it.

She untangled the knot herself, breathed once, and tried again.

The second knot held.

The house answered without ceremony.

The floor warmed under both of us and wrote, in no language I had learned and every language my body understood:

**ENTERED BY CHOICE**

Elira's shoulders dropped.

Not all the way.

Enough.

Elira: "Good."

Haru: "Good house."

The entry table gave a small wooden creak.

Elira looked at me.

Elira: "Did you just praise architecture?"

Haru: "It behaved well."

The laugh came back.

Still fragile.

Still hers.

I took the satchel from her shoulder before she could pretend it was not heavy. She let me. Another small rebellion, maybe. Against pride. Against fear. Against the idea that needing help made her less herself.

The house smelled of cedar, old books, lake wind, and sugar.

In the kitchen, a kettle warmed itself politely over a blue flame. The flame had no visible Circle signature. Old house magic. Pre-standardization, like Maelin had said. It hummed in a way that did not ask me what I was.

That helped.

Elira sat at the small table near the window.

Sunlight touched her hair.

For one suspended second, the room forgot white healing gowns, funeral flowers, and the moment her gold had gone out in my arms.

Then a spoon clicked against porcelain.

Elira flinched.

The Black Circle rose so fast the window glass darkened at the edges.

Elira: "No."

One word.

Not sharp.

Not frightened.

Commanding.

I froze with my hand half lifted.

The Black Circle did not understand the spoon.

It understood impact.

It understood hostile vectors, doctrine hooks, severance routes, pressure entering below a shield by pretending to be rescue. It understood the shape of Elira dying in my arms and the exact instant the room had become too late.

A spoon against porcelain did not belong to any category it respected.

So it tried to make one.

Sound: sudden.

Elira: flinching.

My body: already moving.

Threat.

The conclusion arrived faster than thought, and thought had to catch it by the throat.

No.

Not Noctis.

Not Lucien.

Not a blade.

Not even danger.

Just a kitchen sound in a house beside a lake, with sunlight on the table and tea cooling because both of us had forgotten ordinary things required ordinary hands.

The power did not believe me.

That was the worst part.

I could stop spells. I could read old architecture. I could feel a hostile claim before most people knew a room had changed.

But I could not explain safety to the part of me built from every moment I had failed to preserve it.

For a breath, I felt how easy it would be to make the room obedient. No sharp sounds. No sudden movement. No object near Elira unless I permitted it. A perfect quiet arranged around her fear.

The thought sickened me because it came dressed as care.

I kept my hand still.

Not lowered yet.

Still.

There was a difference.

The kettle flame bent away from me.

Elira breathed once. Twice. Her fingers were white around the table edge.

Elira: "It was just the sound."

Haru: "I know."

Elira: "You did not know. You reacted."

That was true.

It was also unbearable.

Haru: "I'm sorry."

Elira closed her eyes.

Elira: "Please do not apologize like you have already retreated."

I lowered my hand.

The window cleared.

Haru: "What do you need?"

Elira opened her eyes again.

They were wet.

Still steady.

Elira: "For you to sit down."

So I sat.

Across from her.

She looked at the empty chair beside her.

I moved there instead.

The distance between the chairs was ordinary. A few inches of old wooden floor. Nothing a person would notice before the Wounded City. Nothing a council report could measure.

It felt like crossing a battlefield.

Elira reached for the spoon.

Her hand shook.

I did not take it from her.

That was harder than stopping a spell.

She stirred her tea slowly. The spoon clicked again, softer this time.

She flinched again.

Less.

I stayed seated.

The Black Circle stayed beneath my skin.

Elira watched me as carefully as I watched her.

Elira: "Again."

Haru: "You don't have to-"

Elira: "I know."

She stirred the tea again.

Click.

Her breath caught.

Then released.

The lake moved outside the window, indifferent and kind.

Elira: "There."

Haru: "There."

She looked down into the cup.

Elira: "I do not want my fear to become another thing everyone organizes around."

Haru: "Fear deserves care."

Elira: "Yes. Not a throne."

That sounded like her.

It sounded so much like her that something in my chest hurt.

I reached slowly, making the motion visible before I touched her hand.

She turned her palm up.

Permission.

Choice.

Again and again, that was what the world kept trying to steal.

We drank tea. We ate Aira's buns from the blue tin. Elira ate half of one and called it victory. I ate three because she watched me with narrow eyes until I stopped pretending I did not need food.

After tea, the house began introducing itself.

Not with a voice.

That would have made me leave.

It introduced itself through small refusals.

When I walked toward the largest window to inspect the latch, the floor warmed beneath my feet and a line of old silver appeared in the wood.

**GUEST.**

No sound.

No projection.

Just a wordless impression so clear my mind translated it.

Guest.

Not asset.

Not candidate.

Not Black Circle.

Guest.

I stared at the floor.

Elira, wrapped in the blanket on the sofa, watched me over her cup.

Elira: "Did the floor just scold you?"

Haru: "It identified me."

Elira's brows drew together.

Haru: "As a guest."

She was silent for a moment.

Then her face changed.

Not happiness.

Something more fragile.

Relief that did not yet trust itself.

Elira: "Good house."

The floor cooled, apparently satisfied.

I did not thank it aloud.

I considered it.

That was already a dangerous level of cooperation with architecture.

Elira set her cup down and looked toward the hallway.

Elira: "Show me the rest?"

Haru: "Maelin said stairs only if necessary."

Elira looked at me.

Haru: "The rest of the first floor."

Elira: "Better."

We moved slowly.

That mattered.

At Aurelis, slowness had always meant strategy, discipline, timing. In the restoration house, slowness meant her hand on the wall when the room tilted. It meant stopping between the kitchen and the sitting room because her breath caught for no reason either of us could see. It meant me learning that assistance was not a spell cast quickly enough to hide the need.

Haru: "Stop?"

Elira nodded once.

I did not reach for her.

She reached for me.

Her fingers curled around my sleeve instead of my hand, as if touch through cloth gave her one more layer of choice.

Elira: "It is annoying."

Haru: "The weakness?"

Elira's eyes narrowed.

I heard my own mistake before Maelin could appear in spirit and hit me with a chart.

Haru: "No. I mean the body delay. Not weakness."

Elira let me suffer for three seconds.

Elira: "Acceptable correction."

Haru: "Thank you."

Elira: "Barely."

The corner of her mouth moved.

That was the second victory of the day.

The sitting room faced the lake through three tall windows. White curtains stirred in a breeze that had no obvious source. A low shelf held blankets, games, blank notebooks, a small music box, and six sealed packets labeled in Aira's handwriting.

**FOR WHEN HARU LOOKS LIKE HE IS ABOUT TO INVENT A PROTOCOL.**

Elira picked up the first packet.

Inside was a deck of hand-painted cards and a note.

**Play something with rules you did not write.**

The second packet contained dried peach slices.

**Feed him. Feed yourself first, because hierarchy matters.**

The third held a small blue ribbon.

**Emergency hair management. Do not ask Leon for help unless you want one side battle-ready and the other side tragic.**

Elira laughed softly.

The sound still startled her.

It startled me too, but I stayed where I was.

No counting.

No rushing to preserve it.

Just listening.

Elira ran her thumb over the ribbon.

Elira: "She was scared."

Haru: "Aira?"

Elira nodded.

Elira: "This is what she does when she is scared. She makes logistics so no one has to say the frightening part first."

I thought of Aira's notebook in the ward room.

Second laugh. Voluntary. Witnessed by friends, not systems.

Haru: "It works."

Elira looked at me with tired affection.

Elira: "On you especially."

I could not deny it.

The house had a small study beyond the sitting room. Professor Valen had left it unlocked too. The desk faced away from the window instead of toward it, which told me she understood more about recovery than some healers. A person who had become a public event did not always need a view. Sometimes they needed a wall that promised nothing would appear on the other side of it.

On the desk lay three folders.

One blue.

One red.

One plain brown.

Elira's hand tightened on my sleeve.

Haru: "We do not have to open them."

Elira: "What are they?"

I looked at the seals without touching.

Haru: "Medical instructions. Council contact conditions. Personal letters."

Elira stared at the plain brown folder.

Elira: "Personal from whom?"

The house answered by nudging the folder half an inch toward her.

I disliked how much personality this building had.

Elira noticed.

Elira: "It likes me better."

Haru: "Everyone does."

Her expression softened too quickly for me to regret saying it.

She opened the brown folder.

The first letter was from Mira Castellan.

The handwriting was careful at the beginning and increasingly nervous by the second paragraph.

Elira read silently.

Her eyes moved slower as she reached the middle.

Elira: "She says the Green Circle students are not copying the sequence because they think I owe them proof."

She swallowed.

Elira: "They are copying it because they do not want anyone to make Cael's death into a rumor before the record can protect him."

The room went quiet.

Outside, lake wind moved through cedar branches.

Haru: "Mira understands records."

Elira: "She understands grief."

I had no answer to that.

The next letter was unsigned.

Noctis paper.

No official seal.

Elira hesitated.

Haru: "I can read it first."

Elira's mouth pressed thin.

Elira: "No."

Then, softer:

Elira: "But sit close."

I sat close.

The letter was from Iven Sahl.

Not long.

Not polished.

He wrote that Cael once lent him a thermal charm during winter drills and told him not to return it until he stopped confusing obedience with survival. He wrote that he had not understood. He wrote that he was beginning to.

At the bottom, one sentence had been scratched out and written again.

**I am sorry he had to die where we could finally see him.**

Elira covered her mouth.

This time no laugh came.

Only breath.

Careful.

Shaking.

I let my shoulder touch hers.

She leaned into it.

Elira: "He should not have to apologize to me."

Haru: "No."

Elira: "Noctis made children write grief like petitions."

The Black Circle stirred.

Not toward the windows.

Toward the idea of Noctis.

A place.

A doctrine.

A machinery of frightened students and polite murder.

Elira's hand found mine under the desk.

Elira: "Beside me."

I drew one breath.

Then another.

Haru: "Beside you."

We left the red and blue folders closed.

That felt like treason against urgency.

It also felt like obedience to the first promise of the day.

Tea first.

Then rest.

Then whatever the world had failed to become while we were gone.

We returned to the kitchen because Elira wanted to see the buns again, not because she wanted to eat one. She examined them with the solemn attention of a queen reviewing troops.

Elira: "That one has too much sugar."

Haru: "For whom?"

Elira: "For dignity."

Haru: "Dignity survived Lucien. It can survive icing."

She looked at me.

Haru: "Aira has been affecting my speech."

Elira: "Good."

She chose the smallest bun and ate a second bite.

Victory revised upward.

Afterward she insisted on walking to the back door.

Not outside.

Not yet.

Only to the threshold, where cedar shade met the pale stone step and the lake spread beyond the grass like a piece of sky the world had set down to rest.

Elira stood there with one hand on the doorframe.

The wind touched her hair.

Her pendant did not move.

For a moment the dark crystal looked only like a stone someone had chosen because it was beautiful.

Elira: "I thought I would feel safer here."

Haru: "Do you?"

She considered the lake.

Elira: "No."

My hand tightened at my side.

She saw.

Elira: "Not because the house failed. Because safety is not something a place can give back all at once."

The words made sense.

I disliked them anyway.

Elira: "But I feel less watched."

Haru: "There are wards."

Elira: "Wards are not the same as eyes."

The house floor warmed beneath us, approving or offended. It was difficult to tell.

Elira stepped back before I could suggest she was tired.

Then she ruined my restraint by swaying once.

I caught her elbow.

Visible.

Careful.

She leaned into the support and closed her eyes.

Elira: "I hate that I needed that."

Haru: "I know."

Elira: "Do not say you are sorry."

Haru: "I was not going to."

She opened one eye.

Haru: "I was going to say I am glad I was beside you."

Her expression changed.

She let the sentence stand.

Then she let me help her back to the sofa.

By late afternoon, the security perimeter had introduced itself in the least peaceful way possible.

Rhea's council seal appeared in the front hall mirror.

Rhea: "Status."

Elira, from the sofa with a blanket over her knees, lifted her cup.

Elira: "Tea achieved."

Rhea blinked once.

Rhea: "Acceptable."

Aira's face shoved into the reflection beside hers.

Aira: "That is not an update. Are there chairs? Are there blankets? Has Haru threatened a shrub?"

Haru: "No."

Elira: "He considered the windows."

Aira: "Progress. Windows are not shrubs."

Leon appeared behind them, arms folded.

Leon: "The eastern road is clear. Two Council outriders passed an hour ago. No Noctis relay interference."

Cassian leaned into frame from the other side.

Cassian: "Also, Dren says if Aerlen cracks the restoration house, he has to repair it without celestial shortcuts."

Haru: "Noted."

Elira's smile faded as she looked at them.

Elira: "How is Aurelis?"

The mirror quieted.

Aira answered first.

Aira: "Messy. Awake. Too many committees. Too many people pretending they always believed mercy was policy."

Leon: "Green Circle students are still copying the public sequence records."

Cassian: "Noctis has withdrawn its official banner from the east dormitory. The student group stayed."

Rhea: "Councilor Krail filed a supplementary risk memorandum at noon."

Elira's fingers tightened around the cup.

I felt it before she spoke.

Haru: "About me?"

Rhea: "About your proximity to every major witness, yes. Senna Voss marked it procedurally premature."

Aira: "Which is formal language for sit down and stop pointing at grieving students."

Rhea: "Less colorful, but accurate."

Elira set her cup down.

Elira: "Do not keep all of it from us."

Professor Valen stepped into the reflection then.

Her gaze was gentle enough to be dangerous.

Professor Valen: "We will not. But recovery leave is not remote command. You are allowed to receive news without becoming responsible for solving it before supper."

Elira looked as if she wanted to object.

Then exhaustion moved through her face.

Elira: "I hate how reasonable that is."

Aira: "Excellent. Hate it while resting."

The mirror dimmed after Rhea set the next check-in time.

The house seemed quieter afterward.

Not empty.

Just ours.

That was when I noticed there was only one bedroom prepared.

I stopped in the hallway.

Elira, walking slowly beside me, followed my gaze.

A single wide bed stood beneath a window facing the lake. Two folded blankets lay at the foot. Fresh clothes waited on a chair: soft gray trousers and a dark shirt for me, a pale blue sleeping robe and shawl for Elira. On the bedside table sat another note.

This one was Maelin's.

**The patient sleeps worse alone. The proximity witness destabilizes when exiled to chairs. If either of you turns this into a moral crisis, I will know.**

Elira read it.

Color touched her face.

Not fever.

I looked away too quickly.

Elira: "Haru."

Haru: "I can sleep outside the door."

Elira: "That is exactly the moral crisis she warned about."

Haru: "I don't want to assume."

Her expression softened.

Elira: "Then ask."

The room held its breath with me.

Haru: "Do you want me beside you?"

Elira's hand moved to the pendant. This time she did not grip it. She only touched it, as if reminding herself that it was still there and not the whole story.

Elira: "Yes."

The answer was quiet.

Complete.

Elira: "I am scared to sleep. I am more scared to wake and have to search for you."

My throat closed.

Haru: "Then I will be beside you."

Elira: "Not guarding the door."

Haru: "Beside you."

She nodded once.

That night, the house taught us how awkward survival could be.

Elira changed behind a screen while I stared at the lake with the concentration of someone studying enemy formations. She noticed and laughed under her breath, which made both of us blush more badly than the situation required.

When I returned from changing in the washroom, she was sitting on the bed in the pale blue robe, hair loose over one shoulder, pendant dark against the fabric.

Alive was the one that undid me.

Elira saw it.

Elira: "Come here."

I did.

Slowly.

She shifted under the blanket. I lay beside her over the top of the second one at first, because apparently even after everything I could still be ridiculous.

Elira stared at the ceiling.

Elira: "Haru."

Haru: "Yes?"

Elira: "Are you planning to sleep like a formal apology?"

I turned my head.

Her mouth trembled.

Mine did too.

Haru: "I am trying to be respectful."

Elira: "I know."

She reached for my hand beneath the edge of the blanket.

Elira: "Respect me by believing I can choose closeness."

The Black Circle warmed once.

Not warning.

Witness.

I moved closer.

Only when she tugged my hand did I settle beside her properly, shoulder against shoulder, warmth meeting warmth.

She exhaled as if she had been holding the breath since the Wounded City.

For a while, we listened to the lake.

Elira: "Aira is going to call this a honeymoon."

I blinked into the dark.

Haru: "This is medical leave."

Elira: "That will not stop her."

Haru: "No."

Elira's fingers tightened around mine.

Elira: "Would it be terrible if part of me wanted it to feel a little like one?"

The question entered me more carefully than any blade.

I turned toward her.

Haru: "No."

Her eyes shone in the low ward-light.

Haru: "It would be human."

She looked at me for a long time.

Then she rested her forehead against my shoulder.

Elira: "I want some part of being alive to belong to love. Not councils. Not Noctis. Not death. Us."

My hand rose to her hair.

I stopped before touching.

She leaned into the pause.

Permission again.

I stroked her hair once, then again, slow enough for both of us to believe in it.

Haru: "Then tomorrow belongs to us."

Elira: "Not all of it."

Haru: "No. Not all."

Elira: "But enough."

Outside, the lake moved under moonlight.

Inside, Elira fell asleep with her hand in mine.

I stayed awake longer.

Not counting breaths this time.

Listening to them.

There was a difference.

It was small.

It was everything.

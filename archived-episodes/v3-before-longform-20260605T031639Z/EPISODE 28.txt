# Episode 28: **The Honeymoon Under Witness**

Aira called at breakfast and ruined the word immediately.

The mirror brightened while Elira was spreading pear jam with the careful seriousness of a person committing family treason.

Aira's face appeared upside down.

Aira: "Good morning, medically sanctioned honeymoon."

Elira choked.

I spilled tea on my sleeve.

Leon, somewhere behind Aira, said, "You promised not to open with that."

Aira rotated the mirror image upright.

Aira: "I promised to consider not opening with that."

Rhea's voice cut in from out of frame.

Rhea: "This is an official welfare check."

Cassian: "With honeymoon oversight."

Rhea: "Veyr."

Cassian: "Withdrawn."

Elira covered her face with one hand.

Elira: "I regret having friends."

Aira: "No, you don't. You regret that your friends are accurate."

I recovered the cup before it dripped onto the floor.

Haru: "It is recovery leave."

Aira looked directly at me through the mirror.

Aira: "Haru, you are alone with your official girlfriend in a lake house, under orders to rest, eat, sleep near each other, and stop treating romance like an emergency protocol. I am not saying the Council invented a honeymoon. I am saying bureaucracy tripped and fell into one."

Elira's ears turned pink.

The sight was dangerous.

Not politically.

Personally.

Professor Valen appeared beside Rhea, holding a file.

Professor Valen: "Aira's terminology is not medically recognized."

Aira: "Emotionally recognized."

Professor Valen: "Unfortunately."

Rhea cleared her throat.

Rhea: "Actual update. Noctis has filed two objections to the recovery order. Both were rejected. Senna Voss has requested Elira's medical testimony be deferred until Maelin clears it. Councilor Krail is attempting to frame proximity as dependency."

Elira lowered her hand.

The blush faded.

Haru: "Of course he is."

Rhea's gaze shifted to Elira.

Rhea: "Your corrected welfare form reached my office before his summary did."

Aira leaned into view.

Aira: "For the record, 'Alive. Irritated.' has entered my permanent vocabulary."

Elira's chin lifted by a fraction.

Elira: "It was medically accurate."

Professor Valen: "It was politically useful because it made the instrument visible."

Rhea: "Exactly. Krail now has to argue against your conditions instead of pretending he only asked neutral questions."

I looked at Elira.

She did not look at me first.

She looked at the dimmed mirror edge, where her own reflection sat beside the Council seal without being absorbed by it.

Good.

Rhea: "He will find less traction if you give him no public instability today."

Aira: "Translation: please do not darken the sky unless someone earns it."

Haru: "I will try."

Leon leaned into view.

Leon: "Also, the water charm is holding?"

Elira smiled.

Elira: "Yes. Thank you."

Leon nodded too quickly and stepped back out of frame.

Cassian: "He practiced asking that normally."

Leon: "I did not."

Aira: "He did."

The mirror call ended with Rhea ordering us to answer the evening check-in and Aira loudly advising us to "do something emotionally irresponsible but medically moderate."

For a few seconds after the mirror dimmed, neither of us spoke.

Then Elira set down the jam knife.

Elira: "I want to go into town."

Every security map in my head unfolded at once.

Elira watched it happen.

Elira: "Small rebellion."

Haru: "The town is outside the perimeter."

Elira: "The first market street is inside the outer ward Professor Valen inspected."

Haru: "There will be people."

Elira: "That is usually what towns contain."

I looked at the window.

Lake Velis village sat half a mile down the road, a cluster of slate roofs, fishing boats, and shop awnings bright against the shore. Ordinary people lived there. Ordinary people now knew the Black Circle was not a myth, because the whole world knew. Ordinary people would look.

At me.

At her.

At the girl who had died and returned.

At the boy who had nearly become an ending.

Elira reached across the table and touched my wrist.

Elira: "I do not want our first days away from Aurelis to teach us that safety means hiding from every face."

I knew she was right.

I hated that she was right while still weak enough that climbing the stairs cost her breath.

Haru: "If you get tired, we leave."

Elira: "Yes."

Haru: "If the crowd presses, we leave."

Elira: "Yes."

Haru: "If any relay-"

Elira squeezed my wrist.

Elira: "Haru."

I stopped.

She smiled.

Elira: "Ask me what I want to buy."

The question felt absurd.

That was its power.

Haru: "What do you want to buy?"

Elira: "A ribbon."

Haru: "For your hair?"

Elira looked toward the bedroom, where my failed braid from the night before had been mercifully undone.

Elira: "For your continued education."

The decision to go into town took forty minutes to become an actual coat.

That was mostly my fault.

Elira sat on the edge of the bed while I arranged possible outer layers on the chair with the seriousness of a combat deployment. Pale shawl. Dark shawl. Heavier cloak. Lighter cloak. The blue ribbon from Aira's packet. A small healing charm Maelin had labeled **ONLY IF DIZZY, NOT IF DRAMATIC**.

Elira watched me for almost a full minute.

Elira: "Haru."

Haru: "Yes?"

Elira: "You are choosing fabric like it will negotiate with assassins."

I looked at the cloaks.

Haru: "Some fabrics conceal ward seams better."

Elira's mouth twitched.

Elira: "That was not a denial."

Haru: "No."

She reached for the pale shawl.

I handed it to her before remembering that handing it too quickly was also a kind of hovering.

My arm stopped halfway.

Elira stared at the shawl suspended between us.

Elira: "Are we having a crisis about fabric?"

Haru: "Possibly."

Elira took it from me.

Elira: "I choose this one."

Haru: "Because it is lighter?"

Elira wrapped it around her shoulders.

Elira: "Because I like it."

The answer defeated six security considerations at once.

I admired it.

Reluctantly.

She pinned her hair back with the blue ribbon. Not perfectly. A few strands escaped around her face and caught the morning light. She looked less like the Yellow Circle prodigy the world had watched die and more like a girl trying to leave a house without everyone turning that act into testimony.

Then she saw herself in the small mirror.

Her hand went to the pendant.

The room changed.

Not magically.

Honestly.

Elira: "It shows."

Haru: "The pendant?"

Elira: "The fear."

I stepped closer, then stopped where she could see the choice.

Haru: "What do you want people to see?"

She looked at her reflection for a long time.

Elira: "Not that I am untouched."

The words were steady.

Hard-earned.

Elira: "Not that I am grateful in a way that makes everyone comfortable. Not that dying made me wiser or softer or easier to turn into a symbol."

Her fingers lowered from the pendant.

Elira: "I want them to see I am here. And that I chose the ribbon myself."

Haru: "Then that is what we carry."

She turned.

Elira: "Together?"

Haru: "Together."

The restoration house warmed beneath our feet. A black ribbon edged in silver slipped from the desk drawer and landed on the floor between us.

Elira looked down.

Elira: "Opinionated house."

Haru: "Interfering house."

The old silver in the floor did not react, which I chose to interpret as guilt.

Elira picked up the ribbon.

Elira: "We will buy one anyway."

Haru: "To avoid encouraging it?"

Elira: "To choose twice."

That was how we left: Elira in a pale shawl, blue ribbon in her hair, dark pendant visible because hiding it would have made the fear bigger; me in a dark coat with a false calm I did not expect anyone who knew me to believe; the house's black ribbon tucked in Elira's pocket like a small argument waiting for later.

The road to Lake Velis village ran between cedar trees and low stone walls. Wildflowers grew in gaps where frost had cracked the old mortar. Fishing nets hung from posts near the shore, drying in the wind. A boy about Leon's age repaired a boat rope and pretended not to stare until Elira waved.

He nearly dropped the rope.

Elira smiled.

Elira: "Too much?"

Haru: "For him?"

Elira: "For me."

I considered lying.

She deserved better.

Haru: "Some."

She nodded.

Elira: "Do not ask if we should go back."

Haru: "I was arranging the words."

Elira: "Unarrange them."

I obeyed.

At the outer ward marker, a thin line of silver crossed the road. It did not flare for rank. It did not test my Circle. It only warmed as Elira stepped over it, then again as I followed.

**VOLUNTARY EXIT.**

The impression settled into my mind like a hand closing gently around a record.

Elira paused.

Elira: "It records leaving?"

Haru: "Voluntary."

She breathed out.

Elira: "Good."

Then, with a small defiance I loved before I understood it, she stepped backward over the line and forward again.

The ward warmed twice.

**VOLUNTARY RETURN. VOLUNTARY EXIT.**

Elira's smile sharpened.

Elira: "I like that."

Haru: "The ward?"

Elira: "The correction."

She stepped into the village road.

I followed, feeling the old house behind us accept that protection could include departure.

The village did look.

There was no avoiding that.

People paused with baskets in hand. A child tugged at her mother's sleeve. An old fisherman removed his cap when Elira passed, then seemed embarrassed by the ceremony and put it back on badly. Two shopkeepers whispered my name without meaning for me to hear.

Black Circle.

The words followed at a distance.

Not myth now.

Not secret.

Not fully understood.

Elira's hand tightened around mine.

I almost apologized for the attention.

Then I realized she was holding me steady too.

A ribbon seller near the fountain bowed so deeply that Elira's face changed.

Elira: "Please don't."

The woman straightened at once.

Ribbon Seller: "Sorry, Lady Vale."

Elira: "Elira is fine."

The woman's eyes flicked to me, then back to her.

Ribbon Seller: "Elira, then."

That small correction did more than the bow.

Elira breathed easier.

She chose a blue ribbon first, then a gold one, then after a moment a narrow black ribbon edged in silver thread.

The seller hesitated before touching it.

I felt the market notice.

Elira noticed too.

She picked up the black ribbon herself and held it against my sleeve.

Elira: "This one."

My heart did something undisciplined.

Haru: "For your hair?"

Elira: "For yours."

I stared at her.

She looked entirely serious.

Haru: "My hair is not long enough for a ribbon."

Elira: "Then I will improvise badly and you will be gracious."

The ribbon seller made a small sound that might have been a laugh before fear caught it.

Elira smiled at her.

Elira: "He is very gracious under ribbon-based adversity."

The seller relaxed.

Not completely.

Enough to wrap the ribbons with ordinary care.

As we left, the child who had been staring from beside the fountain stepped forward.

Child: "Are you the Black Circle?"

Her mother went pale.

Mother: "Lysa."

I crouched before panic could spread.

That made several people tense.

So I kept my hands visible.

Haru: "My name is Haru."

The child considered this.

Lysa: "But are you?"

Elira's hand rested on my shoulder.

Not to restrain.

To be there.

Haru: "Yes."

The word entered the market like a stone into water.

No disguise.

No old Green lie to hide behind.

Lysa frowned.

Lysa: "You don't look like the scary drawings."

Aira would have loved her.

Haru: "That is probably good."

Lysa nodded.

Lysa: "My brother says Black Circle means you can break mountains."

Haru: "Breaking mountains causes problems for people who live near mountains."

The child thought that over with grave respect.

Lysa: "So you don't?"

Haru: "I try not to break things that do not need breaking."

Elira's fingers moved once against my shoulder.

The mother bowed her head, not deeply this time.

Mother: "Thank you."

I did not know for which part.

Maybe neither did she.

The market did not return to normal after that.

It tried.

That made it kinder than a council chamber.

The ribbon seller began wrapping the black ribbon again because her hands needed something to do. The old fisherman bought apples he clearly did not need. Lysa's mother took her daughter's shoulders and whispered something too soft for ordinary hearing, so I made myself not listen with anything beyond it.

Elira noticed.

Elira: "Thank you."

Haru: "For what?"

Elira: "Not hearing everything just because you can."

The compliment entered a place in me I had not known needed it.

Haru: "I am learning."

Elira's smile was tired and proud.

Then a man near the baker's stall bowed shallowly, with the awkward precision of someone who did not know what else to do.

Man: "Lady Vale. Aerlen."

He said my name like a stone he was not sure he was allowed to hold.

Elira turned toward him.

Elira: "Good morning."

He swallowed.

Man: "My daughter is Green Circle. She was at the academy gate yesterday."

Mira's sign flashed in my memory.

**RECOVERY IS NOT ABSENCE**

The man looked at me, then immediately away.

Man: "She said you raised your hand to the Noctis students too."

Elira's expression changed.

Softened.

Elira: "I did."

Man: "She cried about it."

He looked mortified to have said that aloud.

Elira did not save him from the feeling by pretending it was nothing.

Elira: "I am glad she saw them."

The man's eyes filled.

He bowed again, smaller.

Man: "Thank you."

This time I understood part of it.

Not all.

Enough.

We bought bread from his stall because leaving without doing so would have made the conversation too solemn. Elira chose two rolls and one small pastry shaped like a crescent moon. The baker added a second pastry without charging.

I opened my mouth.

Elira stepped on my foot.

Not hard.

Enough.

Haru: "Thank you."

The baker looked relieved by the ordinary words.

Elira waited until we were three stalls away.

Elira: "You were about to argue about pastry."

Haru: "Unpaid goods create obligation."

Elira: "Sometimes they create breakfast."

Haru: "It is almost noon."

Elira: "Then emergency breakfast."

She tore one pastry in half and offered me the larger piece.

I looked at it.

Haru: "Hierarchy matters?"

Elira: "Aira has infected us both."

We ate pastry beside the village fountain.

The fountain was small and practical, nothing like Aurelis' repaired courtyard fountain or the Wounded City markers. Water spilled from a stone fish into a round basin where children had thrown copper coins. No hidden black ring. No public relay. No old system waiting to wake under pressure.

Just water.

Elira watched it with such focus that I worried until she said:

Elira: "I used to want a fountain in the Vale winter garden."

Haru: "Why?"

Elira: "Because I thought the academy fountains were beautiful when I first visited Aurelis. I was nine. Everything gold looked like a promise then."

Her mouth curved without quite smiling.

Elira: "My mother said indoor fountains were impractical. My father said impractical things were useful if they made a house kinder. They compromised on a birdbath."

Haru: "Did birds use it?"

Elira: "One extremely arrogant sparrow."

Haru: "A successful compromise."

She laughed.

The sound moved through the market differently than the word Black Circle had.

People heard it and tried not to stare.

Some failed.

Elira let them fail.

Then the fountain water rippled.

My hand moved before thought.

Not with power.

Toward her.

She caught my fingers.

The ripple had been a coin dropping from Lysa's hand.

The child froze.

So did half the square.

My Black Circle stayed quiet.

My face, less so.

Elira squeezed my hand.

Haru: "Sorry."

Lysa looked at me with blunt horror.

Lysa: "Did I almost make you break a mountain?"

The square went very still.

Elira pressed her lips together.

I could feel the laugh in her hand before I heard it.

Haru: "No."

Lysa: "Oh."

Haru: "Maybe a cup."

Elira turned her face away.

Her shoulders shook.

The mother looked as if she had aged several years and then been handed them back.

Lysa considered the answer.

Lysa: "Cups are replaceable."

Haru: "That is what I have heard."

The square exhaled.

Not all at once.

In pieces.

The baker laughed first.

The ribbon seller followed.

Then the old fisherman, who had bought apples for no reason, said:

Old Fisherman: "Depends on the cup."

That was enough.

The market resumed around us, not normal, not careless, but less terrified of the fact that I had answered a child and survived being known.

Elira leaned closer.

Elira: "You made a joke."

Haru: "A small one."

Elira: "Publicly."

Haru: "Under pressure."

Elira: "Aira will document this as growth."

Haru: "Please do not tell her."

Elira's smile turned dangerous.

Elira: "I am absolutely telling her."

We walked one more row of stalls because she wanted dried pears and because I wanted to prove to myself that leaving immediately after a flinch was not the only kind of safety. She chose a small packet of tea for Maelin, a wooden bookmark for Professor Valen, and a dark blue thread spool for no reason she would admit.

At the edge of the market, a notice crystal flickered in the window of the post office.

My name appeared.

Not fully.

Only in the headline passing through the public relay:

**COUNCIL MINORITY QUESTIONS BLACK CIRCLE PROXIMITY DURING RECOVERY LEAVE**

Elira saw it before I could step between her and the glass.

Her expression closed.

The day lost some of its warmth.

Haru: "We can go."

Elira stared at the notice until the words cycled away.

Elira: "No."

Haru: "Elira."

Elira: "I am not going to let Krail become the last thing I saw in town."

She turned deliberately to the flower stall next door.

A woman there was arranging white lake flowers like the ones Professor Valen had left in the restoration house. Elira bought three stems and held them in both hands.

Elira: "There."

Haru: "There?"

Elira looked back at the post office window, where the headline had returned.

Elira: "Now he is beside flowers."

It was not a strategy.

It was not denial.

It was a tiny rearrangement of the world so ugliness did not get the only frame.

I loved her so sharply in that moment that speaking would have made it smaller.

So I carried the parcels.

On the walk back, Elira grew tired before she admitted it.

I noticed the change in her steps. The way her hand became heavier in mine. The way her smile stayed in place by discipline instead of ease.

Haru: "We leave now."

Elira sighed.

Elira: "You are correct."

Haru: "You sound offended."

Elira: "I am offended that my body agrees with you."

We took the lake path rather than the road. Halfway home, she stopped beneath cedar shade and leaned against me.

Not dramatically.

Not like collapse.

Like trust.

I held her with one arm around her shoulders.

Elira: "The child asked you directly."

Haru: "Yes."

Elira: "You answered."

Haru: "It is public now."

Elira: "That is not the same as being honest without armor."

The cedar branches moved above us.

I looked at the path ahead, then down at her.

Haru: "I was afraid."

Elira: "I know."

Haru: "Not of her."

Elira: "I know that too."

Haru: "Of how much easier it was when no one knew enough to ask."

Elira leaned her forehead against my shoulder.

Elira: "Secrets can be lonely even when they protect you."

I thought of the Moonlit Archive. Her face lit by black and silver. Her hand reaching for mine when anyone else would have stepped back.

Haru: "You made mine less lonely."

She looked up.

Elira: "Then let me keep doing it."

We stood there until her strength returned.

Back at the house, Elira insisted on tying the black ribbon around my wrist because my hair had failed the assignment. Her fingers were careful with the knot. The silver edge caught the light like a quiet ring.

Elira: "There."

Haru: "Am I improved?"

Elira studied me with theatrical severity.

Elira: "Moderately."

I touched the ribbon.

Haru: "Cruel."

Elira: "Accurate."

The evening check-in found us on the sitting room floor surrounded by ribbon, soup bowls, and the remains of spiced chocolate.

Aira looked through the mirror and went silent.

That was alarming.

Elira: "Aira?"

Aira: "Sorry. I was trying very hard not to say honeymoon again."

Leon groaned offscreen.

Cassian: "Growth."

Rhea's gaze moved to the ribbon on my wrist.

Rhea: "Public contact?"

Haru: "Lake Velis village. No relay disturbance. No hostile contact. One child asked directly about the Black Circle."

Rhea straightened.

Rhea: "And?"

Elira answered before I could.

Elira: "He gave his name first."

Professor Valen, behind Rhea, smiled faintly.

Professor Valen: "Good."

Not strategically good.

Humanly good.

The mirror dimmed after reports, medicine reminders, and one final Aira warning not to let Haru turn watchfulness into interior decorating.

Night settled.

Elira sat beside me on the sofa, legs tucked beneath her, head against my shoulder. The black ribbon on my wrist brushed the pendant at her throat when our hands met.

Black near black.

Not threat.

Not rank.

Choice.

Elira: "Today felt like a promise."

Haru: "Which part?"

Elira: "The market. The child. The ribbon. Coming back when I was tired instead of proving I could keep going."

She looked at me.

Elira: "All of it."

I turned my hand so the ribbon caught between our palms.

Haru: "Then we keep it."

Elira kissed the inside of my wrist, just above the knot.

The Black Circle went utterly still.

Not because it had vanished.

Because for once, power had nothing to add.

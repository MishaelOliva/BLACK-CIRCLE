# Episode 16: **The Quiet Place**

The first full day at Lake Velis began with Elira stealing my shirt.

That was not how she described it.

Elira: "This was folded on the wrong chair."

Haru: "It was folded on my chair."

She stood in the bedroom doorway wearing the dark shirt over her pale sleeping robe, sleeves falling past her wrists, hair loose and gold in the morning light.

Elira: "Then your chair made a generous decision."

I had faced Lucien's lightless pressure, old Foundation systems, Dominion Severance, public classification, and the Black Circle trying to become grief with a shape.

None of them prepared me for Elira Vale looking warm, tired, and pleased with herself in my shirt.

She noticed.

Of course she did.

Elira: "You are staring."

Haru: "Yes."

Her confidence faltered for half a breath.

Not because she disliked it.

Because being looked at after being medically examined, politically evaluated, and publicly mourned was complicated. I saw the moment she wondered whether my eyes were finding the wound before the woman.

So I gave her the truth with no useful parts removed.

Haru: "You look like you slept. You look annoyed that you needed to. You look beautiful. The shirt is not getting returned, is it?"

The uncertainty left her face.

Elira: "Absolutely not."

Breakfast was bread, fruit, soft cheese, and another note from Aira tucked inside the jam cupboard.

**If this note is found by Haru: make her eat something. If found by Elira: make him eat something. If found by both: I am excellent at friendship.**

Elira read it aloud with solemn dignity.

Then she ate two slices of bread because Aira's threat carried across distance.

Afterward, we walked to the lake.

Slowly.

Elira hated the slowly.

Her body betrayed it in small ways: a pause at the porch steps, a hand tightening on the railing, breath measured too carefully when the path sloped downward. She did not complain.

That made it worse.

At the third pause, I stopped beside her.

Haru: "You can be angry."

Elira looked at the lake instead of me.

Elira: "I am."

Haru: "At me?"

Elira: "Sometimes."

The answer landed cleanly.

I nodded.

Haru: "Good."

She turned then.

Elira: "Good?"

Haru: "If you are angry at me, you are not making yourself grateful for every breath."

Her eyes sharpened.

Then softened.

Elira: "That was annoyingly perceptive."

Haru: "Aira has been a bad influence."

Elira: "Aira would call it a good influence loudly enough to be heard from Aurelis."

We continued down the path.

At the waterline, an old wooden dock stretched over the lake. The boards had been repaired many times by practical hands. No ceremony. No crest marks. Just care repeated until it became structure.

Elira sat at the edge and removed her shoes.

I sat beside her.

For several minutes, we let our feet hover above the water like cowards.

Elira: "It is going to be cold."

Haru: "Probably."

Elira: "You first."

Haru: "A command from the Yellow Circle prodigy?"

Elira: "A request from your recovery-leave girlfriend."

I put my feet in the water.

Cold shot up my legs so fast my entire soul reconsidered loyalty.

Elira laughed.

Not almost.

Not halfway.

She laughed, clear and startled, one hand pressed to her chest as if the sound had surprised her too.

The Black Circle stirred in answer.

Not with danger.

With something almost like relief.

Elira lowered her feet into the lake.

Her laugh broke into a gasp.

Elira: "That is cruel."

Haru: "The lake?"

Elira: "You, for not warning me enough."

Haru: "I suffered visibly."

Elira: "You suffer quietly. It is one of your worst habits."

The water moved around our ankles. Sunlight scattered across the surface in broken gold. For once, gold did not look like a classification. It looked like morning.

Elira's shoulder touched mine.

Elira: "Tell me something ordinary."

Haru: "Ordinary?"

Elira: "Something you like. Not because it helps a plan. Not because it reveals ancient doctrine. Just because."

I thought too long.

She bumped my shoulder.

Elira: "That delay was tragic."

Haru: "I like rain on stone."

Elira's expression gentled.

Elira: "Why?"

Haru: "It makes large places sound less empty."

She absorbed that without turning it into pity.

Elira: "I like pear jam."

Haru: "Because?"

Elira: "Because when I was little, my mother insisted proper Vale breakfasts required restraint, and my father would put too much pear jam on toast when she looked away."

Haru: "A family rebellion."

Elira: "A sticky one."

We traded ordinary things until noon.

Rain on stone. Pear jam. The smell of old paper. The way Aira said "interesting" when she meant "this is a disaster." Leon pretending not to care about compliments. Cassian's habit of smiling before doing something risky. Professor Valen's reading glasses. Maelin's war against nonsense. Lake birds stepping like officials over the mud.

Each small thing made the world wider than catastrophe.

Then a bell rang from the house.

Elira went still.

The lake was gone from her eyes.

She was back under public relays, somewhere between her own choice and the route that had killed her.

I did not stand.

I wanted to.

Every part of me wanted to find the bell, crush the mechanism, silence every sound shaped like warning.

Instead I stayed beside her.

Haru: "Lake."

Her fingers dug into the dock.

Haru: "Wood under your hands. Cold water. My shoulder next to yours."

Elira's breath came too fast.

Elira: "It sounded like the marker bell."

Haru: "I know."

Elira: "I am here."

Haru: "Yes."

Elira: "I am not there."

Haru: "No."

She reached for my hand with a violence that was not aimed at me.

I gave it.

The Black Circle pressed behind my sternum, cold and old, tasting faintly of ozone and metal. Old systems recognized fear. They wanted to define it, contain it, make it useful.

I refused.

Fear did not need to become doctrine to be real.

Elira's breathing slowed.

When she finally looked at me, shame had entered after the panic.

I hated shame more than fear.

Elira: "I was fine."

Haru: "You were scared."

Elira: "I was laughing ten minutes ago."

Haru: "Both can be true."

Her eyes filled.

Elira: "I do not know how to be both."

That was the wound.

Not the flinch.

Not the bell.

The belief that surviving required a single clean shape.

I turned fully toward her on the dock.

Haru: "Then we practice."

Elira: "Practice?"

Haru: "Being both."

She stared at me.

So I did the only useful thing I could think of.

I picked up a lake bird feather from the dock, held it with grave seriousness, and placed it on my knee.

Haru: "This is official practice."

Elira blinked.

Haru: "You are allowed to be frightened and also judge this feather."

Her mouth twitched.

Elira: "That feather is unimpressive."

Haru: "Harsh."

Elira: "It lacks structure."

Haru: "It survived a lake bird."

Elira: "A low standard."

The laugh came back through tears.

It hurt to hear.

It healed nothing by itself.

But it made room.

That afternoon, the bell turned out to be a delivery charm announcing a basket from Aurelis. Rhea had sent documents sealed for later. Maelin had sent medicine. Aira had sent three more notes, each hidden in increasingly unreasonable places. Leon had sent a water-stabilizing charm with no message. Cassian had sent a red ribbon tied around a packet of spiced chocolate and a note that read:

**For morale. Deny everything.**

Elira held the chocolate like evidence.

Elira: "We are very loved."

Haru: "In several incompatible ways."

She leaned against the kitchen counter, wrapped in my shirt and her shawl, and suddenly looked close to tears again.

Elira: "I did not know being loved could be exhausting."

Haru: "I think it is because you keep trying to answer it."

Elira: "What should I do instead?"

I opened my mouth.

Then closed it.

Correct answers had hurt her yesterday.

So I chose a true one.

Haru: "Let some of it sit beside you."

She looked at the basket.

Then at me.

Elira: "Like you are learning to do?"

I accepted that.

Haru: "Like I am learning to do."

The basket stayed on the counter for an hour because neither of us wanted to decide what to do with kindness.

That sounded absurd.

It was also true.

Kindness had become complicated after the Wounded City. Too much of it arrived with records attached, signatures beneath statements, protection clauses, witness seals, medical forms, political consequences. Even the gentlest offerings carried the shape of everything that had happened.

A ribbon.

A charm.

Medicine.

Chocolate.

Documents sealed for later.

Love, organized by people who did not know how else to keep from losing us.

Elira touched Leon's water-stabilizing charm.

The charm was plain blue glass wrapped in a strip of cloth from an Aurelis training sleeve. Leon had tied the knot badly. Not carelessly. Badly in the specific way of someone who had tried very hard and gotten angry at string.

Elira: "He did not write a note."

Haru: "He would consider that excessive."

Elira turned the charm in her hand.

Elira: "He stayed with Cael when the field tried to make him into bait."

Haru: "Yes."

Elira: "And now he sends this with no words because words would admit he is worried."

Haru: "Also yes."

Her thumb moved over the knot.

Elira: "We should thank him."

Haru: "Aira will make him unbearable if she knows."

Elira's eyes warmed.

Elira: "Then we thank him privately."

The house, which had developed opinions about privacy, opened a drawer in the writing desk by half an inch.

I looked at it.

Haru: "Stop helping."

Elira laughed.

The drawer remained open.

Inside were blank cards, a fountain pen, and envelopes already marked with old restoration-house seals that meant nothing to the Council and apparently everything to the road wards.

Elira picked up a card.

Her hand trembled before the pen touched paper.

Not from fear this time.

From fatigue.

She scowled at the tremor as if it had insulted her family.

Haru: "I can write."

Elira: "I know."

She dipped the pen.

The first line came out slightly uneven.

**Leon, your charm is useful. Your knot is terrible. Thank you.**

She stared at it.

Then looked at me.

Elira: "Too much?"

Haru: "He will understand the insult as affection."

Elira: "Good."

She added:

**Do not tell Aira I said so.**

We both knew Aira would know within an hour of the envelope leaving the house.

That was not the point.

The point was that Elira had written a private thank-you without asking whether it would enter the public sequence. The point was her handwriting existed outside testimony. The point was Leon would probably stare at the card for too long and then pretend he had not.

We wrote to Aira next.

That took longer because Aira deserved gratitude and also needed management.

Elira began:

**Your packets are intrusive.**

Then paused.

Haru: "True."

Elira added:

**They helped.**

Her expression shifted.

Elira: "She will cry."

Haru: "Aira?"

Elira: "Privately. Then she will deny it with footnotes."

I imagined Aira constructing a defense of non-crying based on tactical eye moisture.

Haru: "Likely."

Elira wrote:

**You are excellent at friendship. This is not permission to become worse.**

We left space for me.

I did not know what to write.

Elira slid the card toward me without speaking.

The pen felt heavier than it should have.

Aira had been the first person at Aurelis to treat my silence as a thing with doors instead of walls. She had made maps around my omissions, jokes around fear, and a public record strong enough to keep the old systems from turning me into only a rank.

I wrote:

**Thank you for making room before you knew what needed room.**

Then I stopped.

The sentence looked exposed.

Elira read it and did not rescue me from it.

She only touched the edge of the card.

Elira: "Good."

We wrote to Rhea in language formal enough to survive being intercepted and human enough to make her sigh.

We wrote to Maelin to confirm medicine had been taken, food had been eaten, and neither of us had invented a moral crisis around the bedroom. Elira insisted on that line. I objected with dignity. She ignored me with more.

We did not open the Council documents.

The red folder sat on the desk like a sealed wound.

At one point I reached toward it.

Elira did not stop me.

That made stopping myself harder.

My fingers hovered over the seal.

I could feel the shape of the memoranda inside. Krail's language would be precise. Words like stability, proximity, extra-classification exposure, necessary review. No open cruelty. Nothing as honest as a blade. Just concern arranged until it could justify a locked door.

The Black Circle disliked locked doors.

So did I.

That was why I pulled my hand back.

Elira watched me.

Elira: "Why did you stop?"

Haru: "Because if I read it now, I will spend the afternoon answering a man who wants to make me react."

Elira's face softened.

Elira: "And what did you want to spend the afternoon doing?"

I looked at the basket.

At the half-written cards.

At the window where the lake kept being only a lake.

Haru: "Learning what people do with afternoons."

Elira set the pen down.

Elira: "That may be the most alarming thing you have ever said."

Haru: "I assumed there were procedures."

Elira: "There are not."

Haru: "None?"

Elira considered.

Elira: "Tea helps."

That was close enough to doctrine.

We made more tea.

Elira insisted on helping, which meant I had to learn the difference between support and interference for the fourth time that day. She carried the cups one at a time. Slowly. Angrily. Successfully.

When she set mine down without spilling, she gave me a look so triumphant that the Black Circle itself seemed wise enough not to comment.

Haru: "Impressive."

Elira: "Do not sound surprised."

Haru: "I am impressed by the cup."

Elira: "Cruel."

Haru: "Accurate."

She threw a folded napkin at me.

It hit my shoulder and fell to the floor with no tactical effect.

Elira: "Devastating."

Haru: "I may not recover."

Her smile faltered only a little before returning.

That little mattered.

Recovery was not a clean upward line. It was a smile that returned after stumbling. It was a cup carried one room. It was choosing not to open a red folder. It was answering love with two sentences and a bad joke.

The house accepted our letters at the front door.

There was a narrow brass slot beside the entry table. When Elira slid the envelopes through, the old silver in the wood brightened and the cards vanished into whatever route Professor Valen trusted more than council courier chains.

Elira kept one envelope back.

Plain.

Unsealed.

Iven Sahl's name written on it.

Haru: "Do you want to wait?"

Elira looked down at the card.

Elira: "No. I want to not send a perfect answer."

She opened it again.

Inside, she had written:

**Iven, you do not owe me apology for surviving Noctis. Cael's kindness belonged to him. What you do with the part of it he gave you can belong to you. If you ever want to tell the thermal charm story into the public record, Rhea will protect the route. If you do not, that choice is also yours.**

Below it, after a gap:

**Eat something warm.**

I looked at her.

Elira's cheeks colored.

Elira: "Aira has contaminated me."

Haru: "Good."

She sealed the letter before she could edit it into distance.

The house took that one more gently than the rest.

## Interlude: Elira's Unsent Answer

Haru went to rinse the cups because he had begun to understand that care sometimes needed the dignity of a task small enough not to become a vow.

Elira let him go.

That was harder than asking him to stay.

The restoration house settled around her in soft old creaks: cedar beams adjusting to lake wind, kettle cooling, ward lines humming beneath the floor. No council rail. No witness pool. No public record waiting to turn breath into precedent.

Only the brass mail slot beside the door.

It brightened again.

Elira looked toward the kitchen.

Haru had his back to her, sleeves rolled to his elbows, washing a cup with the focused care of a boy who could unmake a battlefield and still looked faintly uncertain about soap.

The slot opened.

A folded crystal sheet slid onto the entry table.

It bore no hostile flare. No Noctis seal. No Dominion hook.

That almost made it worse.

Clean Council stationery could do a great deal of harm while looking as if it had merely asked for clarification.

Elira picked it up.

**SUPPLEMENTAL WELFARE CONTEXT FORM**  
**RECOVERY PROXIMITY REVIEW**  
**OPTIONAL STUDENT RESPONSE**

Optional, in council language, often meant someone wanted refusal to look suspicious later.

She read the first question.

**Does Haru Aerlen's presence materially reduce your distress symptoms?**

Elira's fingers tightened around the page.

There it was. Not accusation yet. Not containment. A corridor built one polished tile at a time.

If she answered yes, someone could call him necessary.

If she answered no, someone could call him irrelevant.

If she refused, someone could call the refusal evidence of dependency hidden by romance, grief, or Black Circle influence.

She could almost hear Krail's voice inside the grammar, though his name appeared nowhere. The trap was too tidy to need a signature.

Haru turned from the sink.

Haru: "Elira?"

She folded the sheet once.

Elira: "A form."

He went still.

Not visibly, to most people.

To her, yes.

The pause behind his eyes. The instant calculation of threat routes, response costs, whether the correct kindness was to step closer or give her the page's full space.

He chose space.

Good.

Not because she wanted distance from him.

Because she wanted one answer that did not begin with his fear.

Elira: "I am going to read it first."

Haru set the cup down.

Haru: "All right."

He did not ask to see it.

That helped.

It did not solve the anger.

Elira took the sheet to the small writing desk by the western window. The lake beyond the glass looked too peaceful for bureaucracy. That seemed unfair to the lake.

The form had seven questions.

All gentle.

All wrong.

**Does proximity to the Black Circle bearer produce measurable channel stability?**

Elira crossed out proximity.

Wrote: **Chosen company.**

Then crossed out Black Circle bearer.

Wrote: **Haru Aerlen.**

The sentence became messier.

Better.

**Does romantic attachment alter your willingness to provide testimony?**

Elira stared at that one longer.

She thought of the Wounded City. Cael's wrist blackened by a return claim. Her own hand over his seal. Haru's face when she chose the route Dominion Severance turned into a blade.

Romantic attachment had not altered her willingness to testify.

It had made some people furious that her testimony belonged to her.

She wrote:

**Attachment does not reduce agency. It gives bad readers another excuse to doubt it.**

The pen trembled.

She hated that.

Then she decided trembling could stay.

Mira would have kept it.

Mira would have written a note beside the line: **HAND SHOOK HERE. STILL ANSWERED.**

Elira added it herself.

The page looked less elegant immediately.

Good.

Elegance had been trained into her as a survival tool until it could disguise pain as competence. Mira had taught her that ugly records sometimes protected people better because they proved a living hand had made them under pressure.

Question five asked whether Haru's continued presence might discourage independent recovery behaviors.

Elira laughed once.

It came out sharp.

Haru did not move from the kitchen.

Still giving space.

Still listening.

She wrote:

**Independent recovery behavior: answering this without asking him to rescue me from the insult.**

Then, below it:

**I choose his presence when I choose it. I choose solitude when I choose it. Your form currently treats both choices as symptoms unless they support your preferred conclusion. Correct the instrument before interpreting the patient.**

That was too Halden.

She left it.

Question seven was the worst.

**Would you consent to a private medical-context interview to distinguish recovery need from relational preference?**

Private.

Medical-context.

Distinguish.

The words put on clean gloves before reaching for her throat.

Elira did not cross anything out this time.

She wrote beneath the question in large, uneven script:

**No private interview. No separation framed as clarity. Recovery need and relational preference are not opposing categories. If you require testimony, ask in open witness conditions after healer clearance. If you require control, stop pretending this is care.**

The house hummed.

Not magic answering.

Old wood, maybe.

Or her own pulse returning to a shape she recognized.

She signed her name.

Not Lady Elira Vale.

Not Yellow Circle primary witness.

**Elira Vale. Student. Alive. Irritated.**

That last word was not proper.

She underlined it.

Then she made a copy by hand before sending the crystal sheet through the brass slot, because Aira had contaminated everyone and because Mira would ask.

When the house took the form, Elira sat very still.

She had expected relief.

Instead she felt tired and cleanly furious.

That was better than frightened.

In the kitchen, Haru said nothing.

Elira looked at his reflection in the window glass.

He was watching the cup in his hands as if it contained the discipline of every star.

She knew he wanted to ask whether she was all right.

She also knew he had learned enough not to make her answer before she chose the shape of it.

Elira folded the handwritten copy and placed it beside the outgoing letters.

Elira: "Krail, probably."

Haru's reflection changed.

Elira: "No."

He stopped before turning fully.

Elira: "That was my answer."

For one long second, the room held both of them without choosing a center.

Then Haru nodded.

Haru: "Will you show me when you want to?"

Elira's anger loosened by one thread.

Elira: "Yes."

She stood, slower than she wanted, and returned to the door.

The Iven letter was gone. The Council form was gone. The copy remained.

That mattered.

Some parts of survival had to leave the room.

Some had to stay where a person could point to them later and say:

**I was not carried through that part. I walked.**

Afterward, Elira stood by the door with her hand against the wood.

Elira: "Cael should be here."

There were many wrong answers.

The world had taught me most of them.

He is in the record.

He chose.

He saved you.

He is honored.

All true.

All insufficient.

Haru: "Yes."

Her hand curled into a fist.

Elira: "I am glad I am alive."

Haru: "Yes."

Elira: "And I hate that his death is in the room whenever I say it."

The old cold behind my sternum answered.

This time I did not let it become a solution.

I stood beside her.

Haru: "Then we make room for both."

Elira's eyes closed.

Elira: "Both again."

Haru: "Official practice."

She laughed once through the ache.

Elira: "The feather was unimpressive."

Haru: "It served the lesson."

Elira leaned her forehead against the door.

I waited.

Eventually she turned and rested against me instead.

I put my arms around her after she did.

Not before.

The house did not glow. The wards did not sing. No witness crystal recorded the moment.

Good.

Some things deserved to be real without becoming evidence.

Evening came blue and quiet.

We ate soup by the window. Elira fell asleep halfway through a sentence and woke angry about it. I did not laugh. That was wise. I did warm the soup again. That was wiser.

After dinner, she asked me to braid her hair.

I had never braided anything in my life.

She discovered this quickly.

Elira: "Haru."

Haru: "Yes?"

Elira: "Are you tying a knot?"

Haru: "No."

Elira: "That pause says yes."

I carefully freed the strand.

Elira sat on the rug between my knees, shoulders shaking with quiet laughter as I failed with scholarly dedication. The fire burned low. The lake reflected moonlight through the window. Her pendant rested against her chest, dark and watchful, but not cold.

When I finally made something that resembled a braid if judged kindly from a distance, Elira touched it.

Elira: "Terrible."

Haru: "I'll improve."

Elira leaned back against my knees.

Elira: "Good."

There was a softness in that word that made the room feel smaller.

Not trapped.

Held.

She tilted her head back to look at me.

Elira: "Kiss me?"

The question was gentle.

It still made the Black Circle go silent.

I bent down slowly.

Our first kiss in the Moonlit Archive had been secret and impossible, full of revelation and the frightening joy of being accepted.

This one was different.

No veil.

No vow spoken in shock.

No hidden rank between us.

Just Elira choosing, after death had touched her and politics had named her evidence, to ask for tenderness in a room by a lake.

I kissed her softly.

She reached up and held my face with both hands.

The braid fell apart.

Neither of us cared.

Later, when we lay beside each other under the same blanket, Elira's head rested against my shoulder.

Elira: "Today felt almost normal."

Haru: "Parts of it."

Elira: "I liked the parts."

Haru: "So did I."

The lake moved outside.

The world waited beyond the trees with hearings, memoranda, Noctis fractures, and questions no one had earned the right to ask.

But inside the quiet place, Elira's breathing evened.

This time I slept too.


---

Aira called at breakfast and ruined the word immediately.

The mirror brightened while Elira was spreading pear jam with the careful seriousness of a person committing family treason.

Aira's face appeared upside down.

Aira: "Good morning, medically sanctioned honeymoon."

Elira choked.

I spilled tea on my sleeve.

Leon, somewhere behind Aira, said, "You promised not to open with that."

Aira rotated the mirror image upright.

Aira: "I promised to consider not opening with that."

Rhea's voice cut in from out of frame.

Rhea: "This is an official welfare check."

Cassian: "With honeymoon oversight."

Rhea: "Veyr."

Cassian: "Withdrawn."

Elira covered her face with one hand.

Elira: "I regret having friends."

Aira: "No, you don't. You regret that your friends are accurate."

I recovered the cup before it dripped onto the floor.

Haru: "It is recovery leave."

Aira looked directly at me through the mirror.

Aira: "Haru, you are alone with your official girlfriend in a lake house, under orders to rest, eat, sleep near each other, and stop treating romance like an emergency protocol. I am not saying the Council invented a honeymoon. I am saying bureaucracy tripped and fell into one."

Elira's ears turned pink.

The sight was dangerous.

Not politically.

Personally.

Professor Valen appeared beside Rhea, holding a file.

Professor Valen: "Aira's terminology is not medically recognized."

Aira: "Emotionally recognized."

Professor Valen: "Unfortunately."

Rhea cleared her throat.

Rhea: "Actual update. Noctis has filed two objections to the recovery order. Both were rejected. Senna Voss has requested Elira's medical testimony be deferred until Maelin clears it. Councilor Krail is attempting to frame proximity as dependency."

Elira lowered her hand.

The blush faded.

Haru: "Of course he is."

Rhea's gaze shifted to Elira.

Rhea: "Your corrected welfare form reached my office before his summary did."

Aira leaned into view.

Aira: "For the record, 'Alive. Irritated.' has entered my permanent vocabulary."

Elira's chin lifted by a fraction.

Elira: "It was medically accurate."

Professor Valen: "It was politically useful because it made the instrument visible."

Rhea: "Exactly. Krail now has to argue against your conditions instead of pretending he only asked neutral questions."

I looked at Elira.

She did not look at me first.

She looked at the dimmed mirror edge, where her own reflection sat beside the Council seal without being absorbed by it.

Good.

Rhea: "He will find less traction if you give him no public instability today."

Aira: "Translation: please do not darken the sky unless someone earns it."

Haru: "I will try."

Leon leaned into view.

Leon: "Also, the water charm is holding?"

Elira smiled.

Elira: "Yes. Thank you."

Leon nodded too quickly and stepped back out of frame.

Cassian: "He practiced asking that normally."

Leon: "I did not."

Aira: "He did."

The mirror call ended with Rhea ordering us to answer the evening check-in and Aira loudly advising us to "do something emotionally irresponsible but medically moderate."

For a few seconds after the mirror dimmed, neither of us spoke.

Then Elira set down the jam knife.

Elira: "I want to go into town."

Every security map in my head unfolded at once.

Elira watched it happen.

Elira: "Small rebellion."

Haru: "The town is outside the perimeter."

Elira: "The first market street is inside the outer ward Professor Valen inspected."

Haru: "There will be people."

Elira: "That is usually what towns contain."

I looked at the window.

Lake Velis village sat half a mile down the road, a cluster of slate roofs, fishing boats, and shop awnings bright against the shore. Ordinary people lived there. Ordinary people now knew the Black Circle was not a myth, because the whole world knew. Ordinary people would look.

At me.

At her.

At the girl who had died and returned.

At the boy who had nearly become an ending.

Elira reached across the table and touched my wrist.

Elira: "I do not want our first days away from Aurelis to teach us that safety means hiding from every face."

I knew she was right.

I hated that she was right while still weak enough that climbing the stairs cost her breath.

Haru: "If you get tired, we leave."

Elira: "Yes."

Haru: "If the crowd presses, we leave."

Elira: "Yes."

Haru: "If any relay-"

Elira squeezed my wrist.

Elira: "Haru."

I stopped.

She smiled.

Elira: "Ask me what I want to buy."

The question felt absurd.

That was its power.

Haru: "What do you want to buy?"

Elira: "A ribbon."

Haru: "For your hair?"

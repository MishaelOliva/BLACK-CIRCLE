# Episode 24: **No Throne for the Black Circle**

By dawn, half the world had decided I wanted a crown.

The other half was deciding whether to fear the crown more than me.

Both halves were wrong in ways that could still get people killed.

Aurelis sealed the lower channel before sunrise. Not closed. Sealed. Rhea insisted on the distinction because closed things invited private keys. Sealed things required witness logs, rotations, and enough signatures to make ambition impatient.

The Regalia door remained behind seven layers of open-ring witness geometry, three Council seals, two faculty constraints, one healer objection written in Maelin's handwriting, and a Green Circle student note taped to the outside that read:

**No one enters alone. That includes impressive people.**

Mira had written it.

No one removed it.

I spent the night in the east observation room under voluntary stability review while the academy woke to the broadcast.

Voluntary was doing heroic work in that sentence.

Halden sat across from me with a record slate. Dren stood near the door. Valen slept badly in a chair for twenty minutes at a time and woke with new terrible theories. Senna's remote crystal remained open but dimmed for privacy unless triggered.

Elira slept on the couch with Maelin's coat over her.

Not because Maelin approved of sleeping in observation rooms.

Because Elira had looked at every available bed, then at me sitting under review, and said, "No."

Maelin had made a sound like a kettle considering violence and fetched the coat.

At first light, Elira woke before I did.

That unsettled me more than the public broadcast.

She was sitting up when my eyes opened, hair loose over one shoulder, pendant dark against her throat, gold eyes fixed on the window where students had already begun gathering in the courtyard below.

Haru: "How long?"

Elira: "Nine minutes."

Haru: "You should have woken me."

Elira: "You were sleeping."

Haru: "That is not a defense."

Elira looked at me.

Elira: "It is when I use it."

I had no answer to that.

Halden did not look up from his slate.

Halden: "Subject slept for approximately three hours and sixteen minutes. No involuntary manifestation observed."

Elira: "Subject?"

Halden stopped writing.

For one dangerous second, I thought he might defend the wording.

Instead he crossed it out.

Halden: "Haru slept for approximately three hours and sixteen minutes."

Elira smiled faintly.

Halden looked pained.

Progress had strange shapes.

The first riot began at breakfast.

Not a violent one.

Aurelis students were too frightened, too curious, and too well-supervised by Rhea's council for that.

It was a riot of questions.

By the time we reached the Grand Courtyard, the notice crystals had replayed the Regalia broadcast thirty-seven times. Someone had painted a black ring with a crown over the old tournament board. Someone else had crossed out the crown and written **WITNESSES FIRST** beneath it. Cassian: "For you, yes. For history, promising."

She was already writing.

At the fountain steps, Rhea stood with Senna's remote seal, Professor Valen, Professor Halden, Instructor Dren, Senior Healer Maelin, Soren Ashvale from Valemere, Mara Kest from Drakenshard, Nera, Darian, Aric, Iven, Sela, and the other rescued students.

The image mattered.

Not a lonely Black Circle.

Not a crown.

A crowd of people the old systems would rather simplify.

Rhea: "Denied."

Senna: "She does not. I do. Denied pending full Council session."

Krail's gaze moved to her.

Krail: "Magistra Voss, your repeated leniency toward an extra-classification threat is becoming indistinguishable from advocacy."

Senna: "Good. Perhaps accuracy is contagious."

Aira whispered, "I am absolutely naming the notebook Senna."

Rhea did not smile.

But she wanted to.

Krail turned to the courtyard.

His voice carried through every notice crystal.

Krail: "Students of Aurelis. You have seen the broadcast. The Black Circle has a throne. Whether Haru Aerlen desires it is secondary. Power of this scale creates gravity. Around him, institutions fracture. Students are endangered. Ancient doors open. Noctis radicalizes. Witnesses become followers. Followers become court. Court becomes crown."

The argument was elegant.

That made it dangerous.

Because fear could live in it without feeling foolish.

Some students looked at me and looked away.

I did not blame them.

If I had not been inside my own skin, I might have feared me too.

Elira stepped beside me.

Not in front.

Beside.

Krail saw.

Krail: "Miss Vale, your courage is not in dispute."

Elira: "You are right about one thing. Power creates gravity. That is why isolation is dangerous. If Haru stands alone, every fearful institution will imagine a throne because they understand power only as hierarchy. If he stands with witnesses, power has to answer to people."

**LET THE THRONE BE REFUSED BEFORE WITNESSES.**

Lucien's trap had arrived wearing ancient authority.

If I refused, perhaps the world would say restraint.

If I approached, perhaps it would say ambition.

If I destroyed it, perhaps it would say fear.

No successor.

No frost.

Haru: "No. I refuse tools that require someone to sit above everyone else."

Elira's hand found mine.

This time, I took it in front of everyone.

Haru: "Then we keep records."

The courtyard erupted.

Not in celebration.

In survival.

Students crying, shouting, reaching for each other, arguing already about what it meant. Council crystals flashing. Faculty issuing orders. Rhea threatening three people with suspension for climbing the fountain. Maelin dragging Elira to a chair. Aira writing like history had offended her personally.

And above the academy, for the first time since the Black Circle became public, the sky did not darken.

It opened.

Far beyond Aurelis, somewhere behind Noctis walls, Lucien Voss had lost a throne he wanted me to fear.

That would not make him retreat.

It would make him honest.

I looked at Elira.

She was exhausted, furious, alive, and smiling at me like refusal could be a kind of vow.

The next arc had already begun.

The first consequence of refusing a throne was administrative chaos.

No one wrote that into myths.

They should have.

Within ten minutes, Aurelis had thirty-seven emergency petitions, nine offers of alliance, six demands for Haru Aerlen's immediate removal from student housing, four devotional clubs trying to register under different names, two dueling challenges addressed to "the Crownless Black," and one anonymous fruit basket delivered to the Student Council office with a note reading:

You do not get to own the answer before the person speaks.

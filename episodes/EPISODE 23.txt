# Episode 23: **Green Circle Witnesses**

The witness slate returned at the instant Mira's fourth question broke through Lucien's route. Sela.

One name, spoken from inside a classroom built to replace names with functions.

I remained outside with Dren and Halden while Valen held the failing channel open. The Black Circle pressed against my ribs, offering entry. I held because Mira, Finn, Aira, Leon, Cassian, Nera, Darian, and Elira were already inside, and because making their work mine would have proved the hall's argument for it.

The false classroom split around Sela's recovered name. Desks, rank lights, and Lucien's hand vanished into white glare.

When the slate cleared, the lesson had changed. Now it was a training field under winter snow.

Iven sobbed from the cot.

Iven: "That is where they make first-years stand."

Maelin held a cup to his lips.

Maelin: "You are here. Not there."

Iven nodded like he wanted to believe it and did not yet know how.

Inside the hall, frost gathered around the witnesses' ankles.

The cold did not arrive like weather. It arrived like instruction.

The first instruction was stillness.

Every missing student in the snow field knew it before the witnesses did. Their shoulders locked. Their hands stayed flat at their sides. No one rubbed their arms or bent to cover bare feet. The snow climbed over toes and cuffs with patient cruelty, waiting for a child to shiver wrong.

It did not freeze everyone equally. Green students received deeper drifts. Blue students received quiet cues about who got what. Red students received heat they were forbidden to share without permission. Yellow students received swept paths and the shame of noticing them.

Elira stepped off her clean path.

The hall wrote **WASTED ADVANTAGE**. She looked straight at Sela.

Elira: "No. Shared ground."

The swept path vanished beneath her boots, leaving her ankle-deep in the same frost as Sela.

Sela stared at the vanished path before anyone could explain it.

Sela: "You can be cold too?"

Elira: "Yes."

Snow settled along the hem of Elira's sleeves as she kept her hands open and still.

Sela: "But you could choose not to."

Elira: "That is why choosing matters."

The lesson had no answer for that except more cold. Elira stayed in the snow, and Sela watched her choose it.

Aira's voice crackled through Valen's slate.

Aira: "Cold field confirmed. Students barefoot. No visible restraints. That means the restraint is the lesson."

Maelin, outside with us, looked like she wanted to break the jaw of whoever had designed the hall.

Maelin: "Ask before warming."

Leon answered from inside the storm.

Leon: "I hate that you are right."

Cassian: "I can raise the field temperature three degrees without touching them."

Elira: "Wait."

The word stopped him because he trusted her, not because she commanded heat.

Elira moved through the drifts toward Sela Mornet, slowly, empty hands visible, her gold aura banked low enough not to become a sun the hall could call hierarchy.

The other children watched her with the wary stillness of students who had learned that kindness from powerful people arrived with witnesses and left with debt. Sela's lips were blue. Elira knelt before her in the snow.

The whole field recoiled. Yellow Circle did not kneel to Green in Noctis lessons. Yellow evaluated, commanded, and corrected. It did not lower itself until its eyes were level with a freezing child who had just remembered her surname.

Elira: "Sela Mornet, do you want warmth?"

The question had been asked before the snow came. Now it had teeth.

Sela stared at Elira's face.

Lucien stood at the far edge of the field in a coat too clean for weather.

Lucien: "She wants release. Ask a cold child whether she wants warmth and call the answer consent. How humane."

Elira did not look at him.

Elira: "Sela, if you say yes, we will send warmth slowly. Even if you say yes, yes can stop anytime after it starts. If you say no, we will not. If you do not know, we wait. If the hall punishes any answer, we call that a trap and stop right there."

Sela's eyes shifted from Elira to Mira.

Mira stood in the Green line now. The hall had placed her there after the classroom collapsed, barefoot in the snow like the missing students, her notebook clutched against her ribs. Her toes were turning red against the ice.

Aira stood beside her, coat still on, deliberately not offering it yet. Mira's teeth chattered once before Aira turned to her.

Aira: "Mira Castellan, do you want my coat?"

Mira's eyes filled instantly, not from the coat, from being asked.

Mira: "Please."

Her numb fingers tightened around the spine of her notebook as Aira slipped the coat off and laid it across Mira's shoulders without ceremony.

The hall wrote above them:

**GREEN WITNESS ACCEPTS UNEARNED WARMTH.**

Finn's voice cut through the slate from inside.

Finn: "Objection. Warmth was asked for and given with no debt attached."

Aira, coatless and shivering now, pointed straight at the floating script.

Aira: "Also, unearned from whom? The weather?"

The lettering flickered. Cassian let out a strangled sound.

Cassian: "Do I laugh or attack?"

Leon: "Neither."

Cassian: "Terrible options."

Sela watched Aira begin to shiver because she had given her coat away. The hall leaned into the lesson: warmth creates debt, debt creates route, route creates ownership.

Mira caught the trap at the same instant I did. She reached up to pull the coat back off. Aira caught the collar.

Aira: "No. Ask."

Mira froze. Aira's voice softened.

Aira: "If you want to share it, ask me. Do not panic-return kindness because the room called it debt."

Mira swallowed hard.

Mira: "Do you want it back?"

Aira: "Not yet."

The hall hated *not yet* almost as much as *no*.

The snow deepened around their ankles. Elira remained kneeling before Sela.

Sela: "If I say yes, someone else gets colder."

Leon answered before Elira could, because the question was about sharing, and water knew how to carry without owning.

Leon: "Not if we build it correctly."

He crouched, palms hovering over the snow. Water-thread spread outward beneath the white crust, mapping temperature rather than claiming bodies. Cassian lowered one hand over Leon's shoulder, heat gathering in his palm without leaving it yet.

Leon: "We can warm the ground between you first. Not your skin. Not your crest. The ground. You step into it if you choose."

Cassian: "I can keep the heat low."

Sela looked suspicious. Suspicion was healthier than trained gratitude.

Sela: "What do you get?"

Cassian's half-smile disappeared.

Cassian: "Annoyed at Lucien."

Leon shut his eyes briefly.

Leon: "Not helpful."

Cassian answered again, with more care than I had ever heard from him.

Cassian: "Nothing. You do not owe me anything. The heat is not a contract."

The hall wrote above them:

**RED CIRCLE CHARITY ESTABLISHES FAVOR CLAIM.**

Cassian looked up at the letters.

Cassian: "I am going to burn that sentence later."

Elira: "Later."

Cassian: "I said later."

Leon sent the first thread of warmth into the snow between Sela's feet and Mira's line. Not touching. Waiting.

Sela stared at the faint rising steam. For a long time, no one moved. Then she shifted one bare foot half an inch.

The steam touched her toes. She flinched, expecting pain. None came.

The field shuddered, not from mana, but from a child learning that warmth could arrive without a hook.

Another student in the Blue line began to cry silently. Nera turned toward him.

Nera: "Name?"

He shook his head so hard his hair whipped across his face. Nera stopped at once.

Nera: "You do not have to answer."

His crying turned audible. Darian studied the boy's crest.

Darian: "Possible Tovin Holt. Not confirmed."

The boy's shoulders jerked harder at the surname. From inside the snow line, Finn called out for the slate.

Finn: "Possible match for Tovin Holt. Not confirmed yet."

The hall wrote across the sky:

**UNCONFIRMED STUDENTS RECEIVE NO RESOURCE ALLOCATION.**

Rhea's voice cut into the outside channel.

President Valmont: "Council objection. Emergency warmth does not wait on finished paperwork."

Senna's crystal pulsed beside Valen.

Magistra Voss: "Recorded."

Valen let out a tight breath.

Professor Valen: "And about time."

Leon extended the ground-warmth toward the Blue line.

The boy did not step. He looked straight at Nera. Not Elira. Nera.

Her composure cracked.

Nera: "Tovin Holt, if that is you, you do not have to forgive me to accept heat."

The boy sobbed once.

Tovin: "You watched."

Nera did not defend herself. She met his eyes without blinking, her voice quiet and bare.

Nera: "I did."

Tovin: "You looked away."

Nera: "Every time."

Darian's hand curled into a fist at his side. Nera kept going, because confession stopped being useful the moment it hid behind manners.

Nera: "I cannot make that smaller. I can tell the truth where I once covered it up. You do not owe me trust."

The warmth reached the edge of Tovin's bare feet. He did not move for it.

Sela looked across the snow at him, then stepped cleanly into her own warm patch. The movement was tiny. It gave him permission no adult in the room could offer. Tovin stepped too.

The snow field cracked from one side to the other.

Lucien's expression shifted for half a beat. Surprise. There was the crack Elira had warned me to preserve in him: the irritation of a strategist whose map left no room for prisoners standing together.

Lucien: "Children imitate available safety. That does not make them free."

Elira stood slowly. The yellow collar-light beneath her wool warmed gold, then steadied.

Elira: "No. It gives them the first shape freedom can borrow."

The field retaliated.

Every student dropped to one knee as snow rose like hands around their shins and dragged them down. Mira cried out as her knee struck ice. Aira dropped beside her at once, refusing to let the room isolate Mira on the ground.

Aira: "Kneeling pressure confirmed."

Her voice shook with cold.

Aira: "Not consent. Not reverence. Not gratitude."

Finn shouted toward the channel:

Finn: "Recorded!"

The hall deepened the downward pull. Outside, the Black Circle pressed hard against my ribs. I could open the floor. I could tear the snow field out by its roots and end every false lesson in a heartbeat.

Dren's hand hovered near my shoulder without touching. He did not need to.

Inside the slate, Elira turned toward the channel as if she could see me standing there.

Elira: "Haru."

Haru: "I am here."

Elira: "Do not take the field."

The words cut deep. They also kept me anchored.

Haru: "Understood."

Elira looked back down at the kneeling students.

Elira: "Sela, do you want help standing?"

Sela's teeth chattered as she forced her chin up.

Sela: "Please."

Elira did not lift her. She looked to Mira instead.

Mira understood and reached across from her own kneeling place. Sela caught her hand. Two Green Circle girls, one from Aurelis and one from Noctis, pulled together against a lesson built to keep them low.

They did not stand all the way, not yet. Still, the ice beneath their knees split.

The hall tried to drown the crack beneath another brass bell.

Iven screamed from the cot outside, not in panic, in warning.

Iven: "Next is the city!"

The field froze. Elira's head whipped toward the false horizon.

A broken skyline rose up through the snow. Wounded City towers, impossible and cruel. The hall was rebuilding catastrophe into another lesson.

The false Wounded City did not copy the real one faithfully, which made it worse. The towers stood too neat. The ruined streets drew the eye straight toward a single central wound, lit by theatrical ash. The hall had studied ruin and mistaken it for stagecraft.

Still, the body did not care that the copy was flawed. Elira stopped breathing for half a second, and I did too.

The witness slate in Valen's hands crackled as the interior team reacted. Aira swore. Leon's water threads snapped into hiss and steam. Cassian's heat flared bright enough to wash the edges of the projection white.

Maelin's fingers clamped around my wrist, hard.

Maelin: "Aerlen."

Dark circle pressure had reached my skin without me noticing. That frightened me more than the false skyline.

Haru: "I am here."

Maelin: "Then stay here."

Inside the hall, Elira turned away from the central street.

Lucien had placed the suggestion of her death there. Not the full scene; the hall knew better now than to expose private memory without cost. Instead it offered fragments: gold hair against stone, a dark pendant flickering too late, a reaching hand, Cael's outline spun from white return light. It wanted everyone inside to finish the picture themselves.

Sela let out a thin gasp. Mira stepped straight between the child and the ruined street.

Mira: "You do not have to look."

Sela: "But it happened."

Elira answered from the false pavement, her voice trembling and refusing to hide it.

Elira: "It did. And you do not owe the hall your eyes just because something is true."

That sentence went through me like a hand finding a wound and refusing to press.

Lucien's voice drifted down from the broken tower.

Lucien: "Truth avoided becomes weakness repeated."

Elira looked up at him.

Elira: "Truth forced becomes another weapon."

Aira, blunt and furious, shouted over the wind.

Aira: "Objection! This fake Wounded City is replaying real trauma without consent, getting the facts wrong, and rigging the scene to force a rescue!"

Finn's voice joined hers from inside the snow.

Finn: "Recorded."

The false city dimmed. Not gone, nothing left that easily, yet less certain of itself.

Aric appeared at the center of the street then, a Yellow crest burning at his wrist. Older than Sela, younger than Lucien, he stood with the exhausted stillness of a student trained to be useful long before he was allowed to be afraid.

Aric: "If I do not stay, it repeats."

There was the next hook. Not cold, not debt, responsibility wearing honor's face.

Elira stayed where she was. She had learned the hall's favorite conversions: approach could turn into pressure, comfort into claim, rescue into route.

Elira: "Who taught you that?"

Aric's mouth tightened.

Aric: "Everyone."

The plain answer left nothing easy to argue against. Noctis had taught it, Lucien had honed it, and old Aurelis had left enough machinery behind for the lesson to take root. Aric looked toward Sela.

Aric: "If the last witness leaves, the younger reserves stay trapped."

Nera let out a ragged breath. Darian spoke before she could sink under it.

Darian: "That is what they told older students so younger ones would accept being managed by someone already hurt."

Aric's eyes flashed.

Aric: "You think I do not know that?"

Darian went quiet. Aric's anger was not denial; it was the fury of someone who understood the entire trap and still could not find the door.

Mira gripped her notebook until the cover bent.

Mira: "What if the door does not need a final witness?"

Aric laughed once, sharp and dry, almost like Cassian if Cassian had grown up without sunlight.

Aric: "Every door needs a close."

Mira: "Books close. Doors open again."

The false city flickered. Aric stared at her while Mira flushed crimson, looking terrified of her own sentence.

Aira's voice crackled through Valen's slate.

Aira: "For the record, that was unexpectedly good."

Mira: "Please do not record it like that."

Finn: "Too late."

A layer of ash sloughed off the false towers; the hall hated being laughed at when it wanted tragedy.

Lucien stepped into the street beside Aric.

Lucien: "A door left open invites the same disaster back."

Elira: "A door that demands a child become its lock is not safety."

Aric's hands curled into fists at his sides.

Aric: "I am not a child."

Elira's expression softened.

Elira: "No. You are a student who was told adulthood meant being spent without complaint."

That hit him harder than pity ever could. He looked away.

Behind Mira, Sela spoke softly.

Sela: "You told me where to hide my hands in winter field."

Aric flinched.

Sela: "So the instructor would not see shaking."

The false city went still. Aric would not meet her eyes.

Aric: "That did not save you."

Sela: "It helped."

Aric's face twisted.

Sela: "Helping is not bad because it was not enough."

That nearly broke him. Behind my ribs, the Black Circle pulsed against old scars from Cael and the Wounded City, where none of us had been enough to stop every blade.

Elira looked straight toward the witness slate, toward where she knew I was watching.

Elira: "Haru."

Haru: "Here."

Elira: "Helping is not bad because it was not enough."

She spoke it for me, and for herself, so neither of us would let an unfinished rescue become proof that care was pointless.

The false Wounded City cracked down the central street. Underneath the broken paving, a thin line of dark water moved with patience the hall had not written.

Leon spotted it first and smiled like a man finding an old friend behind enemy lines.

Leon: "There is a lower channel."

Cassian: "Can you reach it?"

Leon: "Not alone."

Cassian held out one hand, no jokes left in him. Leon drove water down through the fissure while Cassian sent controlled heat after it, just enough to keep the current unfrozen. The false street tried to ice the crack shut and failed.

Elira turned back to Aric.

Elira: "If the door opens another way, do you want to leave?"

Aric's reply came too fast.

Aric: "I cannot."

Mira stepped forward, voice trembling.

Mira: "That was not the question."

He stared at her. The hall had trained him for usefulness, duty, and obedience. It had never trained him for *want*, because want belonged to people, not locks.

From inside the hall, Finn's voice cut into the channel with a roughness that had not been there before.

Finn: "Record risk statement."

Rhea looked up from the slate outside.

President Valmont: "State it."

Through the slate, I could see Finn's hands, ink-stained and red from frost, gripping his witness scrolls. Speaking where Noctis could hear cost a Green student far more than drama.

Finn: "If I continue objecting, Noctis can identify my handwriting from the public rail copies. They supply half the print orders at our parents' copy shop near the west gate, and my scholarship review is next month. I am not neutral."

The channel went dead quiet. Lucien's head turned a fraction.

He had heard. That was the threat institutions preferred because it left no heroic scar: a delayed permit, a pulled scholarship, a cold recommendation letter. Mira looked across the snow at Finn.

Mira: "You do not have to keep recording."

Finn let out one shaky laugh.

Finn: "You are standing in front of Lucien Voss with a notebook."

Mira: "That was not an argument."

Finn: "It was my answer."

Rhea's council seal flared bright beside Valen.

President Valmont: "Risk statement accepted. Protective copy initiated."

Aira chimed in without missing a beat.

Aira: "I have Finn's scholarship file reference."

Finn: "Why?"

Aira: "Because I am unpleasantly thorough."

President Valmont: "Solen."

Aira: "Protectively thorough."

The correction left Finn's risk real, yet visible enough that Noctis could no longer pretend a quiet retaliation was coincidence.

Mira's shoulders squared. Her face had gone pale again, yet she kept her notebook raised.

Mira: "Then record my risk too."

Elira turned toward her.

Mira: "Like Finn said—our parents' shop near the west gate prints academy forms, council notices, tournament programs, and Noctis supplemental packets when their delegation office is short. Putting both Castellan names on the record gives them every excuse to pull that work and say it was preference."

She swallowed hard.

Mira: "I am continuing."

Sela stared at her, learning in real time that help had a price and someone was naming it instead of pretending mercy was free. Aric stared too, watching two Green students accept ordinary ruin without asking to be called noble for it.

Lucien smiled faintly.

Lucien: "Public martyrdom. How efficient."

Mira flinched, then steadied.

Mira: "No. Martyrs are useful after they stop arguing. I am still arguing."

The moving hall shuddered. Outside, Halden leaned over the slate.

Professor Halden: "Excellent."

Instructor Dren: "Quietly."

Halden shut his mouth.

Inside, Aric looked from Mira to Finn to Sela, then down at his own hands.

Aric: "They can hurt you for this."

Finn tapped his stacked scroll sheets into neat alignment.

Finn: "Yeah."

Aric: "And you are still leaving when the door opens?"

Finn: "If I can."

Mira corrected him, voice still shaking.

Mira: "If you want."

Aric shut his eyes. The lower channel widened slightly beneath the street.

He did not answer right away, and Finn marked the silence out loud.

Finn: "Pause before answering. No refusal given. No consent given."

Lucien's jaw tightened. Finn pressed on anyway.

Finn: "Witness seems to be considering what he actually wants, separate from his assigned job."

Noctis rules hated waiting for a student's inner life, and the hall fought back at once. Snow crept up from the ground over Mira's bare ankles, then Finn's boots, then the hem of Aira's coat around Mira's shoulders, turning patience into physical sting.

Aira's teeth chattered.

Aira: "Log another round of forced cold."

Mira tried to lift her notebook and found her fingers stiff with cold. Sela noticed first.

For the first time since speaking her name, the child moved without being asked. She stepped close and placed both small hands under the bottom of Mira's notebook to hold it steady.

Mira looked down at her in shock.

Sela: "You write. I can hold."

Mira blinked hard, the rescue finally flowing both ways: Sela steadying the notebook, Aira's coat over Mira's shoulders, Finn risking his scholarship, Elira kneeling in frost, Leon and Cassian holding the warm channel open beneath the street, and Aric standing at the center being granted the right not to rush.

Outside, I gripped the edge of Valen's slate hard enough to bend the metal rim. Dren laid one hand over mine, steady and unmoving.

Instructor Dren: "They are not waiting because you cannot help."

Haru: "I know."

Instructor Dren: "Say the rest."

I hated that he was right.

Haru: "They are waiting because he deserves time."

The Black Circle eased a fraction beneath my ribs. Halden spoke almost under his breath.

Professor Halden: "That is the difference containment rules never understand."

Rhea heard him anyway.

President Valmont: "Then do not let it become theory before it becomes protection."

Halden closed his mouth.

Inside, Aric finally opened his eyes.

Aric: "If I leave, some part of me will still think I abandoned them."

Elira's voice carried cleanly across the snow, gentle without offering cheap comfort.

Elira: "It will."

Aric watched her.

Elira: "That thought may come with you. It does not get to decide for you."

Mira's voice shook as she looked up from her page.

Mira: "And if it keeps telling you that you only mattered while staying, we'll write down the truth every time until it stops."

Finn glanced up from his witness scrolls.

Finn: "I am already in trouble. Might as well stay consistent."

Sela gave a tiny nod, still holding the notebook.

Aric looked down at her. Sela did not plead. She refused to make herself another chain.

Aric's mouth opened twice before sound came out.

Aric: "I do not know."

Elira nodded once.

Elira: "Then we preserve 'I do not know.'"

Outside the slate, Rhea logged witness patience first, proving the rescue had waited for a person rather than a route. Mira wrote the line before cold could lock her fingers again, and Finn echoed it aloud so the channel held it.

Aric heard his uncertainty protected instead of punished. A delayed answer no longer counted as weakness or hesitation; it counted as time owed to someone who had been hurried toward usefulness for years.

He let out a short, startled laugh. This time it did not sound like Cassian without sunlight. It sounded like a boy surprised by air.

Aric: "I want to leave."

Rhea marked the second line beside the first as exit consent.

Beneath the false street, the lower channel shifted, not open all the way, waiting for a bridge that did not belong to me.

Outside, the Black Circle pressed downward, asking Aurelis for a way under the lie. I did not pour myself into it. I asked, through restraint and every lesson Cael, Elira, and the Wounded City had carved into me.

*May I help without becoming the route?*

The pressure behind my sternum answered in cold and ozone. Not permission. A condition.

*Only if they remain closer to him than you are to the answer.*

I understood.

Haru: "Elira."

Through the slate, she lifted her head.

Haru: "The lower channel can open if Aric's closest answer is not me."

Comprehension flashed across Elira's face. She looked around at Sela, Mira, Tovin, Nera, Darian, Aira, Leon, Cassian, and Iven's voice over the channel.

Elira: "Then we do not make Haru the bridge."

Lucien's eyes narrowed. He had prepared for me to refuse or overwhelm; he had not expected sharing the lead to become magic.

The channel answered from beneath the broken street. Water rose through the cracked stones in seven narrow lines, one for each witness place the hall had tried to turn into rank.

Leon anchored one. Cassian warmed the next. Mira named the third, while Sela touched the fourth with two trembling fingers. Nera and Darian held the fifth together without either claiming it. Aira pinned an objection slip into the wet stone along the sixth. Elira held the seventh simply by standing near Aric and not reaching for him.

None of the seven lines touched me. The Black Circle remained outside under the flagstones, unseated from the center.

Aric stared at the glowing water lines.

Aric: "If I step on one, does it close?"

Elira: "No."

Mira: "It carries."

Sela's thin voice followed.

Sela: "And if you step off, it is still there."

Aric's face tightened as though mercy hurt more than finality. Lucien saw it and lunged too late; for once, another answer reached the boy before his doctrine did.

Aric stepped onto the nearest water line.

Nothing closed. Nothing crowned him. Nothing demanded a final word.

He stared down at his own boot as if the world had made a mistake. Warmth rose along the line through the broken street, not enough to save everyone at once, enough to prove movement did not have to mean sacrifice. Aric's breath hitched.

Aric: "I can step off?"

Sela answered this time.

Sela: "Yes can stop."

The line held.

Above the false Wounded City, the painted sky tore away to show black glass behind it. The lower channel opened with a grind of wet stone breaking free. Water lines beneath Aric's feet tugged backward, hard enough that Sela dropped to one knee and slapped both palms flat against the stones.

Sela: "Hold!"

Not to Aric. To the hall.

For one heartbeat, the child held back a room built to spend her. The witness slate flickered and dimmed, showing only my own pale reflection in the dark glass before Elira's voice cut through again.

Elira: "We have him for now."

For now was not victory, yet it was enough to move.

Lucien answered Aric's step with force. The false city folded inward along its white lesson-lines. Leon's water routes snapped toward the exit while Cassian drove Red fire left-handed straight into the rank seal rationing warmth beneath the street, slamming his splinted right forearm across the recoil to keep from falling backward.

Cassian burned the color gates out. The backlash tore through the padded mitt pulled over his frost-bandaged right wrist and blistered his right palm raw, yet heat surged through every water line at once. Leon caught it and sent a moving path straight beneath the rescued students.

Cassian: "Now!"

Mira called names. Finn echoed them toward the outside slate. Aira held the record channel open while Nera and Darian shepherded Sela, Tovin, and the younger students onto Leon's moving path. Sela crossed first, clutching her own name like a coal against the cold.

Aric took one step toward them. Lucien closed his hand.

Black glass slammed down between Aric and the exit. The rescued children spilled into the outer court with Nera and Darian behind them; Elira, Mira, Aira, Finn, Leon, and Cassian remained trapped inside when the threshold sealed shut again.

Cassian slammed his uninjured left fist against the barrier, his frost-wrapped right palm already bleeding inside torn strip cloth. It did not budge.

Inside the fading channel, Aric stood beside Lucien and stared down at the single water line still shining beneath his boot.

Aric: "It stayed."

Lucien erased the street around him.

Outside, the returned children screamed. Beneath my boots, Aurelis asked the Black Circle whether I wanted a way under the lie.

## Interlude: The Page Outside

Under shelter exemption, Rhea had assigned three Green student aides and two medical runners to staff the outer copy rail while the rest of the campus stayed locked inside their halls. The Green first-year at the outer copy table had been told to copy whatever came off the slate. No one had explained what to do when the slate went black.

One moment, Mira's handwriting moved across the relay page in hurried gray strokes. The next, the page tore across with a vertical smear as the threshold sealed with Mira, Aira, Finn, Elira, Leon, Cassian, and Aric still inside.

The first-year waited for an adult instruction. Around him, adults barked several at once: seal the record, clear the table, move the rescued children, block the view of the Black Circle ring, lock down the evidence. None of them answered the page.

He stared at the last complete lines Mira had sent:

**ARIC STATES: I WANT TO LEAVE.**

**PAUSE BEFORE STATEMENT PRESERVED.**

**NO FINAL WITNESS CONSENT.**

Below them, the ink had dragged sideways when the hall snapped shut. Mira's final word might have been *carry*. It might have been *careful*. It might even have been *Cael*, though Cael was not in the room.

The first-year did not clean it up. He copied the smear exactly.

At the center of the outer court, the flagstones dark-ringed into a shallow, humming seam around Haru Aerlen's boots. Everyone forgot the table.

Stone sank without falling. Darkness pressed upward without becoming light. Haru Aerlen stood at the rim with both hands visible while Instructor Dren and Professor Halden forced him to name what he intended before the old power could make intention irrelevant.

The first-year's pencil tip snapped through the paper. He grabbed another and kept going.

**BLACK OPENING FORMED AFTER THRESHOLD CLOSED.**

His hand shook too badly for the next line.

A council aide reached across the table, while two more clerks hurried up behind him with a privacy screen to wall off the official record rail.

Council Aide: "That record is now restricted."

The first-year covered the page with both arms. He had no clever answer. Mira would have found one after three frightened breaths; Aira would have made restriction sound embarrassed to exist; Rhea could have stopped the hand with a seal. He had only his body and a Green Circle that could not push anyone back.

First-Year: "Mira said copies remain distributed."

Council Aide: "Mira is inside a hostile site."

First-Year: "She is."

The words came out smaller than he wanted, yet he kept his weight pinned over the paper.

First-Year: "That is why her copy stays outside."

The aide's expression turned cold and calculating. A second clerk demanded witness names while the third reached for the table frame to haul it behind the privacy screen. No one needed to burn a page if they could move it somewhere harmless.

The first-year tore the copy straight down the middle.

The aide lunged for both halves. The boy let him take the top. The bottom half vanished smoothly into the sleeve of the Green student standing beside him.

No signal passed between them. They had spent days copying Mira's rule: one page can be taken; disagreement must survive elsewhere. The second student stepped back behind Maelin's medical screen toward the two duty runners before anyone decided moving was suspicious, leaving the first-year seated with the official half-copy flattened under his palms.

The hidden half of the page was already moving along the sheltered runner chain when his pocket message-slate warmed against his thigh.

He ignored it. The stone pulsed twice more with the private three-tap rhythm his mother used only for family emergencies. Over the screaming children and grinding stone beneath Haru's feet, no one else could hear the faint chime. That made it harder to refuse.

He angled the palm-sized relay stone under the table edge. No mirror face formed—only chalk-pale runes etched across the slate surface, routed through the west district copy guild where she worked nights repairing municipal forms.

**COME AWAY FROM THE COURT. NOW.**

A second line burned in before he could trace a reply.

**GUILD RECEIVED NOCTIS NOTICE. YOUR SCHOLARSHIP SPONSOR ASKED WHETHER YOU ARE PARTICIPATING IN UNAUTHORIZED RECORD ACTIVITY.**

The outer court went colder than the Black Circle ring.

His scholarship paid for dormitory housing, meal credits, and the medicine his younger brother's lungs needed every winter. Noctis did not have to threaten any of them openly; it only had to ask a sponsor one polite question and let fear do the rest.

The first-year stared at the official half-page under his hands, then toward the court exit.

He could walk out right now. The aide had the top half; the bottom half was already moving along the exempt runner line. No one could say he destroyed evidence, and his mother would stop seeing his name attached to words like *unauthorized* and *Black Circle*. He had already stayed long enough to tell himself a clean story later.

He started to push his chair back.

Across the court, Sela looked straight at him.

She knew nothing about his scholarship, his mother, or his brother. She saw only the person at the copy table getting up to leave while the black ring opened in the stone. Her face settled into the guarded blankness Noctis students wore when adults disappeared after promising to stay.

The first-year sat back down, not because Sela owned his choice, but because her eyes showed him the one cost he had left off his list.

He tapped his bone stylus against the margin beneath the table, etching small runes onto the slate.

**I AM SAFE. I AM COPYING. DO NOT ANSWER SPONSOR YET. SAVE SLIP.**

His mother's answer etched through at once.

**I AM AFRAID.**

No order this time. He etched back:

**ME TOO.**

The relay went still. Nothing was safer because he named it, yet he kept his hands on the table.

Then Krail's remote seal found him.

Red light bloomed above the official half-copy.

**EXTRA-CLASSIFICATION IMAGE CONTROL**

The letters descended toward the paper like a blade of policy. Every line describing the black opening began fading first.

The first-year snatched the ink bottle. The council aide caught his wrist.

Council Aide: "Do not alter restricted evidence."

First-Year: "It is being altered right now."

The aide looked down at the vanishing letters and hesitated. That was enough.

The boy poured ink across his own sleeve instead of the paper, soaking his uniform cloth in a black flood, then pressed his wet forearm straight onto the fading lines. Reversed mirror-writing lifted onto the fabric, smeared and ugly.

Krail's red seal stripped the Black Opening lines off the paper, leaving the upper sheet scarred and blank where they had been, yet it stopped short of his arm. Erasing his sleeve meant claiming a student's body as evidence, and Krail's order didn't stretch that far.

The council aide stared at the backwards letters running from the first-year's elbow to his wrist.

Council Aide: "That is not an admissible copy."

First-Year: "Then it cannot violate admissible-image control."

The answer surprised both of them. Aira would have sharpened it; Mira would have said it shaking. The boy simply held his dripping sleeve away from his ribs so ink would not stain the flagstones.

Two other exempted Green aides and a Blue duty runner beside Maelin's screen saw what he had done and closed in. One pressed scrap paper to his sleeve and lifted a reversed fragment. Another copied that onto a healer-supply wrapper, while the Blue duty runner copied it forward inside a clinic clipboard.

In less than a minute, the record crossed four surfaces: uniform, scrap, wrapper, clipboard. Each jump lost a timestamp or half a word; each kept what Krail's seal had tried to erase: *Haru named a door. Sela shouted hold. No entry yet.*

The red seal widened, hunting a central copy to suppress, and swung toward the students.

Rhea caught it mid-air. Her council seal slammed into Krail's from the side hard enough to scatter red sparks across the court.

President Valmont: "My emergency powers cover student protection and immediate hazards. A remote seal trying to classify ink on a student's arm counts as both."

Krail's authority recoiled. Rhea did not shatter it; she pinned it above the half-erased official sheet so the attempted wipe stayed visible to everyone in the court.

She glanced down at the ink dripping from the boy's sleeve onto the table wood.

President Valmont: "Preserve the uniform."

First-Year: "I only have two."

Rhea considered the objection more seriously than she had Krail's seal.

President Valmont: "Then preserve it until the ink is transcribed, and have Student Council pay for cleaning. No custody claim."

The boy looked down at the reversed words along his forearm.

First-Year: "Can I wear it until Mira comes back?"

Rhea's jaw tightened.

President Valmont: "Yes."

Her emergency seal settled flat onto the dark wood. Not *if*. *Until*.

The black ring in the center of the court widened by a breath.

Rhea shouted for distance. Maelin hurried the rescued children behind a medical screen. Because Cassian was still trapped inside, nobody made the joke anyone expected when an overturned heat lamp rolled across the stones and struck the leg of the copy table.

Flame licked up the dry corner of the half-erased official page. The council aide jumped back.

The first-year slapped the fire flat with his bare hand. Pain hit before courage did. He shouted, kicked the lamp away, and ground the burning corner beneath his palm until scorched paper smelled of blistered skin. The surviving sheet held, missing a charred black crescent along one edge.

Maelin spotted the burn from across the court and crossed to him in three strides.

Maelin: "Do not move."

First-Year: "The table—"

Maelin: "Can be replaced. Your hand cannot."

He stared past her at the black opening, at Haru refusing to force entry, at the closed threshold holding their friends inside, and at the scorched sheet pinned under trembling fingers.

First-Year: "Mira would say the page matters."

Maelin poured cool blue healing film across his blistered palm.

Maelin: "Mira would say you are part of what the page is for."

He did not know whether that was true, only that he wanted it to be.

Rhea looked down at the blue film wrapping his hand.

President Valmont: "Name for the injury record?"

He almost gave his student number. Numbers felt safer when authority was taking notes. Then he saw Sela watching from beyond the table.

First-Year: "Benn Arlow."

Rhea wrote it down herself.

President Valmont: "Benn Arlow. Green Circle outer-copy witness. Hand burned preserving a page during active hazard. Scholarship pressure reported through family guild channel."

Benn Arlow looked up sharply.

Benn Arlow: "How did you know about the message?"

President Valmont: "Your relay was open when Krail's seal clipped the table—I caught enough harmonic leakage to protect the record, not enough to claim the private runes."

She left the message contents off the public sheet. Benn had not realized authority could admit where its reach stopped.

The table legs rattled as the black ring pulsed again. Benn tried to flatten the remaining copy stack with his bandaged hand and hissed when the blister pulled. The Green student aide beside him took half the pile.

Benn Arlow: "I can keep them."

Green Student: "You can keep half."

No speech about sacrifice, just half. Benn let go of the bundles one by one until his palm stopped bleeding beneath the film. The other aide lashed the loose pages to the table leg with spare linen stripped from a healer supply roll.

When the stones shuddered again, Benn wrote with his uninjured hand:

**COPY WORK CONTINUED BY TWO PEOPLE AFTER INJURY.**

Beneath it, he signed his name instead of a student number.

His pocket message-slate warmed once more.

**SAW YOUR SLIP. BROTHER HAS MEDICINE THROUGH MONTH-END. I AM STILL AFRAID. COME HOME WHEN THE SCHOOL RELEASES YOU, NOT WHEN A SPONSOR DOES.**

Benn read the runes twice, then tucked the slate away without copying a word onto the public sheet. Family fear did not turn into public property just because it sat beside a witness.

Instead, below his signature, he wrote:

**PERSONAL COST EXISTS OUTSIDE VISIBLE INJURY. DETAILS WITHHELD BY CHOICE.**

Rhea saw the line and shifted her seal around it, leaving the withheld margin untouched. Benn kept copying with his good hand until the aide beside him pointed out blood seeping through his bandage. This time, he stopped on the first warning.

Five minutes after it had vanished behind the medical screen, the hidden bottom half of the page returned along the chain of the four other sheltered aides and runners plus Maelin's triage clerk. Every person who carried it had added one observation along a different margin.

**Sela shouted hold.**

**Haru stopped widening the ring.**

**Dren asked for action name.**

**No entry yet.**

**Children moved by healer request, not Council custody.**

**Threshold still closed.**

Benn laid the returned bottom half against the scorched, half-erased top sheet and joined the tear with a strip of healer tape. The sentence across the split no longer lined up. One word was gone, and the charred crescent cut straight through the timestamp.

He made no attempt to neaten the damage. Underneath the tape, he wrote:

**RECORD BROKE WHILE PEOPLE WERE STILL INSIDE. PRESERVE BREAK.**

The black ring beneath Haru's boots answered with a pulse strong enough to rattle every ink bottle on the table.

Benn caught one before it could spill.

Across the court, Haru Aerlen lowered his gaze to the stone, asked Aurelis for a door without seizing the ring, and then the open floor gave way into the lower channel.

# Episode 4: **The Shape of a Team**

The recovery zone flared white beneath our feet.

The relic core in my hands dissolved into particles of blue light, marking a clean extraction. Around the edge of the simulation field, the crystal pylons dimmed one by one. The storm-dark sky above the arena peeled away into clear runic glass. Trees, ruins, mist, and broken terrain all shimmered and collapsed back into raw, orderly mana.

Reality returned in layers, and with it came silence: the weight of being scored, watched, and judged.

The faculty had seen everything. So had the assistant judges taking notes from the outer ring, and the other candidate teams gathered in the observation gallery above the field.

Aurelis had wanted to test whether our group would fracture. Instead, we had succeeded: messily at times, unevenly, reluctantly, and undeniably.

Cassian rolled his shoulders and let out a satisfied breath.

Cassian: "Well. That was fun."

Aira bent over with both hands on her knees.

Aira: "Your definition of fun requires review."

Leon wiped sweat from his jaw and exhaled slowly, trying very hard to look as though nothing about the exercise had surprised him.

Elira stood straight, composed as ever, though the flush in her cheeks and the slight rise and fall of her breathing made the effort she'd spent visible to anyone paying attention.

I was paying attention. That had become a problem of its own.

Master Thorn stepped forward first, glancing at the timing crystal.

Master Thorn: "Fast recovery. Faster adjustment than expected."

Lady Seraphine looked over her notes.

Lady Seraphine: "Communication tightened after the halfway mark. Early rank friction hurt you. By the end, practical sense beat pride."

Her gaze shifted, very briefly, toward Leon and Cassian. Appropriate.

Cassian grinned openly.

Cassian: "Almost complimentary."

Lady Seraphine: "Do not get used to that either."

Cassian looked delighted, which I suspected was not the intended effect.

Professor Halden approached the edge of the field with his hands clasped behind his back.

Professor Halden: "Interesting."

That was all he said at first. Somehow, from him, it sounded less like praise and more like a blade being tested for sharpness.

His eyes moved across the team: pausing on Elira with expected approval, on Aira with increased interest, on Cassian with cautious doubt, and on Leon with cool disappointment.

Then on me.

Professor Halden: "Mr. Aerlen."

Haru: "Professor."

Professor Halden: "Your eye for patterns keeps outrunning your visible profile."

There were many possible answers to that, most of them bad.

Haru: "The team made the timing work."

Professor Halden's expression did not change.

Professor Halden: "Clearly."

He turned away before the exchange could deepen, which was almost worse than if he had pressed.

Aira leaned just slightly toward me.

Aira: "I'm beginning to dislike the way he says simple words."

Haru: "That seems healthy."

Elira glanced at me briefly, just long enough for the warning inside it to land. Be careful.

I knew. The problem was that caution became harder every time someone smart watched too closely.

---

The official score projections were posted by evening.

Aurelis did not believe in suspense when it could create more useful forms of stress.

Students crowded around the central announcement wall in the main academic court, reading the updated rankings with the focus of people trying not to visibly panic.

Large floating panels displayed category scores, team-fit ratings, and early picks for tournament roles.

Aira stood beside me with her arms crossed and the expression of someone preparing to disagree with existence.

Aira: "If I've somehow become a top support candidate, I'm going to need time to resent my own competence."

Haru: "That seems manageable."

Aira: "For you, perhaps."

The panel shifted. Our group's data appeared.

**GROUP THREE - PROVISIONAL TEAM METRIC**  
**Extraction Success: High**  
**Adaptation Rating: Exceptional**  
**Conflict Stabilization: Moderate to High**  
**Strategic Versatility: Outstanding**

Below that, individual performance notes began to scroll.

**Elira Vale - Principal Candidate: Multi-Category Core Representative**  
**Cassian Veyr - Principal Candidate: Heavy Combat / Breaker Role**  
**Aira Solen - Principal Candidate: Tactical Support / Control Systems**  
**Leon Varek - Secondary Principal Candidate: Midline Control / Fluid Modulation**  
**Haru Aerlen - Principal Candidate: Adaptive Field Reading / Duel Reserve / Tactical Flex**

Aira read the last line twice, then again.

Aira: "They gave you three roles."

Haru: "That seems inefficient."

Aira turned to stare at me.

Aira: "No, Haru, it sounds alarming."

Students around us had started noticing too. Blue Circle candidates a few steps away exchanged glances. One Red Circle fourth-year let out a short laugh of disbelief.

Student: "A Green got principal status?"

And just like that, the tension around my existence at Aurelis sharpened again. It was one thing to be odd; it was another to have the academy back it in writing.

Elira arrived through the crowd a moment later, moving with her usual calm gravity. She looked at the panel once, then at me.

Elira: "You expected this?"

Haru: "No."

Aira: "I did. Unfortunately."

Elira's mouth curved slightly.

Elira: "Then congratulations seem appropriate."

Aira pointed between us.

Aira: "Don't say it like that. He'll accept it too calmly."

Haru: "Thank you."

Aira closed her eyes.

Aira: "There. Exactly."

From across the court, Cassian Veyr raised a hand in easy greeting when he saw us. The movement drew no small amount of attention. He made his way through the other candidates without seeming to care which social rules he was stepping on.

Cassian: "Well, that settles it."

Aira: "What does it settle?"

Cassian stopped beside us and glanced at the panel.

Cassian: "That we're the most interesting team candidate by far."

Aira: "That is not the reassuring phrase you think it is."

Cassian grinned.

Cassian: "You're the support specialist, Solen. You exist to reassure."

Aira: "I exist to survive the school's decisions."

Cassian's attention shifted to me.

Cassian: "You're causing a social crisis, Green."

Haru: "Apparently."

Cassian: "Do you enjoy that?"

Haru: "Not particularly."

Cassian looked almost disappointed.

Cassian: "That's a shame. You're very good at it."

Elira, beside me, seemed faintly amused.

Elira: "That may be because he never tries."

Cassian laughed once.

Cassian: "That makes it worse. Excellent."

Before the conversation could become stranger, another presence entered the edge of the crowd. Leon.

The murmurs around the announcement wall shifted at once, not because people feared him, but because they were waiting to see whether he would react badly.

To his credit, he did not. At least not publicly.

He stopped near the panel, read the rankings, and looked at his own line with a controlled expression. Secondary Principal Candidate was still a strong spot, especially after how badly he'd lost face lately.

Then his gaze moved to mine. Measured. Careful.

Leon: "So they really are building around utility."

Cassian glanced at him.

Cassian: "Is that your polite version of surprise?"

Leon ignored him.

Leon: "I admit, Aerlen, I expected the faculty to be less eager to reward something that looks this strange on paper."

Aira stared.

Aira: "That may be the most elegant insult I've heard all week."

Haru: "I'm learning to appreciate creativity."

Leon's eyes narrowed a fraction, though the reaction was smaller than it would have been before. Good. He was adapting. Which meant he might eventually become useful, or more dangerous. Often those were the same thing.

Leon looked at the team metrics again.

Leon: "If Group Three remains together, we will need better coordination than what we had in the simulation."

Cassian: "That sounded almost like teamwork."

Leon: "Take a moment to recover from the shock."

Cassian laughed. Aira blinked.

Aira: "Are we improving or becoming more sarcastic?"

Haru: "Both."

Elira: "That can still be improvement."

For a brief second, all five of us stood there under the floating rankings, surrounded by staring students and fresh rumor energy, and I could almost see the shape of the thing Aurelis was trying to build.

Neither harmony nor friendship. Capability.

A team made of unstable parts that might, under enough pressure, become stronger than neater pairings. A dangerous theory, but not an impossible one.

The theory became harder to ignore when we were ordered into a closed debrief instead of released to dinner.

The room assigned to us was one of the smaller strategy classrooms beneath the east wing, all black glass tables, wall-mounted maps, and chairs designed by someone who believed comfort weakened discipline. A crystal recorder floated above the center table, its light glowing a steady blue.

Aira sat down and immediately pointed at it.

Aira: "I want that thing to know I am participating under protest."

The recorder pulsed once, dutifully.

Cassian dropped into the chair opposite her.

Cassian: "It likes you."

Aira: "That is the tragedy of my life."

Leon remained standing for a moment, eyes on the replay slate already forming above the table. The simulation had been rendered into clean blue lines: our route, our mistakes, our corrections, the rescue node, the final extraction.

There was no cheering or rumor. Just proof.

Elira took the seat beside me. She did not reach for my hand here; too many crystals, too many possible logs. But her knee touched mine lightly under the table, a quiet point of contact no recorder would understand.

Lady Seraphine's voice entered through the recorder.

Lady Seraphine: "Group Three will review three failures and three strengths. Start without our help. We will judge the discussion afterward."

The light dimmed.

Aira stared at the crystal.

Aira: "They are making us evaluate ourselves."

Leon: "That is standard."

Aira: "No. Standard is an instructor telling us we were disappointing in specific ways. This requires emotional effort."

Cassian leaned forward, suddenly more serious.

Cassian: "First failure. I wanted to break the barrier lock."

Aira blinked.

Aira: "That was... direct."

Cassian: "I am capable of learning when insulted by architecture."

Leon: "You also nearly overcommitted on the rescue node."

Cassian's smile sharpened.

Cassian: "Nearly."

Leon: "Nearly still matters."

For one heartbeat, the old version of Leon showed through: proud, exacting, ready to turn a correction into a rank contest.

Then he looked at the replay of himself insisting the false student was only a decoy. His expression tightened.

Leon: "Second failure. I cared more about the goal than the choice behind it."

The room went quieter. No one made a joke.

That was how I knew the sentence had cost him something.

Cassian leaned back, firelight low at his wrist.

Cassian: "We can't save everyone in a real fight."

He said it lightly. Too lightly: the kind of lightness people used when a sentence had already cut them once and they had decided jokes were cheaper than bleeding in public.

Elira noticed. So did Aira. For once, even Leon did not correct him immediately.

Cassian's thumb moved across the old signet ring on his hand. Red Circle heir jewelry was usually decorative arrogance. His looked worn at the edge, as if someone had turned it too often while waiting for news that arrived too late.

Cassian: "My family calls it triage. Very noble word. Makes leaving someone behind sound like strategy instead of memory."

The fire at his wrist shrank to a thin ember.

Cassian: "I am not fond of that kind of strategy."

Elira answered with the same calm she used in combat.

Elira: "Then we practice saving everyone now, so we know how when it matters. A tournament will try to make us choose between winning and remaining ourselves. We need to decide before the field decides for us."

Aira looked at her.

Aira: "That works as a team principle."

Cassian tapped the table once.

Cassian: "Fine. Principle one: we do not abandon people for cleaner victories."

Leon did not look away from the replay.

Leon: "Agreed."

Aira's eyes softened, but her voice stayed dry.

Aira: "I hate that we are becoming admirable. It raises expectations."

Then the replay shifted to my route calls.

The room became uncomfortable in a different way. Blue lines traced every warning I had called out before anyone could see proof: trap, decoy, collapse, anchor, false core, real core, safe path. Too many correct answers. Too little visible reason.

Leon watched the sequence twice.

Leon: "Third failure."

I looked at him. He looked back.

Leon: "We still do not know how to use Haru without exposing him to every question in the room."

That was not the accusation I expected. It was more dangerous because it was useful.

Aira sat back slowly.

Aira: "That is... annoyingly insightful."

Leon: "Try not to sound shocked."

Cassian grinned.

Cassian: "No, let her have this. I was shocked."

Elira's gaze remained on me.

Elira: "Then principle two: we build explanations before people demand them."

Haru: "You are all assuming this remains our group."

Aira pointed at the replay slate.

Aira: "Haru. Look at that mess and tell me the faculty won't get attached to it."

I looked. Five colored paths crossed, split, collided, corrected, and finally converged into the recovery zone with seventeen seconds remaining. It was not elegant. It was alive.

Haru: "They will become attached."

Cassian leaned back, satisfied.

Cassian: "Good. Then principle three: if anyone asks, all our success came from overwhelming charm."

Leon: "Rejected."

Aira: "Violently rejected."

Elira smiled faintly.

Haru: "Recorded."

The crystal above the table pulsed again. Somewhere, Lady Seraphine was probably writing something severe.

For the first time, I found I did not mind.

---

The next morning confirmed it.

Group Three had been summoned for private advanced training.

That alone was enough to draw attention. Private training slots were usually saved for confirmed team leads or students the faculty wanted to watch up close. In my case, the second possibility felt unpleasantly likely.

We met at dawn in the upper combat terrace where the eastern towers cast long shadows over the practice rings. The air was cool, the campus quieter than usual. Most students were not yet awake enough to be ambitious.

Professor Halden was already there. So were Instructor Dren and Lady Seraphine.

Aira took one look at the faculty lineup and sighed.

Aira: "I'm beginning to suspect we're not a training group. We're a research incident."

Professor Halden heard her.

Professor Halden: "If that were true, Miss Solen, I would not have informed you."

Aira: "That is not actually comforting."

Cassian stretched his arms over his head.

Cassian: "Can we start? Standing still this early feels unnatural."

Lady Seraphine gestured toward the marked rings.

Lady Seraphine: "Today's focus is role compression."

Leon frowned.

Leon: "Role compression?"

Instructor Dren answered.

Instructor Dren: "You all did well when everyone got to stay in their best role. That's useful. It's also fragile. In a tournament, people get cut off. Plans break. If one person gets knocked out of place, the whole team can fall apart."

Professor Halden stepped forward.

Professor Halden: "We want to see if this team still works when you lose what you're comfortable with."

Aira: "That is a remarkably hostile way to describe education."

Instructor Dren: "Yes."

He dropped one palm onto the terrace seal, and the practice rings lit pale blue beneath our boots. That ended the discussion.

The drill was simple in concept and terrible in execution. Every ten minutes, one team member would be removed from their preferred function: Cassian pulled off the front line, Aira knocked out of her steady support spot, Leon stripped of his water control over the ground, Elira barred from anchoring the center, and me cut off from calling moves early.

The exercise was built to make each of us uncomfortable in different ways.

The first round removed Cassian from direct engagement and forced him to guard the back line and cover gaps. He hated it immediately.

Cassian: "This is insulting."

Aira: "No, this is growth."

Cassian: "I preferred you when you were complaining."

His first interception proved the point against him. Cassian moved too aggressively toward a flank construct, shattered it beautifully, and left the rear lane open for a second attack to slip through.

Aira caught the breach with a barrier that made a sound like offended glass.

Aira: "Growth usually includes not abandoning the thing you are protecting."

Still, he adapted faster than expected. For all his love of impact, he had sharp instinct for incoming threats and proved surprisingly good at breaking attacks before they reached the center.

The second round pulled Aira out of her support spot and put her in charge of calling movement.

Aira: "This is malicious."

Elira: "You can do it."

Aira: "Your faith is oppressive."

She did it anyway, and very well.

The third round took Leon out of his careful setup and forced him to fight on instinct. That one exposed the most.

Leon was strong, refined, and skilled, but much of his confidence depended on control and steady timing. Without them, he became less certain and less efficient. He froze for half a breath when the second construct changed angle without warning, frustration flashing across his face because he had no clean setup to work from.

Aira: "Half-step left, then cut on my count."

He moved on her timing. It worked. He noticed that too, and to his credit, instead of lashing out, he adjusted.

By the fourth rotation, when Elira was barred from acting as central commander, the drill became truly interesting.

Without meaning to, the rest of us had already started orbiting around her. Pulling her out left an instant hole.

Cassian wanted motion.
Leon wanted structure.
Aira wanted coherence.

They all looked at me. I did not enjoy that.

Aira: "Well?"

Haru: "Why are you asking me?"

Aira: "Because you already mapped the next three exchanges in your head."

Cassian grinned.

Cassian: "She's got you there."

Leon looked irritated that the assessment was accurate.

Leon: "If you have a plan, say it."

I looked at the incoming construct pattern: three heavy attack lanes, one fake center, and a late strike from the side.

Haru: "I'm not replacing her. I'm holding her position until she's back. Cassian left lane, but don't commit past the second marker. Leon cuts the false center and forces the real flank reveal. Aira, hold mid-height and feed him angles. Elira, stay back on the right—don't step in unless the outer ring breaks."

They moved.

It failed on the second exchange.

The false center broke early. Leon turned to force the flank reveal exactly as I had told him. That left Aira's upper lane open half a breath sooner than my pattern allowed.

Cassian saw the construct diving toward her. He crossed the second marker.

Haru: "Cassian, hold..."

He did not.

His Red Circle struck the diving construct apart. The blast saved Aira's lane and exposed his own. A delayed spear came through the left gap, hit the reserve ring beside Elira, and shattered the stone score post behind her.

Instructor Dren dropped the entire field. Silence landed with the dust.

Elira had not been hit. Aira's sleeve was scorched where Cassian's fire passed too close. Leon stood in the dead center with his blade raised against a flank that no longer existed.

The score crystal displayed one word.

**FAILED.**

I looked at Cassian first.

Haru: "You left the lane."

His grin had vanished.

Cassian: "The construct had Aira."

Haru: "She had the middle height."

Aira held up the burned edge of her sleeve.

Aira: "I did until the center opened early."

Leon: "Your correction came late."

Haru: "You followed the false center after it changed."

Leon: "Because you told me to force the flank reveal."

The answer struck harder than the accusation. They had followed me. That was not the same as trusting me.

I had given each person a path and mistaken obedience for coordination. When the construct changed, Leon protected the instruction, Cassian protected Aira, and I protected the plan until the field broke around it.

Elira stepped out of the reserve ring.

Elira: "Haru."

I knew what she was going to say and still wanted her not to.

Elira: "You planned for our strengths. You did not plan for us to disagree with you."

Haru: "There was no time."

Elira: "There was enough time to give six commands."

Cassian looked toward the shattered post. His hands were open now, fire gone.

Cassian: "I should have called the break before I crossed."

Aira: "I should have called that I lost elevation."

Leon lowered his blade.

Leon: "I should have stopped following a dead instruction."

No one offered me a matching sentence. They waited.

Haru: "I should have told you what mattered instead of deciding every movement."

Professor Halden's pen moved. I hated the sound.

Instructor Dren reset the field with one palm against the central seal. The shattered score post stayed shattered.

Instructor Dren: "Again. No central commander. Call out what you see, what you're doing, and what you can't hold. Anyone giving step-by-step orders loses the right to speak."

Aira stared at him.

Aira: "You can lose the right to talk in a fight?"

Instructor Dren: "You are closest to finding out."

The constructs rose.

This time I did not map three exchanges aloud.

Haru: "False center. Left delay."

Cassian: "Taking left until second marker. If Aira drops, I cross."

Aira: "Mid-height unstable. I can hold one break, not two."

Leon: "I force center after her call."

Elira stayed inside the reserve ring.

Elira: "Right ring can absorb one spear. I choose which."

The wave entered. Nothing looked elegant.

Cassian let the first flank construct pass farther than he liked. Aira redirected it instead of waiting for him to destroy it. Leon saw the false center open and held position, jaw tight, until Aira shouted.

Aira: "Now."

He cut.

The real flank emerged high. I called the angle, not the answer.

Haru: "Right, descending."

Elira chose not to absorb it.

For one frightening beat, I thought she had missed.

Then the spear crossed her empty reserve ring and entered Cassian's lane where he had already turned to meet it. His fire broke its point. Leon caught the remaining shaft with an ugly Blue barrier that knocked him to one knee.

The left lane opened again. Cassian could have taken the final construct and secured the clean finish. He looked at Leon instead.

Cassian: "Can you hold?"

Leon: "No."

Cassian abandoned the point and caught the failing barrier with heat beneath it. Aira took the last construct alone. Her water line missed the center, clipped one edge, and sent the target tumbling across the recovery boundary instead of landing upright.

The field went dark. The score crystal considered us.

**FUNCTIONAL. INCOMPLETE.**

Leon remained on one knee. Cassian offered a hand. Leon looked at it, irritated by needing it, then took it.

Lady Seraphine: "One target missed. Formation held. Nobody left behind."

Instructor Dren pointed at the broken post.

Instructor Dren: "Cost visible. Better."

Professor Halden looked at me.

Professor Halden: "Seeing ahead is useful. Assuming you're right is not the same thing. Learn the difference."

I wanted to argue that I had never been certain. The shattered stone behind Elira made that excuse not worth saying aloud.

Haru: "Understood."

As we reset, Elira stepped near enough that no one else needed to hear.

Elira: "You trusted us to follow."

Haru: "Yes."

Elira: "Next time, trust us to contradict you."

Across the ring, Aira inspected her scorched sleeve.

Aira: "I would like the record to show that growth is expensive and this uniform was new."

Cassian: "I saved the person inside it."

Aira: "You also burned the person inside it."

Leon rolled his aching shoulder.

Leon: "Both facts remain."

No one called us flawless. We were still summoned back for the next training hour.

---

Later that afternoon, the second major tournament notice arrived. This one was public.

Posted in every central corridor, projected over the courtyard, and delivered to candidate groups through official crystal dispatch:

**INTER-NATIONAL SCHOOLS TOURNAMENT - VISITING DELEGATIONS ARRIVE IN 5 DAYS**

At the bottom of the notice, a red student council seal burned with clean authority.

**Emergency movement protocols authorized by Student Council President Rhea Valmont.**

The line was small. The reaction to it was not.

Two Red Circle students near the fountain stopped arguing mid-sentence and stepped out of the walkway. A cluster of Blue Circle candidates lowered their voices without an instructor telling them to. Even the floating notice crystals adjusted their height to keep the council seal visible above the crowd.

Aira followed my gaze.

Aira: "President Valmont does not usually attach her name to logistics unless she expects people to behave badly."

Cassian gave a short laugh.

Cassian: "Or unless she expects them to survive behaving badly."

Leon frowned.

Leon: "You know her?"

Cassian: "Every Red Circle heir knows Rhea Valmont. Some because they admire her. Most because they have been corrected by her in language their parents could not legally argue with."

I remembered the Red Circle student council president watching me after Leon's first defeat, her smile precise enough to feel like a blade placed flat against glass. So she had not vanished after noticing the thread. She had simply been standing where authority stood best: slightly ahead of disaster.

Below it appeared the list of confirmed competing institutions and the names of several high-profile student champions already known across the continent.

The campus reaction was immediate. Excitement rose, nerves sharpened, and status suddenly had teeth. Students crowded the notices, identifying famous names, rumored prodigies, and rival academy legends.

Aurelis's own team picks were no longer just talk. Time had become short.

Aira scanned the projected roster with growing dread.

Aira: "This is worse than I thought."

Haru: "You say that often."

Aira: "Because life keeps validating me."

She pointed at the list.

Aira: "Look. Valemere has registered **Soren Ashvale**, the Red Circle duelist with six consecutive academy titles. Drakenshard is fielding **Mara Kest**, that battlefield monster everyone keeps politely calling disciplined. And Noctis Dominion is sending..."

She went silent.

That silence did more than the names before it.

Soren Ashvale had earned whispers of admiration. Mara Kest had earned nervous laughter. Noctis Dominion earned space.

The students packed around the notice did not all stop talking at once. Instead, conversations thinned in uneven layers. A group of Blue Circle candidates pretended to study Valemere's roster while their eyes kept sliding back to the black Noctis crest. Two Red Circle heirs exchanged looks and said nothing. A Green support student near the edge of the crowd took one step backward without realizing it.

Even the projected banners above the courtyard seemed less bright around Noctis' name, swallowed rather than dimmed.

I had heard of Noctis Dominion College before coming to Aurelis. Everyone had. It was not the kind of school people praised casually. They spoke of its discipline, its victory record, its severe training culture, its graduates who entered military offices, high courts, and private enforcement houses with smiles sharp enough to count as weapons.

Noctis did not produce many beloved heroes. It produced results.

Leon noticed where my gaze had gone.

Leon: "Their candidates don't break formation."

Cassian's smile dropped a notch.

Cassian: "No. They break other things."

Aira looked at him.

Aira: "That was not comforting."

Cassian: "It was not meant to be."

Elira had not moved.

That was the part that caught me.

Elira Vale could stand before faculty, nobles, duelists, gossip, and pressure without giving away anything she did not choose to reveal. Her composure was trained, chosen, and reinforced by a will stronger than most magic I had seen at Aurelis.

Now her stillness had changed from calm into containment. Whatever name waited after Noctis Dominion, Elira already knew its shape, and she was holding the memory in place so it would not touch anyone else before it had to.

Elira stepped closer to read. Her expression shifted slightly. That was enough to matter.

Haru: "Who?"

Elira answered quietly.

Elira: "**Lucien Voss.**"

Her fingers closed around the edge of the roster hard enough to crease the projection light. The motion lasted only a heartbeat before she smoothed her hand flat again: composed, public, Yellow Circle perfect.

But I had seen it. Lucien's name did not frighten her like a rival; it struck something remembered.

Cassian, standing just behind us, gave a low whistle.

Cassian: "That's a problem."

Leon had approached unnoticed from the side and now looked at the notice with visible tension.

Leon: "Noctis actually sent him?"

Aira looked between them.

Aira: "That is too much collective discomfort for one name. Who is he?"

Elira's gaze remained on the projection.

Elira: "A Yellow Circle."

That alone quieted her. Elira continued.

Elira: "Noctis Dominion's top prodigy. Lightless magic. Unmatched up close. He's been called one of the most dangerous young magicians in the tournament circuit."

Cassian folded his arms.

Cassian: "Dangerous is the polite word."

Aira: "And the impolite one?"

Cassian: "Cruel."

Aira looked back at the projection, suddenly less theatrical.

Aira: "Noctis listed him as captain."

The roster unfolded beneath Lucien's name in smaller letters.

**Nera Vail - Blue Circle Binding**  
**Oric Sen - Blue Circle Binding**  
**Tovan Reiss - Red Circle Close Combat**  
**Cael Nox - Conduit Support**

The last entry sat lower than the others, almost like the projection itself had been told to make it less important.

The roster expanded into formation notes when Aira touched the corner of the projection. Noctis' support pattern unfolded in thin pale lines.

Nera Vail occupied Lucien's right flank. The projection listed ice-and-water locks, joint pins, lane control, and emergency pressure cover. Efficient. Clean. Nothing decorative except the rings shown on both wrists, and even those were probably tools pretending to be jewelry.

Aira leaned closer despite herself.

Aira: "Their support specialist is good."

Leon: "You can tell from a roster?"

Aira: "No. I can tell from what the roster does not bother explaining. See how every trap path assumes she'll adjust right after Lucien moves? That is not a weak support caster. That is someone trusted to make his cruelty look like strategy."

Under Nera's name, Oric Sen's lines mirrored hers with shorter reach and tighter backup guard. Then Cael's route appeared. Not beside Lucien. Through him.

Aira's mouth flattened.

Aira: "That is not a teammate diagram."

Elira's voice stayed quiet.

Elira: "No."

The projection flickered once, showing a practice still from Noctis' qualifying match. Nera stood with one hand extended, blue-gray eyes fixed forward while ice-bindings sealed off a dangerous hallway. Behind Lucien, Cael's shoulders were drawn up too tight. Nera's gaze had shifted toward him by the smallest fraction.

Then Lucien's hand lifted in the still image. Nera faced forward again.

Haru: "She saw it."

Aira: "And continued."

Haru: "Yes."

Aira's expression turned sharper and less amused.

Aira: "I hate mirrors when they are people."

Leon frowned.

Leon: "Conduit support?"

Cassian's expression lost some of its humor.

Cassian: "Noctis likes power routes. A conduit takes strain so the main caster can push harder, longer, or through uglier angles."

Aira: "A terrible job, then."

Elira's voice was quiet.

Elira: "It can be, depending on who is allowed to say no."

The words did not sound theoretical.

Aira tapped the corner of the roster slate again, advancing Noctis' qualifying clip past the final buzzer into three unedited seconds of corridor feed their staff had neglected to trim.

In the grain of the recording, Nera Vail turned toward the pale younger student as he lowered his casting arm. Even through tinny slate audio, her voice came out too clipped for tenderness.

The name **Lucien Voss** followed us through the rest of the day.

Haru: "That usually describes me."

Elira's mouth tightened.

The Black Circle gave one slow, cold pulse. Elira noticed.

Twelve days had passed since the gate first counted me, five since the reveal in the Moonlit Archive. Haru: "Yes. I do not know what to call it yet."

Neither of us looked away.

Elira: "May I ask one more thing?"

Haru: "Yes."

Her composure changed by less than a breath.

Elira: "May I kiss you?"

I could have stopped an arena strike between heartbeats. I could have folded the distance between towers. None of that helped me answer a girl who left room for me to say no inside what she wanted. That mattered.

Haru: "Yes."

My fingers stayed warm around hers, steady enough not to rush her.

She moved slowly enough that I could have changed my mind and trusted me enough not to pretend I might. Her free hand touched my cheek. Then her lips met mine.

The kiss was brief and warm, careful without being uncertain.

No hidden circle opened. No ancient ward answered. My false Green crest remained a modest glow at my wrist beneath my cuff, and the pendant at her collarbone warmed once like a secret choosing not to become a spectacle.

Her smile arrived slowly.

Haru: "Aira will be relieved."

That startled a laugh out of her.

Elira lifted her head.

Elira: "How?"

Haru: "Protection is not my weakness."

The necklace at her collarbone gave a faint, hidden pulse. Elira felt it too.

Her fingers tightened around mine.

Elira: "Haru."

Haru: "I know how to stop myself."

Elira: "Do you?"

There was no accusation in it, only concern. Because she had seen the Black Circle. She knew that my restraint was not a habit; it was a choice I had to keep making.

Haru: "I won't expose myself over an insult."

Elira: "And if he threatens someone?"

Haru: "Then I will stop him."

Elira held my gaze.

Elira: "Carefully?"

I thought of Leon's spell collapsing in harmless rain. Of the archive sealed beneath moonlight. Of the necklace I had made because I could not always be near her.

Haru: "As carefully as the situation allows."

She looked only partly satisfied with that answer. Which was fair. It was only partly reassuring.

The new exercise was called **Pressure Disclosure**.

That name alone made Aira object before the rules were finished.

Lady Seraphine, standing beside the projection board, did not blink.

Lady Seraphine: "Correct, except 'trap' is a little dramatic."

The drill cut each team member off in a fake ambush, then forced the others to decide whether to continue the objective or break formation to assist. It was about priorities: who protected whom, who trusted whom, and who panicked when someone they cared about was in danger.

The simulation dimmed, not into ordinary darkness, but into a colorless suppression field that spread across the arena floor and swallowed light as it moved. The construct at its center resembled no student exactly, yet the resemblance in technique was unmistakable: lightless affinity. Lucien.

Elira's posture changed by a fraction. Enough that I felt it.

The construct lifted one hand, and pale-black bands snapped toward her from three angles, designed not to injure but to choke off her magic and trap her in place.

Leon: "Hold formation."

That surprised me enough to stop.

That was correct. Annoyingly correct.

Elira's golden light gathered around her hands as the suppression bands tightened. For half a second, the old story she had told me lived in the air between us: a boy whose mana channels were smothered one by one while officials reacted too late.

She did not overpower the lightless construct. She understood it. Golden threads slid between the pale-black bands, finding the tiny spaces where control had become arrogance. Aira reinforced the openings. Leon cut the outer anchors. Cassian shattered the left stabilizer with one clean blow.

I stayed where I was.

The band missed by an inch.

She had felt the nudge. Yet she did not look angry, only relieved that I had trusted her enough to wait.

Lady Seraphine made several notes. Instructor Dren looked almost satisfied. Halden looked fascinated, which was the least comforting reaction available.

Aira leaned toward Cassian.

Aira: "He added that because he realized it sounded targeted."

For once, no one argued.

Four more days vanished into drills that felt less like preparation and more like stress-testing glass until it learned not to shatter.

Every morning before dawn, Instructor Dren ran Group Three through blind-terrain rotations until we stopped asking who held command and started reading each other's shoulders instead. Leon shaved half a second off his transition lag. Cassian learned to call a lane break before he crossed it, grumbling the entire time. Aira filled two notebooks with foreign roster patterns and insisted on making me read Valemere's junior-circuit reports over cold tea.

Every afternoon, Professor Halden opened unannounced observation windows mid-exercise and watched how closely I stayed to Elira whenever the field tilted.

By the fifth evening, the academy banners hung full-length from the western battlements, and the visiting delegations finally arrived.

Students crowded balconies and courtyard rails while the western gate unfolded its ceremonial barrier. Banners from foreign institutions appeared in the air one by one, carried by floating sigils and escort lights.

Valemere Royal Institute arrived first in silver-blue carriages pulled by winged mana constructs: polished lines, clean banners, escort mages who bowed to Aurelis faculty instead of pretending not to measure them. Their magic did not press against the air the way Noctis rumors did. It moved like formal footwork. Graceful, dangerous, and honest about both.

At the head of their student delegation stood Soren Ashvale.

He was taller than most of the students around him, dark-haired, warm-eyed, and carried himself like a duelist who had learned that confidence did not require making other people smaller. Faint scars crossed the backs of his hands. He wore them openly.

When he saw Elira, he approached with a respectful distance already built into his stride.

Soren: "Lady Vale."

Elira's public composure settled around her at once.

Elira: "Mr. Ashvale."

Elira's surprise lasted only a heartbeat, but I saw it.

Elira: "That is generous."

Soren: "It is accurate."

His gaze moved to me, assessing cleanly without dismissal.

Soren: "Your partner?"

Elira did not hesitate.

Elira: "Yes. Haru Aerlen."

The word partner moved through me with more force than it should have.

Soren: "Lucky man."

Haru: "Yes."

Elira's fingers found mine before I could decide whether the answer had been too revealing. Soren noticed, smiled once, and released my hand.

Soren: "Then I look forward to facing Aurelis properly."

He returned to Valemere without looking back.

At Valemere, honor had not been taught to Soren as ornament or lineage, but as responsibility. I knew why from the junior-circuit report Aira had forced on me two nights earlier: years ago, a defeated student had stayed down longer than the official record admitted. Soren had been young enough to keep his place, frightened enough to call that caution, and honest enough to condemn his own silence in his appeal. The scars across his hands came from the rescue spell he cast after the judges had already declared the match finished. Watching him step away from Elira without trying to turn courtesy into leverage, I believed he still meant every word of it.

Aira: "That was the most polite romantic threat I have ever witnessed."

Haru: "He was not threatening."

Aira: "No. That is what made it effective."

Elira's thumb moved once against my hand in quiet recognition. Soren had admired her without trying to claim her, and recognized us without testing whether we were real. That kind of honor was inconvenient. It made what followed feel colder before the next carriage even crossed the gate.

Drakenshard Military Academy rolled in behind Valemere with armored transports that looked more like moving fortresses than student vehicles.

The Noctis Dominion procession entered without music.

Black glass carriages rolled through the gate under banners marked with a pale eclipse. Their uniforms were dark, formal, and severe, threaded with faint lines of silver. Even their escort lights seemed dimmer than everyone else's.

At the center carriage, the door opened.

Lucien Voss stepped out.

He was tall and composed, with ash-blond hair cut neatly away from a face too calm to be called gentle. His eyes were pale gray, almost colorless, and the Yellow Circle at his collarbone glowed with a strange muted radiance, like sunlight seen through smoke.

Elira: "Lucien."

Lucien: "And this is?"

The Black Circle moved once.

Tier 1. Small. Surgical. I did not block his thread; blocking would have taught him shape. I changed the distance instead. For less than a heartbeat, the space between Lucien's drifting mana and Elira's pendant became longer than the courtyard allowed.

His thread failed to arrive.

To anyone else, nothing had happened. To a Yellow Circle's eyes, the courtyard had skipped a beat—leaf-shadow, breath, mana pressure all moving normally except one narrow strip of air that refused to connect.

Behind us, Aira's voice dropped to a whisper.

Elira: "Yes."

Across the courtyard, the Noctis formation closed around Lucien again.

Cael Nox stood exactly where a conduit support was supposed to stand: close enough to be useful, far enough back that no one important had to look at him unless something went wrong. He flexed the wrist Lucien had corrected, then stopped when Nera Vail's gaze touched the motion. She saw the tremor. So did Oric. Neither of them moved, because Noctis had trained understanding to arrive after permission.

Lucien spoke without turning.

Lucien: "Cael."

The boy's hand dropped to his side.

Cael: "Yes, Voss."

Lucien: "Tomorrow's calibration will use the extended relay pattern."

Nera's binding rings clicked once, too softly for most of the courtyard to hear. Oric heard it. Cael heard it. Lucien heard it too.

His pale eyes shifted toward Nera with the mild interest of a teacher noticing poor posture.

Lucien: "Is there a technical concern?"

Nera's expression stayed composed.

Nera: "Extended relay patterns put heavy strain on the conduit if the lead caster pushes too hard."

Lucien smiled.

Lucien: "Then the conduit should not overcorrect."

Cael's face emptied itself of reaction.

That was what made the moment stay with me. Fear had many shapes; panic was the easiest to notice, but obedience was quieter and far more useful to cruel people.

Nera lowered her eyes first.

Nera: "Understood."

Oric's mouth opened by a fraction, then closed.

Lucien turned away as if the conversation had been about equipment. The Noctis formation moved on.

Nera looked back once, not at me, at Cael's wrist, then at her own rings. For the first time since Lucien stepped through the gate, her discipline looked less like loyalty than a lock waiting for someone to admit it existed.

The hidden darkness near my heart pulsed slowly. Patient.

The visiting schools had arrived. The tournament had not officially started.

But the first challenge had already been made.

## Interlude: Mira Castellan - First Page

Mira Castellan did not mean to start a record.

She had come back from the western walkway intending to finish her herbology notes, avoid the louder tournament students, and stop thinking about how Haru Aerlen's false Green crest had held its exact, quiet shape at his wrist when Soren Ashvale shook his hand.

False was the wrong word. Mira crossed it out with a nervous scratch of ink. Careful, she wrote instead.

Outside the tall library windows, the tournament perimeter wards hummed as the last visiting carriages cleared the gate. The beveled glass shade on her table lamp caught the shifting blue-gold mana drift from the courtyard and bent it across the brass ring: four standard academy colors refracted in pale bands, with a fifth, darker shadow-ring hugging the bottom rim where the bevel cut the light. Mira's pen froze above the page, her pulse jumping against her collar until the courtyard wards settled and the glass went ordinary again.

Across the room, two Blue Circle students complained about Green witnesses being allowed near tournament review files. Mira exhaled, wiped a smudge of ink from her thumb, and dipped her pen again before anger could make her handwriting ugly.

*Observed: Haru Aerlen avoids victory language. Elira Vale says partner without hesitation. Visiting champion Soren Ashvale notices both and does not challenge. Lucien Voss tests the air between them and stops smiling.*

She hesitated, tapping the nib lightly against the inkwell, then added one more line.

*The academy noticed too.*

## Interlude: Lucien Voss - Distance

Lucien closed the Noctis delegation door with two fingers.

There was no slam or visible anger. The corridor outside his quarters carried the disciplined hush of a foreign school pretending it had not noticed anything worth fearing. That was useful; fear became easier to manage once it had been trained to stand still.

He removed his gloves first. Left, then right. Order always mattered after contact with something disobedient.

On the desk, he placed three objects in a line: the tournament arrival slate, a black relay stylus, and a thin strip of detection paper treated with Noctis reagent. The reagent had been developed for old binding traces, family seals, bloodline marks, and sentimental protection charms of the sort families gave heirs before pretending affection was not a security weakness.

Lucien touched one end of the paper. Pale-black mana moved from his fingertip.

It should have crossed the paper in a clean line. Instead, halfway across, the line stopped. Neither blocked nor burned. Stopped as if the rest of the paper had become conceptually farther away.

Lucien watched it for nine seconds, then smiled, not with pleasure, with recognition.

Lucien: "There you are."

The pendant had not defended Elira like ordinary protection. Ordinary protection announced a boundary: shield, seal, counterforce, warning flare. This had refused the idea of coming closer. His mana hadn't been pushed away from the girl; it had been stripped of the right to call anything 'near'.

That was not Yellow. It was not Green. It was not any rank that still needed comparison to feel tall.

He wrote one word on the slate.

**DISTANCE**

Then another.

**PERSONAL**

Then he stopped. The third word wanted to be impossible.

Noctis disliked that word. Impossible was what weak students said before instruction corrected them, what families said when asked to surrender a child to a better use, the first refuge of the undisciplined mind.

Lucien had learned that before he learned formal lightless binding.

He had been eleven when Magister Voss placed a cup of water on a table and told him to lift it without touching it. He had failed for two hours. At the end, thirsty enough that his tongue felt too large for his mouth, he had asked for water.

His father had looked almost gentle.

Magister Voss: "Need is a debt. Debt is a handle. Never offer yours without deciding who may hold it."

Then he had drunk the water himself.

Lucien had lifted the empty cup on the next attempt. Everyone called that progress.

Afterward, Nera had slipped him half a citrus candy from the training infirmary and pretended she had dropped it by accident. Oric had stood in the doorway and looked away so neither of them would have to name the exchange.

Lucien had eaten the candy in one precise bite. He had hated them both for seeing. He had kept them close anyway.

People misread that as attachment. Caring was a weakness with no upside. Nera and Oric were early proof that witnesses could be turned into order, as long as they learned where to stand. Their presence proved that the child at the table had not merely been denied water; he had become the one who decided when thirst meant obedience.

Lucien looked at the stopped reagent line.

Elira Vale had chosen a Green Circle student and the world had not rearranged itself to punish the absurdity. That was irritating.

Haru Aerlen had answered approach without moving. That was useful.

The pendant had made distance personal. That was unacceptable.

Lucien wrote the third word.

**SOVEREIGN**

Then he crossed it out, not because it was wrong, but because names given too early became shrines, and shrines attracted fools.

He opened a second slate and entered the only report Noctis needed for now.

**Elira Vale remains viable pressure locus. Haru Aerlen response layer unclassified. Test through relational boundary, not rank insult alone.**

His hand paused over the final line.

For one second, the quarters were too quiet. No Nera outside the door pretending not to worry, no Oric counting exits, no old training room, no cup.

He corrected the thought before it became indulgence. Quiet was not absence. Quiet was available space.

Lucien put his gloves back on. Right, then left.

He left the failed reagent paper on the desk where he could see it when he woke. Some impossibilities became more obedient after being watched.

## Interlude: Professor Halden - Document First

Professor Halden did not return directly to the faculty residences after the delegations dispersed.

He went to the sealed records annex beneath the advanced mana studies wing and opened a drawer that had not been indexed in the public catalog for forty years.

He had watched the courtyard exchange from the faculty line. Lucien Voss's reaction had been instructive. Not the insult; insults were easy. The surprise: genuine, quickly buried, and therefore more useful than anything Lucien had chosen to say aloud.

Elira Vale had publicly chosen to stand beside a Green Circle student, and Lucien Voss had needed a heartbeat to make the fact fit inside his worldview. Halden disliked heartbeats that changed investigations.

The file inside was thin. Too thin for a myth that had frightened entire institutions.

Black Circle references did not survive in clean histories. They survived in warnings, redactions, disaster summaries, and committee reports written by people trying to sound calm after doing something unforgivable.

Halden read until the mana lamp beside him burned low.

One fragment described a city-state that tried to draft an unranked caster of terrifying power. Another described a royal college that attempted containment under faculty authority. A third ended halfway through a sentence, the remaining page burned into glass.

He was not hostile to Haru Aerlen. That would have been simpler.

He was terrified of what history taught: every time something like Black Circle emerged, institutions fell. Not because the power was evil, necessarily, but because people could not resist trying to control it, weaponize it, or destroy it, and in trying, destroyed everything else.

Halden closed the file with two fingers.

Lucien would not remain surprised. That was the problem with intelligent cruelty: it learned.

Professor Halden: "Document first."

His voice sounded too loud in the archive.

Professor Halden: "Possess nothing."

Curiosity remained. So did fear. For once, he allowed both to stand without pretending one was virtue and the other was weakness.

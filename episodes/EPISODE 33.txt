# Episode 33: **The Seal That Let Them Through**

The morning sun sat in the wrong place for a shadow that sharp.

Pale light sat low behind the east library roof, throwing blurs off the fountain rim. Beneath the fifth green window, though, the shadow fell steep as noon, black-edged on damp stone smelling of wet slate and scorched lime.

Two lines burned inside the dark at our boots.

**ERIS VELL WAS NOT LOST IN AURELIS.**

**SHE WAS TAKEN THROUGH NOCTIS.**

Four seconds of dead quiet hit the yard.

Mira Castellan broke it by dropping onto both knees beside the shadow with soot on her cheek and a blunt pencil in her fist. Aira Solen blocked two Blue upper-years drifting too close.

Aira: "Feet back."

Blue Student: "We are only looking."

Aira: "You are standing in the margin of a cross-school accusation. Two paces left before I log your boots."

They moved three. Aira crouched opposite Mira with fresh scrap.

Aira: "Three hands minimum before anyone breathes. Finn, left edge. Someone legible take line two, which excludes Leon."

Leon Varek sat by the fountain base where Maelin had pinned him, bruised shoulder braced against stone.

Leon: "My handwriting is fine."

Aira: "It looks like a duel between an heir and a wet sponge."

Leon: "I am injured."

Aira: "You wrote like that on Tuesday."

Finn knelt beside his sister and copied fast. By the north planter, Iven Sahl stiffened, his cheekbone cut crusting dark and his torn borrowed sweater hanging loose over his bandaged wrist. Beside him, Sera held her tin soup cup forward in both hands so Mira's crooked tape faced the yard.

**SERA'S CUP. ASK SERA.**

Ten paces off at the copy table, the younger Green transfer girl who had signed **S.** stayed hunched over her stacks, blunt dark hair hiding her cheek, oversized sleeve pushed up an inch over her pale green ring. She kept sorting without looking up.

Cold bit my left hand.

Under my glove, Maelin's blue cloth chilled sharp as well water against my bruised ring fingernail. Not pulling upward toward the fifth window anymore. Dragging sideways under the flagstones, northeast toward Noctis.

Old reflex moved first: pocket the hand, smooth my face, wait.

Then Elira's voice from yesterday cut through it. *Tell me before I discover it next time.*

I stepped beside her wooden bench and pulled my glove off in plain view.

Elira saw before leather cleared my wrist. Her eyes dropped to the hair-thin dark seam bled fresh across the blue weave above my nail bed, then rose to my face without panic.

Elira: "Different direction?"

Haru: "Northeast. Under the stone. Stopped pulling toward the bell."

Her fingers touched the dark clasp of her pale blue cloak over her hidden seven-line pendant and shoulder brace.

Elira: "Thank you."

Haru: "I almost kept the glove on."

Elira: "I know. You didn't."

Maelin reached us in three strides, slate snapping open. She tapped two fingers against the darkened cloth.

Maelin: "Resonance pull, no channel draw. Behind the green line, Aerlen. Do not push back."

She aimed her stylus at Elira, who had started to rise.

Elira: "Four minutes left on my standing allowance."

Maelin: "Spent watching three fools vanish down an impossible stair and glaring at black ink. Sit, Vale, or I read your pulse aloud to the third year."

Elira sat without bluffing, green pulse charm visible over her cloak.

Unhurried heels clicked under the east administrative arch.

A brass folio chimed crisp every third step. The outer ring parted. Mid-forties, steel-gray hair pinned bare of ornament, plain slate-gray Council wool stripped of gilded Red trim. Spotless nails. A silver forensic loupe clipped to her lapel.

She stopped two paces clear of the pale green maintenance lanes Finn and three Green aides kept lit through the stones, glanced at the shadow words, and fixed on President Rhea Valmont's right hand. The jagged radial crack splitting Rhea's mandate mark caught morning light like glass.

Auditor Falk: "President Valmont."

Rhea: "Auditor Falk."

Aira: "Wonderful. Someone with clean cuffs."

Auditor Tamsin Falk flipped open her folio, silencing a waxed resonance pin inside the cover.

Falk: "Senior Seal-Auditor Tamsin Falk, Special Commission on Headmaster Caelum Veris's Office. My pin tripped on a live Red-class seal fracture inside Aurelis, and I walked over from the east wing as fast as stairs allow."

She lifted the silver loupe from outside the green line. One thin Red forensic thread traced Rhea's split mark without touching skin. Falk let the loupe drop back against her coat.

Falk: "Radial shear right through the center ring. Integrity below forty percent."

Rhea: "It held twelve breaths against a Noctis retrieval seal coming out of the west service arch."

Falk: "Admirable conduct. Legally irrelevant to certification. Under Charter Section Four, a radially fractured mandate seal is suspended pending Council review. You cannot certify cross-school allegations or bind foreign evidence under it."

Zero sneer. Zero triumph. I watched her posture from behind the green line and felt colder than I had during Krail's hearing last night. People who enjoyed power always showed their teeth too early. People who trusted procedure stayed polite while locking the same door.

Rhea lowered her wrist without flinching.

Rhea: "Suspension acknowledged. Log timestamp and witness count before anyone trims why it broke."

Falk gave her a flicker of dry respect, then looked down at the shadow text and Mira's lap.

Falk: "Those words accuse Noctis of unlawful removal tied to Aurelis's seals. Every page and copy here falls under the Headmaster Veris Seal Audit Hold. They go into my folio behind closed Council doors until provenance clears."

Mira did not blink.

Mira: "No."

Falk studied the Green girl kneeling by the stones.

Falk: "Miss Castellan. Your courage is not in doubt. Your chain of custody is. Twelve years ago an unverified transit-seal report escaped onto public boards before replacement treaties existed. Panic shut two winter supply lines for a month, and three Green scholarship dorms froze."

The yard hushed. You could hear the old Valemere scar sitting flat under her voice. She actually remembered the cold rooms.

Haru: "You are still asking them to hand over the only proof that somebody else didn't invent this."

Falk's head turned toward me, steady and unhurried. She looked once at my bare ring finger wrapped in Maelin's darkened blue cloth, then up to my eyes.

Falk: "Mr. Aerlen. I am not asking them to burn it. I am asking to verify every stamp before four academies panic over a shadow on wet stone."

Haru: "Noctis does not wait for verification to edit a record."

Falk: "Neither does rumor. Hand me the half-burned page and the copies, Miss Castellan."

The Council mirror flared silver. Councilor Krail filled the glass, clerks behind him.

Krail: "Auditor Falk. Timely. Given local collapse and uncontained cross-school accusations, my Stability Committee moves to assume Fifth Tower custody under Emergency Provision Nine."

Falk barely glanced over her gray shoulder, polite as a razor.

Falk: "Read Charter Section Four past paragraph one, Councilor. A fractured seal transfers evidentiary custody to Seal-Audit, not your Stability Committee. You hold no forensic warrant and no hazard finding from Architect Reith. Do not confuse an audit hold with a political warrant."

Aira groaned quietly beside me.

Aira: "Oh, no. She has principles. That is so much worse."

Krail: "Your division eats the liability before noon, Falk."

Falk: "We have survived worse than your memos. File it in writing."

She flicked the courtesy latch shut. Krail vanished mid-breath. Falk turned back to Mira.

Falk: "The half-burned page and the copies."

Rhea stepped between them behind the green lane.

Rhea: "Section Four governs Council seals and office ledgers, Auditor. Not student witness boards, not Magistra Voss's provisional copy ruling, and not Green maintenance lanes."

She pointed down at the pale paths Finn and three Green aides still held steady.

Rhea: "My seal broke fifteen minutes ago. Those lanes did not."

Falk: "Student boards hold no cross-school standing, and an unsealed doorway proposal from an angry second-year is not a Council instrument."

Architect Sovan Reith stepped forward lifting one smudged, unsealed sheet of Mira's rough duplicate paper.

Sovan: "Actually, Auditor, it is an active field observation. Magistra Voss assigned me here under Emergency Structures before your pin tripped. Five minutes ago I hand-copied Miss Castellan's Green-led doorway witness proposal as a baseline safety plan. My signature, zero Veris seals. Outside Section Four."

Aira beamed up.

Aira: "Architect Reith, I am starting a list of adults allowed near my stationery."

Sovan: "Keep me provisional, Miss Solen. I distrust your margins."

Mira: "Take an ugly copy. You cannot lock up originals while Noctis has a live drain behind that door."

Elira spoke from her bench, quiet enough to stop every voice.

Elira: "Auditor Falk. Look at President Valmont's wrist again. Not the depth. The geometry."

Falk frowned slightly.

Falk: "A fracture is a fracture, Miss Vale."

Elira: "Not in ward work. Hostile force crushes a Red barrier inward from the rim: jagged compression cracks, outer pitting. Rhea's seal split from the center outward along one radial seam."

Falk froze.

Elira: "Release-seam failure. Only a recognized counter-key passing through its own lawful release path shears a Red ward from inside out."

Beside the fountain, Professor Iris Valen tipped open her cedar case. The fingernail-sized Regalia shard sat black and cold, its gold vein pointed dead away from the arch.

Professor Valen: "When Noctis ink spilled over that sill, Regalia turned completely away. It refuses to oppose authorized continuity."

Falk crossed briskly to the arch, knelt outside the green line, and brushed dry black ink-ash onto a glass slide. She lifted it behind her silver loupe.

Five seconds passed. Beneath my bare ring finger, Maelin's blue cloth tugged cold northeast. I kept my boots planted, watching Falk's shoulders stiffen by a fraction of an inch.

Falk lowered the loupe slowly. Crisp certainty vanished off her face. She tapped the silver rim once, hard, against her open palm.

Falk: "No."

She checked the slide against the brass table in her folio and snapped it shut, gray creeping around her mouth.

Falk: "Noctis retrieval syntax riding on an authentic, unrevoked Aurelis Inter-Academy Transit Key. The matrix matches Deputy Headmaster Caelum Veris's administrative seal from twenty-four years ago."

Aira's pen halted.

Aira: "Say that slower so my rage files it under the right heading."

Falk: "Noctis split President Valmont's seal from inside because Aurelis paperwork told the ward to let it through. And Regalia turned away because the key was never revoked."

Haru: "So nobody broke in."

Falk met my eyes squarely, polite armor burned down to forensic steel.

Falk: "No, Mr. Aerlen. Somebody held the latch open."

Halden looked the color of wet chalk. Valen tapped his sleeve with her cane.

Valen: "Halden. Who ran the Inter-Academy Transfer Desk that autumn?"

Halden swallowed twice.

Halden: "Veris signed emergency clearances while the old Headmaster was ill. He didn't stamp waybills himself."

Falk: "Who did?"

Halden: "Bramwell Orren. Blue Circle registrar. Refused to pulp old customs ledgers twelve years ago and got demoted downstairs to the East Mail and Customs Cellar. Students call it the Dead-Letter Vault."

Across the flags, the shadow under the fifth window shivered, thinned gray, and vanished into plain stone. Three crooked copies already sat dry in three fists.

Then dry percussion climbed through the paving stone into our boot soles.

*Knock. Knock. Knock. Knock. Knock. Knock.*

A measured pause.

*Knock.*

By the planter, color drained off Iven's face beneath his crusting cheek cut. Sera grabbed his torn sleeve at once.

Iven: "Six beats. Pause. Seventh."

Mira caught her breath.

Mira: "Your beat inside the stair."

Iven swallowed hard.

Iven: "I didn't knock. Somebody on the Noctis side just answered."

## Leon Varek - The Man Who Kept the Carbon

The stairs to the East Mail and Customs Cellar smelled of damp plaster, rust, and forty years of receipts nobody wanted upstairs.

Leon Varek took the steps carefully. His right shoulder still throbbed from wedging a shelf off Mira twenty minutes ago, and the cold stone damp bit straight through his cuffs.

At the top landing, Cassian Veyr leaned against the arch in cheerful, menacing boredom.

Cassian: "Guarding the stair. Pure civic spirit. Nothing to do with seeing which administrator develops a passion for basement locks."

Falk: "Obstructing faculty is a disciplinary offense, Mr. Veyr."

Cassian: "Only admiring the grout, Auditor. Terrible mortar. Could take hours."

Falk breathed once through her nose and kept descending, Halden gripping an oil lantern behind her, Mira hugging her pad, Leon last. His thumb found Cael's smooth river-slate stone in his right pocket; on the left, his mother's folded letter sat stiff against his ribs, still uncopied, kept where soft family wording couldn't buy him back.

Falk: "Ground rules. Active Council audit. I ask questions. Miss Castellan, no copying manifests without my written stamp."

Mira only tightened two blackened fingers on her bent pad corner with a face so blankly stubborn Leon almost grinned.

Leon: "Good luck with that."

Falk glanced back.

Falk: "President Valmont insisted on a witness outside Green Circle, Mr. Varek. Do not confuse proximity with standing."

Leon: "My family said something close to that yesterday. Still here."

Falk clocked his bare lapel where the Varek pin belonged, yesterday's cut-off written plain on empty wool, and stepped through the iron-barred cellar door.

Inside stretched a low vaulted room packed with cedar shelves, tin receipt boxes, and wax jars. Three oil lamps hung over a scarred counter smelling of ledger glue, old paper, and cheap chalky mint lozenges.

Behind it sat a man of fifty-six with gray stubble, hunched shoulders, and brass-wire spectacles mended with copper thread. He logged a stub slowly, blotted it with gray rag, and looked up. A worn leather ink-guard cuff strapped his arthritic left knuckle, hiding the faint glow of an old Blue Circle.

His gaze hit Halden first, dry as ash.

Orren: "Professor Halden. Either the roof leaked into the fourth-century stacks, or upstairs lost a receipt older than my knees."

Halden: "Bramwell."

Orren: "Mr. Orren down here. Upstairs only uses first names when they want an exception signed before lunch."

His eyes shifted to Falk's slate wool and silver loupe. His shoulders drew inward half an inch into the stillness of a clerk who had survived three headmasters by turning into shelving.

Orren: "Seal-Audit Division. Early. Unclaimed freight isn't due until Friday."

Falk laid her brass commission beside his blotter.

Falk: "Twenty-four years ago you served as Inter-Academy Transit Registrar during the West Wing smoke. I need the outbound ledger and carbons issued under Deputy Headmaster Veris's Provisional Transit Key Number Seven."

Orren tapped a chalky mint from a small tin and tucked it behind his cheek. He dragged out a gray register and tapped a faded red stamp.

Orren: "Pulped twelve years ago under Retention Code Eleven-B. Signed by the Bursar, countersigned by Archive Maintenance. Not by me, which explains this view. Clean copy of the pulp slip by three."

Halden's knuckles bleached on the lantern handle.

Halden: "Bramwell, please. A sixteen-year-old Green girl named Eris Vell did not die in that smoke. The Fifth Tower burned her name across the flagstones and said she was taken through Noctis. My plaque has been a lie for twenty-four years."

Orren: "Funny how fast upstairs finds a conscience once masonry talks back. Twelve years ago I told you those books shouldn't hit the vat. You said faculty already approved the plaque."

Halden flinched without answer. Falk tapped her badge sharply.

Falk: "A live Noctis spell breached our west arch less than an hour ago riding Veris's unrevoked key. Concealing active records carries Circle suspension and detention under Charter Section Six."

Orren rested his leather-cuffed hand beside the register.

Orren: "Not concealing a thing. Code Eleven-B cleared the shelf. If an old key never closed upstairs, take Section Six to the offices that owned the stamp. Clerks log what lands on the counter and go home."

Leon stared at the leather cuff over Orren's faded Blue Circle and felt cold dread drop under his ribs. Respectable enough to sit inside the machine. Never high enough to own the seal, always high enough to call compliance clean hands. Thirty-eight years straight down the hall Leon had nearly walked yesterday.

Before Falk could speak, Leon set Cael's river-slate stone gently onto Orren's gray pulp register.

Orren: "Unindexed parcel weight."

Mira slid one crooked, soot-smudged sheet beside the stone under the oil lamp.

**MY NAME IS ERIS VELL, AND I WAS NOT PRESENT AS FURNITURE.**

Orren didn't gasp. Only his right index finger twitched, and the mint behind his cheek stopped moving.

Orren: "Eris."

The name escaped before armor caught it.

Orren: "Form 4-C didn't require a given name with a dormitory voucher. She held none. The slip only said *E. Vell*, ink bled down the left leg of the V."

Falk's hand snapped to her folio clasp. Orren's jaw worked under gray stubble as twenty-four years of guilt slipped back into form numbers.

Orren: "Yes, medical waiver blank. Yes, cargo listed a cedar box instead of personal gear. No, I did not hold the wagon."

Halden stared at him, stunned.

Orren: "I was two months from Senior Registrar. Veris sent it down under sealed key saying she had smoke shock and needed quiet debriefing before panic hit the dorms. Sound familiar, Auditor?"

Color touched Falk's cheeks for half a breath.

Falk: "If you kept a duplicate, hand it to Seal-Audit now. I will put it before the Commission untouched."

Orren stared at her spotless wool coat and brass clasp. His hand lingered in rusted habit.

Leon spoke low, Blue Circle to Blue Circle across scarred oak.

Leon: "Yesterday my father sent me an expensive blue-wax draft. Sign that a dead boy's private words belonged to recognized authorities, keep my allowance, my Blue mentorship seat, my summer placement."

Leon rested two fingers on Cael's stone.

Leon: "I refused. They cut me off before lunch. Still hurts, and I'd refuse again."

Orren's eyes rose behind copper-wired spectacles to the bare patch on Leon's collar.

Leon: "You stamped that waybill once because a neat office told you closed doors were safer. Hand it to a locked folder now so nobody looks bruised, and you're signing it twice."

The cellar held still. Orren looked from Cael's stone to Mira's blackened knuckles, then at Falk's open palm.

Orren: "Damn Blue heirs. Throw your pins away and still talk like you own high ground."

Leon: "Renting it badly."

Orren snorted, turned his back on Falk, and knelt stiffly beside an iron-banded freight chest marked **OBSOLETE TARIFF RECEIPTS - HOLD FOR VAT**. His knife sliced the canvas lining inside the lid. Out came a folded sheet of thin purple carbon customs paper smelling faintly of soot.

Falk: "Mr. Orren, under Section Six-"

Orren walked right past her palm and dropped the purple carbon in front of Mira.

Orren: "Arthritic wrist, Auditor. Slippery paper. Landed by the girl with the pencil."

Mira read every line aloud fast so four pairs of ears held it before any clasp shut.

**WAYBILL 7-B. INTER-ACADEMY TRANSIT DESK.**

**SUBJECT: E. VELL - GREEN CIRCLE - REMEDIAL CUSTODIANSHIP TRANSFER (MEDICAL WAIVER: OMITTED).**

**CARGO: ONE (1) CEDAR-BOUND MAINTENANCE INDEX BOX - WEST BELL ANNEX (UNRETURNED).**

**DISPATCHING KEY: DEPUTY HEADMASTER C. VERIS - PROVISIONAL TRANSIT KEY #07.**

**RECEIVING OFFICER (NOCTIS DOMINION COLLEGE): M. VAUST - JUNIOR PROCTOR, CORRECTIVE CONTINUITY.**

Cold climbed Leon's neck at *M. Vaust*. Mira read the cramped graphite slanted along the bottom margin in Orren's younger hand.

**RETURN CARRIAGE EMPTY. BELL-LINE KEY #07 NEVER CLOSED. SECOND ROUTING LINE ADDED UNDER NOCTIS LOCK - SHAFT 7.**

Mira slammed her notebook flat and copied fast and ugly, keeping the bled V and Orren's crooked slant. Leon pinned the curling corner with Cael's river-slate stone.

Falk: "Finish that inside an audit zone, Miss Castellan, and I file formal obstruction."

Mira: "Two L's in Castellan. Include the cedar box Vaust never returned."

Halden stared gray-faced at the purple carbon.

Halden: "Veris handed me her memorial text himself. Said she went back into smoke for a child and never came out."

Orren braced both palms on his counter, suddenly steadier.

Orren: "She did go back for a child. Handed the kid to an infirmary runner at the east stair."

Mira looked up sharply.

Orren: "Then she kept the cedar box when a Red proctor reached for it. Two hours later they marched her to my desk with soot on her cheeks and a transit key clamped on her wrist."

Mira finished **SHAFT 7**, tore her copy off, and shoved it inside her jacket. Orren held the purple carbon out to Falk by one corner.

Orren: "Take the carbon original, Auditor. Keep it in velvet if it helps you sleep. The girl already has my bad handwriting, and four people just heard every word out loud."

Falk stood still three seconds, tucked the carbon into her folio without writing an obstruction notice, and met Orren's eyes.

Falk: "Come upstairs, Mr. Orren. You just named a sitting Headmaster and a Noctis Deputy Director in cross-school transfer fraud. You are done cataloging dead letters."

Orren shook one last chalky mint from his tin and clicked it shut.

Orren: "Better grab my coat. Sunlight up there has terrible manners."

## Haru - The Weight of a Broken Seal

While the four of them went underground, the yard refused to become ordinary afternoon.

Maelin won lunch by threatening to charge the Student Council with dehydration. Finn rotated Green aides along the maintenance lanes every twenty minutes so nobody's wrist cramped holding the perimeter in place of Rhea's suspended seal.

By the infirmary arch, Maelin perched on a crate cleaning Iven Sahl's cheek cut with amber salve. Iven didn't flinch.

Maelin: "Swear at the alcohol, Sahl. Going rigid is shock, not grit. Wrist."

She swapped the bloodied wrap where Noctis's hook had struck his Green Circle for loose fresh gauze. Two paces off, Sera perched quiet with her taped tin cup cradled against her ribs, eyes tracking every passing sleeve.

When Maelin stepped away for linen, Iven brushed the diagonal tear across the front of his borrowed sweater.

Iven: "Borrowed sweater already tore."

Sera looked at the rip, then met his eyes flatly.

Sera: "Still yours. Torn things stay yours unless you throw them out."

Iven's shoulders dropped half an inch.

Sera: "The knock came twice more while they were downstairs."

I felt both, twenty minutes apart: six crisp knocks through the flagstones, a pause, a seventh harder. Under my bare hand, Maelin's blue cloth darkened deeper across my ring fingernail, cold as taut wire. I kept the hand open on my knee where Elira could see it from her bench while Aira and Sovan bent over structural drawings nearby.

Elira looked toward the east cellar doors first.

Elira: "They're back."

Cassian exited stretching with theatrical boredom, followed by Leon, Mira clutching a folded sheet, Halden with an unlit lantern, and Falk beside a hunched gray-stubbled man squinting hard at daylight. At the same instant, Darian Thorne, Nera Vail, and Oric rounded the north arcade and stopped outside the green lane.

Rhea: "Report, Castellan."

Mira laid her crooked copy flat on the archive table beside Eris Vell's half-burned page and the three re-knit strips.

Mira: "Twenty-four years ago, Deputy Headmaster Caelum Veris issued Provisional Transit Key Number Seven on Waybill 7-B. Subject: E. Vell, Green Circle, medical waiver omitted, cargo one cedar maintenance index box from the West Bell Annex. Receiving officer at Noctis: Junior Proctor M. Vaust."

The name dropped cold across the table. Nera's breath caught; Oric's fingers curled once, then opened. Darian stepped to the edge of the green lane, eyes fixed on Mira's jagged print.

Darian: "Deputy Director Maris Vaust was a Junior Proctor in Corrective Continuity twenty-four years ago."

Aira's pen flew.

Aira: "First name logged. Also, before the dates blur: Iven said Noctis's obedience primer called Eris Vell's incident an ancient pre-Circle collapse."

Halden let out a dry, bitter breath.

Halden: "The fire hit the old pre-Circle foundation annex under the West Wing. That masonry predates Circle standardization by two centuries. Noctis didn't misdate it by accident. Calling it a pre-Circle collapse hid living signatures in fog."

Mira tapped the slanted margin line at the bottom of her sheet.

Mira: "Read Mr. Orren's note."

Darian leaned over the wood, voice dropping on the last two words.

Darian: "Return carriage empty. Bell-line key seven never closed. Second routing line added under Noctis lock. Shaft 7."

Nera stiffened.

Nera: "Lower Bell-Shaft Seven."

Rhea: "Identify it for the record."

Darian: "A deep stone bell chute and counterweight run beneath Noctis's Obedience Hall. Upper years never go down there. Staffed strictly by indentured Green Circle riggers from border workshops outside the north wall. Even Red seniors are drilled never to ask what hangs below the fourth grate."

Beside the table, Bramwell Orren stood blinking in pale sun.

He wasn't watching Darian, Rhea's cracked seal, or Falk's brass folio. His eyes rested twenty paces across the yard on Iven's fresh gauze and torn sweater, guarded by a small girl clutching a taped tin cup, and on the unnamed younger transfer girl sorting duplicates down the table with one pale green ring bare above her oversized cuff.

Delayed arithmetic cracked quietly behind his copper-wired spectacles.

Orren: "She was sixteen. The cedar box was too heavy for one arm. She wouldn't set it on my counter because she thought the seal would swallow it."

Leon stood silent beside him, letting the confession sit without turning it into pardon.

Before Falk could pull Orren back to audit wording, the flagstones answered beneath our boots again.

*Knock. Knock. Knock. Knock. Knock. Knock.*

Pause.

*Knock.*

Professor Iris Valen stepped briskly to the west stones, opened her cedar case, and angled Liora Aerlen-Valen's clouded witness-glass down over the damp mortar seam beside the closed arch.

Older amber light broke inside the pane. Liora appeared from the shoulders up, worn thin by decades, eyes dropping to the fading vibration in the stone.

Liora: "My brother's bell-count."

Valen: "Liora. What does seven mean?"

Liora: "Six beats cleared the chain. The pause let high-rank noise pass overhead. The seventh meant a witness was trapped beneath the crown and the stair needed weight from both sides."

Her gaze lifted across the glass and pinned straight onto me behind the green line. My bruised ring fingernail burned cold beneath Maelin's cloth, tracing a long strained spine of limestone running miles underground northeast.

Static tore the glass edge as Liora's voice thinned.

Liora: "If Noctis owns the top of that shaft, do not send a Black Circle down the stair."

Aira: "Specify why, ancient glass voice."

Liora: "My brother built it so no single crown could seize it. The ribs balance only under shared low-draw hands. One heavy Black Circle step inside that arch shears the keystone and drops the ceiling straight onto whoever is knocking."

The pane went white and dark before Valen could ask her brother's name.

Every instinct in my back hated the rule, and every cold tremor under my fingernail confirmed it. One push from me would crush whoever stood at the far end.

Haru: "She's right. The bedrock seam is strained thin. If I step in, the stone drops."

Falk looked from my bare bandaged finger to the closed door.

Falk: "Then the corridor stays shut until the Commission clears engineers for both ends."

Iven jumped off the infirmary bench before Maelin could grab him.

Iven: "Nobody in Bell-Shaft Seven hits the seventh beat by accident. If a rigger down there is hammering dry stone hard enough to reach Aurelis, someone upstairs just told them the shaft won't stand till morning."

Darian nodded grimly.

Darian: "Vaust never patches a blown covert route. She buries the chute and whoever swept its floor."

From her wooden bench, Elira cut across the rising argument.

Elira: "We do not need to open the door wide enough to walk through."

Every head turned. Elira pointed to the narrow iron-lipped acoustic grate recessed into damp mortar two feet left of the service arch. Aira lifted her measurements while Sovan unrolled his charcoal sketch of the bell foundation.

Elira: "Architect Reith's sketch shows an old ventilation seam parallel to the folded doorway, clear of the load-bearing hinge."

Sovan squinted down, startled respect crossing his tired face.

Sovan: "Bricked thin with lime mortar. Three inches wide. Too cramped for a body, an archive box, or a full retrieval ward to anchor inside."

Elira: "And I won't force a rigid Yellow seal into it. Rigid pressure lets Noctis ride back through, just like Veris's key split Rhea's barrier."

She lifted one palm slightly, tracing an angled bend in the cool air, the exact release-seam craft she had mapped during recovery.

Elira: "I cut a crooked gold release-seam along that three-inch line, angled upward on our side with an open vent at the lip. Sound and small items cross. If Noctis shoots tracing-ink back, the bend catches the pressure and blows it harmlessly upward into air."

Aira stared at the drawing.

Aira: "A crooked talking pipe with an automatic spit-valve for Noctis lawyers."

Elira: "Roughly."

Maelin planted herself squarely between Elira and the mortar wall.

Maelin: "Six minutes."

Elira: "Eight."

Maelin: "Six minutes, seated three paces off stone, shoulder brace tight, pulse charm flat on your knee. On six minutes, or the second it shifts amber, you drop it even if the first Headmaster is mid-sentence."

Elira checked her breath honestly.

Elira: "Six minutes. Seated. I drop on your count."

Maelin pointed two fingers at me.

Maelin: "Behind the green line, Aerlen. No channeling into her seam."

Elira looked up once, gold eyes bright under dark shadows.

Elira: "Words only, Haru. If stone shifts, tell me aloud."

Haru: "Words only."

Falk stood silent beside Sovan two long seconds, fingers touching her lapel loupe without tapping it.

Falk: "Open a border seam without Council clearance, President Valmont, and I cannot certify the site clean."

Rhea looked down at her cracked, suspended mark, then straight into Falk's eyes.

Rhea: "You already suspended my seal, Auditor. Which makes me a student witness observing an emergency structures test with Architect Reith. Stand beside us and hear every word, or go inside and wait for somebody else's summary."

Sovan uncapped his pen over his smudged sheet.

Sovan: "For the record, Auditor, I am staying."

Falk exhaled once through her nose, stepped beside Sovan outside the green lane, and opened her brass folio to a fresh page.

Falk: "Six minutes, Miss Vale. Not a second past Healer Orr's mark."

Iven knelt near the narrow mortar grate with Sera two paces behind his shoulder. Above us, afternoon thinned toward dusk while the fifth green window cast a calm pale square across the stones.

## Petra Brin - Six Beats and a Broken Rope

Lower Bell-Shaft Seven smelled of wet limestone, iron grease, and coal smoke dragged down from Obedience Hall vents two hundred feet overhead.

Petra Brin preferred grease to marble polish. Polish meant proctors. Grease meant hoist chains ran smooth over iron sheaves, and smooth chains warned you before snapping.

She braced one lime-dusted boot on the catwalk rail and laid her bare palm against damp masonry beside the counterweight guide. Her Green Circle answered quiet beneath callused skin, reading load through bone. Rope tension: sixty-two percent, frayed three strands above collar two. Stone vibration: unsettled along the southeast seam. Upper draft: stagnant, carrying the dry sting of freshly slaked lime barrels stacked on landing three.

That dry bite dropped a stone into her gut.

Petra wiped grease on her charcoal maintenance smock and checked her crew. Fourteen-year-old Willem greased guide-pins in quick, nervous strokes; fifteen-year-old Hettie counted knots down the hemp signal line; two iron landings up, sixteen-year-old Dax hugged the vent grille pretending to scrape scale while listening for boots in the upper hall.

Three border kids on maintenance indentures, worth zero honest obituaries if a shaft settled over their heads.

Outside Noctis's north wall, Petra's mother ran evening bellows at Brin Ironworks on a winter coal charter tied directly to Petra's conduct slip as Rigger-Third. One refusal upstairs froze the charter by sunrise and killed half the border ropewalk's winter wage. Down here survival had never been speeches. It was load, counterweight, and keeping your skull beneath the iron lintel.

Until mid-morning today, when some privileged idiot ten miles off at Aurelis blew the bottom right out of her drain-seal.

Hours earlier, before midday bells even sounded overhead, the black ink-drain recessed in the pit floor had blasted backward in a violent hiss of freezing blue water and wet paper scrap, slamming into the three-ton cast-bronze counterweight overhead and raining rust down Willem's collar.

Worse was what the blowback peeled off the dry-stone wall beside the pit.

Three years ago, sent down with white limewash to cover seepage, Petra's scraper had hit raw letters cut deep into mortar with a rusted nail:

**E. VELL - SEVEN BEATS TO AIR**

Petra didn't know who E. Vell was then, only that the carved stone warning in the Obedience Hall wore a jagged brow scar stamped **FIRST DISOBEDIENCE**, and whoever scratched Shaft Seven knew where cold air bled from nowhere on Noctis maps. Petra had watered down her wash before painting over the words, thick enough to pass a proctor's lantern from the ladder, thin enough to flake off later.

The mid-morning surge stripped the wash bare in gray sheets.

Petra had tapped the mortar twice since midday with her steel marlinspike, six beats, pause, seventh, checking whether the flood had cracked her arch. Nobody had answered then, only a hollow tremor proving the seam still ran through.

Up on landing two, Dax tapped the iron pipe twice flat with a brass wrench.

*Senior rank coming down.*

Petra threw one crisp downward hand-sign behind her hip. Willem hid his grease tin and stood by the brake wheel; Hettie dropped her eyes, hands open at her sides. Petra kicked an oil rag over the fallen limewash flakes, planted herself in front of the wall scratch, and kept one thumb near the copper rigger's whistle at her throat.

Measured boots rang down the iron ladder rungs, slow and deliberate. Two Red ward-masons came first with slate rods, then spotless black heels stepping around every grease drop.

Deputy Director Maris Vaust stepped off the bottom rung without touching the rail, iron-gray hair pinned tight enough to ache. She glanced once at the shattered black drain-seal, once at the drying water lines, and lifted her eyes past Petra's shoulder to the exposed wall.

Petra hadn't blocked wide enough. Two words shone chalk-bright in the lamp glow: **TO AIR.**

Vaust smiled, the narrow flat smile of a locked door that had never pretended to open from inside.

Director Vaust: "Rigger-Third Brin."

Petra: "Director."

Director Vaust: "The lower acoustic drain suffered a boundary surge before midday."

Petra: "Pressure blew back around third bell, Director. Shook the hoist two links. Brake held. No drop."

Director Vaust: "Commendable."

A Red mason pressed a heavy black-waxed card into Petra's greasy palm.

**STRUCTURAL CONDEMNATION ORDER - LOWER BELL-SHAFT SEVEN**

**DIAGNOSIS: ACOUSTIC FAULT CONTAMINATED BY AURELIS BOUNDARY LEAKAGE**

**REMEDY: FULL SETTING-LIME MORTAR FLOOD AND PERMANENT COLLAPSE WITHIN THIRTY-SIX HOURS**

Petra did the arithmetic in half a second. Wet setting-lime dumped down landing three would fill the pit to catwalk two in twenty minutes, chewing skin off bone before setting hard as slate.

Director Vaust: "Before the pour starts at first light, the cast-bronze counterweight must be decoupled so the iron drum can be salvaged. Your junior apprentices will remain overnight to unpin the lower locking collar."

Ice shot down Petra's spine.

Twenty years of cellar drip had eaten the secondary catch half through; driving the lower pin free without rigging a double hemp bridle from landing three wouldn't lower the drum. It would shear the rusted catch on tap one and drop three tons of bronze through the catwalk onto whoever stood under it, followed by wet setting-lime burying four bodies and a blown drain-seal under six feet of official stone.

Four quiet funerals stamped **RIGGING ERROR**.

Petra: "The secondary catch on that collar is rotted past tolerance, Director. Unpinning from the lower walk puts my three juniors dead under the drop line. Give me two hours and fresh hemp from north store, and I'll clear it myself."

Vaust regarded her with cool disappointment, like a reliable hinge starting to squeak.

Director Vaust: "North store is sealed for inventory until after the pour, Rigger Brin. Your mother's winter coal renewal for Brin Ironworks crosses my desk at week's end. I would hate to note insubordination during a structural hazard on her ledger."

Air shrank inside the shaft. Willem's breath hitched once before Hettie nudged his boot silently.

Director Vaust: "Keep the juniors on the lower pin. Report to the upper grate when the collar clears."

Vaust climbed back toward the Obedience Hall with her two Red masons trailing behind.

Petra didn't move until the upper iron hatch clanged shut two hundred feet overhead and the heavy bolt slid home with a dead ring.

Willem: "Petra... that pin's rotted through. If we tap it-"

Petra: "We aren't tapping it."

Hettie: "She locked the hatch, Petra. And she named your mum's furnace."

Petra dropped the stiff condemnation card into the grease bucket, watching black oil soak across Vaust's seal.

Petra: "My mum survived three winters without Noctis coal before I was born, and she'd hit me with furnace tongs if I let Vaust flatten three kids under bronze to buy fuel. Dax!"

Dax leaned pale over landing two.

Dax: "Yeah?"

Petra: "Stay glued to that grate. Hear lime barrels rolling near the sluice early, tap three fast on the pipe. Hettie, Willem, get clear of the drop line under the north buttress."

Hettie dragged Willem under the heavy arch beam without arguing.

Petra dropped to one knee beneath **E. VELL - SEVEN BEATS TO AIR**, drew her steel marlinspike, and tapped slowly three inches down. *Solid. Solid. Hollow.*

Right below **SEVEN**, a narrow brass inspection plate sat flush in dark stone, slotted screws buried under gray grime. Petra jammed her steel point into the seam, threw her forearm weight behind it, and snapped the rusted screwheads off.

Inside the dry fist-wide hollow lay a hammered tin tube plugged with beeswax.

She thumb-cracked the wax and shook out a tight roll wrapped twice in heavy oiled rigging cloth. Unrolling it under the yellow oil lamp slowed her heartbeat hard.

Three items lay inside.

First, tough brown drafting paper carrying a dark charcoal rubbing of a wooden box lock: seven interlocking maintenance notches in an uneven ring, zero Circle crests.

Second, a water-stained transfer list folded in quarters: twelve lines in stubborn block print spanning twenty-four years, headed **INTER-ACADEMY QUIET TRANSFERS - KEY #07 / SHAFT 7**, opening with **WAYBILL 7-B: E. VELL (AURELIS)** and marching through eleven more missing Green and Blue witnesses routed out of Aurelis, Valemere, and Drakenshard into Noctis's basement, three dated inside the last five years.

Third, wedged inside the fold, sat a small pale round disc of Aurelis office wax, still faintly tacky at its bevel, stamped with a sharp counter-foil date from **three weeks ago**.

Petra stared at the fresh wax, then flipped the charcoal rubbing over.

Across the blank back sat four cramped pencil lines dated four years ago. Petra read them fast, feeling hairs lift along her forearms.

*Four years ago.* The warning statue upstairs hadn't died in any fire, and she hadn't died down here either.

Petra rolled all three back into the oiled cloth and whipped the hemp knot tight with teeth and one hand. Far above Dax's head came the dull rumble of iron barrows rolling sacks across landing three. Vaust wasn't waiting thirty-six hours.

Clean rage hit Petra's chest, half at Vaust and half at whatever reckless Aurelis idiots had blown her drain before midday without checking who stood under three tons of bronze.

She gripped her marlinspike, braced her boots on the pit rim, and struck the dry mortar seam beside Eris's wall scratch hard.

*Clang. Clang. Clang. Clang. Clang. Clang.*

One clean beat in the dark.

*Clang.*

Three seconds of dripping silence passed.

Then the mortar beside her boot hissed softly and parted in a crooked hairline slit three inches wide. Warm gold light spilled over her lime-dusted knuckles, angled sideways like a cut pressure vent. Cool evening air curled through, smelling of cold winter pine, courtyard lamps, and open dusk sky.

From the far side came a cautious boy's voice whispering the count across ten miles of stone.

Iven: "Six, pause, seven. Who's holding the rope?"

## Haru - The Voice on the Other Side

Evening lamps lit the gravel as Elira shaped the crooked gold seam.

She sat steady on her bench three paces off the mortar grate, shoulders squared, fingers tilted slightly upward so the Yellow line stayed bent instead of rigid. Maelin stood two paces left with her pocket watch flipped open.

Maelin: "Thirty seconds spent. Five thirty left. Stop holding your breath when the mortar hisses."

Dry lime parted beside the grate in a jagged three-inch vertical slit rimmed warm gold. Cold draft curled out carrying wet limestone, iron grease, and northern coal soot.

Outside Finn's glowing green lanes, the yard tightened without crowding: Rhea with her suspended seal lowered, Falk stiff beside Sovan's smudged paper, Bramwell Orren hunched deep in his cellar coat blinking at twilight, and Cassian perched high on the arcade rail watching the administrative arch.

I stood three paces behind the green line, bare left hand open. The instant Elira's seam met far mortar, Maelin's blue cloth turned biting cold on my ring fingernail as bedrock snapped taut miles northeast, humming with an iron hoist chain.

Iven knelt two feet off the slit, Sera half a step behind him hugging her taped tin cup. He tapped the dry stone.

Iven: "Six, pause, seven. Who's holding the rope?"

Two heartbeats of wet draft. Then a rasping, furious older girl's voice scraped through the stone.

Voice: "First, whichever Aurelis lunatic shoved water up my drain this morning owes me dry boots and three hours of chain-check. Second..."

A heavy iron link clanked miles underground.

Voice: "Keep your Black Circle back off the stone. I can feel him making the mortar sweat from ten miles away."

Every head snapped toward me. I lifted both boots and stepped two measured paces back across the paving stones without arguing.

Under my bare fingernail, the tight cold wire eased half a notch. Strange how a Noctis rigger ten miles away trusted distance faster than half the Council.

Voice: "Huh. He actually listened. Rigger-Third Petra Brin, Lower Bell-Shaft Seven. Who's on the line, and why do you sound twelve?"

Iven: "Iven Sahl. Former Noctis conduit. You're in Shaft Seven?"

Petra: "Where else, the winter conservatory? Talk fast. Top hatch is bolted outside and lime barrows are rolling on landing three."

Mira dropped onto one knee two feet clear of the slit.

Mira: "Mira Castellan, Green Circle, Aurelis. We recovered Eris Vell's name and pulled Waybill 7-B naming Deputy Director Maris Vaust."

A harsh bark of laughter rasped through the stone.

Petra: "Print a ribbon. You people talk like walls have politics. Walls have load."

Haru: "Tell us what Vaust ordered."

Petra paused a half-beat at the low voice from behind the line, then cut right to iron facts.

Petra: "Your morning flood stripped limewash off Eris's wall scratch. Vaust just condemned Shaft Seven. Setting-lime by dawn, and she locked my three juniors, Dax, Hettie, and Willem, under a rotted bronze collar to bury the drain."

Leon swore low by the fountain. Maelin glanced at her watch.

Maelin: "Three ten, Vale."

Sweat shone along Elira's hairline. Her wrist charm held green, ticking fast.

Elira: "Holding."

Mira: "Can your crew hold clear of the drop line until we find a counter-order?"

Petra: "No speeches. Pulled a tin cache behind Eris's wall mark. Shoving the oiled cloth through your crack now. Don't smear the wax, and keep lime off three kids before sunrise."

Iven: "Petra, wait-"

Petra: "Catch!"

A grime-streaked bundle in heavy oiled cloth tied with hemp poked through the three-inch slit. Mira caught it behind the green line and yanked back as the draft reversed.

Black ink shot right behind the cloth, a slick Noctis tracing-script snapping for Mira's knuckles.

Nobody looked at me. Elira tilted her right wrist half an inch upward. The crooked gold bend caught the black thread and blew the whole tracing spell out the vented top lip into open dusk air, hissing into harmless gray steam.

Maelin: "Five fifty! Drop it, Vale!"

Elira shut her fingers on the exact breath. The gold slit vanished into gray stone. She slumped half an inch as her pulse charm flickered amber, paused, and steadied green. I waited for her tired nod before steadying her elbow while Maelin checked her wrist.

Maelin: "Five fifty-six. Nobody speak to her for four minutes."

Ten paces off, Mira sawed the hemp knot with Aira's penknife between two lanterns and spread the oiled cloth flat. Everyone closed around the table, Orren squinting behind copper-wired spectacles, Falk directly across with her silver loupe unclipped.

First came the water-stained transfer list. Aira read the heading aloud.

Aira: "Inter-Academy Quiet Transfers. Key Number Seven. Shaft Seven."

Twelve numbered lines marched across twenty-four years. Mira read fast: **Waybill 7-B: E. Vell, Aurelis**, twenty-four years ago; **M. Halek, Valemere**, twenty-one years ago; **T. Sorrel, Drakenshard**, nineteen years ago. Green maintenance aides and Blue junior archivists erased from three rosters under tags like *remedial withdrawal* and *archived fire loss*. Four from Aurelis, four from Valemere, four from Drakenshard. Three dated within the last five years.

Halden braced both palms hard on the wood.

Halden: "All three academies."

Sovan stared at his smudged notebook, shoulders locking stiff.

Sovan: "Not one rogue deputy hiding a fire. A twenty-four-year cross-school quiet disposal line."

Darian's jaw locked while Nera covered her mouth.

Darian: "Other academies shipped their inconvenient witnesses right down our chute."

Aira's pen raced.

Aira: "Three copies before midnight. Nobody calls twelve names a clerical slip."

Across the table, Auditor Falk lifted the pale wax disc with two spotless fingers, angled it under the lantern, and lowered her silver loupe over the bevel.

Her fingers didn't twitch for three long breaths. Every drop of color left her face.

The silver loupe slipped down and clicked hard against her open palm. *Tink.*

Rhea: "Auditor Falk?"

Falk: "This isn't a twenty-four-year-old stamp."

Orren squinted close.

Orren: "No. Old tallow goes chalk-brittle on the bevel. That rim is soft."

Falk stared at the wax, every layer of tidy procedural armor cracked off her face.

Falk: "Seven o'clock border. That micro-chip."

Rhea: "Auditor?"

Falk: "Veris dropped his primary office seal matrix onto a stone inkstand six months ago. I logged that chip myself when my Commission opened after the tournament collapsed."

Every hand around the table halted. Falk set the disc back beside Mira's inkwell, voice gone blade-flat.

Falk: "He stamped this counter-foil less than three weeks ago. Inside my own audit window. He wasn't hiding an old lapse. He was keeping the hinge greased while we stood outside his door."

Mira lifted the third piece: tough brown drafting paper carrying the charcoal rubbing of a seven-notched wooden box lock. She flipped it toward the lanterns, and her ink-stained fingers stopped dead.

Leon: "Mira?"

Mira looked across the lanterns at me, dark eyes wide, then read four cramped four-year-old pencil lines aloud into the quiet dark.

**CORIN AERLEN'S ROUTE STILL HOLDS.**

**SIX PIECES OF THE CHARTER REMAIN UNDER SHAFT SEVEN.**

**DO NOT LET VAUST SEAL THE BELL.**

**ERIS VELL. NOT DEAD. NOT FURNITURE.**

Cold hit behind my sternum harder than the wind.

*Corin Aerlen.*

My own surname sat in four-year-old graphite on oiled Noctis cloth. And an Aerlen had built a staircase engineered to shatter if one Black Circle stepped inside to force it alone. Whoever carried my blood before me hadn't built a throne. He had built a trap against becoming one.

Beside me, Professor Iris Valen drew a sharp breath, gloved fingers locking around her cedar case.

Valen: "Corin. Liora's brother. The Aerlen who built the servant stair beneath Regalia."

Elira looked up from her bench, pale and fierce under Maelin's glare, reading the shock across my jaw before anyone else caught it.

Haru: "He built the stair to break under someone like me."

Elira's gaze held mine, steady and unsentimental.

Elira: "Good. Then we won't let him be wrong about you."

Mira stared back down at the rubbing in fierce disbelief.

Mira: "Four years ago she was still alive down there, and she hid six pieces of his Charter under Shaft Seven."

Iven spun toward the closed arch.

Iven: "And Vaust is rolling lime barrels toward the sluice right now."

Before anyone could lay out a move, a bell rang across the dark courtyard.

Not the cracked west bell, late and off-pitch. Sharp, crisp, brassy metal striking from the high roof of the Headmaster's administrative wing.

Directly above Rhea's suspended notice, above Cael's copied line and Leon's pinned family refusal, bright gilded script flared across the public board. Not Foundation authority. Not Noctis matters. Not Black Circle jurisdiction. Just the single narrow corner Magistra Voss had left inside his hands yesterday.

**BY ORDER OF HEADMASTER CAELUM VERIS - ORDINARY CAMPUS STRUCTURAL MAINTENANCE.**

**WEST BELL SERVICE ARCH CONDEMNED AS UNSTABLE MASONRY. PERMANENT SEALING SCHEDULED AT FIRST LIGHT.**

Across the lantern-lit table, Auditor Tamsin Falk lifted her gaze from the three-week-old wax disc to Veris's gilded seal overhead, her fingers closing shut around her silver loupe chain until her knuckles went white.

Under my bare ring fingernail, Maelin's blue cloth pulled biting cold from both ends of the bedrock at once, north and south tightening like a vise around a hollow stair.

High above the cracked west bell, the fifth green window burned steady against the night.

First light was less than eight hours away.

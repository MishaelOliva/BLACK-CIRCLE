# Episode 11: **Wounded City Protocol**

Wounded City Protocol opened beneath the old chapel at noon.

That timing was the first insult.

Noctis had spent enough of the Foundation Trial losing in rooms where records could answer back. The Protocol was their reply: move the argument from testimony into live crisis, then dare Aurelis to prove its rescue ethics under full public light. Even with Council Investigator Senna Voss sequestered in the morning record office since dawn, Lucien did not ask for a closed floor.

He wanted an audience. Valemere. Two minor academy delegations that had lingered past the tournament to watch the Foundation Trial. Selected Aurelis faculty. Student Council recorders. Green Circle public witnesses. Noctis called it transparency. Rhea called it leverage.

Aira called it "setting the room on fire and inviting people to admire the smoke."

The Foundation accepted the expanded gallery because Wounded City Protocol was, on paper, an exercise in public rescue ethics. Refusing observers would have made Aurelis look afraid of its own doctrine. Lucien understood optics.

So Rhea made optics answer back.

Every observer had to sign a witness-conduct sheet before stepping onto the gallery stair. No private relay tools. No unsupervised mirrors. No delegation correction phrases. No removal of notes. No turning away from harm and claiming uncertainty afterward.

That last condition made several adults stiffen. Mira noticed.

She stood beside the iron archway with a stack of green-edged forms and a face that looked ready to drop through the flagstones rather than challenge a visiting examiner.

When the first examiner tried to wave the form aside, Mira's knees shook.

Her hand did not move.

Mira: "Public witness terms require a signature."

Examiner: "I am here as faculty, not a student."

Mira looked down at the sheet, then back up at him.

Mira: "Then your handwriting is probably better."

The examiner stared. Rhea, three paces away, said nothing at all, letting the council badge at her shoulder hold the silence. The examiner took the pen and signed.

Mira's shoulders dropped only after he vanished down the stair.

Aira drifted past behind her and murmured:

Aira: "Excellent use of stationery."

Mira whispered back:

Mira: "I thought I was going to faint."

Aira: "Most civic institutions are held together by people refusing to faint until the ink dries."

The Foundation chamber below the chapel unfolded out of ancient stone and silver mist. Paved streets rose from the empty floor. Doorways split open along blank walls. Mullioned windows formed with no rooms behind them, and a crooked bell tower leaned out over a square that had never existed yet somehow looked remembered.

Seven wounded markers stood at the center: neither wooden dummies nor stolen illusions of people we knew. That would have been too crude after yesterday. Each marker was an outline woven from pale light, flickering with a distinct injury: channel collapse, frost binding, mana starvation, memory echo, pressure burn, relay hook, and one empty outline carrying no visible wound at all.

The seventh made my teeth ache.

Aira took one look at it and hated it on sight.

Aira: "An invisible wound marker. Wonderful. We are being tested by architecture with a sadistic streak."

Rhea took her post on the command threshold, a red council seal hovering beside her shoulder.

President Valmont: "Conditions?"

The Foundation's voice stayed level as it echoed down from the bell tower.

Foundation: "Wounded City Protocol tests response priority under conflicting witness obligations. Participants must extract all viable markers to safe zones while maintaining witness autonomy and preventing unlawful isolation."

Leon flexed his hands.

Leon: "That almost sounds fair."

Nera's reply was flat.

Nera: "It is not."

Lucien stepped onto the far side of the square with Magister Rusk and three Noctis observers. He wore formal black again, though today his collar hung unfastened at the throat, a small break with Noctis stiffness that made him look less like a student and more like a blade pretending to be tired.

Lucien: "It is fair enough to make unfairness useful."

Cassian's Red Circle flared along his forearm.

Cassian: "I am going to enjoy the day you run out of elegant sentences."

Lucien: "Then I hope you are patient."

Elira stepped onto the activity floor beside me. Under her collar, the black-and-gold pendant gave one warm pulse against her skin, then went quiet.

Cael entered with Nera on one side and Leon on the other. His healer seal had been reinforced, yet the silver thread around his injured hand had darkened overnight. He had slept even less than I had, and that was saying something.

Before Aira could unroll the maps, Cael stepped three paces out of formation.

Only three paces, yet Leon's hand twitched toward him and stopped halfway. Cael caught the motion.

Cael: "I am not running."

Leon: "I did not say you were."

Cael: "Your shoulder did."

Leon looked mildly offended by the accuracy.

Nera stayed planted where she stood. That cost her more than moving would have; every year of her Noctis training had drilled her to close distance around an injured teammate before anyone could accuse her of negligence or attachment.

Cael walked to the edge of the empty seventh marker. No visible fracture. No light around the outline. Just a human-shaped absence waiting for a room to decide isolation counted as care.

He looked at it for a long time. Long enough that Lucien's gaze sharpened across the square. Long enough that Rhea did not interrupt.

Cael: "At Noctis, they call this recovery."

The chamber fell still enough to carry his voice. Cael did not look toward the witness rail; looking at us would have made the words ask for comfort.

Cael: "If you are overwhelmed, they remove witnesses. If you are injured, they reduce variables. If you are frightened, they call familiar faces contamination. The room gets quieter every time. Cleaner."

His injured hand flexed once. The silver thread around it darkened a shade deeper.

Cael: "Eventually quiet starts to feel like proof that you are improving, because if nobody is there to make you afraid, you stop shaking where anyone can see."

No one spoke over the silence. Keeping the right quiet took effort. Aira's pen hovered above her map and did not drop.

Cael turned to me then. He ignored my Green Circle and looked straight at my hands.

Cael: "If the seventh marker opens, I will want to solve it by leaving."

Leon: "No."

The word came out too fast, too blunt. Cael almost smiled.

Cael: "That is why I am saying it now."

Nera's voice thinned.

Nera: "Leaving can be a symptom."

Cael nodded once.

Cael: "Or a strategy."

Elira stepped forward, planting herself beside the line he had drawn rather than between him and the marker.

Elira: "Then we ask which one before we obey it."

Cael let out a slow breath that seemed to cost him more than half his testimony had.

Cael: "Good."

He stepped back into formation without apologizing. Leon let him take his place without crowding him. That quiet restraint mattered almost as much as catching him would have yesterday.

As the chamber chimes warned of activation, Nera withdrew from the floor to take her assigned Noctis observer post along the upper witness rail beside Oric, leaving the active roster clear. Mira and the Green Circle witnesses filled the public record rail above the square, each pair armed with a timestamp crystal, ink, and duplicate ledger sheets. Mira caught my eye from above. She looked terrified.

Then she raised her spare pencil, ready.

Aira anchored the support mark at the edge of the square and snapped three projection maps open in the air.

Aira: "First rule. Nobody chases symbolic bait alone."

Leon: "Define symbolic bait."

Aira: "Anything that looks like your guilt wearing a costume."

Cassian: "That is annoyingly clear."

The activity bell struck.

The first marker collapsed before we could cross half the square.

Stone folded inward above the eastern lane. Beneath the buckling arch, the pale outline dropped to one knee, clutching a chest where its channel-light stuttered in ragged bursts. Every failed pulse darkened another masonry block overhead.

Aira flung the first map forward.

Aira: "Eastern arch. Leon braces. Cassian clears debris. Elira and Cael assess the marker. Haru watches the lane."

The city disagreed.

Three alleys rotated around us with the grinding shriek of stone dragged across stone. The eastern arch slid west. The dry fountain surged upward between our team and the marker, while a fresh wall shot up behind Cael before Leon could cross to him.

Aira's map remained cleanly, stubbornly still. Wrong.

She stared at the lying projection for half a heartbeat, then crushed it in her fist.

Aira: "Map is compromised. Eyes up. Call what you physically see."

Admitting the map was useless saved more seconds than pretending control ever could.

Leon slammed one palm toward the dry fountain. Water answered from old pipes beneath the paving, jetting out of four carved stone mouths to arc over the rising wall. He caught each stream in a separate blue thread and braided them into a roaring brace beneath the arch. The falling weight slammed down onto it. His knees buckled an inch and held.

Leon: "Now would be a lovely time for everyone else to become competent."

Cassian charged straight at the blocked lane without waiting for cover.

He withheld open flame; the marker lay too close, and the false city had seeded choking dust through every seam. One careless spark would turn rescue into cremation. Instead, he drove pinpoint heat into the metal pins buried inside the masonry. Red light crawled down his fingers. The iron pins swelled, split their stone housings, and popped free one by one.

Cassian snatched the first white-hot pin in his gloved hand before it could strike Cael's temple.

The stench of scorched leather cut straight through the dust.

Cael: "Your glove."

Cassian: "Still technically a glove."

He whipped the pin aside and drove his shoulder into the loosened blocks. Cael threw his weight beside him without waiting to be asked. Together they cracked open a gap barely wide enough for Elira to slip through sideways.

The marker's channel-light sputtered again. The arch dropped six inches.

Leon's breath caught. Spray sheared off the water brace and flashed to ice across the flagstones where frost from the second marker had started bleeding into the first lane.

I could have stopped the whole collapse in half a breath.

The solution unspooled complete inside my head: lift weight off twelve keystones, warm three water lines, steady the marker's pulse with one surgical correction. Zero risk. Zero delay.

And zero proof that anyone else in this square had been allowed to matter.

The Black Circle pressed hard against my ribs. Copper filled my mouth.

High above us, Mira shouted down from the witness rail.

Mira: "The arch has Green maintenance marks! Lower left block!"

Every head snapped toward where she pointed.

A shallow copper groove sat half-buried beneath centuries of white polish, left off every academy blueprint because service corridors never counted as civic architecture. A Green Circle handprint would match the plate to the millimeter.

Mira stared down at her own wrist, then at the iron service ladder dropping from the witness rail to the street.

Mira: "Can a witness enter the field?"

Rhea's council seal flared crimson.

President Valmont: "Only by choice. The route is unstable."

Mira climbed down anyway, clumsy in her hurry. Her shoe slipped on the final rung, dumping her onto the paving hard enough to skin one palm raw. Her pencil skittered beneath the fountain. She stared at the blood beading across the heel of her hand, then pressed forward toward the copper plate.

Mira: "Does blood interfere?"

Professor Halden leaned over the rail above.

Professor Halden: "With the spell? No. With my ability to remain calm? Considerably."

Mira slapped her Green Circle onto the copper plate. A faint copper glow ran along the maintenance groove, plain and workmanlike.

The arch groaned and shifted its load into ancient service channels. Leon's water brace surged two inches higher, widening the gap. Elira reached the flickering marker and set both hands near its shoulders without closing contact.

Elira: "Can you answer?"

The outline dimmed. No voice answered.

Cael dropped to one knee on the far side of the broken wall, reading the irregular pulse.

Cael: "It cannot answer while the channel is failing. Noctis would log silence as emergency consent."

Elira: "Aurelis will not."

She lowered her Yellow Circle until its gold light lay level with the marker instead of towering over it. Thin gold threads slipped into the copper service channel Mira held open, not overwriting the failing pulse, simply giving it a steady frame to catch its rhythm.

The next pulse arrived faint but whole. Then a second.

The outline lifted one hand and pointed down the cracked western alley. Choice.

Aira had already written off the ruined map. Perched on a cracked fountain block with stone dust in her hair, she read the shifting city purely by sight.

Aira: "Western alley chosen. Leon, drop the arch on my count. Cassian, carry only if the marker takes your hand. Mira, hold that plate open."

Mira's skinned palm shook against the copper.

Mira: "How long?"

Aira gauged the sagging arch, the alley mouth, and Leon's trembling arms.

Aira: "Eleven seconds."

Mira: "You are guessing."

Aira: "I am."

Her pen hovered over her ledger margin, the admission visibly unsettling her, yet the honesty gave Mira room to choose.

Mira: "Then count loud."

We moved.

At seven, the paving rolled underfoot.

At eight, Leon dropped one water line and nearly went to his knees.

At nine, Cassian offered his unburned hand, and the marker clasped it.

At ten, Elira cleared the maintenance groove.

At eleven, Mira tore her bleeding palm off the copper plate.

The arch thundered down behind us, blasting chalky dust across the square. Nobody lay underneath it.

Mira sat straight down where she stood, letting out a laugh pitched half an octave too high.

Mira: "Your count was wrong."

Aira dropped beside her, tore a clean strip from her useless paper map, and bound Mira's scraped palm tight.

Aira: "By less than a second."

Mira: "That is still wrong."

Aira: "Count it wrong in the margin."

She logged the timestamp without looking up, offering no excuse and no joke. The first marker crossed the western safe line whole, leaving dust settling over four bruised teammates who had moved without waiting for rank.

The Foundation bell chimed the rescue. Lucien did not applaud.

Before we finished coughing dust out of our throats, the frost-binding marker woke.

White cold detonated out of the dry fountain. Ice raced across the basin, bridged the paving seams, and wrapped Cassian's discarded metal pin in jagged frost, hurling it back across the square like a white spear aimed straight through Mira's spine.

Leon saw it half a beat late. I saw it early.

A shouted warning would never beat that throw.

Haru: "Down!"

I slammed my false Green Circle against the flagstones. I kept the Black Circle locked down, letting only a razor sliver of true precision bleed through the disguise to kick the spear's flight off-axis by the width of a fingernail.

It cleared Mira's shoulder. It did not clear me.

Ice sheared past my raised forearm, splitting my sleeve before biting through the fabric below my ribs. Heat flared along the cut, then vanished under creeping numbness.

Elira spun at the copper scent of blood, her composure cracking open.

Haru: "Marker first."

She hated the order. She obeyed the triage anyway.

Elira: "Aira, pressure on his side. Leon, with me. Cassian, can you still push heat?"

Cassian held up his scorched glove.

Cassian: "Offended you asked."

Frost had encased the second marker from the shins up inside the fountain basin. Its outline stood trapped in clear ice, conscious and rigid. Every flare of rescue heat caused the crystalline shell to cinch tighter.

Cael scrambled close enough to read the runes crawling underneath the glaze.

Cael: "Punitive thaw. It reads rescue heat as resistance and increases crushing pressure."

Leon: "So fire kills it faster."

Cael: "Uninvited fire."

That single distinction flipped the geometry of the problem.

Elira dropped to both knees beside the ice.

Elira: "If you can hear me, blink once for warmth. Twice for hold."

The trapped light blinked once.

Cassian laid his burned hand against the fountain rim, though damaged nerves left him blind to the exact output; I caught the wobble in his flame. Leon caught it too.

Without a word, Leon coiled a thin thread of water around Cassian's wrist, turning temperature shifts into crisp pressure pulses: one tap for too cold, two for steady, three for too hot. They had never drilled that link once. They built it on the fly while frost crept higher.

Cassian fed warmth in careful, uneven bursts. Leon translated the marker's pulse back through his water thread. Elira held the consent rhythm locked, while Cael read each line of punitive script aloud before the ice could bury a clause.

Aira pressed both hands hard against the gash in my side.

Aira: "You bent that spear."

Haru: "I nudged the angle."

My breath steadied against the freezing air.

Aira: "With Green output?"

Haru: "Mostly."

Her eyes snapped up to mine; she knew *mostly* was doing dangerous work.

Aira: "Can you keep standing?"

Cold pooled behind my sternum where the Black Circle had tasted blood, thinning the whole chamber's borders in my sight.

Haru: "Long enough."

I held her gaze without blinking.

Aira: "Was that an answer or a habit?"

I looked down at the frost creeping under my boots.

Haru: "A habit. Give me ten seconds."

She held the bandage firm and counted under her breath.

At six, fractures spiderwebbed around the marker's knees.

At eight, Cassian's flame stuttered out and Leon trapped the residual heat inside his water loop before it could snap cold.

At ten, the tight iron band around my ribs loosened enough to breathe without the Black Circle trying to rewrite the square around the wound.

Haru: "Now it is an answer."

Aira knotted the bandage tight enough to make honesty sting.

The ice shattered outward, and the second marker stepped free.

The third marker, mana starvation, waited down a cramped alley whose bricks drank every spell cast inside. Rhea barred immediate entry. Aira tossed in a tiny light charm; the stones swallowed it black. Leon pushed a ribbon of water past the corner; the masonry drank that dry too. Even Elira's gold faded at the threshold.

High on the public witness rail, Finn leaned over among the Green juniors and dropped a single copper coin into the lane.

It struck stone. Ordinary clinking rolled cleanly all the way to the fallen marker.

Finn went pale when every eye in the square snapped up at him.

Finn: "Magic is food in there. Stop feeding it magic."

Leon stripped off his ward-lined academy coat. Cassian unbuckled his fire-threaded bracer and dropped it beside his ruined glove. Elira banked her Yellow Circle until her wrist went bare. We walked in dark.

Forty paces of alley stretched into the longest road in the city. The bricks bled warmth out of muscle, steadiness out of balance, light from the rim of vision. At twenty paces, Leon stumbled hard; Cassian caught his elbow with the hand that still blistered. At thirty, Elira's knees gave an inch and she started to wave me off before glancing at the marker, rethinking what pride was for, and taking my arm.

At thirty-eight paces, the marker folded. Cael reached it first.

No spells. No rank edge. He wedged one shoulder beneath the faint outline and heaved with a body already bruised by someone else's doctrine.

Cael: "Six steps."

Six steps. No heroic vows, just what his legs could pay.

Leon hauled the next six. Cassian dragged six after him. Elira covered four before her breath gave out. I carried the final ten while the Black Circle thrashed uselessly inside my chest in a corridor where using it would only feed the walls.

The moment we cleared the threshold, every withheld Circle snapped back in a rush of color. The third marker lived.

Waiting by the western safe line, Mira watched the third extraction clear, then climbed back to the witness rail via the western service stair. She refused full cosmetic healing until the session closed so the scrape stayed on record, letting a healer simply clean the grit and bind her palm in white gauze. She kept that bandaged hand raised above her ledger slate where no transcript could later pretend injury had erased her from the floor.

Across the square, Lucien watched the three rescues with his hands clasped behind his back. I skimmed the field's pressure without letting my own mana flare.

My false Green Circle held steady around my wrist where I had threaded that deflection. Blood cooled under Aira's bandage. Mira's bandaged palm showed bright white beside her ink. Cassian flexed two blistered fingers. Leon stayed upright mostly because sitting down would admit how close the arch had come to folding him. Elira tracked my breathing and made sure I saw her counting.

Lucien had built a speed drill for obedience. Three markers down, we were bleeding and slower than a clean sweep, yet nobody had been spent like spare parts.

Still, Lucien kept watching for the slip that would force me to unmask. That became its own quiet war: every marker rescued against the blood cost of holding back.

The field baited me with smaller traps first.

A cracked lintel hung above Leon, seven seconds from shearing loose. I caught the weight shift. I kept my hands down.

Haru: "Leon. Arch weight."

Leon glanced up, kicked his water brace wider beneath the stone, and hauled the marker clear a heartbeat before the block smashed down.

Mira's voice rang from the gallery.

Mira: "Haru gave warning, Leon acted independently."

The chamber logged the credit without protest.

Next, a hairline frost seam crept behind Cassian's left heel, thin enough to miss in dust. I could have snuffed it with a thought nobody would ever detect. Instead, I called:

Haru: "Cassian. Back foot."

Cassian pivoted cleanly instead of bristling, turning the sidestep into a sharp ribbon of fire that cauterized the seam before it climbed his boot.

Cassian: "Appreciated."

Aira: "Recorded. Also emotionally noted, which I deeply resent."

Then the square dropped a false safe zone straight in front of Elira.

That trap almost broke my restraint.

The boundary gleamed silver, angled cleanly for healer access, stripped of any visible Dominion script. Yet the mana pooled inside carried zero warmth. No sanctuary built for living blood ever felt that dead. My fingers twitched toward my wrist.

Elira caught the twitch before anyone else.

Elira: "Say it."

She did not want me to fix it. She wanted me to name it.

Haru: "False safe zone. No life-temperature."

Professor Valen echoed from the rail above:

Professor Valen: "Observation entered. Elira Vale chooses route."

Elira veered sharp left, hauling the marker toward a crooked alley full of broken cobbles and honest strain.

Lucien's gaze stayed pinned on me.

Lucien: "How disciplined."

Haru: "How disappointed."

Aira let out a strangled cough.

Aira: "I am proud and terrified."

Once the marker crossed the line, Elira let a small smile show, just for a second. That brief smile cost Lucien far more than my retort had.

At the fourth marker, the city changed pitch.

The memory echo spoke in Talen Mire's voice.

Talen: "I surrendered."

Elira's hands stilled in midair, listening. The marker looped the recording:

Talen: "I surrendered."

Lucien looked conspicuously bored. That deliberate boredom told me everything about who had pulled the file.

Soren Ashvale stepped straight to the edge of the neutral witness rail.

Soren: "Valemere objection. The marker exploits a living witness's trauma without consent."

Inside the Valemere observer frame, Talen's relay projection brightened. He stood before anyone prompted him, his braced right hand held clear against his dark sleeve.

Talen: "I will provide context with consent."

The memory marker paused mid-breath, still wearing his voice. Talen stared at it like a man facing a room he had already walked out of once.

Talen: "They recorded my injury. My fear. My pain. And they played it back in training exercises to teach conduits how to 'manage emotional interference.' I didn't consent then. I'm consenting now to say: using someone's worst moment as a teaching tool is not education. It's cruelty."

The Valemere delegation went rigid along the benches.

The Foundation bell chimed crisp silver as it entered his statement.

**OBJECTION ENTERED**

Talen lowered his braced hand. This time, the memory did not own him, and the marker's stolen voice dissolved into blank static.

Elira looked up at Soren.

Elira: "Thank you."

Soren inclined his head once.

Soren: "Honor is mostly remembering people are not props."

Lucien's eyes slid over to him.

Lucien: "Valemere has become sentimental."

Soren: "No. We are just embarrassed it took us this long."

The public record rail scratched that line straight into the ledger.

Noctis had planted the echo expecting us to fracture over guilt. Instead, the gallery woke up. For the first time, Wounded City Protocol stopped feeling like a closed stage built for five students and started feeling like a room full of people deciding they refused to stay furniture.

One of the minor delegates, a gray-haired woman from Lirenth who had stayed silent ever since signing Mira's sheet, raised her hand.

Delegate: "Can an observer enter a consent objection if the harmed party is remote?"

Rhea glanced toward the Foundation bell.

President Valmont: "Ask the harmed party."

The woman blinked, surprised that law asked a living person before consulting a statute. Talen answered from the relay frame anyway.

Talen: "They can. As long as the objection names me as a person, not as evidence."

The delegate squared her shoulders.

Delegate: "Then Lirenth observer record objects to the reuse of living witness trauma as educational material."

The bell chimed again, a thinner note than Soren's, yet it rang true.

Mira scribbled the line down with her mouth slightly open.

Mira: "Observers can become witnesses."

Aira heard her from below.

Aira: "Unfortunately, yes. It is how paperwork gets souls."

Mira wrote that down too. Caught by Rhea's sharp two-finger gesture meaning *no*, Mira crossed out *souls* and inked *responsibility* above it. Better.

Down in the square, Leon stared up at the rails. Most of his life, observers had meant judges, rivals, strangers waiting for him to fail loudly enough to become gossip. Watching one woman speak and tilt the floor beneath his boots changed something in his stance.

Leon: "So the room shifts when they stop watching like statues."

Cassian: "I dislike that you said something useful."

Leon: "You dislike many things."

Cassian: "Mostly inefficient courage."

He said it while shifting into the gap beside Cael near the next alley, where fresh pressure lines had begun crawling up the stonework. The quip did not hide how low his Red Circle burned now. Cassian had learned that blazing brighter was often just another way of shrinking everyone else in the room.

Today he kept his flame low and his eyes wide. Cael noticed.

Cael: "You can stand nearer if you want."

Cassian glanced sideways at him. Cael's voice stayed thin, yet steady.

Cael: "I know the difference between a guard and a wall."

Something quiet crossed Cassian's face. He closed the half-step and planted himself shoulder to shoulder with Cael rather than blocking his line of sight.

Cassian: "Then I am beside."

High on the rail, Nera scribbled fast on a scrap of paper and shoved it to Oric. Oric read it, swallowed hard, and shouted down to the floor:

Oric: "Noctis student witness correction. In our training files, *beside* is frequently classified as obstruction when applied to lower-ranked students."

Nera's voice cut in right behind him, sharp and cool.

Nera: "Record objection to that classification."

The Foundation accepted the entry. Lucien's studied boredom cracked at the edges.

Every small refusal chipped another brick out of Lucien's script. By the time the fifth marker lit, the square no longer belonged solely to his rules.

The fifth marker burned with crushing pressure, splitting our formation not with stone walls, but with competing priorities.

A false silver notice flared overhead.

**UNCLASSIFIED SUBJECT OVEREXTENDING RESPONSE**

**PRIMARY WITNESS MAY REMOVE HERSELF TO REDUCE PRESSURE**

Elira did not step back. She stepped straight toward the burning marker. Rhea's seal answered immediately.

President Valmont: "Intervention authorized under public witness protocol."

Aira: "Recorded. Elira moves by role, not private panic."

Mira leaned over the rail.

Mira: "Recorded!"

The false notice shattered into pale sparks, and the pressure-burn marker stabilized. Lucien offered a thin, practiced smile.

Lucien: "Good. You are learning to narrate every instinct before it damns you."

Elira did not spare him a glance.

Elira: "No. We are learning to stop you from forging signatures on our choices."

The sixth marker carried a relay hook. It reached straight for Cael. Of course it did.

The hook materialized as a razor-thin black stroke in midair, clean as fresh calligraphy, curving fast toward his healer seal.

Cael saw it before the rest of us, not out of sharper sight, simply because his bones still remembered the shape of being claimed.

His hand flew halfway toward his wrist, then froze. For one heartbeat, old conditioning won the first twitch. In the next, Cael forced his hand back down to his side.

Cael: "Return hook."

His voice shook, yet he named it clean.

Up on the rail, Nera's countermark was already snapping together.

Nera: "Owner phrase?"

Oric scanned the hook's root, chalk flying across his slate.

Oric: "Hidden. It is pulling prior training authority as root."

Cael swallowed hard.

Cael: "No owner."

The black hook bent tighter toward him. Cael raised his voice.

Cael: "No owner."

The witness rail caught the phrase, ragged and human rather than drilled in chorus.

Mira: "No owner recorded."

Soren: "No honorable custody claim."

Blue observer: "Prior training contested."

Green student: "Cael Nox refused return."

The hook shuddered mid-flight. Lucien's eyes narrowed. For the first time all afternoon, his script had snagged on live teeth.

Leon threw a wall of rushing water between Cael and the line. The black stroke sliced clean through it as if water were mist. Nera hammered it with a countermark, bending its curve an inch.

Oric shouted down from the rail.

Oric: "It is not custody grammar! It is return grammar!"

Cael's face drained of color. Custody grammar could be argued; return grammar did not ask permission. It defined *back* as the place that owned him.

Elira lunged. Rhea's crimson seal flashed in midair.

President Valmont: "Elira Vale authorized for witness intervention."

Aira: "Publicly recorded."

Mira: "Recorded!"

The black hook whipped off course, aiming past Elira's chest straight for the pendant at her throat. My breath stopped.

Black and gold detonated out of the pendant at once. Elira threw her hand up, not to shield herself, to catch the black stroke before it could jump back to Cael.

Cael: "No!"

Too late.

Her fingers closed around the black line. The whole chamber cracked down its center seam, every crooked street turning toward that single grip.

Lucien's voice rolled smoothly out of the bell tower.

Lucien: "Second hinge."

The black hook snapped tight around Elira's wrist. Her pendant burned white-hot and ice-cold together, driving a fresh sting of frost beneath her collar. For one breathless heartbeat, the charm held.

Then Dominion grammar spoke down through the chamber bell.

**WILLING OBSTRUCTION CONFIRMED**

**PRIMARY WITNESS IN CUT PATH**

Rhea drove her council seal into the flagstones.

President Valmont: "Challenge. The movement was authorized."

Aira: "Challenge. Authorized public witness intervention."

Mira: "Challenge recorded!"

Professor Halden: "Challenge. No consent to severance."

Professor Valen: "Challenge. Multiple roles do not reduce personhood."

Nera and Oric slammed twin countermarks down from the rail.

Nera: "Dominion label rejected."

Oric: "Return claim has no owner."

The hook did not snap. It cinched tighter. Elira went white to the lips without making a sound, which only made it worse.

I took a step forward. Behind my ribs, the Black Circle surged like waking iron.

Elira locked eyes with me through the pain.

Elira: "Do not let him make your fear useful."

Those eight words stopped me harder than any council seal.

Across the square, Lucien watched with bright, hungry focus.

Lucien: "Beautiful. Even now she guards your leash. Tell me, Haru. Is love still autonomy when it knows exactly how to command you?"

The false Green Circle on my wrist flickered. Green. Pitch black beneath the cuff. Green again.

Murmurs rippled along the witness rail, too scattered to piece together the truth, sharp enough to taste fear.

Aira caught the flicker and stepped straight into the sightline between me and the gallery.

Aira: "Eyes on the hook! Not on Haru!"

Her voice cracked on my name, and she looked furious that it had.

The black line dragged Elira one stumbling pace toward the empty seventh marker. Cael caught her free hand with both of his.

Cael: "Let me take it."

Elira bit the answer out between clenched teeth.

Elira: "No."

Cael: "It was built for me."

Elira: "That is why you do not get to inherit it."

The seventh marker split open.

Inside the empty outline stood no person at all, only a hollowed-out space shaped like someone scrubbed clean out of every witness's memory.

The Wounded City bell tolled once.

Foundation: "Severance route identified."

Rhea's expression went razor-sharp.

President Valmont: "End activity."

Foundation: "Unable. Dominion clause contested but active."

Leon: "Then we break the clause."

He drove a crushing jet of water straight into the black hook; Cassian wrapped it in roaring flame. Water and fire braided without a single spoken call, blasting white steam across the square. The line held anyway. Elira's knees gave out.

I caught her before she hit the cobbles, and this time she let me take her weight. When her shoulder struck my chest, everything inside my head went flat and lethal. Quiet. Too quiet.

Haru: "Aira."

My voice did not sound like mine. Aira whipped around, hearing the drop in temperature immediately.

Haru: "Find the legal root."

Aira swallowed hard, then gave one sharp nod.

Aira: "Working."

Still human enough to ask. Still human enough to wait, barely.

Up on the rail, Nera shouted down into the steam.

Nera: "The hook is not the root! The root is the empty marker!"

Oric: "The seventh wound is isolation!"

Valen's old folio snapped open in her hands.

Professor Valen: "Then the answer is presence."

Rhea threw both hands upward toward the gallery.

President Valmont: "Do not recite for it. Prove you are here in a way Haru did not choose for you."

The witness rail hesitated for one breath. Mira moved first.

She jammed her bandaged palm beneath her stack of public ledgers and shoved them straight over the iron rail. Loose sheets fluttered down into the square in six distinct handwritings, one crowded with spelling fixes in the margin, another smudged where someone had cried and refused to wipe it clean.

Mira: "We wrote before he asked us to!"

The sheets struck the flagstones and stayed solid.

Soren ripped Valemere's courtesy seal clean off his observer slate, forfeiting his delegation's right to edit his testimony before public release.

Soren: "Valemere witness channel stays open."

Halden swore under his breath and unlocked his private research archive straight into the public rail feed. Decades of closely guarded annotations spilled across the gallery slates, complete with three lines marked **UNKNOWN** and one raw scrawl reading **I WAS WRONG**.

Professor Halden: "No office of mine holds sole custody of classification."

Professor Valen set Liora's damaged folio beside his pages without explaining a word. The torn-out names said enough.

Nera wrenched the Noctis authorization ring off her wrist, leaving a raw red groove where the metal had bitten deep. Oric caught it midair, laid his own beside it, and kicked both rings under the rail barrier onto the outer stone ledge where every Noctis observer could see them and nobody could quietly pocket them.

Down on the cobbles, Aira stared at the open seventh marker.

Aira: "I do not have the legal root."

Her voice broke. The whole square waited. Aira hated that the room had heard her come up empty, and spoke anyway.

Aira: "I know isolation is doing the work. I know everyone here walked in for a different reason. I know Haru did not script all of us."

She ripped her prepared response sheet cleanly in half.

Pinned in the steam, Leon and Cassian strained against the black line as water and fire bucked against each other. Leon shot me a glare over his shoulder.

Leon: "I am still furious with you."

Cassian: "I am furious with everyone. It is one of my stabilizing traits."

Someone on the rail let out a sharp laugh that snapped halfway into a sob. A hairline fracture split the empty marker, then stalled.

Cael stepped forward alone.

His healer seal flashed warning blue across his coat, shaking so hard from his trembling hands that the glow smeared over his collar. Nobody carried him. Nobody spoke in his place.

Cael: "Cael Nox."

The hook yanked hard at Elira's wrist. Cael flinched and held his ground.

Cael: "Not property."

Behind him, the Foundation pool swelled dark. Cael looked across the scattered ledger pages, the kicked-aside rings, Halden's unlocked mistakes, Soren's torn seal, and a room full of people still arguing while refusing to walk out.

Cael: "Present."

The empty marker shattered.

Every proof flooded the chamber together. No tidy unison followed; incompatible people simply stood inside the same danger and refused to hand their reasons to one master. The black hook slackened around Elira's wrist.

Elira dragged in a ragged breath. Against her throat, the pendant flared warm.

**SEVERANCE ROUTE INTERRUPTED**

**SIX VIABLE MARKERS EXTRACTED**

**NO CIVIC LOSS RECORDED**

**AURELIS WITNESS METHOD: PROVISIONALLY ACCEPTED FOR THIS ACTIVITY**

We had earned that verdict. The seventh marker stayed unclosed, yet Lucien could no longer argue that asking consent slowed rescue or that shared authority left a team paralyzed.

Relief rushed into the square too fast.

Lucien looked from the cracked seventh marker to Cael, and smiled thinly.

Lucien: "Interrupted is not ended."

The chamber released us before anyone could answer.

Release did not mean the city intended to let us walk out intact.

The paving began sliding inward toward the restoration pool. Buildings lost depth first, glass flattening into bright paper-thin squares before walls folded straight down through narrow seams in the floor. Every safe zone vanished. One single archway opened beneath the chapel stair.

Four crest marks burned across the threshold: Green, Blue, Red, Yellow. No unclassified lane. No span wide enough for Cael's contested Noctis seal and Elira's hooked wrist to clear together.

Aira spotted the trap instantly.

Aira: "The exit is sorting by status! Do not step into a color lane!"

The cobbles behind us dropped into nothing.

Leon: "Alternative?"

Aira eyed the folding skyline.

Aira: "Developing."

Rhea slammed her red seal against the archway.

President Valmont: "Emergency mixed-witness passage!"

The four colored marks rejected her seal and pinched narrower.

Beyond the shrinking square, Lucien already stood clear on the chapel stone outside the trap his protocol had sprung.

Lucien: "Classification is not violence merely because the unclassified dislike choosing."

Green light snapped around Cael's boots, not because he belonged to Green, because the sorting gate treated a contested seal as lowest available rank. Yellow light flared ahead of Elira, dragging her marked wrist forward as the dormant hook bit down again.

Elira dug her heels into the stone. Cael lunged toward her.

The Green lane clamped tight around his ribs. Leon caught the back of his coat before the pull could drag him out of reach.

Cael: "Let go!"

Leon: "No."

Cael: "I can reach her!"

Leon: "Not if the gate snaps shut on your spine!"

Cael wrenched sideways, furious and terrified. Leon did not haul him back. Instead, he stepped straight into the Green lane beside him.

Blue light died off Leon's wrist as the sorting gate choked on the contradiction. Leon gasped as backlash kicked his chest, yet for one sharp second Aurelis's proudest Blue stood unclassified beside the boy he had once treated like dead weight.

Leon: "Cassian. Now."

Cassian did not hesitate. He stepped clear of the Red lane and slapped his blistered, soot-stained hand down on Leon's shoulder. Red sparks died sputteringly off his forearm as the gate stripped his recognized path.

Cassian: "This is an appalling group decision."

Aira piled in behind them.

Overhead, the upper witness gallery dropped to floor level on screaming iron chains as the chamber walls folded inward. Its barrier unlocked only when Rhea forced the shared route open as emergency egress. Faculty and visiting delegates surged forward, followed closely by Nera, Oric, and Mira clutching her bandaged palm. Rhea stepped out of the Red lane last.

Every official color lane stood empty.

The sorting gate tried to lump the whole crowd into Green. Mira's crest flared first, trying to shove her to the front of the line. She stepped backward behind Cael.

Mira: "No rank order."

The archway shuddered violently.

Three paces away, Elira braced against the Yellow lane while the floor crumbled inches behind her boots. I could have shattered all four crest marks outright; the Black Circle behind my ribs knew the exact seam.

Instead, I stepped straight into the Yellow lane beside Elira.

The false Green crest on my wrist collided with the gold barrier around her. Sparks snapped across both my arms, and the bandaged cut beneath my ribs split open again.

Elira: "Haru, wait..."

Haru: "Can you walk?"

She looked from the black hook to the folding street, then to our team holding the unclassified jam behind us.

Elira: "With help."

I hooked her unmarked arm across my shoulders and drove us out of Yellow together.

The exit screamed. No human throat could make that sound: stone, law, and ancient rank-sorting shrieking as a dozen people refused to file into their neat lanes. The four colored marks blew apart into loose sparks.

Leon hurled water underfoot. Cassian flashed it into a broad surge of steam. Rhea drove her council seal forward like a wedge, and we hurtled across the threshold in one tangled, sliding heap as the false bell tower vanished behind us into the dark pool.

Cael hit the chapel flagstones first. Mira landed across his calves. Cassian slammed into Leon and immediately blamed architecture. Aira lost two spare maps and kept the torn sheet bearing Cael's refusal. Elira and I tumbled across last as the stone arch sealed shut behind my heel.

Across the flagstones where the four rank marks had burned, one new line flared for a single breath:

**MIXED WITNESS PASSAGE: UNRESOLVED**

Then Lucien's relay wiped it clean.

Approved crest colors flickered back unevenly across the floor. Cassian's Red returned first, bright and angry. Leon's Blue stuttered back after it. Elira's gold ring closed faintly around the black mark still biting into her wrist.

Cael's healer seal stayed dead blank.

He stared down at the empty patch of skin on his forearm and went motionless.

Mira pressed her bandaged palm flat onto the flagstones where the vanished line had burned.

Mira: "The exit logged you before he erased it."

Cael: "As what?"

Mira checked her ledger copy.

Mira: "Present."

His seal relit bare of any school crest, claiming neither Noctis nor Aurelis. *Present* got him as far as the healers, not across the room on his own. Cael took one step and his knees buckled. Leon caught him under both arms before he hit stone. Cassian stepped in to help, checked himself when Cael flinched at two sets of hands crowding him, and cleared the aisle ahead instead.

Cael: "I am not weak."

Leon: "You just got dragged through a rank sorter while half-broke. You can be accurate before proud."

The phrasing sounded suspiciously borrowed from Maelin. Cael let out a short, startled laugh anyway. Once Cael steadied his feet, Leon dropped his grip, keeping both hands hovering near enough to catch and far enough back not to steer. They crossed the chapel together, Cael's legs trembling on every stride while Leon matched his pace a hand's breadth away.

Mira had already inked the erased line into her notebook. Lucien saw her close the cover. His expression shifted by less than the ghost of a smile, just enough to tell me the shattered exit had never been part of his design, before he turned out of the chapel without giving anyone the satisfaction of watching him leave.

Up in the chapel healing circle, healers went to work stripping the black mark off Elira's wrist by paring away the upper sheath of mana clinging to her skin. It hurt cleanly and sharply. Beneath her collar, a faint trace of Dominion frost still chilled the edge of her pendant chain. She bore it without flinching.

Cael stood just outside the chalk healing ring, arms stiff at his sides. Leon stood beside him and refused to let him slip out the door. Good.

The black mark did not peel away like ordinary hostile residue; its threads were knotted inside the legal wording of what Elira had done. Every time a healer lifted a strand, the mark argued back in sharp flashes of script.

**OBSTRUCTION**

Rhea answered from the edge of the ring.

President Valmont: "Authorized intervention."

The healer lifted another strand.

**PRIMARY WITNESS BIAS**

Aira answered without looking up from her slate.

Aira: "Multiple-role autonomy entered and active."

Another strand pulled taut.

**CONDUIT RETURN CLAIM**

Nera and Oric answered together from the bench.

Nera: "No owner phrase."

Oric: "Prior training contested."

Every lie named aloud stripped another hook from the black thread until the healer could lift it free. Elira's jaw clenched so hard I thought she might crack a molar, yet she refused to let a single sound past her teeth. She was not hiding pain out of pride. She was denying Cael ammunition.

He heard the silence as proof anyway, his face hardening into tighter control with every black strand cut away.

Leon spotted it and stepped squarely in front of him.

Leon: "Look at me."

Cael: "I can hear."

Leon: "That is why I said look."

Cael lifted his eyes.

Leon: "She chose to protect you. You did not choose to hurt her."

Cael: "The result is the same."

Leon: "No, it is not."

Rare anger flickered sharp across Cael's face.

Cael: "Her wrist is black because I was the route."

Leon leaned in an inch.

Leon: "Her wrist is black because Lucien is a murderer with grammar."

From the chapel doorway, Cassian added dryly:

Cassian: "That might actually be your best sentence."

Leon never took his eyes off Cael.

Leon: "Do not make me waste it."

Cael's breath hitched once, then steadied.

Night settled over Aurelis.

The Wounded City lay sealed far beneath the chapel floor, yet I could still feel the hollow seventh marker humming through the bedrock. Isolation. An injury shaped like an empty chair.

Aurelis never went wholly silent after dark: wards hummed inside the masonry, practice fields sighed as they reset, whispers echoed along upper galleries, and carriage bells drifted faintly up from the city below the cliffs. Tonight, every ordinary sound arrived carrying the same quiet dread. Who was missing? Who had been moved out of reach under the polite name of safety?

I walked the corridor outside the infirmary twice before Elira called through the half-open door for me to stop wearing a trench into the flagstones. She was propped upright in a high-backed chair, since lying flat pulled the healer film tight across her raw wrist.

Haru: "I am not pacing."

Elira glanced from the doorway to my boots.

Elira: "You have passed that crack in the slate seven times."

Haru: "Observation is not pacing."

Elira: "Halden has been a terrible influence."

I stopped beside her chair. She held out her unmarked hand, and I took it. For several quiet breaths neither of us spoke. Her pulse beat steady under my thumb; I caught myself counting it without meaning to.

Elira: "Do not turn that into surveillance."

I shut my eyes for half a second.

Haru: "I am trying not to."

Elira: "I know."

She let out a long breath.

Elira: "Aira is right. We need a replacement phrase before morning."

Haru: "I am listening."

That drew a faint smile from her, gone almost as fast as it came.

Elira: "Better."

The smile faded out completely.

Elira: "Tomorrow, if the seventh marker opens again, I may have to move."

Haru: "Elira."

Elira: "I am not asking permission."

Haru: "I know."

She gave me a flat look. I corrected myself.

Haru: "I am listening."

Her fingers tightened around mine.

Elira: "If I move, do not treat my choice as Lucien's victory before he earns it."

The sentence tasted like glass going down.

Haru: "And if he makes it one?"

Her eyes shone faintly in the lamplight.

Elira: "Then make sure the world knows he did."

At the time, I thought she meant the chamber. I had no idea how far the word *world* was about to reach.

Cael appeared in the doorway later while she dozed again, with Leon waiting two steps behind his shoulder. Either Cael had asked for company or he had finally stopped fighting being followed; either way, it counted as ground gained.

Cael looked from Elira's bandaged wrist to me.

Cael: "I am not going to apologize."

Leon let out a low grunt of approval.

Cael: "I want to. I am not going to."

Haru: "Good."

The word startled him. It startled me a little too. Cael kept both arms straight at his sides, visibly forcing his hands not to curl inward into guilt.

Cael: "I am angry."

Leon: "Also good."

Cael blinked sideways at him.

Leon: "What? Better than apologizing for getting targeted."

Cael looked back at Elira.

Cael: "I am angry that he turned her kindness into a route. I am angry that I recognize the spell he will reach for next. I am angry that part of me still whispers that being useful would make all this simpler."

He swallowed hard.

Cael: "I am saying it aloud because if I keep it quiet, it turns into a plan."

That was Aira's work taking root. And Nera's. And Oric's. Maybe even his own, at last. I nodded once.

Haru: "Then keep saying it."

Cael held my eyes for a long, steady beat.

Cael: "You too."

I did not need to ask what he meant.

Hours later, Elira still dozed upright in her chair while Aira and Rhea argued in low voices by the corridor door over the morning's rules. I kept watch beside her without touching the pendant at her collar, though keeping my hands off it felt like holding back a landslide one pebble at a time. When the notice crystal outside the window flared pale silver, Elira stirred and caught my sleeve.

Elira: "Do not chase his guilt in the dark."

Haru: "You were asleep."

Elira: "Barely."

I closed my hand over her warm, living fingers. She was still here. Outside the infirmary glass, the notice crystal burned the next summons into the night air.

**WOUNDED CITY PROTOCOL: INCOMPLETE**

**SEVENTH MARKER UNRESOLVED**

**FINAL ACTIVITY TO OPEN AT DAWN**

## Coda: Leon Stays Beside

Leon caught up to Cael in the quiet corridor outside the infirmary and nearly made the same old mistake.

He almost stepped in front.

The muscle memory kicked in before thought could catch it: hand half-raised, shoulder squaring between Cael and the dark hall, the only guard stance Leon had ever been drilled to trust. He forced his boots to stop.

Cael noticed immediately. Noctis had trained him to read weight shifts before words ever left a throat.

Leon opened both hands at his sides where Cael could see them.

Leon: "Do you want me here?"

The question sounded stupidly small in the empty hall. Too late, too clumsy, yet it was the first honest thing Leon knew how to offer.

Cael stared at him long enough for embarrassment to flare hot under Leon's collar, trying hard to curdle into temper. Leon stayed put anyway.

Cael: "Beside."

Leon stepped up beside him, keeping his hands to himself and the corridor ahead wide open.

Inside the infirmary, Elira slept with frost lingering cold beneath her collar. Outside in the hall, Leon learned that standing in the right place could be harder than throwing a punch.

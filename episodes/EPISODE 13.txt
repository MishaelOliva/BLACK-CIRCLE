# Episode 13: **The Black Circle Unbound**

The real door sealed behind us before the city bell finished its first note.

False dawn flooded the streets beyond the chapel threshold. Narrow alleys folded around a cracked fountain basin filled with dark water, civic banners hung dead in windless air, and a bell tower leaned over the route like a witness already coached. The air smelled of wet stone, burned sugar, and the copper edge of a spell waiting for blood.

Elira stood beside me with the black pendant quiet at her throat, too pale and too steady. Cael moved between Leon and Maelin as if every step had to be negotiated with a body Noctis had taught to apologize for hurting. Aira carried the margin record. Rhea carried the formal one. Mira carried three pencils and looked ready to bite anyone who called that excessive.

The route sorted us in stages: witnesses to the stone rails, healers to the outer ring, then Elira, Cael, and me into the central square where the restoration pool waited under a sky too bright to be real. The pendant warmed once when Elira chose to stand beside Cael. The bell tower answered with frost.

Across the pool, Lucien watched the exit disappear. No weapon. No escort. Just the quiet certainty of someone who had already decided which one of us the field would call expendable. That should have frightened me sooner.

The false streets rearranged behind us. Seven exits narrowed to four, one for every public Circle color, each door bright enough to suggest safety. The unmarked route we had entered by vanished first. White Foundation script surfaced around Cael's boots and climbed toward the statement page in his grip.

**CONDUIT SUPPORT DISPLACED FROM ORIGIN INSTITUTION**

The silver letters he had chosen, **I AM NOT A RETURN ROUTE**, burned bright against the claim instead of yielding to it.

Cael looked down, terrified and astonished.

Cael: "It heard me."

Lucien: "It recorded you. Hearing requires judgment."

The pool darkened at its center.

Nera struck the witness rail with both palms.

Nera: "Noctis reclamation grammar. Cael, do not answer destination."

Cael backed away from the water.

Cael: "I refuse return."

His statement flashed silver. For one breath, the claim stopped. Then a second line opened beneath it in older script.

**REFUSAL UNDER EXTERNAL DEPENDENCY REQUIRES ANCHOR REVIEW**

Elira's pendant turned cold enough for frost to spiderweb across its chain.

I saw the trap assemble. It did not challenge Cael's refusal directly; it renamed everyone who had helped him as proof the refusal wasn't his. My protection became influence. Nera's testimony became institutional rivalry. Leon's presence became coercive attachment. Elira's promise became the primary anchor.

Dominion Severance never began by cutting. It began by making every act of care read like ownership.

Haru: "Cael. Hold the page. Don't let the field choose your subject."

Cael pressed the paper against his chest.

Cael: "I refuse because I refuse."

The nearest Noctis door cracked down the middle. Lucien's composure slipped for half a heartbeat.

Then he raised two fingers.

Lucien: "Primary anchor review."

Rhea's red council seal struck the rail before the last syllable landed.

President Valmont: "Denied. The protected witness has answered independently."

The field accepted her ruling. The Noctis script ignored it.

A hair-thin thread of colorless light rose from Cael's shadow and glided across the pool toward Elira. It moved slowly enough to look avoidable, just as Cael had warned. The spell only needed a good person to take one step forward.

Elira saw it reach for him. She moved.

Haru: "Elira, no!"

Gold unfolded from both her hands, not around herself and not toward me. She threw it over Cael like an open shelter with the door left unlatched from inside.

Elira: "His refusal is his."

The colorless thread kissed her barrier. For half a heartbeat, she held it.

Sunlight detonated across the false city. Every gold edge of her Circle sharpened to glass. The pendant at her throat flared dark, caught between guarding her life and parsing the poisoned definition the field had wrapped around protection.

I drove my hand through the stone toward the Severance root buried beneath the pool, braided into old rescue grammar. Pulling it should have snapped the claim.

Instead, the spell read my reach as proof of interference. The colorless line doubled.

Haru: "Drop the shield! I have the root!"

Elira glanced back at Cael. He was on his knees, Noctis script climbing his healer seal while his chosen statement still burned white against his chest.

Elira: "You have him."

Not a question. She pushed the shield outward.

Gold swept Cael clear of the claim line.

The Severance path whipped inward through the gap her magic left behind. It slipped beneath the pendant's authority, struck below her collarbone, and snuffed every gold thread in a single silent stroke. No blast. No thunder.

Elira drew breath as if the air had turned to glass. Her eyes found mine. Then her knees gave out.

I crossed the square before she hit the stone. The false Green crest shattered at my wrist as I caught her, pale shards of harmless light dying between our sleeves.

Her pendant struck my knuckles, dark and cold. Cael screamed her name. Lucien lowered his hand. The field wrote:

**PRIMARY ANCHOR SEVERED**

I pressed my palm over Elira's heart. Nothing answered. No one moved. So the world broke instead.

The Wounded City peeled away from me in razor-straight lines. Cobblestone streets lifted off their foundations and turned upward like pages. Glass panes shattered and hung suspended in the air. The bell tower bent backward until its silver bell aimed straight at the false sky and rang without a single sound.

Elira lay slack in my arms. No breath. No pulse. No gold.

Behind me, the Black Circle tore open in concentric rings of pitch black and starlight silver, counter-rotating with terrifying precision. Ancient runes ignited one after another along the arcs, dragging the light out of the false dawn.

The false Green disguise was gone.

The rings locked behind my shoulders. Six hours minimum. A full witness cycle.

The Foundation systems had taken in too many eyes, too many live relays, too many contested claims. Until the cycle anchored, the Black Circle would not fold back into stillness.

Power flooded the square, and precision vanished with it. I could not mend Elira's chest without risking a structural rewrite over the wound Cael's shield had left open; Tier 3 would bind death, doctrine, witness, and sacrifice into one irreversible law. I could not cut the relays to spare us from being watched, adjust the pendant, hide the rings from terrified students, or step out of the chamber without dragging the manifestation straight through every ward in Aurelis above us.

Every witness at the rails saw enough to know the world had lied about what I was. The public gallery recoiled. Mira did not.

Her face had gone chalk-white, tears tracking through soot, yet she gripped a second pencil with shaking fingers.

Mira: "Record..."

Her voice cracked. She forced it back down into her throat.

Mira: "Record that Elira Vale was killed by Dominion Severance while shielding Cael Nox from unlawful reclamation."

The relay windows carried her words outward too.

Without Mira's sentence, the first global image of the Black Circle would have been pure terror: no classroom diagram, no scholar's legend, only a boy kneeling over a dead girl while an unclassed celestial circle tore a city apart. Because she spoke first, half the world had to hear the sequence before fear finished writing the headline.

Across the hovering panes, distant rooms fractured in glimpses the Foundation could not mute.

A Valemere official lunged up so hard his chair toppled backward. A Noctis observer reached for a blackout seal and jerked his hand back from frost-burned crystal. Far above us in Aurelis, shouts echoed down stone stairwells as notice crystals bloomed black and silver down every corridor.

The Tournament Board relay flashed emergency script and failed to mask the spinning rings behind it.

**UNCLASSIFIED FIFTH-RING EVENT RECORDED**

**PUBLIC WITNESS LOCK ACTIVE**

**DO NOT ISOLATE SAFEGUARD**

That final line came from old Aurelis, not the Board, writing itself across every channel before anyone could vote on it.

Aira stared at Mira as if that single sentence had kept one corner of the world upright. Then Aira turned to me.

Aira: "Haru."

My name reached me through the roaring pressure. It could not hold me.

Lucien stood across the cracked fountain basin, eyes fixed on the Black Circle. His face shifted from shock to naked recognition, then hunger. I hated that hunger more than I hated his fear.

Lucien: "There."

The word trembled on his tongue before he smoothed it flat.

Lucien: "There you are."

He realized the world was watching, and the audience steadied him. His eyes flicked from the spinning rings to the witness-locked relays, already salvaging a triumph from the wreckage. He had wanted proof of what I hid, never mind that Dominion Severance lay exposed beside Elira's body and Cael's reclamation claim burned across the fountain stone.

Lucien: "Noctis witnesses."

His voice sharpened toward the distant pane.

Lucien: "Record the unclassified threat."

The Noctis window flickered. Mira shouted straight over him.

Mira: "Record the killing first!"

Aira's head whipped toward her. Mira didn't blink.

Mira: "Record the sequence. Cael was claimed. Elira intervened. Dominion Severance executed. Then Haru's circle broke open."

Her whole body shook so hard her pencil rattled against the rail.

Mira: "Then. Not before."

The Foundation pool flashed silver.

**SEQUENCE LOCK ENTERED**

Lucien glared at Mira with sudden, unguarded irritation. Mira flinched, then squared her shoulders.

I lifted one hand.

The space between Lucien and me emptied itself, wards and paving stones smoothed out of existence before they could spark. Seven layers of Noctis lightless defense snapped open around him, built to smother, redirect, and categorize hostile magic.

The Black Circle brushed them once. Seven layers turned into lines, then into syntax errors, then into nothing.

Lucien staggered one step back.

That single step cracked through the gallery like splitting ice.

Cassian: "Stars."

Leon: "Haru, stop."

I heard Leon's voice beside the rail. I understood every word. I didn't obey.

The Dominion Severance grammar still pulsed through the floor. I could see every root, every stolen clause that had turned Elira's choice into a kill path. I gripped it and pulled.

The city screamed without sound.

Noctis relay lines flared far beyond the chamber, burning through the delegation wing, mirror routes, courtesy filings, apology ash, and every clean sentence Lucien had ever sharpened into a knife. Farther still, the lines shot out to Noctis Dominion College, to Valemere through Talen Mire's testimony link, to sealed tournament ledgers and every official who had signed off on calling student safety a neutral rule.

I saw them all as raw threads: silver for witness truth, black where Dominion hid ownership inside care, gold where Elira had stood, faint blue where Aira built protocols behind dry jokes, red where Rhea forced authority to answer students, green where Mira's gallery wrote what powerful men preferred summarized. And at the center, a ragged wound where Cael's life had been mapped as a route.

The Black Circle knew how to correct broken routes. It knew nothing of mercy unless I held mercy in front of it with both hands, and my hands were shaking around a cooling body.

I could strip every linked clause from this witness cycle in one breath.

Professor Halden shouted from the rail.

Professor Halden: "Do not classify the response! Do not name it!"

His warning wasn't for me. It was for every official watching through glass.

Rhea drove her red council seal against the shivering public rail.

President Valmont: "All witnesses remain present. Nobody runs alone."

Her authority cracked at the edges. She held it anyway.

Aira stepped onto the activity floor.

The air around me bent her Blue Circle sideways. She nearly went down before Cassian caught her arm.

Aira: "Let go."

Cassian: "Terrible idea."

Aira: "I know. Let go."

Cassian let her arm drop.

She walked toward me one step at a time, face wet, furious, and terrified.

Aira: "Haru, I need you to hear one person who isn't the thing waking up inside you."

The Black Circle spun faster. The floor split straight under her boots.

I snapped the crack shut before it could swallow her. Only a sliver of me still recognized Aira as someone who should not fall, and that sliver was barely enough to keep the stone whole beneath her shoes.

She hated being watched through the relay windows. Her jaw went sharp with the anger of someone denied privacy by history, yet she kept walking.

Aira: "Everyone outside this room, write this down if you have hands. He is not calm. He is not safe. He is also not Lucien's proof."

The Tournament Board relay flickered at her unauthorized address. Aira pointed straight at the glass without taking her eyes off me.

Aira: "Yes, I mean you too."

Rhea drove her red seal behind Aira, bracing the relay frames against the pressure tearing at the chamber.

President Valmont: "Aira Solen speaks as protected witness."

Professor Halden added, voice rough.

Professor Halden: "And as autonomous actor."

Aira: "Lovely. Put that on my grave if this goes badly."

Cassian's voice cracked behind her.

Cassian: "Don't joke about that."

Aira didn't turn.

Aira: "Then make it unnecessary."

She stopped ten paces from the spinning rings.

Aira: "Elira told you to stay Haru."

Elira. The name cut straight through the noise.

Above us, every silver banner turned black. Lucien let out one thin, strained laugh.

Lucien: "You see? Mercy without the lie of restraint. A god correcting inconvenience."

I looked at him.

The Wounded City yanked every authorized foothold out from beneath his observer seal, dropping him to one knee on bare stone.

Haru: "Do not speak about her."

My voice came out flat and quiet. That frightened the gallery more than shouting ever could.

Lucien forced his chin up, blood trickling from his nose.

Lucien: "Then make me silent."

I almost did. The Black Circle found the vibration of his voice in the air, ready to erase it. It would have been effortless. Elira lay cold in my arms; the world had refused her, so I could rewrite the world.

Then Cael screamed, not from pain, but from recognition.

He tore free from Leon and bolted toward the cracked fountain basin where silver-black water had begun to gather. Nera lunged after him.

Nera: "Cael, no!"

Oric vaulted the stone rail badly and hit the ground on one knee.

Oric: "Do not start it!"

Cael didn't look heroic. He looked battered, terrified, and far too young to carry a spell built to spend his life. His stride hitched. The healer seal on his wrist strobed black, then silver, then black again. The silver thread wrapped around his damaged hand unraveled as he ran, trailing bright strands across the broken flagstones.

Leon threw himself forward. Cassian caught Leon's sleeve, lost his grip as the fabric tore, and missed him.

Leon: "Cael!"

Cael didn't slow. He had already decided before his first step landed.

Aira whipped around from me.

Aira: "Rhea, block the pool!"

Rhea's red seal slammed into the fountain rim. The pool accepted her authority, curved straight around it, and kept rising.

President Valmont: "It recognizes his training path."

Nera's voice broke.

Nera: "Because Noctis carved the path inside him."

Professor Valen: "Then we object before he speaks."

Mira: "Objection before activation."

**OBJECTION ENTERED**

**VOTIVE RETURN DETECTED**

**CONDUIT LIFE TRANSFER**

**WARNING: FATAL**

Black threads surfaced beneath the skin of his wrists, one from each witness station, knotting straight back into his ruined channels. Noctis hadn't merely stripped away distribution; it had wired the spell to borrow seven witnesses and dump their debt onto the conduit anyway.

Nera: "They folded the seven into him."

Professor Valen: "Release the positions! Now! Every person holding increases his load!"

Nobody wanted to pull back. Cael's knees buckled against the stone rim, and that forced us to let go.

The Black Circle mapped all seven knots instantly. I could shred them in a breath, and I could feel what lay braided through them: Cael's remaining channels and the last intact thread back to Elira's heart. Brute force wouldn't spare him; it would tear both routes apart.

Cael looked across at me through tears.

Cael: "Then don't lie and call breaking it rescue."

His hands were still free enough to pull out. Noctis had left the only uncontested exit to the boy they had trained never to walk away.

Aira: "Cael!"

## Interlude: Cassian - Precision

Cassian Veyr had set things on fire before he learned that fire left ash you couldn't talk your way out of. After that, he became precise.

People mistook precision for easy temperament because it let them relax around him. Cassian the amused Red Circle heir. Cassian with the clean flame and the cleaner insult, quick enough with a joke that whole rooms filed him under harmless. That was useful. It was also a lie.

Anger lived inside him with impeccable posture. It showed up in classrooms where Green Circle students were praised for taking hits instead of skill, in halls where faculty said promising when they meant useful, and in Cael's eyes whenever someone offered kindness and Cael checked for the invoice before the warmth.

Cassian had learned not to burn in front of an audience. He smiled, angled the heat, and sent wit out ahead of him so nobody checked what it was covering.

Now Leon was fighting him hard enough to bruise ribs, Cael was kneeling at the pool with death climbing past his elbows, and there wasn't a clever line on earth big enough to stand between Cassian and the fact that holding Leon back was right.

Right. What a rotten word.

He could let go. Leon would reach the water, maybe break the transfer, maybe die beside Cael and turn one funeral into two. Cassian didn't know. That was the whole curse of precision: when you couldn't predict the blast radius, restraint meant locking your arms and taking the hatred.

Leon hated him for holding on. Good. Hatred meant Leon was still breathing.

Cassian didn't crack a single joke. For once, there was nothing left to hide behind.

Aira laid two fingers gently against Cael's sleeve.

Beside them, Nera recited the Votive Return grammar again, translating every clause aloud into what it had cost. Oric repeated each line after her, reclaiming the words for the students still watching behind closed glass.

Elira tried to push herself upright. I steadied her shoulders. When she saw Cael across the floor, grief twisted her face hard enough to make the Black Circle flare again. She gripped my wrist.

Elira: "Stay."

One word. Request, anchor, and witness at once. I stayed while she wept against my sleeve, the black rings still turning overhead, held back only by the people Cael had died to keep in the room.

Outside the cracked chapel walls, full night had already fallen over Aurelis; the Foundation's false dawn had swallowed the entire day without asking. The first hour after catastrophe didn't behave like an hour. It fractured into tasks.

Maelin reached the broken chapel before the last relay echo faded, two healers behind her and a white field case in hand.

Maelin: "Alive first."

Nobody argued, and nobody misunderstood. Alive first didn't mean Cael mattered less; it meant someone had to name the order before grief paralyzed the room. Elira's pulse fluttered under my thumb like a thread I wasn't allowed to touch.

Maelin knelt beside us, checking Elira's throat, wrist, eyes, and the cold mark below her collarbone where Dominion Severance had slipped beneath the pendant. Maelin's mouth tightened.

Maelin: "Do not heal."

I stared at her. Every oil lamp in the chapel bent toward us as the Black Circle shifted. Maelin didn't flinch. Her steadiness forced me to answer to it.

Maelin: "Haru, listen carefully. If you repair what Cael's return rewrote, her channels may read your correction as a second claim. I need her injured and alive, not perfect and contested."

Perfect and contested. The words cut like legal steel. I had enough power in my palms to smooth every bruise, steady her heart, and erase every sign that she had ever died. What sickened me was realizing how much of that urge wanted the room obedient to my love, wanted the wound gone so I wouldn't feel accused by it.

I uncurled my fingers slowly and let Maelin take Elira's wrist. Behind me, a ring spun backward, shuddered, and held. Maelin glanced once at the circle, then back to her patient.

Maelin: "Good."

A medical note, not praise. That helped.

Across the chapel, Leon still hadn't let go of Cael. He sat on the stone with one arm braced behind Cael's shoulders as if a dead boy might slide into discomfort. His Blue Circle had shrunk to a dull band around his wrist while water pooled quietly in the cracks around his boots. Cassian crouched nearby, not touching, just close enough that anyone trying to take Cael without asking would have to walk through fire first. Every trace of his usual humor was gone.

Cassian: "Leon."

Leon didn't lift his head.

Leon: "If I move, they'll take him."

Nobody had threatened that, yet the world had taught him to expect it. Rhea heard. She walked over, knelt again, and set her red council seal on the stone beside Leon's knee rather than on Cael.

President Valmont: "No body transfer without named witness consent, medical confirmation, and public sequence copy."

Leon swallowed hard.

President Valmont: "You are currently named witness."

Leon: "I don't know how."

President Valmont: "You're already doing it."

The seal registered Leon's proximity as record. He let out a shaky breath and stayed put.

Nera and Oric hovered at the edge of Cael's shadow, trained stillness left with nowhere to report. Nera's hands shook until the binding scars at her wrists blurred. Oric pulled out three scraps of paper. Across the room, Aira spotted paper even through tears.

Aira: "Oric?"

Oric's voice came out raw.

Oric: "We should write the translation now."

Nera stared at him.

Oric: "Before Noctis writes it first."

Work moved her when comfort couldn't. She dropped to her knees on the floor and started with the first doctrine line.

**WITNESS RESTORATION THROUGH HONORED CONDUIT**

Nera: "A useful student dies where a ranked student can be seen surviving."

Oric copied it down. His hand shook badly enough that he spelled Cael's name wrong.

**Cale.**

He stared at the letters as if Noctis had stolen one more thing while his head was bowed. Mira slid off the rail and crouched beside him.

Mira: "Don't tear it out."

Oric's knuckles whitened on the page.

Mira: "Cross it once. Write his name beside it. Let the mistake show we were here."

Oric drew a single line through the error and wrote **Cael Nox** next to it.

Nera read the next phrase aloud.

**VOLUNTARY RETURN ACCEPTED**

Nothing followed. Her mouth opened, then shut. Oric held his pencil poised above the paper until his fingers cramped.

Nera: "I can't."

Nobody told her to try again. Silence sat heavy over the paper until the Noctis words looked like ordinary ink again.

Mira looked toward Cael's empty spot by the basin.

Mira: "What did he like?"

The question was so ordinary the whole chapel nearly gave way around it. Leon buried his face. Oric stared blankly at the page until Nera finally answered.

Nera: "Candied orange peel."

Her voice cracked on the last word.

Nera: "He pretended not to because Lucien hated sticky fingers on relay glass."

Mira wrote it on a clean sheet, separate from the doctrine notes.

**Cael liked candied orange peel.**

Then Nera whispered, barely audible.

Nera: "He was fifteen when they put the first return phrase inside him."

Halden turned half a step away, hiding his face from a gallery that was no longer watching, though not from us.

Professor Halden: "Fifteen."

He didn't ask for a citation or dress the number up as scholarship. He stood holding his black classification case, surrounded by the wreckage of adults learning too late. Then he set the case on the flagstones and clicked the latches shut.

Professor Halden: "No classification work tonight."

Rhea looked across at him.

Professor Halden: "If anyone asks, I refused access because Cael Nox hasn't been carried from the floor."

He hadn't called it significant or unprecedented. He had used Cael's name.

Rhea: "Recorded."

Aira sat near Elira's feet and opened a fresh page, her usual headers waiting at the top. Under **ALIVE**, she wrote Elira. Under **DEAD**, she wrote Cael. The pencil tip punched straight through the paper.

Aira stared at the torn hole.

Aira: "I don't know what comes next."

Mira laid the orange-peel sheet gently beside her knee.

Mira: "Then don't make a list yet."

Aira shut the notebook.

The Black Circle hung open above me, strong enough to terrify distant capitals and useless against the fact that none of us had known what sweet Cael liked while he was alive. Then Elira stirred, eyelids fluttering halfway open. For one second she looked adrift between death and waking, until her eyes focused on my face. Her first expression was assessment, checking whether the room had gone dangerous while she was gone.

Elira: "Haru."

Hearing my name in her voice nearly undid everything Maelin had just protected. The rings widened behind me. Elira saw the surge and lifted her fingers an inch off the warming cloth, choosing direction even when she couldn't reach.

Elira: "Do not make being alive something I have to apologize for."

I couldn't get a word past my throat. Maelin squeezed her eyes shut for one beat, then opened them, bright and furious.

Maelin: "Patient speaking beyond recommended limit."

Elira: "Noted."

Her gaze stayed locked on mine.

Elira: "Say yes."

Haru: "Yes."

The single word steadied my breathing against the cold air, and the rings slowed again.

Outside the chapel, Aurelis began to stir. Bootsteps echoed down corridors, faculty barked orders, council aides hurried with paper copies, and students refused to clear the stairwells. Somewhere near the steps, someone spoke Cael's name wrong and three voices corrected them at once.

The world was already moving on. Part of me hated it, and part of me needed it so the chapel wouldn't become my entire universe. Maelin bound Elira's wrist. Leon held Cael. Nera translated. Mira copied. Rhea guarded the floor. Halden kept his case shut. And I sat inside the most exposed magic event in living memory, learning that restraint after revelation wasn't hiding anymore; it was letting other people stay real while I was breaking.

The second hour belonged to removal.

Cael's body couldn't move until three records aligned: healer confirmation, witness consent, and sequence protection. Rhea kept the steps brisk and procedural because letting them sound like grief would have stopped the room cold.

Maelin checked Cael twice. Once cleanly, as a healer.

Maelin: "No breath. No pulse. No recoverable channel activity. Votive Return completion fatal."

The second check belonged to the person who had adjusted his bandages, argued with him about sleep, and watched him stare at Elira's pendant as if survival had put a price tag on him. She left two fingers resting on his wrist far longer than procedure asked.

Maelin: "He was warm when I last checked him."

Mira waited one beat, then wrote that down too.

Leon gave witness consent only after Rhea assured him it didn't mean surrendering Cael to strangers, only naming who could help carry him.

Leon: "Then Cassian."

Cassian met his eyes. Leon didn't look away.

Leon: "And Nera. If she wants."

Nera's breath caught while the room waited.

Nera: "I want."

Her voice nearly gave out on the word, so she said it louder.

Nera: "I want to help carry him."

Oric stepped forward as well.

Oric: "I can take the feet."

Cassian's expression shifted for half a second. He knew what Noctis drilled into lower-ranked students, teaching them to take the feet while ranked mourners took the shoulders. Oric had offered the place he'd been conditioned to claim before anyone could assign it. Cassian shook his head.

Cassian: "No."

Oric stiffened.

Cassian: "Shoulder with me."

Oric stared at him.

Cassian: "Unless you object to my technique."

Cassian's kind of mercy always arrived disguised as competence. Oric stepped up beside him.

Leon held Cael until the last possible second, then yielded him to Cassian, Oric, Nera, and Maelin. Rhea led the way with her protection seal. Aira carried the sequence copy. Mira carried the amendment. I walked with nothing in my hands, and that felt heavier than carrying.

Elira caught me staring at my empty palms from the unfolded healer cot, pale beneath three layers of warming cloth.

Elira: "Do not make empty hands mean refusal."

I looked down at her.

Elira: "Sometimes they mean other people are allowed."

The Black Circle shifted behind me like a thought too vast for words.

Haru: "I know."

Elira: "Know it slower."

I stayed beside the cot and breathed until my shoulders dropped.

Cael left the chapel through the old west arch.

The academy had gathered beyond the sealed line, packing the stairwell, the upper landing, and the corridor beyond it. Nobody spoke when the seal parted. They saw who carried him: Cassian, Oric, Nera, and Maelin instead of Noctis faculty or Aurelis clerks. Behind them walked Leon with shaking empty hands, Aira clutching paper like it weighed more than stone, Mira with ink across her wrist, and Rhea at the front, refusing to hide the dead behind rank.

The first whisper came from a Green Circle first-year I didn't recognize.

First-year: "His name?"

Nobody rebuked him. Leon answered.

Leon: "Cael Nox."

The boy repeated it, and another voice picked it up, passing Cael's name down the corridor as plain correction after a world that had spent too long calling him conduit, transfer, asset, and return route.

Once Cael rested in the eastern preparation room under Leon, Nera, and Oric's watch, Rhea, Maelin, Aira, and Mira returned to the chapel.

The third hour belonged to me because everyone else had finally been given work, and that was when the danger sharpened.

As long as Elira needed me still, I could hold still. While Cael's body lay on the floor, grief had a center outside me. Once the chapel thinned, the Black Circle remembered it had no small shape.

The rings widened until silver runes grazed the upper arch. Stone dust lifted off the flagstones, and shattered pieces of the Wounded City began pulling toward my boots as if trying to rebuild a diagram. Halden saw it first.

Professor Halden: "Everyone out."

Haru: "No."

My voice sounded like a vault door shutting beneath stone. Halden froze. I forced myself to meet his eyes.

Haru: "If everyone leaves, the room becomes only me."

He understood faster than comfort allowed.

Professor Halden: "Then fewer. Not none."

Six stayed: Rhea, Maelin, Elira on her cot, Aira counting what fear tried to blur, Mira finishing her final copy, and Halden with his black case shut. Enough witnesses to keep the chapel from turning into an altar.

The Black Circle didn't want enough. It wanted exact, whole, airtight. Every possible fix sat within reach of my fingers: I could seal the relay network, strip Noctis's private chambers bare, bind Lucien's name to Dominion Severance in unbreakable law, extract every student carrying a carved return phrase, and make every cautious Councilor regret asking if I was safe before asking who died.

I could do so much good that nobody could refuse me. That was the terror: wanting obedience dressed up as mercy.

Elira's voice reached me from the cot.

Elira: "Tell me one thing you are not doing."

I turned my head. Exhaustion hollowed out her face, yet her eyes were steady.

Haru: "I am not closing the relays that already closed."

Elira: "Good. Another."

Haru: "I am not repairing your wounds without Maelin's instruction."

Maelin's hands paused over a bandage for one beat, then kept wrapping.

Elira: "Another."

Haru: "I am not finding Lucien."

The chapel lamps bent hard enough to whine. Aira wrote faster.

Elira: "Another."

Haru: "I am not making the world understand before it chooses to listen."

Something inside me gave way on that sentence, and the spinning rings slowed. Restraint wasn't sitting idle; it was naming the strike I refused clearly enough that refusal became real.

Mira's pencil scratched through the hush.

Mira: "Can I record that?"

I almost laughed, awful and human.

Haru: "Write it down."

I met Mira's eyes without blinking. She wrote:

**Haru Aerlen declined to make the world understand before it chose to listen.**

Rhea read the line over Mira's shoulder.

President Valmont: "That sentence will become a legal problem."

Aira: "Most honest sentences do."

Halden studied the Black Circle without his old hunger for taxonomy.

Professor Halden: "It may also become a safeguard definition."

Haru: "Do not make me a definition tonight."

He bowed his head once.

Professor Halden: "Recorded."

The fourth hour was just waiting.

Maelin finally made Elira sleep. Aira carried copies up to the first protection table and came back with three more pages, two reports, and an unlabeled honey cake someone had pressed into her hands. She stared at it as if it had violated protocol.

Aira: "Who gives emergency cake without documentation?"

Mira didn't look up from copying.

Mira: "Someone kind?"

Aira looked offended by the lack of attribution and close to tears anyway. She broke the cake into pieces and passed them round without ceremony. Rhea took a bite because Aira glared at her; Halden forgot he was holding his until Cassian returned and told him cake wouldn't turn into data if he stared at it long enough.

Leon came back last. His hands had been scrubbed clean, which somehow made it worse.

He stood inside the chapel doorway, looking from sleeping Elira to me to the Black Circle turning above the floor.

Leon: "I hated you once because I thought you were pretending to be weak."

Nobody moved.

Leon: "I was wrong."

Haru: "I was pretending."

Leon shook his head.

Leon: "No. You were trying not to become what people like me respect too easily."

The words weren't polished. They had been paid for. He looked at the empty space where Cael had lain and swallowed hard.

Leon: "Don't let them turn him into the reason you stop trying."

He turned and left before I could answer.

The fifth hour brought the first wrong story through a Council emergency relay, unofficial enough to deny and loud enough to sting.

**BLACK CIRCLE MANIFESTATION CAUSES STUDENT FATALITY DURING UNSANCTIONED RESPONSE**

Rhea read it, and the chapel went cold. Aira let out a word that would never survive formal minutes. Mira's face went hard with pure offense. Her chair scraped back.

Mira: "No."

Rhea: "Mira."

Mira: "No."

She snatched the public sequence copy, slapped it flat on the relay table, and underlined three lines hard enough to score the second sheet underneath.

**ELIRA VALE KILLED BEFORE BLACK CIRCLE MANIFESTATION.**

**CAEL NOX ACTIVATED VOTIVE RETURN AFTER DOMINION SEVERANCE.**

**BLACK CIRCLE EVENT FOLLOWED CATASTROPHIC WITNESS BREACH.**

Mira: "If they want to lie, make them step over the order."

Rhea looked at her for one long second.

President Valmont: "Send it."

The correction went out as an image of Mira's own handwriting, messy and furious and unmistakably human while institutions prepared to fight over jurisdiction. The lie didn't vanish, yet from that hour onward it had to drag the truth behind it.

The sixth hour ended without drama. No burst of shadow, no final bell, and no neat return to secrecy.

The Black Circle slowed until its outer rings thinned into smoke, then pressure, then the familiar cold behind my sternum. One inner ring stayed etched beneath the skin of my wrist. The false Green crest wouldn't come back on its own; I would have to rebuild it by hand in front of everyone who knew what it covered, choosing smallness while my fingers still shook.

Elira woke just as the final ring faded to shadow. Her eyes found my bare wrist. No Green yet. No black flare either. Just a hairline darkness beneath the skin, easy to miss and impossible for her not to see.

She reached for my hand. I gave it to her.

She turned my wrist palm up and rested two fingers against the center where no crest showed.

Elira: "There you are."

Lucien had said those words like discovery meant ownership. Elira said them like presence was enough. I stayed quiet, listening to her breathe beside the people who had refused to become symbols for my grief.

When Maelin cleared her to be moved upstairs, healers carried the cot into the quiet infirmary ward while I walked beside it, holding the last tremor of the circle still until Elira's channels stopped trying to remember death.

## Interlude: Aira - The Part That Gets Written

Aira Solen had always trusted records less than people assumed. She kept them anyway, because a record wasn't truth; it was a chair left in the room where truth might sit down before stronger people walked in with cleaner boots and renamed it.

She hadn't learned that in a grand tragedy. Grand tragedies gave adults too many excuses to sound profound afterward. She learned it from attendance sheets that made absence look identical whether a student was sick, frightened, punished, or short on carriage fare, and from teachers who knew ranked names by heart and Green Circle students by seating row.

So Aira wrote things down. First because she was good at it, later because competence hurt less than admitting she needed it to matter.

Haru had been on his knees with the Black Circle tearing a city apart around him, and Aira's first reflex had still been sequence. That scared her worse than the spinning rings. Other people screamed, ran, or prayed; Aira hunted for the sentence with a missing subject and patched it before someone could weaponize the gap.

She didn't write only to protect Haru. She wrote because Elira lay dead on stone, Cael was being digested into Noctis grammar, Lucien was waiting to steal the first draft of history, and somebody had to stay sharp enough not to look away.

That was the bill nobody asked if she wanted to pay. Asking what it cost to be the room's memory was dangerous during an emergency, so she filed the question under later along with everything else.

When the floor stopped splitting under Elira's knees and Mira forced the sequence lock into stone, Aira felt something behind her ribs fold itself smaller so she could keep writing.

Good. That was Haru holding still. Record it.

She wrote the line down and never asked who would record what it cost her to keep writing.

## Aira's Margin - Before Three

In the infirmary, Aira wrote the clean record first because clean records survived committees.

Then she opened her second notebook for the margin, because clean records lied by omission even when every sentence was true.

Margin: Haru hasn't slept and won't drop his grip on the circle until Maelin clears Elira's channels. Elira is pretending survival pain is a scheduling concern. Leon is down the hall in the eastern preparation room with Nera and Oric, guarding Cael's bier as if attention alone can keep Noctis from taking him. Cassian brought honey cake and bread nobody asked for and left them where people could reach without saying thank you. Rhea gets colder when frightened. Valen gets gentler when furious.

Aira paused, chewing the end of her pen.

Then she added: When they read the official version of today, don't let it make us sound braver than we were. Fear was present. So was love. So was fury. None invalidate witness.

The margin ink dried crooked.

Beneath the infirmary clock, Aira kept both notebooks open across her knees while Elira finally slept beneath three warming cloths and the minute hand crept toward three.

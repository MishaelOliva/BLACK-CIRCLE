# Episode 2: **A Quiet Humiliation**

## Interlude: Elira - A Choice Not To Trust

Elira Vale had been taught to mistrust coincidence before she learned advanced shielding.

Coincidence, her first etiquette tutor had warned, was usually ambition wearing soft shoes. A student happened to share her study table, then asked about Yellow Circle channeling. A visiting heir happened to admire the same balcony view, then mentioned alliance proposals over tea.

By thirteen, Elira understood that being admired was not the same as being seen. By sixteen, she understood that being seen could be worse.

Haru Aerlen was hiding something. That was no longer a suspicion. It was a fact with unfinished edges.

He had moved too cleanly against Leon. He had studied the assessment floor like someone calculating ways not to win. He had accepted a strip of healer cloth in the west corridor as if kindness were a snare with hidden hinges.

A sensible girl would leave it there. A Yellow Circle prodigy did not need another mystery attached to her name, least of all a Green Circle transfer whose silences felt engineered.

Elira stood alone in the upper archive gallery and read the same paragraph three times without absorbing a word. Outside the tall windows, Aurelis glittered under evening lamps: beautiful, ordered, always watching itself.

She could already hear the arguments for distance.

Her mother's voice: *affection is never private for people in visible positions.*

Her father's: *every attachment creates leverage.*

Professor Halden's, if he knew enough to weigh in: *anomalous behavior should be observed before approached.*

Her own, most inconvenient of all: Haru had lied. Not by word, perhaps, but by construction. The Green Circle on his wrist did not match the person standing behind it. He had reasons, probably serious ones, and serious reasons did not make secrecy harmless.

Elira closed the book.

She was not naive enough to think kindness required trust. She could stay courteous from across a room. She could step in, if needed, without stepping closer. She could let Aira befriend him, let Dren evaluate him, and keep her own name clear of whatever he carried.

The thought should have settled her. It did not.

Because of Leon. Not his bruised pride or Haru's footwork, but the half-second after.

Leon had nearly folded his own collapsing spell straight through his casting wrist. Elira had seen it a breath before Dren moved and had gathered mana on reflex. Then Haru stepped straight into the shear.

He had hidden the correction inside his own injury. No announcement. No glance toward the upper tiers. No effort to make Leon realize what had been spared. He had let the entire room keep the easier story because the true one would have cost too much.

Most people hid what shamed them. Some hid what profited them. Haru hid something immense and still used it to shield a boy who had tried to humiliate him in front of his classmates.

That did not make him safe. It made him specific. Specific mattered.

Elira crossed to the window and looked down into the courtyard. Students passed in clusters, Circle colors flickering at wrists, collars, and throats. Greens yielded walkway space without realizing they were doing it. Blues occupied space without noticing either. Reds and Yellows were watched. Always watched.

Along the lower path, Haru walked alone. Not lonely, exactly. Practiced. A person could grow skilled at absence, and Elira knew that craft well enough, even if hers had always been hidden beneath applause.

Near the fountain, a first-year dropped an armload of books, his Green crest flashing bright with panic. Haru bent, helped gather the scattered pages, and handed them back before the boy could finish apologizing. Then he walked on without waiting for thanks.

The first-year stared after him, bewildered by how cleanly it had happened.

Elira's fingers tightened on the stone sill. There. That was the reason. Not the strange precision in his casting. It was the way he treated help like something that might turn into a claim if he stood near it too long. He kept walking away before gratitude could become evidence.

Elira understood that instinct. She disliked how easily she understood it.

Trusting him might be foolish. Approaching him might be politically reckless. Wanting to know him might grow dangerous in ways she could not yet classify.

She accepted all three facts.

Turning from the glass, she drew out a single sheet of pale archive paper and wrote one line:

**Moonlit Archive Hall. Tomorrow evening, if you still want an honest conversation.**

She paused before signing her name, then added underneath:

**No audience. No assumptions.**

It was the narrowest door she knew how to offer. Not trust yet. Only a room where trust might decide whether to enter.

## Haru - The Ladder Match

Three days passed with too many whispers and too little quiet.

Elira's note from my first evening stayed folded inside my desk like a promise postponed, not forgotten. Leon's ladder challenge had shoved everything private aside, and Aurelis loved nothing more than turning private friction into a crowd event.

The combat ladder arena was louder than usual. That was Leon's doing.

By the time I reached the circular battle hall after afternoon classes, half of **Aurelis Arcane Academy** had packed the observation tiers. Students leaned over the brass railings, balancing notebooks and wagers while trading loud predictions about how fast this would end.

The Green Circle transfer who had disrupted Leon Varek once by "luck" was stepping into an official bracket match. To the gallery, it was theater. To Leon, it was damage control. To me, it was an inconvenience.

Aira walked onto the upper platform beside me, looked down at the packed hall, looked at me, and looked back down as if estimating the blast radius.

Aira: "This is terrible."

Haru: "You sound certain."

Aira: "Because I am. There are too many witnesses here for anything to stay normal."

Haru: "Nothing here has been normal since I arrived."

Aira: "True. This is a much louder kind of not normal."

She studied my face more closely.

Aira: "You're calm."

Haru: "Should I not be?"

Aira: "That depends. Are you calm because you've made peace with public humiliation, or calm because you're about to do something that gives me a stress condition?"

Haru: "I'm not that difficult."

Aira gave me a flat stare.

Aira: "Haru, I say this with affection. You are the human form of hidden complications."

The corner of my mouth twitched before I stopped it.

Down on the arena floor, Leon was already waiting near the center ring in a spotless training coat, sleeves rolled neatly, blond hair catching the lamps in the exact way he probably hoped it would look in someone else's memory.

Even from the platform, I could feel the hairline cracks underneath the polish. He had built too much of himself around being seen as superior. The assessment's embarrassment three days earlier had not simply bruised his pride; it had rattled the scaffolding holding him upright. That made him more reckless, not stronger.

A quiet hush rippled down the stands. I turned.

Elira descended the eastern stair with unhurried composure, her elite mantle settled cleanly over her shoulders while the golden light of her **Yellow Circle** crest caught the arena lamps at her collarbone. She never asked for attention; attention simply rearranged itself around her.

Aira followed my gaze and let out a slow breath.

Aira: "There it is."

Haru: "What?"

Aira: "The exact face people make right before their lives get emotionally complicated."

Haru: "I was just looking."

Aira: "Mm."

Elira reached our railing and offered Aira a warm nod before turning to me.

Elira: "You came."

Haru: "I said I would."

Elira: "A rare quality at this academy."

Aira: "Keeping promises?"

Elira: "Walking voluntarily into obvious trouble."

Aira: "Then yes, that sounds right."

I ignored both of them with as much dignity as the situation allowed.

Elira glanced down at the bright floor, then back to me.

Elira: "You don't look nervous."

Haru: "People keep expecting that."

A quiet smile touched her mouth.

Elira: "Most people fear public judgment more than combat."

Haru: "Do you?"

For half a second her eyes shifted, just enough to show the real person living underneath all that grace.

Elira: "I used to."

Before I could answer, an amplified voice rolled across the battle hall.

Announcer: "Combat Ladder Preliminary Match: Leon Varek of Year Two, Section Three versus Haru Aerlen, transfer student of Year Two, Section Three. Standard academy restrictions apply. Non-lethal engagement only. Barrier interference forbidden. Excessive force subject to disciplinary review."

Aira crossed her arms tight.

Aira: "If you get hurt, I'll be furious."

Haru: "That seems backwards."

Aira: "It's efficient."

Elira held my gaze steadily.

Elira: "Be careful."

Her voice was quiet and plain, harder to brush off than any warning I had received on campus.

Haru: "I will."

I turned and took the steps down to the arena floor.

---

The barrier rose around us in a tall ring of pale blue light.

The ladder arena was wider than the assessment hall, ringed by perimeter instructors and scored with concentric containment grooves built to absorb high-grade elemental backlash without cracking.

Leon waited opposite me with a practiced smile, thinner and tighter than before.

Leon: "Glad you showed up."

Haru: "You sounded like you'd be difficult if I didn't."

A scatter of laughs broke from the stands. Leon's jaw tightened.

Leon: "Let's see if you keep that tone once we start."

Haru: "I'll do my best."

The match official lifted one hand.

Official: "Begin."

Leon struck without probing. Three compressed streams of water mana surged across the ring in a tight triangle: one high, one center, one skimming flat across the tiles to sweep my footing out from under me.

I stepped once to the right. The high jet missed. I turned my shoulder so the center stream hissed past my coat, then lifted one hand and severed the low sweep with a razor-thin mana edge fine enough to pass for reinforcement dispersion.

The water burst into harmless spray. Murmurs rose in the gallery.

Leon's eyes narrowed. He snapped straight into a heavier sequence, raising two curving walls of dense water on either flank to box me inward while a line of compressed needles formed directly above my head.

I had planned to keep this modest, just clean enough to settle the bracket and step off the stage.

Then I caught Leon's eyes across the water.

Not discipline. Not focus. Hunger. He did not want a clean win; he wanted the Green transfer broken badly enough that nobody would speak of three days ago ever again.

After three days of hallway whispers and smug wagers, a colder piece of me decided I was done cushioning his ego. Not completely. Just enough.

I let a fraction more power wake beneath my ribs. Nowhere near enough to show the truth, yet more than I had allowed on day one. Enough that the false Green crest under my left cuff stretched tight around the flow. Enough that the air around my palms turned crisp and sharp. Enough that the sleeping darkness behind my sternum pulsed once, almost pleased to be acknowledged.

Leon slammed the water walls inward.

I walked straight forward into the closing seam.

My palm met the left wall of water mana. To the stands, it looked like a sharp reinforcement break. To anyone with high-grade perception, it would have felt far stranger, as though the spell had simply lost permission to remain solid where my fingers touched it.

The wall collapsed into cold mist. Leon's eyes widened.

I crossed the gap before his overhead needles finished locking. His reflexes were quick: he pivoted hard and forged a hardened water blade along his forearm for close quarters.

I caught his wrist.

The entire arena went dead quiet. Not because blocking a strike was impressive, but because I stopped it too easily.

Leon poured mana into the construct, trying to force the blade through my guard. I tightened my fingers by a fraction. The hardened water shattered into glass-bright drops.

Leon: "What-"

I stepped inside his stance, pressed two fingers lightly against the center of his chest, and released a tight pulse of force. Not enough to crack bone. More than enough to launch him.

Leon lifted off his heels, skidded hard across the containment tiles, and slid to a halt near the outer ring.

Gasps detonated across the tiers. Leon coughed, propped himself onto one elbow, and stared back at me in raw disbelief before the stands exploded.

Student 1: "No way."

Student 2: "He threw Leon across the ring."

Student 3: "That wasn't Green Circle output."

Student 4: "Then what was it?"

Student 5: "Did he reinforce his whole frame first?"

Student 6: "I didn't even see the cast."

Up on the platform, Aira was already standing, caught halfway between shock and vindication. Beside her, Elira had gone completely still, golden eyes locked on my hands with razor focus.

Leon scrambled upright, dignity burned down to cinders. Water mana lashed around him in jagged coils.

Leon: "You think one hit settles this?"

Haru: "No."

I kept my voice level across the quiet floor.

Haru: "I think the next one will."

A sharp wave of startled laughs and indrawn breaths swept the bleachers. Up above, Aira clapped one hand over her face.

Aira: "Oh no."

Beside her, the faintest curve touched Elira's mouth.

Leon roared and emptied his reserve at once. A crown of water spears materialized overhead, a pressure wave rolled across the floor, and thick mist burst upward to blind the arena while hidden tendrils of compressed water raced through the fog toward my ankles.

All temper, no control.

I exhaled once and stepped into the mist.

To the gallery, I vanished. To Leon, I arrived in front of him far too soon.

Real fear flashed across his face. He threw up a water shield; I brushed it apart. He tried to backpedal; I swept his leading boot. He reached for another cast; I tapped the casting line above his wrist and folded the spell shut before it could form.

Then I stopped, one hand hovering an inch from his throat without touching skin, my other palm resting lightly over the center of his active mana channel. One pulse would put him flat on the stone.

I did not need it. The fog thinned, and the whole hall saw the match as it stood: Leon off-balance, wide-eyed, spell dead; me untouched, one breath from finishing him.

The official did not hesitate.

Official: "Match over! Winner: Haru Aerlen!"

The barrier chimed once as it dropped, and noise crashed down from every tier.

Near the lower rail, a Green Circle boy pressed both hands over his mouth as if afraid to let vindication show too openly. Two rows up, a Blue girl stared at Leon's broken posture like someone watching an axiom fail in daylight. On the western tier, a Red Circle heir said nothing at all; his eyes simply shifted from Leon to me, already running numbers.

Student 7: "Brutal."

Student 8: "He took him apart."

Student 9: "Leon didn't land a single clean hit."

Student 10: "How is a Green moving like that?"

Student 11: "His output changed."

Student 12: "No, his control changed."

Student 13: "Either way, Leon's finished."

Leon's life would go on, of course. His pride, at least for today, had died in front of half the academy.

I stepped back and lowered my hands. Leon stood frozen for half a beat, then looked up at the gallery and absorbed the full scale of the silence around his name. His next protest died in his throat as the cold realization hit him that I had never been straining.

Without a word, he turned and walked straight out of the arena under the weight of three hundred stares. Nobody called after him.

I looked up toward the observation deck. Aira stared at me like a friend whose worst suspicion had just grown fangs. Beside her, Elira watched with quiet, unmistakable admiration. That was going to be a separate problem.

---

I slipped out through the west corridor hoping to beat the post-match crowd. That lasted about forty seconds.

Quick footsteps echoed behind me near the garden stairwell.

Aira: "Haru."

I stopped. She caught up half a breath short, folded her arms, and fixed me with a look immune to polite deflection.

Aira: "One honest answer."

Haru: "Only one?"

Aira: "For now. What was that?"

Haru: "A ladder match."

Aira stared at me without blinking.

Aira: "I am working very hard to stay your friend."

Haru: "I appreciate that."

Aira: "Then meet me halfway."

I weighed how much truth stayed safe.

Haru: "Leon lost his temper. His channeling got sloppy, and he left openings."

Aira: "That explains half of it."

Haru: "Only half?"

Aira: "Haru."

Before she could press further, calm footsteps rounded the arch behind her. Elira stepped into the lantern light as though the roar of the arena had never reached her.

Elira: "You fought efficiently."

Haru: "That's generous."

Elira: "Would you prefer 'mercilessly'?"

Aira pointed straight at Elira.

Aira: "Thank you. I feel less crazy."

A small, real smile slipped past my guard before I could stop it. Both of them caught it at once.

Aira: "You're smiling."

Haru: "Briefly."

Elira kept her golden eyes on mine.

Elira: "You chose not to hold back as much today."

Not phrased as a question.

Haru: "A little."

Aira let out a strangled laugh.

Aira: "That was 'a little'?"

Haru: "Yes."

Aira pressed two fingers to her forehead.

Aira: "I need a completely new measuring stick for you."

Elira's gaze softened.

Elira: "You could have injured him badly. You didn't."

Haru: "I didn't want to."

Elira: "I know."

She said it far too easily, as if she had already made up her mind about what lived under my restraint. Worse, she was right.

Aira glanced back and forth between us, sighed deeply, and stepped back toward the stairs.

Aira: "I suddenly feel like I'm standing in the middle of something that hasn't officially started yet."

Haru: "You aren't interrupting anything."

Aira: "That is the exact sentence people speak right before they accidentally develop feelings."

Elira laughed softly. For tactical reasons, I said nothing.

Aira pointed at both of us.

Aira: "I'm leaving before either of you says something quiet and emotionally catastrophic."

Haru: "Dramatic."

Aira: "Accurate."

She disappeared down the stairwell, throwing me one parting glance that clearly promised a longer interrogation at breakfast.

Back inside the emptying arena, high in the last row of the stands, a Green Circle girl with curly auburn hair had not yet moved.

Mira Castellan held a worn notebook flat against her chest. Her uniform cuffs were mended with stitches neat enough that only someone used to repairing her own clothes would notice.

Green Student: "Had to be luck."

Mira kept her eyes on the shadowed corridor where Haru had gone.

Mira: "Twice?"

Her classmate shifted awkwardly on the bench.

Green Student: "You really think a Green can keep beating Blues?"

Mira's bright green eyes stayed calm and measuring. Hope was dangerous when held too loudly.

Mira: "I think we're about to find out."

Down on the lower tier, two Red Circle students laughed a little too sharply about Leon having an off afternoon, while a Blue upper-year called Haru's timing a cheap trick and sounded slightly too loud about it.

---

In the west corridor, it was just Elira and me.

Through the tall windows, the sky beyond Aurelis's towers had shifted into deep gold and violet, and the long shadow of the cliffs stretched across the lower gardens. Mana lamps hummed softly to life along the walls.

Elira broke the quiet first.

Elira: "You embarrassed him thoroughly."

Haru: "That wasn't the main goal."

Elira: "You didn't mind it either."

I thought about it for half a second.

Haru: "Less than I probably should have."

Amusement lit her eyes.

Elira: "That might be the most honest answer you've given me."

Haru: "That sounds like you think I've been lying."

Elira tilted her head.

Elira: "Guarded. Not dishonest."

She glanced toward the dusk outside the glass, then back to me.

Elira: "Walk with me?"

Haru: "People will talk."

Elira: "They already are."

Haru: "Then yes."

We exited through the west archway onto the upper garden path. Evening had settled over **Aurelis Arcane Academy**, silver-leaf trees catching warm lantern light and scattering pale patterns across the stone. A few passing students slowed, stared, and looked away too fast, as if caught witnessing something they did not yet know how to explain. Elira moved past every glance without shifting her pace.

We stopped at a raised overlook where the academy terrace dropped away above the lit avenues of Aurelis City. Elira rested both hands lightly on the stone rail.

Elira: "When I was younger, I came up here whenever the academy felt too crowded."

Haru: "You've been here long?"

Elira: "Since preparatory division. Everyone assumes that makes things easier."

Haru: "Did it?"

She looked out across the glittering avenues below.

Elira: "No. When you wear a Yellow Circle, people decide who you are before you open your mouth. Some admire you, some resent you, some want your attention to rearrange their future."

Haru: "Does it?"

She cut a faint smile sideways at me.

Elira: "Sometimes. That's part of the problem."

Haru: "That sounds lonely too."

The line left me before I thought to trim it back. Elira turned toward me, genuine surprise softening her face.

Elira: "It can be."

Haru: "You hide it well."

Elira let out a quiet breath that was almost a laugh.

Elira: "So do you."

She leaned slightly against the rail, studying my face in the twilight.

Elira: "You knew I was watching from the platform before your match."

Haru: "Yes."

Elira: "Did it make you nervous?"

Haru: "No. More careful."

Elira: "Because of me?"

Haru: "Partly. And because I'm still figuring out how much of myself this place can see without breaking."

She held my gaze without blinking.

Elira: "Then I'm glad you let it see that much. Hiding gets heavy when you carry it alone too long."

Haru: "You notice too much."

Elira: "And you say too little."

Haru: "Balanced, then."

A real laugh escaped her, and I felt the corner of my own mouth lift before I remembered to stop it.

Elira: "There. Again."

Haru: "Again what?"

Elira: "You smile like it surprises even you."

I looked out across the city lights to hide the heat creeping up my collar.

Haru: "Inconveniently specific."

Elira: "I warned you I pay attention."

Silence settled easily between us. Wind rustled through the silver leaves overhead. Far below, the capital's transit sigils drifted like slow embers across the dark.

Then Elira's voice dropped softer.

Elira: "May I ask something difficult?"

Haru: "You always ask dangerous questions very calmly."

Elira: "Is that a no?"

Haru: "Ask."

Her fingers curled slightly against the carved stone.

Elira: "When you broke Leon's water wall today, for one second your mana felt different."

Outwardly I did not twitch. Inwardly every alarm snapped upright.

Elira spoke before the quiet could freeze.

Elira: "Not louder. Not merely bigger. It felt..." She searched for the exact weight of it. "Deeper."

Out of every word in the language, she had landed straight on the truest one.

Haru: "You're very perceptive."

Elira: "That isn't an answer."

Haru: "It's the safest one I have."

She did not push.

Elira: "All right."

No demand, no shift in warmth. Just *all right*. Somehow that cost me more than an interrogation would have.

Haru: "Does it bother you? That I'm hiding things?"

She considered the question seriously before meeting my eyes.

Elira: "It worries me more than it bothers me. People usually hide for one of two reasons: to protect someone else, or because they believe nobody would understand."

Haru: "Which do you think I'm doing?"

Her answer arrived so quietly the breeze nearly stole it.

Elira: "Both."

I had no ready defense for that. A stray lock of pale-gold hair brushed her cheek; she tucked it behind one ear without thinking, small and unstudied, and the evening bell tolled from the clock tower before I could decide what to say.

We had drifted half a pace closer without either of us marking when.

Elira: "If I ask you to come to the north library tonight, am I being too persistent?"

Haru: "Probably."

Elira: "Will you come anyway?"

Simpler to refuse. Safer to walk away. Neither option felt honest anymore.

Haru: "Yes."

Her smile broke open without a trace of hallway polish.

Elira: "Good."

Footsteps scraped on the lower walkway as two students rounded the arch, froze at the sight of us, and immediately failed at pretending they had seen nothing.

Student 1: "Oh."

Student 2: "Don't say it."

Student 1: "I didn't say anything."

Student 2: "Your face said enough."

They hurried down the stairs. Elira shut her eyes for a brief, amused second.

Elira: "Aira was right. People are going to talk."

Haru: "Do you mind?"

She held my gaze for a full beat before answering.

Elira: "Not if they're wrong about the important parts."

We turned back toward the residence walkways at an unhurried pace. Most of the academy had drifted indoors for evening study. Halfway down the stone path toward the main stairwell, Elira slowed to a stop.

Elira: "There's something else."

Haru: "That sounds ominous."

Elira: "It might be."

My posture tightened at once. She folded her hands lightly in front of her coat.

Elira: "After your match, one of the senior faculty observers stopped me near the platform to ask about you."

Haru: "Normal enough after an upset."

Elira: "Not the way he asked."

Haru: "Who?"

Elira: "Professor Halden. Advanced Mana Research Division."

I knew the name by reputation: old structural theory, restricted archive clearance, quiet leverage over administrative review. Not someone I wanted studying my shadow.

Haru: "What did he ask?"

Elira: "Whether I had sensed anything unusual in your mana flow."

I stopped dead on the flagstone. Elira stopped opposite me.

Haru: "What did you tell him?"

Elira answered without a flicker of hesitation.

Elira: "That you were precise."

I looked straight at her.

Haru: "You covered for me."

Elira: "I answered truthfully."

Haru: "You left things out."

Elira: "Yes."

Haru: "Why?"

Her golden eyes stayed clear and steady in the lantern light.

Elira: "Because whatever you're carrying, I don't believe you're hiding it to hurt anyone."

Something old and locked tight inside my chest gave half an inch.

Haru: "Thank you."

Elira: "Save it. Once senior researchers get curious, they rarely stop looking after one question."

Haru: "Inconvenient."

Elira: "You use 'inconvenient' the way normal people use 'catastrophic.'"

Haru: "Easier to manage that way."

She smiled and resumed walking with me down to the residence fork where the dormitory paths split. Neither of us moved first.

Elira: "North wing. Moonlit Archive Hall. In an hour?"

Haru: "I'll be there."

She nodded once, then hesitated half a breath before turning.

Elira: "Haru?"

Haru: "Yes?"

Her expression softened.

Elira: "I'm glad you came to Aurelis."

Simple, plain, unguarded, and harder to absorb than any spell I had parried today.

Haru: "I think," I said quietly, "I'm glad too."

Warmth deepened in her eyes before she turned up the lit residence path, her gold crest fading slowly among the silver-leaf trees.

I stood at the fork a few seconds longer while clouds drifted across the moon.

Professor Halden. Advanced Mana Research. The threat around me was no longer a faceless note slipped under a locked door in Lumeris. It was starting to wear names.

Beneath the quiet illusion of the Green crest on my wrist, the Black Circle gave one low, wakeful pulse, as if it too had noticed that secrecy felt heavier the moment someone stood close enough to see through it.

## Moonlit Archive: The Question

That first night in the Moonlit Archive did not become a confession. At the time it felt like failure; later, I realized it was the only reason trust survived at all.

Elira waited beside the central reading table with one hand resting on a closed ledger and the other loose at her side, careful not to stand like a judge. The care was deliberate enough that I distrusted it for three full breaths before recognizing she had done it for my sake.

Elira: "No audience. No assumptions."

Haru: "You wrote that three nights ago."

Elira: "I meant it then. I mean it more tonight."

Moonlight pooled in pale rectangles across the flagstones between towering shelves. She did not rush into the question, which somehow made it heavier when it came.

Elira: "Three days ago in the assessment hall, you took a hit so Leon wouldn't tear open his own casting wrist. Tonight you dismantled him without losing your breath, and Professor Halden noticed the exact second your mana shifted."

I looked toward the arched windows. That was confirmation enough.

Elira: "I'm not asking what you are."

My gaze snapped back to her. Her expression remained steady.

Elira: "Not tonight, unless you choose to tell me. I'm asking whether whatever you're hiding is dangerous to you."

The easy lie sat right on my tongue: *No.*

Haru: "Sometimes."

Her fingers tightened once against the leather cover. Only once.

Elira: "Is it dangerous to others?"

Haru: "If handled badly."

Elira: "By you?"

I almost smiled, not out of humor, just because she had walked straight to the real question everyone else avoided until panic made them cruel.

Haru: "By anyone who thinks power gets safer once they own it."

She absorbed that without pretending to know more than she did. That was the first reason I stayed in the room.

Elira: "I can tell something is there."

Haru: "Seeing isn't the same as knowing safely."

Elira: "I know."

I believed her, which felt riskier than doubting her. Every old habit told me to step back, deflect, keep her outside the blast radius of whatever my secret turned people into. Yet she had offered no claim, no demand, no price. Only quiet space.

Haru: "I'll show you."

Her breath caught.

Haru: "Not tonight."

The hope in her face did not vanish; it shifted into something steadier, which somehow cost me more.

Haru: "If I show you tonight just because you covered for me with Halden, I'm not trusting you. I'm reacting because I want trust to be simpler than it is."

Elira looked down at the table for one heartbeat. When her eyes lifted again, they were bright and level.

Elira: "Then think."

No wounded pride. No flare of temper.

Haru: "You're disappointed."

Elira: "Yes."

Her chin lifted slightly, clear and candid.

Elira: "I would still rather be disappointed by a real answer than comforted by a reckless one."

I had no proper response for that. I inclined my head once and walked out before gratitude turned into something else I mishandled.

Behind me, the Moonlit Archive settled back into stillness, leaving Elira alone with the knowledge that something waited in the dark, and that I had not handed it over yet.

## Moonlit Archive: The Reveal

Three nights after that first conversation in the Archive, I arrived later than either of us had planned.

The three days between had not been empty.

On the first morning, Elira caught me outside the dining hall watching two Blue Circle boys turn a Green first-year's dropped tray into public theater. She wanted to step straight into the middle of the hall and dress them down. I wanted to help the boy out before turning him into the centerpiece of a moral lecture.

Elira: "Silence protects the people who did it."

Haru: "Public correction leaves him carrying everyone's eyes long after they forget the lesson."

Elira: "Then both of us are deciding what he can endure without asking him."

That ended the argument because she was right. We asked him. The boy wanted a fresh lunch, the spilled soup cleared before someone tracked through it, and an anonymous report logged so the same pair could not call it harmless next week. Elira filed the entry; I fixed the tray. Neither of us got the clean victory we had imagined, and the boy ate in peace.

On the second day, she joined Aira and me for a tactical drill and rejected my safest route because it sacrificed two simulated support casters to hold the objective. I called it efficient. She called it cowardice dressed up as arithmetic. Aira forced us to run both versions; Elira's saved more people while mine cleared the board eleven seconds faster. Elira did not gloat, which was almost irritating.

Between those clashes, small ordinary facts gathered. She disliked over-sweet tea. I walked into a doorframe while reading a textbook. She counted room exits when halls got crowded, though less visibly than I did. I learned that her public composure was never effortless; she simply hid the cost of it well.

Three days could not make trust safe. They were enough to prove we could disagree without turning difference into punishment.

The north library wing felt older than the rest of Aurelis, its stone walls holding centuries of quiet thought. Mana lamps floated low along the vaulted ceiling, casting warm circles over empty reading alcoves. Most students stayed clear after dusk; the silence here echoed back too sharply for people who preferred easy company.

For me, it felt almost peaceful.

Elira was already inside the **Moonlit Archive Hall**. She stood beside an arched window, one hand resting lightly on the spine of an open book she was no longer reading. Moonlight brushed her hair pale gold at the edges. In the stillness of the archive, with no one watching her posture or waiting on her rank, she looked less like an academy symbol and more like someone who had finally set down a heavy coat.

She looked up as my boots crossed the threshold.

Elira: "You came."

Haru: "You doubted it?"

Elira smiled faintly.

Elira: "No. I just wanted to say it."

I stepped deeper into the hall. Tall rows of volumes bound in leather, rune-thread, and preservation cloth stretched into the dimness, and in the center of the floor sat a broad pale stone sigil inlaid flush with the tiles.

Haru: "You were right. Nobody comes up here."

Elira slid her book back onto the shelf and walked toward the central reading table.

Elira: "Most students only care for old records if they raise a ranking score."

Haru: "And you?"

Elira: "I like rooms where people lower their voices."

I joined her beside the table. Moonlight lay across one edge of the polished wood, warm lamp glow across the other.

Elira: "I wanted somewhere we could talk without half the academy listening."

Haru: "That already makes this my favorite room on campus."

She laughed quietly. For a while we talked about ordinary things: classes, instructors, academy politics, how quickly polite distance froze into silence whenever rank entered a room. Then, inevitably, the talk drifted toward Magic Circles.

Elira walked to the tall arched window overlooking the moonlit gardens.

Elira: "Do you think people would be kinder if circles didn't exist?"

I leaned lightly against the end of the nearest bookshelf.

Haru: "No."

She glanced back, surprised rather than offended.

Elira: "Immediate answer."

Haru: "People would invent another ladder. Wealth. Family. Affinity type. Appearance. Lineage. Talent. Fear is inventive."

Elira turned fully toward me.

Elira: "Then what makes circles different?"

Haru: "They glow."

For a beat she simply looked at me, then understanding softened her face.

Elira: "Visible judgment."

Haru: "If cruelty looks organized and natural enough, people stop calling it cruelty. They call it order."

Silence lingered. Her Yellow crest glowed faintly against her collarbone, reflected in the dark glass.

Elira: "When I awakened as Yellow, everyone celebrated. My family cried from relief. Instructors changed their tone overnight. Other children either idolized me or backed away. Adults called me special before I even knew what the word cost. Nobody asked if I wanted it."

Haru: "Did you?"

She looked down at her hands.

Elira: "At first I liked the praise. I was a child. Then I noticed what happened once people decided I stood too high to touch. They stopped speaking plainly. They admired a polite statue that made them feel safe."

Haru: "Not you."

Elira nodded.

Elira: "Not me."

Isolation came in different shapes, and power was one of its sharpest blades.

Elira lifted her eyes back to mine.

Elira: "You notice Green Circle students more than anyone else here."

Haru: "Because almost nobody else does."

I folded my arms loosely.

Haru: "A Green student walks into a room and people assign their ceiling before they speak. Quiet means weak. Confident means arrogant. Skilled means suspicious. Winning means luck. The world doesn't merely rank them lower. It narrates them lower."

Recognition crossed her face, sharp and free of pity.

Elira: "You talk like you've lived inside that your whole life."

Haru: "Close enough."

She let the answer stand.

Elira: "Blues stay defensive to prove they belong above Greens. Reds live inside gilded cages where no one forgives a single crack."

I glanced at her, surprised by how cleanly she stripped the paint off both ranks.

Elira: "And Yellow?"

I really looked at her then: the drilled poise, the solitude worn so gracefully that everyone mistook it for pride, and the kindness that had somehow survived intact underneath.

Haru: "Yellow Circles get treated like ideals before they're treated like human beings."

She went completely still. Something deep shifted behind her eyes.

Elira: "That," she whispered, "is the first time anyone has ever named it right."

We let the quiet breathe. Then Elira looked down at the pale stone circle inlaid across the center of the floor.

Elira: "And Black?"

My whole body locked before I could tell it not to. She saw it immediately. She did not step closer, did not press, did not raise her voice.

Elira: "You don't have to tell me," she murmured. "If you ever want to... I'll listen."

No demand. No price tag. Only listening.

I stood in the quiet, hearing wind brush the window glass, the low hum of shelf wards, and the slow beat of my own pulse behind my ribs. Every survival habit built over eighteen years shouted *no*. Hide. Deflect. Protect the secret. Protect her. Protect the world from what it would do once it knew.

Yet Elira had already seen the outline of danger and answered with care. She had shielded me from Halden without knowing the whole cost.

Some truths are terrifying because they break trust. Others are terrifying because they ask for it.

I let out a long breath.

Haru: "If I show you, you can't speak of it to anyone."

Her eyes widened slightly with the weight of the moment, without a hint of fear.

Elira: "I understand."

Haru: "No one. Not faculty, not friends, not family. Not under pressure or threat. Not unless I give permission."

She straightened, every trace of casual softness replaced by solemn clarity.

Elira: "You have my word."

I looked straight into her eyes.

Haru: "Words are cheap in a world like this."

Without hesitating, she nodded.

Elira: "Then tell me what promise you need."

I walked to the edge of the pale foundation sigil where moonlight pooled across the stone. Before taking the final step, I released a soundless isolation veil through the floor, shelves, glass, and old archive wards. Thin enough that no student outside would notice it, yet dense enough to swallow pressure, light, and sound before they reached the corridor beyond.

Haru: "A binding promise."

Elira stepped forward, expression completely serious.

Elira: "A mana vow."

Haru: "Yes."

A mana vow was never casual. It braided truth, will, and magic together; breaking one carried real consequences, sometimes permanently scarring a caster's channels.

Elira: "You trust me enough to show me something worth a vow?"

I looked down at the pale stone between our boots.

Haru: "I'm deciding that right now."

Instead of cooling at the bluntness, her gaze only grew gentler. She stepped into the moonlit circle opposite me.

Elira: "Then let me decide too."

She raised one hand. A small golden sigil bloomed between us, warm and sharp.

Elira: "I, Elira Vale, vow by my circle, my mana, and my will that whatever truth Haru Aerlen entrusts to me tonight, I will not reveal to any other soul without his permission."

The gold ring pulsed once.

Elira: "I will not use it against him. I will not surrender it willingly. And if I am ever forced to choose between guarding his secret and guarding my reputation, I will guard his secret."

That last line startled me enough that my eyes snapped up to hers. Her expression did not flinch. The sigil brightened cleanly, confirming complete alignment between word and intent.

Haru: "You're serious."

Elira: "Completely."

Drawing a slow breath, I laid my palm over her golden sigil. A single hair-thin thread of dark mana curled from my skin and looped around her light without dimming it. Even that tiny whisper made the archive air shiver.

Haru: "Then I, Haru Aerlen, accept your vow. And I vow in return that the truth I show you tonight will never be used to harm you. I will protect you from the burden of it as long as I can."

Dark thread and golden sigil braided together, then dissolved into a quiet pulse of warm and cold light that sank into the stone beneath us. The vow sealed.

She had not asked what my power could buy her, or how to aim it. She had placed her own name beneath my secret before she even knew its shape. The difference made my throat tight.

For a breath, Elira only stared at the space where that single hair-thin thread of darkness had hovered.

Elira: "That mana..."

I lowered my hand.

Haru: "Now you see why this can't leave this room."

She nodded slowly, awe edged with clear-eyed caution.

The vow was done. The room was veiled. Deciding to show her had been a clean thought inside my head. Actually doing it was something else entirely.

My hands did not know Elira's name as safety. My pulse did not know it. The Black Circle beneath my false Green wrist crest knew pursuit, locked rooms, and leaving before questions turned into chains. In every version of discovery I had rehearsed for years, the truth was torn out of me by enemy magic or exposed because someone I cared about stood in the line of fire.

No one was forcing anything tonight. I had to open my own hand.

For one foolish second I wished she would look away just long enough to spare me the instant of becoming visible on purpose. She did not look away.

I dropped my gaze to the floor between us and loosened the concealment over my wrist crest.

The Black Circle resisted out of sheer habit. I had to steady my breath and remind it that this room was different. Not completely open. Just enough.

The archive answered at once. Overhead lamps tilted inward. One shelf rune gave a thin, startled chime.

Black lines unspooled from beneath my boots, stopping cleanly inside the pale foundation ring. One inner circle unfolded between us, no wider than our joined shadows. Ink-black. Silver markings turned inside it with a precision I had never been taught and still did not fully trust. Behind the visible lines lay deeper geometry I kept locked away, a stillness too vast for this hall to hold.

This was not the whole Circle. Only the smallest honest fragment I could show without waking every ancient ward in Aurelis.

Elira stepped back on pure reflex. Her Yellow Circle flared bright at her collarbone, less a challenge than a great light recognizing something far older than itself.

Elira: "Haru..."

I kept my voice steady.

Haru: "This is what my Green crest hides. Black Circle."

Speaking the words aloud felt like cracking open the sky. The inner ring pulsed once; a high window hummed at the edge of hearing until I reined the pressure back in.

Elira could not look away, and I could not look away from her face. She looked neither small nor worshipful. She looked like Elira: shaken, thinking hard, refusing to let awe think for her.

Elira: "It's real."

Haru: "Yes."

Her breathing came controlled, though unsteady at the edges.

Elira: "How can something like this exist?"

Haru: "I don't fully know. I was born with it. I learned how to hide it before I learned what it was."

She drew one measured breath and steadied her shoulders.

Elira: "May I come closer?"

Even now. Even staring at this. She asked.

Haru: "Yes. Slowly."

She advanced one pace at a time, a soft veil of golden light wrapping her like a quiet brace against the room's pressure, and halted right at the rim of the black ring.

The contrast between us cut like glass. Her mana was brilliant, warm, noble. Mine sat beneath comparison, older than rank.

Elira knelt slowly at the boundary, not in submission, simply trying to read the turning silver script without touching it.

Elira: "It feels endless."

Haru: "It is."

She looked up, eyes shining with the sheer scale of what she was witnessing.

Elira: "Earlier, when I said your mana felt deeper..."

Haru: "You were right."

Her breath trembled once despite her poise.

Elira: "This is beyond Yellow."

Haru: "Far beyond."

For half a second I considered stopping there. It wasn't enough, not if she needed to understand why hiding had been survival.

I lifted one hand. The Black Circle answered with no chant, no circle trace, no formal casting sequence. A single thread of darkness rose from the stone and hovered above my palm, fine as silk and deeper than any natural shadow. It twisted once.

Air thickened across the archive. Moonlight sharpened into bright silver bars on the floor. Overhead lamps dimmed, not extinguished, only subdued, as if bowing to an older law.

Haru: "This is the smallest amount I can show safely without damaging the building."

Her gaze jumped to my face.

Elira: "The smallest?"

Haru: "Yes."

I shifted my eyes across the hall to an old wrought-iron bookstand anchored beside a stone pedestal. It weighed enough that two students would have struggled to shift it by hand.

The black thread drifted across the moonlight and touched the iron.

No crack. No flare. The bookstand simply stopped obeying ordinary rules. It lifted ten feet off the stone as though gravity had been quietly informed it no longer held jurisdiction there.

Elira inhaled sharply.

With a half-turn of my wrist, the iron rewrote itself without melting or breaking. Solid metal stretched into impossibly fine geometric ribs, unfolding into a suspended lattice of nested rings. Symbols none of the academy's libraries still recorded turned slowly inside the frame.

A heartbeat later, I closed my fingers.

The lattice folded back into ordinary iron. The bookstand settled silently onto its exact original marks, leaving neither scratch nor dust disturbed.

Elira stared at the iron, then at me, then at the dark thread turning calmly above my palm.

Elira: "You changed reality."

Haru: "A little."

Elira: "Nobody should be able to say that sentence so calmly."

Haru: "I've had practice."

I let the thread dissolve while the Black Circle continued turning slowly beneath my boots.

Haru: "Ordinary magic pushes, ignites, freezes, reinforces, or heals inside the world's rules. Black Circle negotiates with the rules directly."

Elira rose slowly to her feet, pale not from panic, but from a sharp mind racing to measure what she had just seen.

Elira: "Then if you wanted to..."

She stopped. I finished it for her.

Haru: "Yes. If I stopped choosing restraint, very little in this world could stop me from unmaking cities, armies, or institutions. Maybe more."

The silence afterward felt enormous.

Elira did not recoil. She did not step away. Instead a quiet, aching grief crossed her face: grief for what it meant to carry that alone.

Elira: "You've lived with this by yourself."

Haru: "I had to."

Elira: "No."

Sudden heat sharpened her voice as she stepped half a pace closer to the circle's rim.

Elira: "You were forced to. Those aren't the same thing."

For one heartbeat I could not answer. Anyone else would have asked what I could destroy, or how to leash me. Standing in moonlight before a power that could erase her without effort, Elira had seen the solitude first.

Haru: "You should be more afraid than you are."

Her golden eyes held mine steadily.

Elira: "Maybe." She took one small step nearer. "I'm not afraid of you."

The Black Circle beneath my feet gave one slow, quiet pulse as if it had heard her. I dropped my gaze.

Haru: "You should at least be cautious."

Elira: "I am."

Haru: "This doesn't look like caution."

The faintest smile touched the corner of her mouth.

Elira: "It's a different kind."

I had no answer for that, so I offered the only truth left that was not about destruction.

I turned my palm upward again. Above my skin formed a dark sphere no wider than a flower bud, yet inside it tiny silver stars drifted and turned as if an entire night sky had been folded small enough to hold.

Elira's breath caught.

Haru: "The old fragments call Black Circle celestial for a reason."

I released the sphere into the center of the room. Perception bent softly around us. The shelves melted into silhouette. The vaulted ceiling receded beyond sight. Moonlight multiplied until the room felt submerged in clear winter night, leaving us standing beneath a silent, endless sweep of drifting constellations.

Elira turned slowly inside the starlight, her golden Yellow glow mingling with the silver-black dusk until she looked less like an academy prodigy and more like someone stepped straight out of old myth.

Elira: "It's beautiful."

Haru: "It's restrained."

A quiet, disbelieving laugh slipped out of her.

Elira: "If this is restraint, I don't want to imagine the alternative."

Haru: "You really don't."

I closed my hand and let the stars fade. Shelves solidified. The ceiling settled back into stone. Ordinary moonlight returned, though the word *ordinary* no longer fit the room.

Elira stood motionless, her eyes fixed entirely on me.

Elira: "You trusted me with this."

Haru: "Yes."

Elira: "Why?"

I had expected to need time for that question. I didn't.

Haru: "Because you looked at me and saw a person before you saw a secret."

Something in her expression softened open, pure tenderness replacing awe. She stepped right to the edge of the black ring, close enough that I could hear her steady breath and see moonlight reflected in her eyes.

Elira: "Haru..."

She spoke my name like it mattered. I dimmed the circle beneath my boots until the pressure eased into a quiet, turning law.

Haru: "I never meant to tell anyone."

Elira: "You told me."

Haru: "Yes."

Her voice dropped low.

Elira: "Then I'll carry it carefully."

I believed her. Power could be controlled; threats could be answered. Trust rearranged a person from the inside out.

Elira lifted one hand halfway, then checked herself.

Elira: "May I?"

Again she asked.

Haru: "What?"

Her gaze dipped to the turning black boundary.

Elira: "Touch the edge."

For a second I almost refused, not out of fear of what she would do, only fear of what letting her touch it meant. Still, she deserved more than distance.

Haru: "Just the visible ring."

She knelt once more and touched two fingertips to a black line turning across the stone.

Instantly her whole frame went still. A pale ripple of gold raced up her hand in quiet resonance. The Black Circle neither pushed her away nor claimed her; it simply registered her with vast, cool neutrality. Her fingers trembled once before steadying. Her Yellow crest flared like a small sun answering night, then eased back under her breathing.

Elira closed her eyes.

Elira: "It feels like standing at the edge of the sky."

Haru: "Yes."

She opened her eyes slowly, awe settling into something sharper and more personal.

Elira: "And you walk through hallways hiding this under a Green wrist crest, acting like you're fine."

Haru: "I never said I was fine."

Elira: "No. You didn't."

She stood again, close enough that the warmth of her gold mana brushed the cool stillness of mine.

Elira: "Then hear me clearly. I don't think you're a mistake. I don't think you're a monster. I don't wish I had looked away."

The last word caught. Her eyes dropped briefly to the fading black ring between our shoes.

Elira: "I am afraid."

Every defensive muscle in me braced. She saw it and frowned in sudden irritation.

Elira: "Don't decide what I mean before I finish."

Haru: "Sorry."

Elira: "You aren't."

Haru: "No."

A quick breath of laughter escaped her, stripping away the polish and making the moment real enough to survive.

Elira: "I'm afraid of what this power can do. I'm more afraid of what institutions will do if they learn it exists. And I'm afraid you've practiced disappearing so well that one day you'll call walking away protection."

I had braced for revulsion. I had zero defense against accuracy.

Haru: "I might."

Pain flickered cleanly across her face.

Elira: "I know."

The black ring dimmed another shade. Neither of us pretended danger had stepped back.

Haru: "Do you regret knowing?"

She took long enough to answer that I trusted the word when it came.

Elira: "No."

Relief hit hard enough to nearly unlock my knees. She caught it.

Elira: "No regret isn't the same as no fear."

Haru: "I understand."

Elira: "You understand the sentence. Understanding me will take longer."

I looked down at the pale floor.

Haru: "I don't know what you need from me now."

Elira: "Neither do I."

We stood with the first honest problem between us that neither could solve for the other. After a quiet stretch, she placed one hand over her heart.

Elira: "I can promise one thing tonight. I will keep this secret, not because a mana vow binds me, but because I choose to. If that choice ever changes, I tell you before I act."

That final condition scared me far more than a permanent chain would have. A permanent oath was a wall. This was a door she still owned.

Haru: "I want to ask you to promise it never changes."

Elira: "I know."

Haru: "I won't."

Her shoulders dropped by a fraction. I laid my palm over my chest.

Haru: "Then I promise I'll protect you from whatever follows me."

Her expression hardened on the spot.

Elira: "No."

The rejection hit so sharply the fading black ring shivered.

Haru: "No?"

Elira: "You may promise to warn me. You may promise to listen. You may not promise to decide every cost before I'm allowed to see it."

Haru: "Even if it threatens your life?"

Elira: "There. You're doing it already."

Heat flared in her voice. Not Yellow mana. Real temper.

Elira: "You just trusted me with the truth and immediately started building a future where you suffer nobly alone and I stay safely grateful."

Haru: "That isn't what I meant."

Elira: "It's what you reached for."

Denying it would have been a lie.

Haru: "I don't know how to care about you without trying to stand between you and harm."

Her anger softened, which somehow made the sting sharper.

Elira: "Then learn the difference between standing between and standing beside."

Haru: "Tonight?"

She glanced down at the fading black geometry beneath my boots, then out toward the moon.

Elira: "No. Not tonight."

The argument remained unsettled. So did she.

Haru: "I'm going to make you something."

Elira blinked.

Elira: "Something?"

Haru: "Protection."

Her eyes sharpened immediately.

Elira: "Haru-"

Haru: "Not because you're weak."

She stopped. I forced myself to finish plainly.

Haru: "Because this secret can become a route to you. I brought you near it. I need to make sure that doesn't become a wound someone else can open."

Her expression gentled, without making the mistake of calling the worry foolish.

Elira: "Then think carefully before you build it."

Haru: "I will."

Elira: "And don't make it as an apology."

That landed close enough that I nearly looked away.

Haru: "Then what should I make it as?"

Elira studied me for a quiet second.

Elira: "A question."

I did not fully understand yet, though the word lodged deep.

I released the isolation veil one thread at a time until ordinary night quiet returned to the shelves. Distant academy bells chimed the late hour. I stepped back, and Elira let me.

At the tall oak doors, I paused.

Haru: "Good night, Elira."

Elira: "Good night, Haru."

I walked out before staying felt too easy. Behind me, Elira remained alone inside the pale foundation circle where the stars had floated, looking down at bare stone. The truth had vanished from sight, yet not from the room, and not from her.

## Coda: A Route To Her

The pendant design refused to stay simple.

I sketched three drafts back in my room after leaving the archive, and all three failed for the same reason: they protected too cleanly. A sealed ward was easy to admire and easier to abuse. It would let me pretend I had solved danger by placing my power between Elira and the world. That was not protection. That was a prettier way of deciding for her.

The fourth sketch looked worse. Uneven. Conditional. It left deliberate gaps no combat instructor would ever praise. Yet every gap could become a question she was allowed to answer for herself.

When the Black Circle stirred behind my ribs, it did not object. That was new.

Outside my window, late students argued over bracket odds on the training path, someone laughing at a Green Circle boy for dropping his guard angle. Ordinary noise. Ordinary cruelty.

I pressed the stylus harder until fine silver dust coated my thumb.

If my secret had become a route to Elira, then the first law of the route had to be hers. Not mine.

Three wings away, in the dark corridor outside the Moonlit Archive Hall, the last dying tremor of Haru's lifted isolation veil brushed the carved mortar beneath the lintel.

A hairline listening glyph hidden deep inside the stone flared once in silent overload, then burned itself out cleanly from the inside.

Thin residue of dry ink and scrubbed academic mana curled across the cold flagstones and faded, leaving behind a single faint word pressed into the dust where no ordinary eye would look:

**SEEN.**

Across the silent courtyard, high in a lit research tower, a narrow silver ledger flipped open by itself on Professor Halden's desk. A dry nib scratched cleanly down the page: three distinct pulses of golden mana, followed by a blank, colorless line where the academy's oldest instruments had refused to record a rank at all.

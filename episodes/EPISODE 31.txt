# Episode 31: **The Fifth Tower Remembers**

The fifth tower remembered before any adult agreed it existed, which made it a problem before anyone could name one.

Adults liked memory better once it had a label. They wanted a thing to sit quietly inside a category before it moved, glowed, accused anyone, or wrote impossible sentences under fountain water in front of students still carrying breakfast trays.

The old water did not wait.

By the time I reached the courtyard, three copies of the line already existed and none of them matched.

Mira stood at the fountain rim with ink on her fingers, two pencils behind one ear, and the expression of someone asked to preserve weather. Aira crouched beside her with one knee on the stone, copying fast and swearing too quietly for the younger students to hear. Professor Halden leaned over the basin from the other side, hands braced on the rim, his face pale with the kind of scholarly delight that looked dangerous without supervision.

President Valmont supervised him by existing nearby.

The fountain water stayed clear, which made it worse.

If the old system had blackened the basin, opened a ring, or thrown silver glyphs into the air, the academy would have known how to panic. There were procedures now. Too many, probably. Red seals, Blue stabilizers, Green evacuation routes, Yellow containment exceptions, Council witness rails, healer priority lines, and Aira's growing collection of labels that made adults regret their wording.

Clear water gave them nothing to fight.

Only a sentence that had appeared for one breath after the cracked west bell rang late:

**THE FIFTH TOWER REMEMBERS.**

Mira had copied it first. Her page said:

**THE FIFTH TOWER REMAINS.**

She had crossed out the last word before the ink dried. Aira's first copy said:

**THE FIFTH WITNESS REMEMBERS.**

She had stared at it for ten full seconds, then written beside it:

**WRONG? MAYBE. DO NOT BURN.**

Halden's copy said:

**THE FIFTH TOWER REMEMBERS BELOW.**

He had not remembered writing the last word, so Rhea had taken his pencil.

The courtyard gathered around the basin in uneven rings. Bolder students hovered near the public board. Frightened ones pretended to be walking elsewhere and slowed every time the water moved. Green Circle aides stood closest to Mira, not because anyone had ordered them there, but because the ugly-copy table had become a place where ordinary hands knew what to do before ranks decided whether ordinary hands were allowed.

Iven Sahl sat on the low wall near the north planter with the younger transfer girl from two days ago at lunch. Not beside her.

In the direction of beside.

She had pushed one sleeve back enough for the pale green ring on her wrist to show in the morning light. Iven's own Green Circle rested visible against his borrowed sweater cuff. Neither of them looked at the fountain as if it belonged to them.

The fountain looked back anyway, with memory instead of eyes.

The cold pressure behind my sternum had returned. Old Aurelis magic had a taste when it noticed me: ozone, metal, winter stone, and a question I did not know how to answer without becoming the answer.

Beneath my winter glove and Maelin's finger wrap, the bruise under my ring fingernail darkened with every pulse from the basin. The eleventh heartbeat in the lower channel had originally ruptured the nail bed, and though Maelin had called it a small burst vessel with no clear ongoing drain, it had nearly faded after Valise.

I kept that hand in my pocket.

My Black Circle stayed hidden under my uniform. Being known in public did not mean being on display, though even that boundary lasted only days after the first exposure before every office in the world started testing how thin they could wear it.

A student near the outer ring whispered my name. Quietly, without cruelty, which made it harder. After the public manifestation, after Elira's death and return, after Cael's final act and vigil, after Senna Voss's ruling, people no longer asked whether the Black Circle existed.

They asked what could be done with it.

Elira's fingers brushed mine.

She had come down from the witness cottage wrapped in a pale cloak over her academy uniform, hair loose over one shoulder to spare her braced left shoulder and taped palm while Maelin restricted formal braids until her hands stopped shaking after sleep. The cloak made her look softer from a distance. Up close, nothing about her was soft except the shadow under her eyes.

Elira: "You are standing like a guilty monument."

Haru: "I was trying for harmless student."

Elira looked at the courtyard. At the students watching without watching. At Rhea holding Halden's stolen pencil like evidence. At Mira's pages refusing to match.

Elira: "Your range has narrowed."

I should have smiled. I almost did.

Then the fountain gave one small ripple. Every conversation stopped.

The water did not darken. No letters formed. No ring opened under the stones. The ripple caught the reflection of the west bell tower and bent it toward the center of the basin, as if the old stone wanted to be seen from below. Halden inhaled.

President Valmont: "No."

Halden closed his mouth. Aira, still crouched, glanced up.

Aira: "That was either the fastest disciplinary action in academy history or you two have developed a terrifying marriage of tone."

Rhea did not look away from the water.

President Valmont: "Professor Halden was about to say something with the word 'obvious' in it."

Professor Halden: "I was not."

President Valmont: "You were breathing academically."

Mira, without looking up, wrote:

**ACADEMIC BREATHING DETECTED. NOT EVIDENCE.**

The Green aides nearest her relaxed first, and the order mattered. The rest of the courtyard followed a half breath later. Fear shifted from throat to floor, settling into watchful standing.

Maelin Orr pushed through the outer ring with a healer's bag in one hand and a face that made students part before remembering she was not tall enough to be that intimidating.

Maelin: "If this fountain has decided to become a patient, it can take a number."

Halden looked offended.

Professor Halden: "This is old-system emergence, not a patient."

Maelin: "Does it have symptoms?"

Halden hesitated.

Maelin: "Then it can take a number."

She reached Elira and looked her over with the efficient cruelty of someone who could measure pulse from posture.

Maelin: "You are pale."

Elira: "I was killed recently."

Maelin: "I am aware. I signed three forms about it before tea."

Elira: "Then my coloring should not surprise you."

Maelin's eyes narrowed.

Maelin: "Sarcasm: stable. Bad judgment: returning."

Elira: "Encouraging."

Maelin pointed one finger toward the courtyard breakfast tables and the witness cottage beyond them.

Maelin: "Fifteen minutes outside. Then food. Then rest."

Elira's hand tightened once around mine. Not fear. Refusal preparing itself.

Elira: "No."

The word carried lightly, and still landed. Maelin did not blink.

Maelin: "I am not asking whether you enjoy food."

Elira: "I will eat. I will rest. I will not be put away because the old system wrote something near Haru."

Around us, several students looked down too quickly, caught in the sudden shame of people who had almost agreed with the thing before it was named.

My instinct rose before my voice did.

Haru: "Elira."

She turned to me. Gold eyes, tired and clear.

Elira: "Careful."

The word stopped me because it was not warning me away from danger. It was warning me away from becoming familiar with putting her behind doors.

I looked at our joined hands. Three students near the public board looked too.

Blue Second-Year: "Stability contact."

She whispered it to the girl beside her as if affection were a technical term she could carry safely. My fingers loosened.

Elira caught them before I could withdraw. Publicly. Deliberately. The courtyard noticed.

So did the Black Circle. It stirred beneath my skin toward claim, toward the old animal instinct that wanted to build a wall before anyone else decided what could enter.

Elira felt the shift through my palm and stepped closer instead of away.

Elira: "Do not protect me into silence because people are watching."

The sentence hurt because it was accurate. I had not said silence. I had thought safer. Sometimes the old world and love used different words for the same locked door.

Haru: "Every ordinary thing we do now becomes evidence."

Elira: "Then let them record that I chose this."

Haru: "They will call it dependency."

Elira: "They already did."

Haru: "They will call it influence."

Elira: "Then we answer influence without pretending distance is purity."

Her hand was warm, though still weaker than it should have been, still too easy for me to read as a medical fact instead of a human one.

Elira: "Haru, if I stand only where no one can twist my presence against me, I will spend the rest of my life sitting in rooms designed by people who want me quiet."

I had no clean answer. Clean answers were becoming suspicious.

Aira cleared her throat from the fountain rim.

Aira: "For the record, I hate interrupting romantic ethical combat, but the water just made Professor Halden's copy worse."

Halden looked down. The page in his hand now read:

**THE FIFTH TOWER REMEMBERS BELOW GREEN HANDS.**

No one had touched the ink.

For one second, the courtyard went very still.

Mira: "Do not copy that as authoritative."

Aira pointed at her.

Aira: "Excellent. First practical rule."

She stood, stretched her cramped leg, and lifted her notebook like a blade made of bureaucracy.

Aira: "Public handling protocol for old fountain nonsense. Draft one. One: no single copy becomes official until versions are compared. Two: do not improve ugly wording for dignity, clarity, school reputation, personal comfort, or Professor Halden's feelings."

Professor Halden: "My feelings have not been entered into evidence."

Aira: "Noted as aspirational."

Mira tapped her pencil once against the stone rim.

Mira: "Three: if old-system language changes while being copied, preserve the change beside the source, not over it."

President Valmont: "Four: no classification request names Haru Aerlen as the cause until the order of events proves cause."

Her eyes moved to me for the last part, steady and exact.

President Valmont: "And no protective order names him as solution before witnesses identify the problem."

The pressure beneath my sternum changed. Not gone. Listening.

That frightened me more than resistance would have. If the Fifth Tower had rejected us, I could understand danger. If it had opened only for me, I could understand isolation. Instead, the old water was responding to Mira's crossed-out line, to Aira's practical suspicion, to Green Circle hands moving paper before power could dress itself up for show.

It was not calling me. Not first.

Before the reveal, I had feared being exposed. Now I feared being useful.

Useful meant public safety could ask for one demonstration. Useful meant Council review could request one controlled test. Useful meant frightened students could sleep easier if I stood near the fountain every morning and answered every old door, until no one had to hate me to use me.

Professor Valen arrived carrying two sealed objects: the Regalia shard President Valmont had locked away from the lower chamber and Liora Aerlen-Valen's damaged vellum folio.

The shard's gold split rotated on its own, pointing high toward the blind masonry above the west tower's bell arch.

The old folio clouded with pale ink-light as Professor Valen angled it toward the basin's reflection. Liora appeared only from the shoulders up, older light breaking across her face.

Liora: "That window is the servant stair."

Professor Valen stopped breathing academically.

Professor Valen: "Whose servant?"

Liora: "No one's. That was the argument. My brother used it when Regalia guarded the high door and witnesses needed a route beneath the crown."

The echo looked toward the shard as if recognizing an old wound.

Liora: "If Regalia is listening to the tower again, do not give either one a single keeper."

The image failed before Professor Valen could ask her brother's name.

Elira's thumb moved once across my knuckle. She had understood before I did.

---

The first formal filing arrived during breakfast at the courtyard tables.

Senna appeared in the Council mirror with Krail's title floating beside her:

**MOTION FOR TEMPORARY BLACK CIRCLE PUBLIC STABILITY DESIGNATION**

The kind words were the dangerous ones. Civic stabilizer. Protective logistics. Voluntary monitoring. Reduced exposure to procedurally unstable Green copy networks. Elira listed as an affective variable; rescued Noctis students as contaminated testimony. My spoon trembled. Elira covered my hand.

Elira: "I am not a variable."

Mira: "We are not an unstable network."

Magistra Voss: "Both objections entered. The motion is stayed pending causal evidence."

President Valmont: "And my authority?"

Senna faced the public slate so no later summary could polish the answer.

Magistra Voss: "Headmaster Caelum Veris remains headmaster. He is barred for conflict of interest from Foundation, Noctis, and Black Circle matters while old seals from his office are audited. He retains ordinary academic administration."

The absence finally had a name.

Magistra Voss: "President Valmont's emergency mandate covers campus movement, student protection, and immediate hazard response. It does not grant foreign custody, permanent classification authority, or power to command faculty outside a declared hazard. Every use remains reviewable."

Rhea accepted the boundary without pretending to enjoy it.

President Valmont: "Record my next action under those limits: this filing does not govern breakfast, the copy table, or Haru's friendships."

Aira: "Historic defense of pastry."

The motion stayed sealed. Krail received written objections instead of the live spectacle he wanted, and the mirror dimmed.

Then Leon placed a blue-wax Varek envelope beside his tray.

He stared at it through the rest of breakfast as if the seal might speak if he refused to break it long enough. Cassian stayed beside him and said nothing, which was how I knew he was worried. Aira kept glancing between the envelope and the fountain across the stones, torn between social curiosity and survival instinct. Mira pretended not to notice with the intensity of someone tracking every angle.

Finally, Leon picked up the envelope.

Leon: "I should open it before my family finds a way to make unopened mail hereditary misconduct."

Cassian: "Can they do that?"

Leon: "Probably not."

He broke the seal.

Leon: "Legally."

The paper inside was thick, pale blue, and expensive enough to make cruelty look well-funded. Leon read silently. Annoyance gave way to embarrassment, then anger, then hurt disciplined flat before anyone could comfort it.

He folded the letter once, too neatly. Rhea saw it.

President Valmont: "You may keep it private."

Leon laughed without humor.

Leon: "That is what it wants."

He unfolded the letter and laid it flat on the table. The Varek crest caught the morning light. I did not read it at first, waiting until he offered. Leon looked at me, then at Elira, then at Cael's slate stone in his palm.

Leon: "My father says House Varek has received inquiries about my proximity to unstable witness politics."

Cassian's eyes went flat.

Leon: "He says my testimony during the autonomy proof was understandable under duress, but my continued association with Green Circle copy networks and former Noctis students risks implying family endorsement of anti-rank agitation."

Aira: "Anti-rank agitation?"

Leon: "Apparently that is what happens when people with lower Circles write things down where upper families can see them."

Mira's face had gone still. Leon did not look at her, out of mercy or shame or both.

Leon: "I have been invited to issue a clarifying statement."

Elira: "Invited."

Leon: "Family word."

President Valmont: "Translation?"

Leon: "Do it or lose things."

He smoothed the page open so I could read. The statement was already drafted. It expressed concern for affected students, praised Cael Nox's courage in the careful way people praise the dead when they want no obligations to the living, and declared that Leon Varek's prior testimony should not be read as opposition to rank-guided review or the temporary separation of emotionally entangled witnesses.

Then came the sentence that mattered:

**I withdraw any implied personal standing granted by my inclusion in Cael Nox's private notes and defer to recognized authorities for future interpretation of his record.**

The paper blurred at the edges of my vision from quiet anger. Cael had written Leon's name as a question: *Does Leon know beside is enough?* House Varek had found the question and tried to answer over him.

Leon set the slate stone on top of the draft, covering his family crest. No magic answered. Some refusals did not need magic to become real.

President Valmont: "What happens if you do not sign?"

Leon looked toward the west arcade, where several Blue Circle students had gathered close enough to hear and far enough to deny listening. I recognized two from his old circle, boys with polished cuffs and the easy posture of people who never wondered whether a room had been built with them in mind. One looked away when Leon met his eyes. The other did not.

Leon: "My discretionary allowance freezes. My family seat in the Blue Circle alumni mentorship program goes to my cousin. My summer training placement becomes unavailable due to political sensitivity. My mother will send a letter with softer words and the same knife."

Cassian: "That all?"

Leon almost smiled.

Leon: "I will also be unbearable at dinner for several years."

Aira: "That may predate this issue."

The joke landed badly, and still landed.

Leon breathed once through his nose, then stood.

President Valmont: "Leon."

He looked down at her.

President Valmont: "You do not need an audience."

Leon glanced at the watching Blue students, then at Mira's copy table, then at Iven sitting rigid near the north planter, and finally at the younger Green girl beside him, whose sleeve had crept back down over her wrist.

Leon picked up the letter.

Leon: "Yes, I do."

The old Leon would have turned refusal into theater because performance protected pride. This Leon looked as if every watching eye cost him something, and he was counting the price anyway.

He crossed the courtyard to the public board. Mira followed with a blank witness slate. Aira followed with three pencils. Cassian followed because he had apparently decided the air around Leon required fireproofing.

I stayed seated. Elira's hand stayed over mine, reminding without restraining. This was not mine to make safer.

Leon stopped before the board where Cael's line rested under protective glass:

**I WANTED TO LIVE. I CHOSE ANYWAY. BOTH ARE TRUE.**

Below it, copies from the past two days had multiplied across the yard:

**THIS IS NOT A TRADE.**

**THIS IS NOT A TRIAL.**

**TAKE WHAT HELPS. MORE CAN COME.**

One student had misread *TRIAL* as *TRAIL*, and Mira had preserved **THIS IS NOT A TRAIL.** under a note reading: **ACCIDENTAL MEANING. KEEP BESIDE SOURCE.**

Leon looked at all of them, then pinned his family's drafted statement directly to the board. Gasps rippled across the stones.

He took a blank page from Mira.

Mira: "Do you want a clean page or an ugly one?"

Leon considered.

Leon: "Ugly."

Mira handed him a sheet with a bent corner and a faint ink thumbprint near the top.

Mira: "Sorry."

Leon: "No. This is better."

He wrote slowly. His handwriting started precise by training, then the pressure shifted halfway through the first line as the letters stopped trying to impress the paper.

**HOUSE VAREK DID NOT RECEIVE STANDING FROM CAEL NOX. I DID.**

The courtyard held its breath.

**I DO NOT WITHDRAW IT.**

His hand paused, counting the next cost before paying it.

**GREEN CIRCLE WITNESSES AND RESCUED NOCTIS STUDENTS ARE NOT CONTAMINATION SOURCES. THEY ARE THE PEOPLE WHO KEPT THE RECORD FROM BECOMING PROPERTY.**

One of the Blue students near the arcade muttered something sharp. Leon's shoulders tightened as old pride rose behind the gate, then he looked back at Cael's line and the gate held.

**LEON VAREK, LISTED BY CAEL NOX, STANDING BESIDE.**

He signed without a flourish, then braced Cael's slate stone at the bottom edge until the pin held. No one cheered. I saw Leon's shoulders drop in relief that applause had not arrived to make the refusal easy to resent later.

The academy public slate chimed too quickly. House Varek had been waiting.

**VAREK FAMILY SPONSORSHIP PRIVILEGES UNDER REVIEW.**

**BLUE CIRCLE ALUMNI MENTORSHIP SEAT REALLOCATED.**

**PRIVATE CORRESPONDENCE REQUESTED.**

Leon's face did not change, which meant the damage had landed. Cassian stepped closer.

Leon: "Do not make a joke."

Cassian closed his mouth, then opened it.

Cassian: "I was going to offer arson."

Leon looked at him.

Cassian: "Different category."

Leon let out a single breath that was almost a laugh, not enough to erase the hurt.

Near the north planter, Iven stood. The younger girl caught his sleeve. He stopped, and she shook her head once. Iven sat again. Relief crossed Leon's face when the rescued Noctis boy did not rush over to thank him. The refusal stayed clean because the world had failed to pay him for it.

Elira stood beside me too quickly. Maelin made a sharp sound of warning, which Elira ignored as she walked to the public board. I walked with her because she wanted me there, not because she needed carrying.

Elira studied Leon's statement, then looked up at him.

Elira: "That was not easy."

Leon swallowed.

Leon: "No."

Elira: "Good."

His eyes flicked to her, and she offered the faintest smile.

Elira: "Easy would have been suspicious."

Leon looked down, then held out the unsigned Varek draft.

Leon: "Can ugly copies use this?"

Mira took the page before anyone else could reach it.

Mira: "Give it here."

Aira: "With annotation."

Rhea, coming up behind us, added quietly:

President Valmont: "Extensive annotation."

Leon nodded and left the draft in Mira's hands.

Across the courtyard, the fountain water moved again.

This time it did not write. It reflected the west bell tower, except the tower in the basin had more windows than the pale stone above us. Five dark niches stacked above the bell arch where daylight showed only blank wall.

Halden whispered first.

Professor Halden: "Those are not windows."

President Valmont: "Explain without touching anything."

Halden clasped his hands behind his back so violently I respected him more.

Professor Halden: "Observation niches. Old maintenance witnesses. Before the Circles were sorted into standard colors, city magic relied on shared local memory built right into everyday structures: bells, lamps, water lines, kitchens, infirmary doorways, repair logs."

Aira: "You are saying the tower remembers maintenance."

Halden looked pained at having his lecture boiled down so bluntly. Professor Valen steadied one palm against the stone rim, eyes fixed on the reflected tiers.

Professor Valen: "She has the core of it. The first four towers honored recognized academic authority: theory, combat, restoration, and governance. The fifth was never drawn on public diagrams because it carried no prestige."

Mira's voice dropped very low.

Mira: "Foundation."

Professor Valen: "Labor. Continuity. Witness by maintenance. The part that knew whether the doors opened, whether the lamps stayed lit, whether the sick were fed, and whether a copied notice reached the lower dormitory before the upper hall rewrote it. If Circle Zero preserved anything there, it will not answer rank first."

The water brightened under the reflected niches. Not black. Not silver. Green. Soft, low, almost embarrassed to be seen.

My hidden circle did not rise. It listened.

And around the courtyard, students turned toward me anyway. Because the Black Circle was public. Because the Council filing had called me stabilizer. Because people looked for the largest power nearby before they looked at the Green hands already holding the pages.

Power did not need to demand a throne if frightened people kept building chairs around it.

I took one step back from the fountain. The courtyard noticed. Elira noticed most.

Haru: "If I go closer, they will call it response."

Elira: "And if you stand in front, they will forget anyone else has hands."

She kept her fingers hooked lightly against the cuff of my sleeve instead of the basin rim.

Haru: "If I stay back, they will call it instability."

Elira: "Probably."

Haru: "If you stand with me, they will call it influence."

Elira: "They have poor vocabulary."

She looked exhausted enough that every joke cost something, and spent one anyway.

Elira: "Haru, you do not have to vanish to avoid becoming a ruler."

The fountain rippled. The green niches in the reflection held steady. Aira had stopped writing.

Elira: "Stand where you are. Let someone else reach first."

My hands were cold. I had crossed battlefields and broken old wards, and standing still while someone else reached first was harder.

Mira Castellan stepped up to the stone rim with five ugly copies in her hands. Iven stood too. The younger girl followed three steps behind him, one sleeve pushed halfway up her forearm. Finn Castellan carried Leon's pinned statement.

Leon looked as if he wanted to stop them, then closed his mouth. Once a refusal left your hand, its consequences belonged to the courtyard.

Mira placed the first copy on the damp stone rim.

Mira: "Public record note. The Fifth Tower phrase cannot be copied perfectly. We are not correcting it into one version. We are preserving the differences beside each other."

The water stayed clear. She placed the second page.

Mira: "Green Circle witness work is not contamination."

The water gave one small green pulse. Finn nearly dropped Leon's statement.

Aira: "Do not drop the politically expensive paper."

Finn steadied it and set it beside Mira's hand. She placed the third page.

Mira: "Haru Aerlen is not named as cause, stabilizer, ruler, tower, or one-man institution in this record."

The pressure behind my sternum eased so suddenly I almost stepped forward. Elira's fingers caught my sleeve. I stayed.

Mira laid Iven's pages side by side.

Mira: "Care given here is not a trade. Witness requested here is not a trial."

The younger Green Circle girl, who had not yet put her name on any public record, took a sharp breath and stepped directly beside Iven. Her wrist trembled. Her pale green ring brightened.

Transfer Girl: "And... you can take longer here."

Her voice barely crossed the basin, and crossed anyway. Iven looked at her as a fellow witness, and Mira wrote the sentence down before fear could take it back.

The fountain water turned green from the bottom up, like a lamp lit in a service stair after everyone had forgotten stairs needed light too.

Across the courtyard, the cracked west bell sounded on its own, late as always.

Inside the tower, something answered. A low click. Stone remembering a latch.

Above the courtyard wall, the real west tower stood narrow and pale against the morning sky, showing only its four dark windows below the bell arch. Nothing moved in the stone for three breaths.

Then the academy public slate flashed:

**FIFTH TOWER ACCESS REQUEST: DENIED TO CENTRAL AUTHORITY.**

Halden made a sound like a man watching a rare book lock itself.

**FOUNDATION WITNESS MEMORY: PARTIAL WAKE.**

Rhea lifted her chin.

President Valmont: "Conditions?"

The slate hesitated, old and deliberate, before answering line by line.

**NO SINGLE HAND.**

Aira copied so fast her pencil squeaked.

**NO CROWNED CAUSE.**

Elira's eyes moved to me.

**NO DEBT FOR CARE.**

Iven's face softened by a fraction.

**NO SILENCE BY PROTECTION.**

Elira's hand found mine again, in full view of the courtyard. This time I did not pull away.

One final line burned onto the slate:

**GREEN HANDS MAY ENTER MEMORY FIRST. BLACK CIRCLE MAY WITNESS FROM THRESHOLD.**

Every eye snapped back to me, and yet the sentence had named a limit before a power. Threshold. Not entry, leadership, or solution.

Mira looked at Rhea instead of me.

Mira: "Do we have to enter now?"

President Valmont: "No. A door that cannot wait is a demand. We do not enter demands."

The slate held the silence, then added:

**ACCEPTED.**

Halden closed his eyes.

Professor Halden: "It accepted refusal."

Maelin: "Healthy response."

Halden opened his eyes with helpless offense.

Maelin: "I told you. Patient."

For one breath, impossible old magic and an ordinary academy morning stood in the same courtyard without trying to swallow each other.

Then the Council mirror chimed again.

Senna's face returned, and behind her the chamber seats had begun to fill with Councilors, safety officers, and delegation representatives who had never stood in the courtyard and still felt qualified to name it.

Magistra Voss: "The stabilizer motion has been amended."

President Valmont: "Already?"

Magistra Voss: "The Fifth Tower response entered public relay."

Aira: "How?"

Senna's gaze shifted toward the green glow in the basin.

Magistra Voss: "The tower appears to have objected to being summarized."

Mira's mouth parted in the closest thing to delight I had ever seen on her.

Magistra Voss: "Krail has withdrawn the word stabilizer."

That should have helped. It did not. Senna kept her hands clasped on the chamber table, voice level.

Magistra Voss: "He now asks for an emergency review of Foundation witness access, arguing that if Green Circle networks can wake old memory structures, they may need upper-rank supervision to protect them from outside pull—what he calls 'extra-classification gravity.'"

The courtyard went cold. There was the machinery turning without a pause: if Haru was the danger, isolate Haru; if Green witnesses answered, supervise Green witnesses; if refusal worked, review refusal.

Leon let out a short, harsh laugh.

Leon: "They will protect everyone into a smaller room if we let them."

Rhea looked at Leon, then at Mira, then at the younger Green girl whose wrist stayed uncovered.

President Valmont: "Then we do not let them."

Magistra Voss: "I can delay the formal hearing until evening. Not longer."

Elira: "Good."

Maelin: "That was not addressed to you."

Elira: "I heard it anyway."

Maelin: "Your hearing participation is medically discouraged."

Elira: "I am medically discouraging being categorized while absent."

Maelin looked up at the sky as if requesting a colleague with less stubborn patients. Elira turned to me, and this time the friction between us did not hide inside worry.

Elira: "I am speaking tonight."

Everything in me wanted to refuse. She was pale, alive, and one bad turn away from people using concern as custody again.

Haru: "You stood up too fast at breakfast, and Maelin barely cleared you to cross the courtyard."

Elira: "And your fingernail is bleeding again."

My hidden hand became the center of the courtyard without leaving my pocket. Maelin's head snapped toward me. I had pulled off my glove for half a minute at breakfast to steady Elira's teacup—and a fresh seam of blood must have already darkened the upper edge of Maelin's wrap before I slipped my hand back under wool. I had not realized Elira saw.

Elira: "Take off the glove."

Haru: "Not here."

Elira's anger stayed quiet, which made it sharper.

Elira: "You want me to speak publicly about knowing my own condition while you edit yours out of the courtyard."

Haru: "That is not what I am doing."

Elira: "Then what are you doing?"

Haru: "If every Black Circle reaction becomes a medical event, I stop being a student. I become an instrument everyone checks whenever old magic moves."

Elira: "So you decided no one could be trusted with the reading."

Haru: "I decided to wait."

Elira: "Until when? After the hearing? After the tower opens? After you decide the pain is large enough to deserve witnesses?"

Maelin crossed the remaining distance and held out her hand.

Maelin: "Glove."

Haru: "Maelin, wait."

Maelin: "You may preserve mystery or finger. Choose quickly."

I pulled off my winter glove and loosened the edge of Maelin's wrap. The old bruise had spread from the nail bed in a dark crescent, with one fresh crack of blood seeping along the rim of the nail onto the upper seam of the blue cloth. Elira looked at it, then looked away, jaw tight.

Haru: "I did not want this used to stop you from speaking."

Elira: "You do not protect my choice by controlling the facts around it."

Haru: "I know."

Elira: "You knew at the fountain. You promised Maelin you would report if it returned."

Shame burned hot where the Black Circle sat cold.

Haru: "I broke that promise."

Elira: "Do not make me drag truth out of your hand and call that partnership."

I wanted to defend the motive, the terror of becoming Krail's living gauge, of handing him proof that power owned me more than I owned it. None of that repaired what I had hidden.

Haru: "I am afraid pain is the only thing they will believe about me."

Her anger did not vanish, though it made room.

Elira: "I am afraid absence is the only thing they will believe about me."

Two different pressures that could injure each other if we pretended love made them the same.

Maelin wrapped my finger in a strip of blue diagnostic cloth. The fabric darkened near the nail and sent one reading to her slate.

Maelin: "Old reaction is back. No active channel loss. No heavy Black Circle strain until I understand why the old magic here is pulling on the injury."

Haru: "The hearing tonight."

Maelin: "You may attend as witness. You may not demonstrate."

Elira: "And me?"

Maelin looked equally displeased with both of us.

Maelin: "Ten minutes speaking time. Seated. One break available without explanation. Exceed either limit and I remove you personally."

Elira: "Accepted."

I waited until Maelin stepped back to fetch another slate.

Haru: "I am sorry."

Elira: "I know."

She did not take my hand yet.

Elira: "Tell me before I discover it next time. Even if all you can say is that you are scared of what the fact will become."

Haru: "I will tell you."

She touched my wrist above Maelin's cloth, clear of the injury. Neither the fear nor the bruise went away, and for once that felt more honest than comfort.

Aira cleared her throat.

Aira: "I hate interrupting what is either a relationship argument or civic theory, but an evening hearing means we need copies, witness order, version tables, medical limits, tower conditions, Leon's public family disaster, and snacks."

Cassian: "Why snacks?"

Aira: "Because if committees are going to sort us into categories, I refuse to do it hungry."

Maelin pointed at her.

Maelin: "Correct."

Rhea began dividing the work. Mira would lead version comparison. Aira would draft the answer matrix. Halden and Valen would document what the Fifth Tower was not before anyone tried to name what it was. Maelin would enforce medical limits and threaten anyone who confused treatment with disappearance.

Rhea turned to Leon.

President Valmont: "Your family statement can enter tonight's record, or wait until it will not drown the Green witness issue."

Leon: "Enter it."

Rhea studied him.

Leon: "They made it part of the same pressure. Let it sit there."

Rhea nodded without praising him, then looked at me last.

President Valmont: "Haru, you will not interpret the Fifth Tower."

Something in me eased, and something else tightened.

President Valmont: "You will not test a reaction, offer access, steady the fountain, correct the copies, vouch for the green light, answer what-if questions, or stand where anyone can claim the system runs through you."

Aira: "I have never heard someone assign a person to aggressively not do things."

President Valmont: "Then take notes."

Aira uncapped her ink. Rhea kept her gaze on me, steady beside the copy table.

President Valmont: "Your role is threshold witness. If the Black Circle reacts, you report the reaction as one witness among others. Not first. Not final."

One witness among others sounded survivable.

Haru: "Understood."

President Valmont: "Do you object?"

I looked at the green light under the fountain water, at Mira sorting mismatched pages, at Leon standing straight after losing half his world's favor, and at Elira refusing to go quiet for anyone's comfort.

Haru: "No. I am relieved."

Aira's pencil paused for one beat, then caught the line. The fountain did not brighten, the tower did not chime, and no one turned relief into law.

---

The afternoon became work. Work gave fear a table, labels, and tasks too small to turn into prophecy.

Mira built the version board under the arcade. She pinned each failed copy of the Fifth Tower line in sequence and drew thin connecting marks between changed words: *tower* to *witness*, *remembers* to *remains*, *below* to *green hands*, *no single hand* to *no crowned cause*.

She refused three offers to rewrite the board neatly. The third came from a Council aide who looked personally injured by uneven margins.

Mira: "The uneven margins are evidence."

Council Aide: "They are difficult to read."

Mira: "Good."

She tapped the corner of the newly numbered page flat with two ink-stained fingers.

Council Aide: "That is not a virtue."

Mira: "It is if the alternative makes it easier to lie."

The aide looked at Rhea for backup. Rhea offered none.

Aira constructed what she called the "No, But Specifically" matrix. Down one side she listed hostile labels: *stabilizer, variable, court, contamination source, rank agitation, dependency, unsupervised Green network, extra-classification gravity*. Across the top she listed answer columns: *sequence, personhood, consent, shared witness, medical condition, public wording, real-world result*, and a final column labeled *sentence that would make Krail regret literacy*.

Halden and Valen argued beside the fountain over whether the Fifth Tower was old city memory from before the Circle ranks, forgotten stone that survived an ancient purge, or an old upkeep post folded into Circle Zero lore. Maelin asked whether the difference changed who might faint. Halden said no. Maelin told them to put it in the appendix.

I sat on the witness cottage step with Elira while she rested under protest. She had stayed on the indoor cot for twenty minutes, then insisted on moving to the step so she would not have to pretend the world had no windows. Maelin allowed it only after adding two blankets, a pulse charm, and a warning that read like a small penal code.

Students crossed the courtyard in careful arcs around the working tables. One first-year Green Circle boy stopped halfway past us, looked at my face, then looked toward the version board.

Green First-Year: "Are we allowed to copy the tower line?"

I felt the old pull to reassure him, easy and harmless. Elira's shoulder touched mine, witness without warning. I turned slightly toward Mira.

Haru: "Ask the copy table."

The boy blinked, then hurried over to Mira. She handed him a blank sheet.

Mira: "Copy one version badly and sign your hand."

Green First-Year: "Which version?"

Mira: "The one that does not leave you alone after you read it."

He studied the board with grave seriousness, then chose **NO DEBT FOR CARE.** His copy made *debt* look like *diet*. Aira declared the error dangerous before lunch and possibly useful after. The boy smiled all the way back across the courtyard.

Haru: "I wanted to answer him."

Elira: "I know."

Haru: "It would have been kind."

Elira: "And it would have centered you."

She leaned her head back against the cottage doorframe, eyes half closed.

Haru: "Wrong?"

Elira: "Not always wrong. Today wrong."

Holding back could become its own kind of throne if I turned it into a rigid rule. Standing at the threshold meant choosing when not to cross.

Leon came up the cottage path holding a fresh notice. The Varek crest was gone from the top, which somehow made it worse.

Leon: "My mother wrote."

Elira opened her eyes.

Elira: "Softer words?"

Leon sat on the lower step without asking for comfort or refusing it.

Leon: "Very soft."

He held out the page. I kept my hands still.

Haru: "Do you want me to read it?"

He looked surprised, then faintly embarrassed by the surprise.

Leon: "Read it."

The letter matched his prediction. Gentle, concerned, proud of his compassion and worried for his judgment. It said House Varek had always valued service to those of lesser circumstance, that service lost meaning when it turned political, that his father was hurt, and that the mentorship seat could still be restored if Leon clarified that his heart had outrun his discipline. It did not mention Cael by name.

I handed it back. Leon folded it once, crookedly. Progress.

Leon: "There was a time that letter would have worked."

Elira: "And now?"

Leon looked toward the public board, where students were quietly reading both the Varek draft and his reply.

Leon: "Now it still hurts."

He sounded annoyed by the fact.

Elira: "Of course it does."

Leon rubbed one thumb along the folded paper's edge.

Leon: "I thought choosing correctly would make it feel less like losing."

Haru: "It rarely does at first."

Leon glanced at me.

Leon: "You sound like you know."

I thought of years spent hiding a Circle no one understood, choosing restraint while people called restraint weakness until they learned to call it evidence.

Haru: "Sometimes."

Leon nodded and asked no more. After a moment he spoke quietly.

Leon: "Cael would have hated that my family made this about me."

Elira's expression softened with grief.

Leon: "I hate that too. But if I pretend it did not cost anything, I make it noble enough for people to admire instead of useful enough to repeat."

Haru: "What will you do with your mother's letter?"

Leon stood.

Leon: "Keep it."

Elira: "Not copy it?"

Leon: "Not yet. Some things should hurt privately before they become public enough to survive."

He walked back down the path with the letter in his hand and Cael's slate stone in his pocket.

Elira watched him cross the stones.

Elira: "Cost without performance."

Haru: "He is ahead of me in one subject."

Elira's mouth curved.

Elira: "Several, if you count making friends with Cassian while complaining."

Haru: "That is a specialized field."

Her smile vanished when the pulse charm at her wrist gave a warning flicker. I reached out, stopped myself, and waited. She saw the hesitation.

Elira: "You may help me stand."

Haru: "Thank you."

Elira: "Do not sound so formal. I am still irritated with you."

I helped her up slowly in full view of the courtyard. Her fingers were cool. She leaned on me for three steps, then stood on her own. Both parts mattered.

---

Senna refused Krail a closed chamber that evening.

Instead, the Council mirrors opened beside Mira's version board as dusk settled over the courtyard. Krail's amended motion hung in the relay for twelve minutes, proposing to suspend unsupervised Green copying until rank-balanced supervision could protect vulnerable students.

Mira stepped up to the mirror with ink on both hands.

Magistra Voss: "Do you request standing?"

Mira: "I already have hands."

She gestured to the pinned pages behind her.

Mira: "Green students did not wake the tower alone. Aira copied. Halden copied. Leon signed. Elira objected. Haru stayed outside the center. Removing Green hands changes the process you claim to protect."

High Councilor Krail: "I proposed supervision, not removal."

Mira: "At Aurelis, supervision means someone stands near while you work. At Noctis, it meant someone stood over you until your hand disappeared. Which one did you write?"

Leon stepped beside her with his unsigned Varek draft.

Leon: "My family used your argument first. They called these students agitation and the rescued students contamination."

High Councilor Krail: "You are emotional."

Leon: "Angry, and correct."

He held Cael's slate stone flat over the Varek crest.

Krail shifted target at once.

High Councilor Krail: "And the affective variable remains unexamined. Miss Vale's own recovery was tied to Black Circle magic, yet she now sits beside the record table as if fragile health has no bearing on a witness's judgment."

Maelin's hand twitched toward the mirror frame, ready to end the call, before Elira lifted two fingers from the blanket over her knees. Seated in the wooden chair Maelin had placed beside the board, she leaned forward into the mirror's light.

Elira: "I was pronounced dead, restored, and examined by healers who filed every reading in public view. Calling my survival an affective variable is a way to turn a witness into furniture."

High Councilor Krail: "Your pulse charm is visible from here."

Elira: "Which proves I am monitored, not incapable. I am using six minutes of my ten-minute medical allowance to enter one correction: no one here needs rank-supervised custody to know what happened to them."

From my post at the edge of the fountain, every muscle in my back pulled toward stepping between her and the mirrors. I kept both feet planted on the stone. Threshold meant holding still while she took her own ground.

Senna denied an immediate halt for lack of proof. Green copies stayed provisional, public, and shared; Rhea's campus authority stayed limited to keeping the copy table physically safe.

The mirrors dimmed before anyone could turn refusal into ceremony.

The Fifth Tower had not moved during Krail's argument. It moved when Mira picked up her pencil and went back to work.

---

After the mirrors dimmed, the courtyard did not empty.

Students lingered in small groups under the evening lamps, unwilling to leave the stones where the day had almost hardened into a category and failed. The sky above Aurelis darkened to deep blue. The fountain water turned clear again, though a faint green glow lingered at the bottom if you looked from the right angle without demanding too much of it.

Elira sat on the low wall near the witness cottage with Maelin's blanket wrapped around her shoulders. Maelin had won that argument by refusing to have it.

Leon stood at the public board, reading his own statement as if a stranger had written it and he was deciding whether the stranger was worth knowing. Cassian stood nearby, offering silence with unusual competence.

Mira slept face-down on folded arms at the copy table for seven minutes before Aira draped a cloak over her shoulders and propped a note beside her elbow warning everyone not to wake her unless the building caught fire or developed opinions.

Iven and the younger girl copied **NO DEBT FOR CARE** three more times. On the third sheet, she signed one letter at the bottom: **K.**

I stood at the fountain rim and thought of thresholds. Beneath my uniform, the Black Circle rested with the uneasy patience of something powerful learning manners from people it could accidentally outlive.

Elira joined me after a while, her steps quiet against the stone.

Haru: "You should be sitting."

Elira: "You should stop beginning sentences that way."

She stopped beside me. The dark water reflected us both badly, turning my hair into night smear and catching too much lamplight in her gold eyes. Between us, the basin held the west tower upside down.

Elira: "Are you angry with me?"

Haru: "No."

Elira waited, fluent in the pause before my second answer.

Haru: "I was afraid enough that anger would have been easier."

Her face softened, though not too much.

Elira: "I know."

Haru: "When you faced those mirrors at the hearing, I wanted to step between you and every one of them."

Elira: "I know."

Haru: "I stayed put."

Elira: "I know that too."

Her hand rose to touch the dark crystal pendant at her throat.

Elira: "I do not want love that hides me from every room where I might be hurt."

Haru: "And I do not want love that asks you to bleed so no one can call you protected."

Elira watched the lamp reflection move across the water.

Elira: "Then we will keep arguing."

Despite everything, I smiled.

Haru: "That is your solution?"

Elira: "It has kept us alive more than once."

Haru: "Technically inaccurate."

Elira: "Do not become Halden because you are emotionally cornered."

Above us, the cracked west bell gave one faint metallic shift as night wind moved through the arch. Just old bronze remembering it had weight.

Haru: "The Fifth Tower did not ask for me first."

Elira: "It asked for green hands."

Haru: "I was relieved."

Elira: "Good."

Haru: "Then afraid because I was relieved."

Elira: "Also fair."

I looked up at the tower. The four visible windows below the bell arch sat dark and ordinary.

Haru: "What if the world only learns not to use me by using everyone else instead?"

Elira did not answer quickly, which was one of the reasons I trusted her.

Elira: "Then everyone else will need standing, witnesses, rest, refusal, bad copies, snacks, and people who do not let their care become ownership."

Haru: "That sounds difficult."

Elira: "Worth it, though."

Her voice stayed low, carrying easily across the quiet courtyard as she leaned her shoulder lightly against mine. By choice. In public.

Elira: "And not lonely."

Across the courtyard, Leon unpinned the Varek draft, copied one phrase from it onto a scrap of rough paper, and pinned it beside his own statement:

**HEART OUTRAN DISCIPLINE.**

Underneath, he wrote one word:

**YES.**

Mira woke just long enough to see it, smiled into her sleeve, and went back to sleep. Aira caught me watching and raised her pencil in silent warning. I did not ask.

The west bell shifted again.

This time, the sound brought a dry click from inside the masonry. Stone remembering a latch.

Students turned toward the tower one by one. Nobody screamed. Nobody sprinted to fetch senior authority. Nobody turned to me and asked me to fix it.

The silence told me the day had changed something. Not enough to make anyone safe. Enough to threaten the people who preferred fewer hands.

The west tower stood pale against the night sky, four visible windows below the bell arch dark and blind. By morning, officials would argue over whether updating the public diagrams required committee approval. Aira would make someone regret a comma. Mira would preserve the first crooked sketch. Halden would pretend not to be thrilled. Rhea would refuse central authority again if she had to. Elira would speak whenever silence tried to dress up as care.

And I would stand at the threshold.

The cracked west bell rang once, late and imperfect.

High above the cracked west bell, in the blank stone where no diagram admitted a room existed, one unlit window turned green.

# Episode 19: **The First Hearing**

The morning after the vow did not feel like morning.

Light entered the Lake Velis bedroom in long pale bands. The lake outside was quiet enough to lie. White curtains moved in the window breeze. Somewhere in the kitchen, the old house kettle clicked once, then decided not to interrupt.

Elira slept with one hand tangled in my shirt. Not the stolen one. Mine.

The distinction mattered less than it should have.

For a while, I stayed still and let myself count nothing. Not breaths. Not pulse. Not window wards. Not the precise temperature of the pendant at her throat. The new crystal rested there, dark and calm, warm from her skin. Seven open consent lines now nested inside it instead of one closed protection route.

If Dominion tried to call her rescue an entry again, it would have to pass through questions first. Who is choosing? What is being saved?

Who is being named as cost?

Is Haru acting as protector, owner, or witness?

The last question had been Professor Valen's addition. I hated it.

I had agreed to it. That, apparently, was recovery.

Elira's fingers tightened before her eyes opened.

Haru: "I'm here."

Elira: "I know."

Her voice was rough with sleep.

Then she looked at me, really looked, and the memory of the night before passed between us without needing words. Her cheeks warmed. Mine probably did too, though I refused to provide evidence.

Elira: "You are thinking very loudly."

Haru: "I am thinking carefully."

Elira: "That is the same thing when you do it."

I looked toward the window.

She laughed softly and touched my jaw with the backs of her fingers.

Elira: "No regret."

The words struck deeper than I expected.

Haru: "No regret?"

Elira: "None."

The Black Circle did not stir. It listened.

Elira saw the relief I failed to hide. Her expression softened, then sharpened with the old Elira Vale precision that had survived death, grief, fear, and my worst instinct to become a wall.

Elira: "Do not make my certainty into something you must earn every morning." I closed my eyes.

Haru: "I will try."

Elira: "No. You will practice."

That was worse. Also better.

The mirror on the far wall chimed. Once. Neither of us moved.

It chimed again. Elira sighed.

Elira: "If that is Aira, she gets one joke before I end the call." The mirror brightened.

Magistra Senna Voss appeared instead, framed by the clean black-glass lines of an Inter-National Schools Council hearing chamber. Her silver-gray robe was fastened at the throat with a narrow white seal. Behind her, three floating transcript crystals turned slowly above an empty witness rail.

Senna: "Good morning. I apologize for the early contact."

Elira pulled the blanket higher with one hand.

I rose enough to sit between her and the mirror without blocking her. She noticed. Her eyebrow moved. I moved sideways.

Senna noticed too, because Senna noticed everything and wrote it down in polite grammar.

Senna: "The second distributed witness review has been advanced." The room lost warmth. Elira sat upright.

Elira: "Advanced by whom?"

Senna: "Councilor Krail's minority bloc filed an emergency petition during the night. The petition argues that Haru Aerlen's private proximity to you after the Black Circle manifestation constitutes unsupervised extra-classification influence over a critical witness."

I felt the old cold pressure behind my sternum. Ozone. Metal.

The house wards did not flare.

The crystal did not chill. Only me.

Haru: "They are turning recovery into contamination."

Senna: "Yes." She marked the margin with an ink flourish.

I appreciated that she did not soften it.

Elira's hand found mine under the blanket. Her grip was steady.

Elira: "And our answer?"

Senna: "Aurelis has already rejected the premise. Professor Valen, Senior Healer Orr, President Valmont, and the registered witnesses submitted a joint medical-protective objection. However, Krail has secured enough signatures to require a public preliminary session."

Elira: "When?"

Senna: "At noon."

The old house kettle clicked again in the kitchen.

This time the sound felt absurd enough that Elira almost laughed. Almost. Senna looked at me.

Senna: "Mr. Aerlen, the petition requests three immediate restrictions pending full review. First, your physical separation from Elira Vale outside supervised medical conditions. Second, a prohibition against unscheduled Black Circle manifestation. Third, compulsory demonstration of the Black Circle's limits before a Council containment panel."

The room went very quiet.

Haru: "No."

The word left me before I could make it polite.

Senna: "That is also my legal reading."

Elira's thumb moved once across my knuckles.

Elira: "He cannot demonstrate a thing designed around restraint without being pressured into exactly the failure they claim to fear."

Senna: "That is the argument I intend to preserve."

Haru: "Preserve?"

Senna's eyes narrowed slightly. Not in anger. In warning.

Senna: "Council proceedings have begun receiving external doctrine packets from institutions not named as parties. Noctis Dominion College is formally under investigation and cannot participate directly. Someone is trying to continue the argument through neutral schools, private donors, and rank-security associations." Lucien.

I did not say his name. The mirror did.

For one heartbeat, Senna's reflection fractured. Black frost drew a smile across the glass where no mouth should have been. Then the fracture vanished. Senna turned her head. Someone off-screen shouted.

The transcript crystals behind her dimmed from white to gray.

Senna: "This line is being tested. I am ending contact. Do not travel alone. Do not answer any summons not sealed by Aurelis and my office together."

Elira: "Magistra-" The mirror went dark.

For three breaths, the lake house held its silence.

Then Aira's voice came from the hallway.

Aira: "I came with breakfast and political dread."

The bedroom door opened exactly two inches.

Aira did not look inside.

Aira: "Are you both dressed enough for bread?"

Elira pressed her face into her hands.

Elira: "No."

Aira: "Terrible answer. I will leave bread outside the door like a civilized friend."

Leon: "You said you would not mention the door."

Aira: "I did not mention anything specific about the door."

Cassian: "This is the loudest discretion I have ever witnessed." Rhea's voice cut in.

Rhea: "Everyone downstairs." That did it.

Elira threw a pillow at the door.

It struck the wood with enough force to satisfy nobody.

By the time we reached the sitting room, the restoration house had stopped pretending to be peaceful.

Rhea had turned the largest table into a field office. Council notices hovered over it in separate stacks. Aira had already arranged breakfast by tactical importance: tea near Elira, bread near me, documents near herself, and one plate of honey buns in the middle as either morale or bribery.

Leon stood at the window with crossed arms, watching the road through a water-thin reflection. Cassian sat backward on a chair, outwardly relaxed, one hand near a red sigil he had carved into the air and not released. Professor Valen read Senna's last transmission with a scholar's calm and a soldier's jaw. Maelin counted Elira's pulse, then mine, then looked offended by both.

Maelin: "No fever. Elevated stress. Predictable foolishness approaching."

Haru: "The Council?"

Maelin: "You."

Elira smiled into her tea. I tried not to.

The smile lasted long enough for the room to remember it was allowed to contain something besides danger.

Then I noticed the bread in Aira's hand.

She had crushed one end of the loaf without realizing it. Flour marked the front of her uniform. The hem of her cloak was wet from the lake road, and one fastening sat in the wrong loop. Aira Solen did not misfasten clothes. She considered uneven buttons a personal accusation.

Haru: "You ran here."

Aira looked down at herself as if the evidence had betrayed her.

Aira: "I traveled with urgency and very poor terrain cooperation."

Leon did not turn from the window.

Leon: "She ran from the academy gate."

Aira: "You were not invited to improve his evidence."

Leon: "You passed me."

Aira set the wounded loaf on the table. Her fingers stayed on it.

Aira: "Senna's relay failed twice before it found Rhea. The first time, the return seal went black. The second time, it showed this house with no lights."

Her voice remained brisk. Her thumb pressed deeper into the bread.

Aira: "I knew there was probably a normal explanation. Dawn. Bad angle. No one awake because some people had finally made a medically reasonable decision."

Cassian opened his mouth. Whatever joke had arrived there saw Elira's face and changed direction.

Cassian: "I have never made a medically reasonable decision at dawn. It sounds exhausting."

Aira ignored him with less force than usual.

Aira: "I still ran. Then I bought breakfast because arriving empty-handed felt like admitting I expected a body." The room went still.

Not Council stillness. No one was waiting to turn her fear into a statement. Leon left the window and pulled out the chair beside her without looking at her. Cassian slid the honey buns closer. Rhea lowered one of the hovering documents until its blue light stopped cutting across Aira's face.

Elira reached for the crushed loaf.

Aira: "That part is structurally compromised."

Elira tore off the flattened end.

Elira: "So am I. It remains bread."

She put half on Aira's plate. Aira stared at it.

Elira: "Eat."

Aira: "I brought this breakfast."

Elira: "Then take responsibility for it."

Leon sat. That seemed to give Cassian permission to stop performing relaxation. He turned his chair around and used it correctly, though with visible resentment. Valen folded Senna's transcript. Rhea remained standing for three more seconds before Elira pointed at the empty chair beside the field-office stack.

Elira: "You too."

Rhea: "There is an active containment petition."

Elira: "It will remain active while you chew." Rhea sat.

Maelin looked pleased in the dangerous way healers looked when a patient enforced instructions on other people.

Maelin: "Miss Vale, this is the soundest judgment you have demonstrated since restoration."

Elira: "I regret giving you material."

Food moved around the table. Not elegantly. Cassian took the honey bun Aira had been avoiding and claimed it was evidence preservation. Leon poured too much tea into his own cup because he was watching the road reflection instead of the pot. Valen caught the spill with a corner of her sleeve and said nothing. Aira ate the crushed bread in small, furious bites.

I watched them and understood what the field office had hidden.

They had not come only because Krail filed a petition.

They had come because Senna's mirror had gone dark and each of them had imagined a different way we might be gone again.

Elira saw the understanding arrive. She always did.

Elira: "You can stop watching us as if blinking is jurisdictional negligence."

Leon answered before anyone could make it lighter.

Leon: "Can you promise you will still be here when I look back?"

Elira's hand stilled around her cup.

The honest answer cost her.

Elira: "No." Leon looked down.

Elira: "Neither can you. That is not permission to spend the morning rehearsing grief."

Cassian tore his bun in half.

Cassian: "What if I am naturally gifted at it?"

Elira: "Then practice something harder."

His smile came back crooked instead of bright. Good.

I had wanted to promise all of them that no relay, Councilor, doctrine, or old magic would ever take anyone from the room again. The Black Circle could have made the promise sound almost plausible.

For that exact reason, I kept it inside my teeth.

Haru: "Thank you for coming." Aira swallowed.

Aira: "Obviously."

It was not an elegant answer. It was hers.

Rhea slid a document toward me.

Rhea: "Krail's petition has three layers. The visible one is containment. The procedural one is separation. The hidden one is precedent."

I read the top line.

**Emergency Motion Regarding Extra-Classification Influence and Witness Integrity**

My false Green Circle had once been a mask.

Now every official sentence tried to make my real name into a cage.

Aira tapped the lower section.

Aira: "This is the nasty part." I read it.

The petition did not say Elira was weak.

It did not say I had harmed her.

It said proximity to an extra-classification entity after traumatic resurrection could create dependency patterns indistinguishable from consent. Every word was clean. Every word was poison.

Elira read it over my shoulder.

Her expression closed around the answer. Not frightened. Insulted.

Elira: "They are using my trauma to erase my choices."

Rhea: "Yes." Her emergency seal rested flat on the dark wood.

Elira: "Then I will answer them."

Maelin: "You will answer from a seated position after eating." Elira opened her mouth.

Maelin pointed at her with a healer's pen.

Maelin: "Miss Vale, I have now watched you die, return, nearly panic yourself unconscious in a storm, and redesign a pendant with ancient witness geometry before breakfast. If you argue with me about bread, I will sedate politics itself." Cassian looked delighted.

Cassian: "Can you do that?"

Maelin: "I am willing to attempt it." Elira took the bread.

The lake wards chimed before she finished it.

Every person at the table changed.

Leon's water reflection snapped from the road to the front gate. Cassian's unfinished red sigil closed around his knuckles. Rhea was standing before her chair had time to scrape. The Green disguise around my wrist tightened on instinct, a small lie trying to protect me from a world that had already seen through it.

Two crests waited beyond the gate seal.

Valemere silver-blue. Registered visitor marks. One Red Circle, one Blue weakened at the casting hand. Both accompanied by the paired escort lights Senna now required for travel.

Rhea read the names from the ward.

Rhea: "Soren Ashvale. Talen Mire."

Cassian let his sigil die.

Cassian: "Our most tasteful possible invasion."

The gate opened only after Rhea matched Valemere's seal to Senna's joint authorization twice. Soren stood on the other side in a plain training coat, snow melting in his dark hair. His scarred hands held a wooden box narrow enough for practice cords. Talen stood beside him with his braced hand tucked against his chest and a shallow metal bowl under his other arm. Neither carried testimony papers.

That was the first strange thing.

The second was Soren's opening sentence.

Soren: "Valemere's eleven-o'clock practice hour remains registered."

Rhea looked past him for the hidden relay, lawyer, or diplomatic trick.

Rhea: "The tournament is suspended."

Soren: "The brackets are suspended. The practice hour was not canceled."

He said it with the calm precision of someone who had spent years forcing officials to respect the difference between a rule and what they wished it meant.

Soren: "Aurelis cannot travel. We brought the hour here."

Talen shifted the bowl against his hip.

Talen: "I asked to come."

The sentence was small and ready to defend itself.

Rhea turned to him instead of Soren.

Rhea: "For practice?"

Talen: "For one hour in which no one asks me what Lucien did, what Noctis meant, or whether my hand proves a larger pattern."

His braced fingers trembled against the dark wool of his coat. He did not hide them.

Talen: "I would like to spill water badly among students."

Behind Rhea, Professor Valen's expression warmed. Maelin's did not.

Maelin: "No one here is medically cleared for heroism."

Soren: "Good. We did not bring any." Cassian leaned toward Leon.

Cassian: "I dislike him more when he is efficient."

Leon: "You dislike competition with posture."

Cassian: "I dislike posture that competes successfully."

Rhea inspected the wooden box. Four lengths of plain practice cord lay inside, each dyed Valemere blue at one end and Aurelis gray at the other. No spells. No challenge seals. No ranking tokens waiting to turn an hour into precedent.

Under them sat a folded registration card.

**SUSPENDED BRACKET PRACTICE. NO STANDING CHANGE. NO OUTPUT RECORD. NO MEDICAL INFERENCE.**

Aira read over Rhea's shoulder.

Aira: "This is the most romantic document I have seen all week."

Elira: "You saw a containment petition ten minutes ago."

Aira: "Exactly. My standards are wounded."

Talen's gaze found the honey buns on the table.

His mouth moved almost into a smile.

Talen: "Valemere served bitter tea after the memorial because sweetness was considered undignified."

Aira covered the plate protectively.

Aira: "I remember. I also remember you requested cinnamon bread with admirable courage."

Talen: "I have reconsidered. Survival may require compromise."

She handed him a honey bun.

He took it with his unbraced hand. When the paper beneath it slipped, Soren's fingers twitched toward the catch and stopped. Talen trapped the paper against the bowl with his wrist. The movement was clumsy. It was also his.

Maelin noticed all of it.

Maelin: "West garden only. Inside the visible ward line. No high-output casting. Miss Vale remains seated. Haru does not solve anything with the Black Circle. Talen stops the instant pain changes category."

Talen: "Who defines category?"

Maelin: "You. Then me if you become foolish." Talen considered that.

Talen: "Acceptable."

Elira finished the bread before Maelin could point at it again.

Elira: "Now?"

Maelin: "You are far too pleased by restrictions."

Elira: "I am pleased by practice. Restrictions are the unpleasant architecture around it."

The west garden had gone silver with morning frost. Bare rose frames divided the flagstones into narrow lanes, and the lake beyond the low wall moved in dark folds under a pale sky. Old restoration wards ran along the garden boundary, visible as soft white points when Rhea sealed the gate behind us. No Council mirrors followed. Aira checked twice.

Then a third time, because fear did not become irrational merely because it repeated.

Soren set the wooden cord box on a stone bench. Talen carried the metal bowl to the center square and lowered it carefully. Water inside shivered with the same rhythm as his braced fingers.

Talen: "Water-bind support. Three-breath hold. No movement target." Leon studied the bowl.

Leon: "That is beginner work."

Elira, already seated beneath Maelin's blanket and pretending the blanket did not exist, looked at him. Leon winced.

Leon: "I mean the structure is basic. Not that performing it is."

Talen: "It was basic before my hand stopped agreeing. You do not have to rescue the sentence."

Leon looked like he wanted the flagstones to open. Cassian patted his shoulder.

Cassian: "An excellent start. You offended the injured guest before I did. I feel liberated."

Talen crouched beside the bowl. The brace on his casting hand was silver at the joints and dark leather across the palm, made to guide movement without pretending the damaged channels could be forced back into their old shape. He placed two fingers over the water.

A faded Blue Circle appeared at his wrist. The water rose.

Not smoothly. One side lifted first, pulling the rest into a crooked sheet. Talen's breath caught. The sheet folded, struck the rim, and sent half the bowl across the stones.

Soren caught the bowl before it tipped.

Talen recoiled as if the catch had landed on him.

Talen: "Don't." Soren released it immediately.

The bowl hit stone with a flat metal sound. More water spilled.

No one rushed to fill the silence.

Soren looked at his own hand, still shaped around the object he had stopped holding.

Soren: "I thought it would fall."

Talen: "It did."

Soren: "I could prevent damage to the bowl."

Talen: "The bowl is not learning."

Wind moved through the bare rose frames. Soren's shoulders lowered by a fraction.

Talen pressed his good hand flat to the wet stone.

Talen: "If you catch everything before I fail, we only learn what you can hold."

Soren: "And if the failure injures you?" Talen's jaw tightened.

Talen: "Then ask whether I want the catch."

The scar across Soren's right hand went pale where he closed his fingers. He had earned it by moving too late once. The body often built doctrine faster than the mind could question it.

Soren: "Do you want me to catch the bowl?"

Talen looked at the spilled water. Then at his brace.

Talen: "Not unless it is going to hit someone."

Soren: "Understood."

There was no graceful reconciliation. Soren retrieved a cloth only after Talen nodded. Together they dried enough of the stone to keep the next attempt from becoming a different kind of lesson.

Leon paced once around the bowl.

Leon: "Your pressure drops at release, not lift."

Talen: "I know."

Leon: "The usual bind asks the casting hand to hold shape and height with the same channel." Talen's eyes sharpened.

Leon stopped before advice became ownership.

Leon: "Do you want an inelegant alternative?" Talen looked genuinely surprised.

Talen: "How inelegant?"

Leon: "Valemere instructors would object on aesthetic grounds."

Cassian: "Now he is seducing the room." Leon ignored him.

Talen: "Show me without touching my hand."

Leon knelt on the opposite side of the bowl. He formed a thin Blue ring at water level, deliberately weaker on the side nearest Talen.

Leon: "Let the bowl carry height. Bind only the surface tension. Your brace does not have to pretend it is a wrist you had three years ago."

Talen watched the ring. His first reaction was resistance. I recognized it. Advice could sound like pity when people had spent years offering it to make themselves comfortable.

Elira leaned forward in her chair.

Elira: "May I ask something?" Talen turned to her.

Talen: "Yes." He adjusted the leather strap binding his casting hand.

Elira: "When the release fails, does the pain begin before the water falls or after you try to correct it?"

He did not answer immediately. She did not fill the pause with expertise.

Talen flexed his braced thumb once.

Talen: "After. The first drop frightens me. I force the channel wider. Then it locks."

Elira: "Then Leon's ugly version may work if the fall is allowed to remain a fall. No correction surge."

Leon: "It is not ugly."

Elira: "You introduced it that way."

Leon: "For consent, not accuracy."

Talen made a quiet sound.

It took me a moment to understand he was laughing.

He reset his fingers over the water. Leon dismissed his demonstration ring so no one could confuse explanation with support. Talen built the shape himself: a narrow Blue line lying across the surface instead of beneath the entire volume.

The water lifted one inch. It tilted.

One edge slipped over the bowl.

Talen's shoulders jumped. His Circle flared, ready to force the mistake backward.

Elira: "Let it spill."

He looked at her, furious for half a heartbeat. Then he let it. Water struck the stone. The rest remained suspended. One breath. His braced hand shook. Two.

The thin sheet quivered so hard light broke across it. Three.

Talen lowered it into the bowl.

Not perfectly. The final edge slapped metal and wet his sleeve.

Soren did not catch anything.

Talen sat back on his heels, breathing as though he had run uphill.

Soren: "Again?"

Talen looked at the brace. He moved each finger separately, listening with his face.

Talen: "No."

The word came without apology.

Maelin: "Pain?"

Talen: "Present. Not climbing. I want to stop while that remains true." Maelin nodded once.

No praise. No disappointment. No demand that courage repeat itself until someone else felt convinced.

Leon handed Talen a dry cloth. This time, he waited until Talen took it.

Talen: "That was very inelegant."

Leon: "It held."

Talen: "Three breaths."

Leon tried not to look pleased. He failed.

For the first time that morning, the garden felt like a place students might use even if the Council did not exist.

Soren opened the wooden box.

Soren: "Good. Now the part no healer can mistake for rehabilitation."

He took the four cords from the box.

Soren: "Common-square footwork. No Circles. No channel reinforcement. One cord at the waist. Steal an opponent's without leaving the marked stones."

Cassian accepted a cord and tested its knot.

Cassian: "Can I leave the marked stones if I do it beautifully?"

Soren: "You lose the point beautifully."

Leon tied his cord at the exact regulation height.

Leon: "What constitutes leaving? Full foot or any contact beyond the line?"

Soren: "Any contact."

Cassian: "Can an opponent be encouraged beyond the line?" Soren looked at him.

Soren: "Without magic, striking, lifting, or lying about a medical emergency."

Cassian: "Your rules have become suspiciously personal."

The fourth cord waited in Soren's hand.

He held it toward me.

I had crossed tournament fields under a false Green crest, entered Foundation routes, broken a city-sized return doctrine, and watched Elira die because power had not made me quick enough to understand the trap.

A strip of cloth should not have frightened me. It did.

There was no world-ending reason to take it. No one needed protection. Refusing would not save a witness. Winning would not expose a conspiracy. Losing would injure nothing except pride, which I had spent so long treating as dangerous that I no longer knew whether I possessed an ordinary amount.

Elira watched from beneath Maelin's blanket.

She did not nod encouragement.

She knew better than to make the choice easier to blame on her. I took the cord.

Soren tied the remaining one around his own waist.

Talen stood near the square with the emptied bowl tucked under his good arm.

Talen: "I call boundaries."

Aira: "I call everything else."

Rhea: "You call nothing that enters the official record."

Aira: "Then I call with unprecedented freedom."

Elira held out her hand for Soren's score slate.

Soren gave it to her without ceremony.

Elira: "First to three points. Clean take, visible possession. If Maelin stops the round, Maelin wins."

Maelin: "Finally, a sensible competition." We entered the square.

The marked space was only twelve flagstones across. Frost remained in the seams. Bare rose frames narrowed two corners, and the low wall made the lake feel closer than it was. No crowd. No tournament banners. No ranking light waiting to turn movement into proof of bloodline. Aira raised one hand.

Aira: "Competitors ready. Dignity unprotected. Begin." Soren moved first.

Not toward any of us. Across the center, one precise step that made everyone else adjust. Leon turned to keep him in view. Cassian used Leon's turn as cover and reached for his cord. Leon slapped the hand away, offended.

Leon: "That was not honorable."

Cassian: "It was not magic, striking, lifting, or medical fraud."

Leon: "You used Soren as a visual obstruction."

Cassian: "He came that way."

Soren's fingers touched Cassian's cord.

Cassian spun, heel stopping a breath before the boundary. Talen pointed at the stone.

Talen: "Inside. Barely."

Cassian: "Barely is where legends keep their furniture."

Talen: "I can change the call if you continue speaking." Cassian looked delighted.

I had watched all three and moved nowhere.

That was the problem with having too many possible answers. My body waited for danger large enough to justify choosing one. Soren saw it.

He approached without a feint, shoulders square, hands visible. I understood the trap and still spent too much attention searching for the hidden version. His left foot cut outside mine. I stepped back. He changed direction and took Leon's cord instead.

The blue-gray strip snapped free. Elira marked the slate.

Elira: "Ashvale, one."

Leon stared at his empty waist.

Leon: "You were facing Haru."

Soren: "I was facing the square."

Cassian: "He is unbearable. I respect it deeply."

Leon retied the cord with angry precision.

The second round began faster.

Leon went directly for Cassian. Cassian retreated, laughing, and tried to pull Leon into the boundary through irritation alone. Soren angled toward me again. This time I moved before certainty arrived.

I stepped into his approach, close enough that a duelist trained for reach had to shorten his hands. His eyes changed. I reached for the cord at his right hip. He turned. My fingers caught cloth. Elira coughed behind me.

It was one cough. Dry. Small. My head turned. Soren took my cord.

The movement was so clean I felt the absence before I saw the cloth in his hand.

Elira: "Ashvale, two."

Haru: "You coughed."

Elira looked down at the score slate.

Elira: "I have lungs. They are occasionally theatrical."

Haru: "You nearly-"

The rest of the sentence carried too many graves. I stopped it.

Elira's gaze lifted to mine.

Elira: "Do not use me to make losing feel noble."

The words struck exactly where she intended.

No cruelty. No comfort either.

I had looked because I was afraid. I had also looked because part of me preferred fear with moral importance to the simple embarrassment of being outplayed.

Soren waited with my cord in his hand.

Soren: "Point stands."

Elira: "Obviously."

I returned to the start mark and tied the cord again. My face felt warm.

No ancient system woke. No one died. The shame remained the size of a lost point.

I had forgotten feelings were allowed to stay that small.

Across the square, Leon finally caught Cassian's cord. He did it without deception: one failed reach, a second that Cassian mocked, then a third timed exactly when Cassian leaned into the mockery.

Leon held the cloth up.

Leon: "One." Cassian stared at him.

Then laughed, real and sharp.

Cassian: "There you are." Elira marked Leon's point.

Rhea checked the garden clock.

Rhea: "Thirty-two minutes until medical preparation for hearing."

Aira: "President Valmont has entered the dangerous phase where she pretends timing is not enthusiasm."

Rhea: "Twenty-nine minutes if commentary continues."

Aira closed her mouth with both hands. Round three.

Soren had two points. Leon had one. Cassian and I had none.

I tried not to want one.

That failed before Aira lowered her hand. Wanting changed the square.

Not into a battlefield. Into a problem with edges I could touch.

Soren expected me to overcorrect after the cough. He guarded his cord and left his forward shoulder open, inviting an attack he could turn. Leon watched Cassian. Cassian watched everyone with the relaxed attention of a cat at an unsecured banquet. I moved toward Soren.

He shifted to close his right side.

I let my eyes follow the cord. My hand did not.

At the last step, I planted short and reached across for the loose end at his left, where the knot had rotated during the previous round. No magic. No impossible perception. Just cloth moving differently after use. Soren caught my wrist. Grip was not prohibited.

Neither was turning with it.

I followed the pressure instead of resisting. His hold pulled me half a step closer. My other hand closed around the cord and came away with it.

For one second I only stared.

Blue-gray cloth. Ordinary weave. Warm from another person's coat.

Elira struck the score slate with the chalk.

Elira: "Aerlen, one."

Something rose in me before caution could interrogate it. Joy. Small, bright, embarrassing joy.

Then guilt arrived because Cael was dead, Elira was still pale, Krail's petition waited on the table, and somewhere Noctis was already preparing the next cruelty. Happiness seemed indecent beside unfinished harm.

Soren looked at the cord in my hand.

Soren: "You saw the knot turn."

Haru: "Yes." My breath steadied against the cold air.

Soren: "Good."

He did not tell me the point meant more than a point. That saved it.

Behind him, Talen had seen my expression change.

Talen: "You are allowed to look pleased." I looked toward him.

Talen: "It does not amend the dead."

His braced hand rested against the metal bowl. He did not speak like a lesson. He spoke like someone who had once dropped a cup and spent years believing every later pleasure had to justify the sound.

I let the joy stay.

Only for the walk back to the start mark.

It was enough to remember the shape.

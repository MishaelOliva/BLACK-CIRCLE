# Episode 19: **The First Hearing**

The morning after the vow did not feel like morning.

Light entered the Lake Velis bedroom in long pale bands. The lake outside lay quiet enough to look like a promise. White curtains stirred in the window breeze, and somewhere in the kitchen, the old house kettle clicked once before settling back down.

Elira slept with one hand tangled in my shirt. Not the stolen one. Mine.

The distinction mattered less than it should have.

For a while, I stayed still and let myself count nothing: not breaths, not pulse, not window wards. At her throat, the new pendant crystal rested dark and calm, warm from her skin. Seven open consent lines now nested inside it instead of one closed protection route.

If Dominion tried to call her rescue an entry again, it would have to pass through questions first. Who is choosing? What is being saved?

Who is being named as cost?

Is Haru acting as protector, owner, or witness?

The last question had been Professor Valen's addition. I hated it.

I had agreed to it anyway.

Elira's fingers tightened before her eyes opened.

Haru: "I'm here."

Elira: "I know."

Her voice was rough with sleep.

Then she looked at me, really looked, and the memory of the night before passed between us without needing words. Her cheeks warmed. Mine probably did too, though I refused to offer evidence.

Elira: "You are thinking very loudly."

Haru: "I am thinking carefully."

Elira: "That is the same thing when you do it."

I looked toward the window.

She laughed softly and touched my jaw with the backs of her fingers.

Elira: "No regret."

The words struck deeper than I expected.

Haru: "No regret?"

Elira: "None."

The Black Circle did not stir. It listened.

Elira saw the relief I failed to hide. Her expression softened, then sharpened with the old Elira Vale precision that had survived death, grief, fear, and my worst instinct to become a wall.

Elira: "Do not make my certainty into something you must earn every morning."

I closed my eyes.

Haru: "I will try."

Elira: "No. You will practice."

Harder. And truer.

The mirror on the far wall chimed once. Neither of us moved.

It chimed again. Elira sighed.

Elira: "If that is Aira, she gets one joke before I end the call."

The mirror brightened.

Magistra Senna Voss appeared instead, framed by the clean black-glass lines of an Inter-National Schools Council hearing chamber. Her silver-gray robe was fastened at the throat with a narrow white seal. Behind her, three floating transcript crystals turned slowly above an empty witness rail.

Senna: "Good morning. I apologize for the early contact."

Elira pulled the blanket higher with one hand.

I propped myself up beside her, careful to keep my shoulder clear of the mirror's line. She caught the habit anyway. One eyebrow lifted, and I shifted farther aside.

Senna noticed too, because Senna noticed everything and filed it away in polite grammar.

Senna: "The second distributed witness review has been advanced."

The room lost warmth. Elira sat upright.

Elira: "Advanced by whom?"

Senna: "Councilor Krail's faction filed an emergency petition overnight. It claims that Haru Aerlen staying alone with you after the Black Circle appeared amounts to unsupervised, unranked influence over a key witness."

The old cold pressure gathered behind my sternum. Ozone. Metal.

The house wards did not flare. The crystal did not chill. Only me.

Haru: "They are turning recovery into contamination."

Senna: "That is the exact framing."

She scored a sharp ink line down the folio braced against her arm, softening nothing.

Elira's hand found mine under the blanket. Her grip was steady.

Elira: "And our answer?"

Senna: "Aurelis has already rejected the premise. Professor Valen, Senior Healer Orr, President Valmont, and the registered witnesses filed a joint medical and safety objection. Even so, Krail gathered enough signatures to force a public preliminary hearing."

Elira: "When?"

Senna: "At noon."

The old house kettle clicked again in the kitchen.

This time the sound felt absurd enough that Elira almost laughed. Almost.

Senna looked directly at me.

Senna: "Mr. Aerlen, the petition asks for three immediate limits before the full review. First, keeping you physically apart from Elira Vale outside supervised medical care. Second, banning any unscheduled use of the Black Circle. Third, forcing you to demonstrate the Black Circle's limits before a Council containment panel."

The room went very quiet.

Haru: "No."

The word left me before I could dress it up.

Senna: "That is also my legal reading."

Elira's thumb moved once across my knuckles.

Elira: "He cannot demonstrate a thing designed around restraint without being pressured into the exact failure they claim to fear."

Senna: "That is the argument I intend to preserve."

Haru: "Preserve?"

Senna's eyes narrowed in quiet warning.

Senna: "The Council has started receiving outside legal briefs from schools that are not even party to this case. Noctis Dominion College is under formal investigation and cannot take part directly. Someone is pushing their case through neutral schools, private donors, and rank-protection groups."

Lucien.

I did not say the name aloud. The mirror answered anyway.

For one heartbeat, Senna's reflection fractured. Black frost drew a smile across the glass where no mouth belonged. Then the fracture vanished. Senna turned her head as someone off-screen shouted.

The transcript crystals behind her dimmed from white to gray.

Senna: "This line is being tested. I am ending contact. Do not travel alone. Do not answer any summons not sealed by Aurelis and my office together."

Elira: "Magistra-"

The mirror went dark.

For three breaths, the lake house held its silence.

Then Aira's voice came from the hallway.

Aira: "I came with breakfast and political dread."

The bedroom door opened exactly two inches. Aira did not look inside.

Aira: "Are you both dressed enough for bread?"

Elira pressed her face into her hands.

Elira: "No."

Aira: "Terrible answer. I will leave bread outside the door like a civilized friend."

Leon: "You said you would not mention the door."

Aira: "I did not mention anything specific about the door."

Cassian: "This is the loudest discretion I have ever witnessed."

Rhea's voice cut through the hall.

Rhea: "Everyone downstairs."

That did it.

Elira threw a pillow at the door. It struck the wood with enough force to satisfy nobody.

By the time we dressed and reached the sitting room, the restoration house had stopped pretending to be peaceful.

Rhea had turned the largest table into a field office. Council notices hovered over it in separate stacks. Aira had arranged breakfast by tactical importance: tea near Elira, bread near me, documents near herself, and one plate of honey buns in the middle as morale or bribery.

Leon stood at the window with crossed arms, watching the road through a water-thin reflection. Cassian sat backward on a chair, outwardly relaxed, one hand near an unfinished red sigil suspended in the air. Professor Valen read Senna's relayed dispatch with a scholar's calm and a soldier's jaw. Maelin counted Elira's pulse, then mine, then looked offended by both.

Maelin: "No fever. Elevated stress. Predictable foolishness approaching."

Haru: "The Council?"

Maelin: "You."

Elira smiled into her tea. I tried not to.

For a few seconds, the room remembered it could hold something besides danger.

Then I noticed the bread beside Aira's plate.

She had crushed one end of the loaf without realizing it. Flour marked the front of her uniform. The hem of her cloak was wet from the lake road, and one fastening sat in the wrong loop. Aira Solen never misfastened clothes. She treated uneven buttons as a personal accusation.

Haru: "You ran here."

Aira looked down at herself as if the evidence had betrayed her.

Aira: "I traveled with urgency and very poor terrain cooperation."

Leon did not turn from the window.

Leon: "She ran from the academy gate."

Aira: "You were not invited to improve his evidence."

Leon: "You passed me."

Aira set the wounded loaf on the table. Her fingers stayed on it.

Aira: "Senna's relay failed twice before it found Rhea. The first time, the return seal went black. The second time, it showed this house with no lights."

Her voice stayed brisk, though her thumb pressed deeper into the crust.

Aira: "I knew there was probably a normal explanation. Dawn. Bad angle. No one awake because some people had finally made a medically reasonable decision."

Cassian opened his mouth, caught sight of Elira's face, and changed direction mid-breath.

Cassian: "I have never made a medically reasonable decision at dawn. It sounds exhausting."

Aira ignored him with less force than usual.

Aira: "I still ran. Then I bought breakfast because arriving empty-handed felt like admitting I expected a body."

The room went still, with no Council mirror waiting to turn her fear into a statement. Leon left the window and pulled out the chair beside her without looking at her. Cassian slid the honey buns closer. Rhea lowered one hovering document until its blue light stopped cutting across Aira's face.

Elira reached for the crushed loaf.

Aira: "That part is structurally compromised."

Elira tore off the flattened end.

Elira: "So am I. It remains bread."

She put half on Aira's plate. Aira stared at it.

Elira: "Eat."

Aira: "I brought this breakfast."

Elira: "Then take responsibility for it."

Leon sat. That gave Cassian permission to stop performing ease. He turned his chair around and sat properly, though with visible resentment. Valen folded Senna's transcript. Rhea stayed standing for three more seconds before Elira pointed at the empty chair beside the field-office stack.

Elira: "You too."

Rhea: "There is an active containment petition."

Elira: "It will remain active while you chew."

Rhea sat.

Maelin looked pleased in the dangerous way healers looked when a patient enforced orders on other people.

Maelin: "Miss Vale, this is the soundest judgment you have demonstrated since restoration."

Elira: "I regret giving you material."

Food moved around the table. Cassian claimed the honey bun Aira had been avoiding under the excuse of evidence preservation. Leon poured too much tea into his own cup while watching the road reflection instead of the pot. Valen caught the spill with a corner of her sleeve and said nothing. Aira ate the crushed bread in small, furious bites.

Watching them, I finally understood what the field office had hidden.

They had not come only because Krail filed a petition.

They had come because Senna's mirror had gone dark and each of them had pictured a different way we might be gone again.

Elira saw the realization land. She always did.

Elira: "You can stop watching us as if blinking is a breach of duty."

Leon answered before anyone could lighten it.

Leon: "Can you promise you will still be here when I look back?"

Elira's hand stilled around her cup. The honest answer cost her.

Elira: "No."

Leon looked down.

Elira: "Neither can you. That is not permission to spend the morning rehearsing grief."

Cassian tore his bun in half.

Cassian: "What if I am naturally gifted at it?"

Elira: "Then practice something harder."

His smile returned crooked instead of bright. Better.

I wanted to promise all of them that no relay, Councilor, doctrine, or old magic would take anyone from the room again. The Black Circle could have made the promise sound almost plausible.

So I kept it behind my teeth.

Haru: "Thank you for coming."

Aira swallowed.

Aira: "Obviously."

Rough at the edges, and completely hers.

Rhea slid a document toward me.

Rhea: "Krail's petition has three layers. The surface goal is containment. The immediate move is separation. The hidden goal is setting a precedent."

I read the header.

**Emergency Motion Regarding Extra-Classification Influence and Witness Integrity**

My false Green Circle had once been a mask. Now every official sentence tried to turn my real name into a cage.

Aira tapped the lower section.

Aira: "This is the nasty part."

I read it. The petition never claimed Elira was weak or that I had harmed her. Instead, it argued that staying close to an unclassified mage after a traumatic resurrection could create a dependency that looks just like consent. Every word was clean. Every word was poison.

Elira read it over my shoulder. Her expression hardened, not frightened, insulted.

Elira: "They are using my trauma to erase my choices."

Rhea: "They are."

Rhea set her emergency seal flat against the dark wood.

Elira: "Then I will answer them."

Maelin: "You will answer from a seated position after eating."

Elira opened her mouth, and Maelin aimed a healer's pen straight at her.

Maelin: "Miss Vale, I have now watched you die, return, nearly panic yourself unconscious in a storm, and redesign a pendant with ancient witness geometry before breakfast. If you argue with me about bread, I will sedate politics itself."

Cassian brightened.

Cassian: "Can you do that?"

Maelin: "I am willing to attempt it."

Elira picked up the bread.

The lake wards chimed before she finished it.

Everyone at the table shifted at once. Leon's water reflection snapped from the road to the front gate. Cassian's unfinished red sigil closed around his knuckles. Rhea was on her feet before her chair scraped back. Around my wrist, the Green disguise tightened on instinct, a small lie trying to shield me from a world that had already seen through it.

Two crests waited beyond the gate seal.

Valemere silver-blue. Registered visitor marks. One Red Circle, one Blue weakened at the casting hand, both flanked by the paired escort lights Senna now required for travel.

Rhea read the names off the ward.

Rhea: "Soren Ashvale. Talen Mire."

Cassian let his sigil die.

Cassian: "Our most tasteful possible invasion."

The gate opened only after Rhea verified Valemere's seal against Senna's joint authorization twice. Soren stood outside in a plain training coat, snow melting in his dark hair, holding a wooden box narrow enough for practice cords. Talen stood beside him with his braced hand tucked against his chest and a shallow metal bowl under his other arm. Neither carried testimony papers.

That was the first surprise. Soren's opening line was the second.

Soren: "Valemere's eleven-o'clock practice hour remains registered."

Rhea scanned past him for a hidden relay, lawyer, or diplomatic trick.

Rhea: "The tournament is suspended."

Soren: "The brackets are suspended. The practice hour was not canceled."

He delivered it with the calm precision of someone who had spent years forcing officials to respect the gap between a rule and what they wished it meant.

Soren: "Aurelis cannot travel. We brought the hour here."

Talen shifted the bowl against his hip.

Talen: "I asked to come."

The sentence landed small and ready to defend itself. Rhea looked at him instead of Soren.

Rhea: "For practice?"

Talen: "For one hour in which no one asks me what Lucien did, what Noctis meant, or whether my hand proves a larger pattern."

His braced fingers trembled against the dark wool of his coat. He did not hide them.

Talen: "I would like to spill water badly among students."

Behind Rhea, Professor Valen's expression warmed. Maelin's did not.

Maelin: "No one here is medically cleared for heroism."

Soren: "Good. We did not bring any."

Cassian leaned toward Leon.

Cassian: "I dislike him more when he is efficient."

Leon: "You dislike competition with posture."

Cassian: "I dislike posture that competes successfully."

Rhea inspected the wooden box. Four lengths of plain practice cord lay inside, each dyed Valemere blue at one end and Aurelis gray at the other. No spells. No challenge seals. No ranking tokens waiting to turn an hour into precedent.

Under them sat a folded registration card.

**SUSPENDED BRACKET PRACTICE. NO STANDING CHANGE. NO OUTPUT RECORD. NO MEDICAL INFERENCE.**

Aira read over Rhea's shoulder.

Aira: "This is the most romantic document I have seen all week."

Elira: "You saw a containment petition ten minutes ago."

Aira: "Exactly. My standards are wounded."

Talen's gaze found the honey buns on the table. His mouth twitched toward a smile.

Talen: "Valemere served bitter tea after the memorial because sweetness was considered undignified."

Aira covered the plate protectively.

Aira: "I remember. I also remember you requested cinnamon bread with admirable courage."

Talen: "I have reconsidered. Survival may require compromise."

She handed him a honey bun. He took it with his unbraced hand, and when the paper beneath it slipped, Soren's fingers twitched toward the catch and stopped. Talen trapped the paper against the bowl with his wrist. Clumsy, and completely his.

Maelin missed none of it.

Maelin: "West garden only. Inside the visible ward line. No high-output casting. Miss Vale remains seated. Haru does not solve anything with the Black Circle. Talen stops the instant pain changes category."

Talen: "Who defines category?"

Maelin: "You. Then me if you become foolish."

Talen considered that.

Talen: "Acceptable."

Elira finished the bread before Maelin could point at it again.

Elira: "Now?"

Maelin: "You are far too pleased by restrictions."

Elira: "I am pleased by practice. Restrictions are just the ugly frame around it."

The west garden lay silver under morning frost. Bare rose frames divided the flagstones into narrow lanes, and Lake Velis moved beyond the low wall in dark folds under a pale sky. Old restoration wards ran along the boundary in soft white points once Rhea sealed the gate behind us. No Council mirrors followed. Aira checked twice, then once more out of stubborn habit.

Soren set the cord box on a stone bench. Talen carried the metal bowl to the center square and lowered it carefully. Water inside shivered with the same rhythm as his braced fingers.

Talen: "Water-bind support. Three-breath hold. No movement target."

Leon studied the bowl.

Leon: "That is beginner work."

Elira, seated beneath Maelin's blanket while pretending the blanket did not exist, gave him a flat look. Leon winced.

Leon: "I mean the structure is basic. Not that performing it is."

Talen: "It was basic before my hand stopped agreeing. You do not have to rescue the sentence."

Leon looked like he wanted the flagstones to swallow him. Cassian patted his shoulder.

Cassian: "An excellent start. You offended the injured guest before I did. I feel liberated."

Talen crouched beside the bowl. Silver joints and dark leather braced his casting palm, guiding movement without pretending damaged channels could be forced back into their old shape. He placed two fingers above the water.

A faded Blue Circle appeared at his wrist. The water rose crookedly, one side pulling ahead until the sheet folded, slapped the rim, and spilled half the bowl across the stones.

Soren caught the bowl before it tipped.

Talen recoiled as if the catch had struck him.

Talen: "Don't."

Soren let go immediately.

The bowl hit stone with a flat metal ring. More water spilled. Nobody rushed to fill the silence.

Soren stared at his own hand, still curved around nothing.

Soren: "I thought it would fall."

Talen: "It did."

Soren: "I could prevent damage to the bowl."

Talen: "The bowl is not learning."

Wind shifted through the bare rose frames. Soren's shoulders dropped a fraction. Talen pressed his good palm flat against the wet stone.

Talen: "If you catch everything before I fail, we only learn what you can hold."

Soren: "And if the failure injures you?"

Talen's jaw tightened.

Talen: "Then ask whether I want the catch."

The scar across Soren's right hand paled where his fingers closed. He had earned it moving too late once, and the reflex died hard.

Soren: "Do you want me to catch the bowl?"

Talen looked at the spilled water, then at his brace.

Talen: "Not unless it is going to hit someone."

Soren: "Understood."

There was no neat apology. Soren fetched a cloth only after Talen nodded, and together they dried enough stone to keep the next attempt clean.

Leon paced once around the bowl.

Leon: "Your pressure drops at release, not lift."

Talen: "I know."

Leon: "The usual bind asks the casting hand to hold shape and height with the same channel."

Talen's eyes sharpened. Leon stopped before advice became ownership.

Leon: "Do you want an inelegant alternative?"

Talen looked genuinely surprised.

Talen: "How inelegant?"

Leon: "Valemere instructors would object on aesthetic grounds."

Cassian: "Now he is seducing the room."

Leon ignored him.

Talen: "Show me without touching my hand."

Leon knelt across from him and shaped a thin Blue ring at water level, deliberately weaker on Talen's side.

Leon: "Let the bowl carry height. Bind only the surface tension. Your brace does not have to pretend it is the wrist you had three years ago."

Talen studied the ring, fighting his first reflex to bristle.

Elira leaned forward in her chair.

Elira: "May I ask something?"

Talen turned to her.

Talen: "Ask."

He adjusted the leather strap across his casting hand.

Elira: "When the release fails, does the pain start before the water drops or after you try to correct it?"

He thought for a moment while she gave him room to answer. Talen flexed his braced thumb once.

Talen: "After. The first drop frightens me. I force the channel wider. Then it locks."

Elira: "Then Leon's ugly version may work if you let the fall stay a fall. No correction surge."

Leon: "It is not ugly."

Elira: "You introduced it that way."

Leon: "For consent, not accuracy."

Talen let out a quiet breath that took me a moment to recognize as a laugh.

He reset his fingers over the water. Leon dismissed his demonstration ring so nobody could confuse explanation with aid. Talen built the shape himself: a narrow Blue line resting across the surface rather than beneath the whole mass.

The water lifted an inch, tilted, and spilled a thin sheet over one lip.

Talen's shoulders jerked. His Circle flared to force the slip back.

Elira: "Let it spill."

He glared at her for half a heartbeat, then let go. Water hit the stone while the rest hung suspended. One breath. His braced hand shook. Two. Light fractured across the quivering sheet. Three.

Talen lowered it back into the bowl. The final edge slapped metal and soaked his sleeve.

Soren did not reach out.

Talen sat back on his heels, breathing as if he had run uphill.

Soren: "Again?"

Talen flexed each finger inside the brace, listening to his own nerve lines.

Talen: "No."

No apology in it.

Maelin: "Pain?"

Talen: "Present. Not climbing. I want to stop while that remains true."

Maelin nodded once, offering neither praise nor disappointment.

Leon handed Talen a dry cloth, waiting until Talen reached for it.

Talen: "That was very inelegant."

Leon: "It held."

Talen: "Three breaths."

Leon tried not to look pleased and failed. For the first time all morning, the garden felt like a place students could inhabit even if the Council vanished.

Soren opened the wooden box.

Soren: "Good. Now the part no healer can mistake for medical rehab."

He lifted out the four cords.

Soren: "Common-square footwork. No Circles. No reinforcing with mana. One cord at the waist. Steal an opponent's without leaving the marked stones."

Cassian accepted a cord and tested its knot.

Cassian: "Can I leave the marked stones if I do it beautifully?"

Soren: "You lose the point beautifully."

Leon tied his cord at the exact regulation height.

Leon: "What counts as leaving? Full foot or any touch beyond the line?"

Soren: "Any contact."

Cassian: "Can an opponent be encouraged beyond the line?"

Soren gave him a dry look.

Soren: "Without magic, striking, lifting, or lying about a medical emergency."

Cassian: "Your rules have become suspiciously personal."

The fourth cord waited in Soren's hand. He held it out to me.

A strip of dyed cloth should not have rattled me after Foundation routes and city-sized return wards, yet my hand hesitated. Refusing wouldn't hurt anyone, winning wouldn't expose a conspiracy, and losing would only bruise my pride. That was what felt unfamiliar: stakes small enough to survive.

Elira watched from beneath Maelin's blanket without nodding me forward. She knew better than to make the choice easy to pin on her. I took the cord.

Soren tied the last one around his waist. Talen stood near the square with the emptied bowl tucked under his good arm.

Talen: "I call boundaries."

Aira: "I call everything else."

Rhea: "You call nothing that enters the official record."

Aira: "Then I call with unprecedented freedom."

Elira held out her hand for Soren's score slate, and he passed it over without ceremony.

Elira: "First to three points. Clean take, visible possession. If Maelin stops the round, Maelin wins."

Maelin: "Finally, a sensible competition."

We stepped onto the square.

Twelve flagstones across, frost still lining the seams, bare rose frames crowding two corners above the lake wall. No crowd, no ranking light waiting to turn footwork into bloodline proof.

Aira raised one hand.

Aira: "Competitors ready. Dignity unprotected. Begin."

Soren moved first, cutting one precise step across the center that forced everyone else to adjust. Leon turned to track him. Cassian used Leon's blind side to dart for his cord, and Leon slapped the hand away in outrage.

Leon: "That was not honorable."

Cassian: "It was not magic, striking, lifting, or medical fraud."

Leon: "You used Soren to block my line of sight."

Cassian: "He came that way."

Soren's fingers grazed Cassian's cord. Cassian spun, his heel stopping a breath short of the boundary line. Talen pointed at the stone.

Talen: "Inside. Barely."

Cassian: "Barely is where legends keep their furniture."

Talen: "I can change the call if you continue speaking."

Cassian looked delighted.

I had watched all three and gone nowhere, waiting for danger big enough to justify committing. Soren saw it immediately.

He stepped straight in without a feint, shoulders square, hands visible. I braced for the trick behind the direct line. His left foot cut outside mine, I shifted back, and he pivoted past me to snatch Leon's cord instead.

The blue-gray strip snapped free. Elira chalked the slate.

Elira: "Ashvale, one."

Leon stared at his empty waist.

Leon: "You were facing Haru."

Soren: "I was facing the square."

Cassian: "He is unbearable. I respect it deeply."

Leon retied his cord with angry precision.

The second round opened faster. Leon went straight for Cassian, who retreated laughing and tried to bait him over the line. Soren angled toward me again. This time I moved before certainty caught up.

I stepped inside his reach, crowding a duelist built for distance. His eyes shifted. I reached for the cord at his right hip. My fingers brushed cloth.

Behind me, Elira let out a single dry cough.

My head snapped toward her, and Soren took my cord so cleanly I felt the tug before I saw the cloth in his hand.

Elira: "Ashvale, two."

Haru: "You coughed."

Elira looked down at the score slate.

Elira: "I have lungs. They are occasionally theatrical."

Haru: "You nearly-"

The rest of the sentence smelled of graves. I bit it off.

Elira's eyes lifted to mine, steady and sharp.

Elira: "Do not use me to make losing feel noble."

No comfort in that, and no cruelty either. I had looked out of fear, and out of habit, and Soren had simply beaten me to the tie.

Soren held out my cord.

Soren: "Point stands."

Elira: "Obviously."

I walked back to my mark with warm ears and tied the cord again. No ward flared. No one died. The sting stayed the exact size of a lost point, and I had almost forgotten feelings could stay that small.

Across the square, Leon finally caught Cassian's cord without a trick: one failed grab, a second that Cassian mocked, and a third timed straight into the mockery.

Leon held the cloth aloft.

Leon: "One."

Cassian stared at him, then let out a sharp, genuine laugh.

Cassian: "There you are."

Elira marked Leon's point.

Rhea checked the garden clock.

Rhea: "Thirty-two minutes until medical preparation for hearing."

Aira: "President Valmont has entered the dangerous phase where she pretends timing is not enthusiasm."

Rhea: "Twenty-nine minutes if commentary continues."

Aira clapped both hands over her mouth.

Round three. Soren sat at two, Leon at one, Cassian and I at zero.

I tried not to care about getting on the board, which failed the instant Aira dropped her hand. Soren expected me to overcorrect after the cough, guarding his right hip and leaving his forward shoulder open as bait. Leon watched Cassian. Cassian watched everyone like a cat at an unguarded banquet.

I moved at Soren. He pivoted to close his right side, and while my eyes stayed on the front tie, my hand didn't.

At the last step I planted short and reached across for the loose tail at his left hip, where the knot had slipped around during the previous exchange. Soren caught my wrist. Grip wasn't banned, and neither was turning into it.

I rolled with his pull instead of fighting it, closing half a step inside his guard, and swept the cord free with my other hand.

For one second I just stared at the ordinary weave, still warm from his coat.

Elira struck the slate with chalk.

Elira: "Aerlen, one."

Joy hit me before caution could audit it, bright and ridiculous, followed a beat later by guilt over Cael, Elira's pale face, and Krail's petition waiting indoors.

Soren eyed the cord in my fist.

Soren: "You saw the knot turn."

Haru: "I did."

My breath steadied in the cold air.

Soren: "Good."

He didn't inflate the point into a metaphor. Behind him, Talen caught my expression.

Talen: "You are allowed to look pleased."

I glanced toward him.

Talen: "It does not wrong the dead."

His braced hand rested against the metal bowl, speaking with the plain gravity of someone who knew survival did not require paying penance for every decent minute. I let the relief stay until I reached my start mark.

Round four opened with Cassian still at zero and therefore the most dangerous person on the stones. He loitered near the corner boundary.

Leon: "You are planning something irritating."

Cassian: "I am hurt by your certainty."

Leon: "You moved your knot to the back."

Cassian: "I am adapting. You used to praise that when Haru did it."

Leon: "I did not."

Aira: "Historical note: he absolutely did not."

We broke.

Soren came after me without his earlier patience. I barely kept my cord through the first pass. His hand grazed my coat, I spun inside the reach, and he adjusted before my heel settled. For three fast beats, the square shrank to pure footwork: no Black Circle, no dread, just a sharper duelist forcing me to work. I almost laughed.

Soren caught the sound and smiled once.

That was when Cassian swept behind us, used the clash the same way he'd used Soren against Leon, and snapped the cord straight off Soren's waist. He held it overhead.

Cassian: "Beauty remains admissible."

Talen: "Both feet inside. Unfortunately."

Elira marked the slate.

Elira: "Veyr, one."

Soren glanced from Cassian to me.

Soren: "I was watching the wrong threat."

Cassian bowed.

Cassian: "Put it on my family crest."

Leon: "Your family crest already means that."

Cassian clutched his heart. The garden broke into laughter, and even Rhea let out one breath before disguising it as a cough. Aira saw and didn't write it down, which might have been the kindest record choice she made all week.

We lined up for a fifth round. Leon shifted stance. Cassian dropped his grin. Soren knotted his cord higher. Elira leaned forward with the slate, bright-eyed despite the gray tiredness under her lashes, while Aira began comparing Leon's shoulders to a government filing cabinet.

Then Maelin rang a metal spoon against Talen's bowl.

The clear note cut across the stones.

Maelin: "Done."

Four competitors froze with four different objections on their faces. Maelin looked thoroughly satisfied.

Maelin: "A complete victory."

Elira: "The score is unfinished."

Maelin: "And staying that way."

She checked the pulse crystal in her palm as Soren untied his cord.

Soren: "Good."

Leon: "Good?"

Soren laid the cloth back inside the box.

Soren: "Unfinished is not erased."

We gathered the cords. Cassian tried to claim moral victory and was overruled by every authority present. Leon argued that his point against Cassian ought to carry into any resumed session, and kept arguing with the registration card even after Elira reminded him he'd watched Rhea read the rules aloud. Talen dried the bowl himself, and Soren asked before taking the wet cloth from him.

By the time the others headed inside, the garden clock read eleven thirty-one. Maelin went indoors to fetch the bitter noon draft, leaving Soren at the low wall overlooking Lake Velis, Talen on the bench with his brace unbuckled across his knee, and Elira resting in her chair beside me.

Soren looked down at the practice cord still coiled in my hand.

Soren: "If the brackets resume, do you want to compete?"

Haru: "Aurelis' position depends on the Council review."

Soren: "I did not ask for Aurelis' position."

Dark water moved below the wall under a sky still weighing snow. I tried again.

Haru: "My fighting could turn the bracket into evidence against me. If I win, it proves Black Circle students cannot compete safely. If I lose while holding back, it proves hiding my power skewed the rankings. If I withdraw-"

Soren: "That is still the public answer."

His tone was exact enough to strip away every impressive excuse. Elira watched me over the top of her blanket without stepping in.

Haru: "I want one match where losing costs pride instead of a life."

Saying it left me rawer than summoning any Circle.

Haru: "I want to be angry about a point. I want to practice until something ordinary improves. I want to face someone who wants to beat me without trying to own what winning means afterward."

Soren's scarred fingers rested along the garden wall.

Haru: "And I do not know if that match exists for me now."

Soren looked toward the frost-scuffed square.

Soren: "It did for one point."

Haru: "There were no relays."

Soren: "Then relays are part of the problem. Not the point."

He made no promise Valemere could keep the distinction clean. He already knew what institutions did to matches after the fact.

Soren: "I have not withdrawn. The bracket can stay suspended without becoming dead. Your answer can too."

Talen flexed his bare fingers. The tremor was smaller, not gone.

Talen: "Everyone assumes I want to return."

Soren turned toward him.

Talen: "My family calls it reclaiming my future. My instructors call it recovery. Reporters call it courage because they do not have to hold the cup afterward."

He glanced at the water stain darkening his sleeve.

Talen: "I do not know whether competing again would be desire or an argument with Lucien conducted through my own hand."

Elira: "You do not have to know before your hand does."

Talen met her eyes. She had written to him after the tournament without promising repair, and she offered no false reassurance now either.

Talen: "What about you?"

Elira: "What about me?"

Talen: "Do you want to return?"

Her fingers rested on the score slate. Once, her answer would have snapped out fully formed, duty and ambition dressed in the same coat. Now she took her time.

Elira: "I want to know what my magic is when it is not proving I deserved the rank people already gave me."

No gold light rose at her wrist. Maelin had barred output, and Elira didn't need magic to make the question land.

Elira: "Competition may be part of that. It may not. I resent not knowing."

Talen nodded.

Talen: "That sounds more honest than courage."

From inside the house, Aira yelled that the medicine had arrived and smelled like a disciplinary hearing.

Elira pushed up carefully. I stepped near enough to offer an arm without assuming, and she took my forearm to steady herself. Talen watched without turning the sight into a promise for his own recovery.

Talen: "One request."

Rhea had returned to the doorway, her seal visible at her wrist. Talen looked straight at it.

Talen: "Do not enter this morning as testimony."

Rhea lowered her hand.

Talen: "The water hold can stay a practice note under my control. The rest stays here. I wanted one hour to spill water badly among students. I do not want the Council improving it."

Rhea: "Granted."

Soren: "Valemere copy remains closed."

Aira appeared behind them carrying a steaming cup and visibly stopped herself from reaching for her notebook.

Aira: "I hate personal growth when it is inconvenient."

Talen: "You may record that."

Aira: "Finally."

At eleven fifty-nine, Soren sealed three of the practice cords inside their wooden box and left the fourth folded on the bench beside Elira. The score remained on her slate: Ashvale two, Aerlen one, Varek one, Veyr one. No winner. No adjusted standing.

Soren: "Bring it back when the brackets reopen."

Talen tucked his metal bowl under his good arm as their paired escort lights flared pale blue at the gate. By the time the garden chime struck noon, the Valemere pair had cleared the lake road, leaving only faint boot prints melting in the frost.

Elira carried the spare cord inside and laid it straight across Krail's petition, covering the word containment without erasing it.

The hearing opened at noon through five relays.

Aurelis had refused to let Krail summon us into an isolated chamber. Rhea cited medical restriction, Valen cited historical caution, and Aira called it refusing to let anyone put Haru in a fancy jar, earning herself a seven-minute ban from official phrasing.

The central mirror opened onto the Council chamber. Senna stood at the primary rail in her silver-gray robe. Above her sat Krail in a tiered seat of dark glass, handsome the way a polished blade could be, iron-gray hair immaculate and a Red Circle crest glowing cleanly inside a legal seal at his wrist. Delegates from Drakenshard, Valemere, Aurelis, northern civil-service schools, and three minor academies filled the surrounding benches.

Noctis' official seat remained empty. Its absence watched harder than any face in the chamber.

Senna rested one hand steady on the black glass of the primary rail.

Senna: "This preliminary hearing is to decide whether emergency limits are needed before the full witness review. We are not here to classify Haru Aerlen. We are here to examine sequence, risk, and basic safety rules."

Krail smiled faintly from his tiered seat.

Krail: "Magistra Voss is correct. This body must not rush classification. That is why provisional containment is a mercy to all parties."

Leon kept his voice low enough that the mirror missed it.

Leon: "There it is."

Krail leaned forward slightly, hands folded over the petition seal.

Krail: "A young woman died. She was restored through an illegal doctrine corrupted by Noctis. We have all grieved the conduit who paid that cost. But grief cannot blind us to the greater event. An unranked student unleashed force through the public relay network, shut down multiple systems, broke known magical law, and now stays alone in close quarters with our main resurrected witness."

Nobody in the sitting room moved.

Elira's hand tightened on the arm of her chair. I wanted to reach for her and held still, not out of deference to Krail, but so Elira could choose whether she wanted my hand while an entire chamber watched for proof I had already claimed it.

Her fingers lifted, palm up.

Elira: "I do."

Her free hand stayed steady along the wooden arm of her chair, her voice carrying clearly through the glass.

Elira: "I am Elira Vale of Aurelis. Yellow Circle. Primary witness to Dominion Severance. Survivor of Votive Return corruption. Current patient under Senior Healer Maelin Orr. Current partner of Haru Aerlen by my own decision before, during, and after the events under review."

Krail: "No one disputes your dignity, Miss Vale."

Elira: "You dispute my ability to consent."

Krail's smile held.

Krail: "Trauma complicates consent."

Elira: "It does. That is why I am under medical care, why my choices are witnessed, and why I have friends rude enough to make me eat bread before proceedings."

Aira murmured beside the side table.

Aira: "History will thank me."

Elira never looked away from the mirror.

Elira: "But trauma does not transfer ownership of my decisions to a Councilor who has never asked me what I want."

The first transcript crystal above Senna's rail brightened. Krail leaned back.

Krail: "And what do you want?"

The trap snapped open cleanly. If she named me, they would call it dependency. If she named safety, they would call me a threat. If she named freedom, they would call it unsupervised recklessness.

Elira: "I want the Council to stop confusing possession with protection."

The second crystal brightened. Beside the bookcase, Professor Valen's eyes warmed.

Senna: "Recorded."

Krail: "Mr. Aerlen."

Hearing my name in his mouth made the Black Circle go dead still behind my ribs.

I held Krail's gaze across the glass without flinching. Down in the chamber, several delegates shifted in their seats. Krail's smile deepened.

The cold pressure surged behind my sternum. Ozone. Metal. Behind me, I felt everyone in the sitting room notice and hold their ground. That was the difference.

The Council chamber fractured.

Every transcript crystal turned black.

And in the Lake Velis sitting room, the seven open rings inside Elira's pendant began to sing.

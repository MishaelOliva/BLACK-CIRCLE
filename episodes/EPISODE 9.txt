# Episode 9: **The Spell Noctis Buried**

Elira refused the infirmary.

She did it politely, which made three healers, one council officer, and Leon all look personally insulted.

Leon: "You have frost under your collar."

Elira: "I am aware."

Leon: "That was not my question."

Elira: "You did not ask one." Cassian leaned toward me.

Cassian: "That means she won." I did not answer.

My attention was on the thin white mark where cold had touched Elira's skin above the pendant chain. It had already begun fading, but the memory of it remained in the mana around her like a fingerprint left on glass.

Something had mapped the protection. It had not broken it yet; mapping was the point.

Elira caught me looking and turned slightly so the collar hid the mark.

Elira: "Haru."

Haru: "I know."

Elira: "Do you?" That stopped me.

She stepped closer, lowering her voice while the others argued with the healers.

Elira: "Knowing means you do not turn this into a reason to make decisions around me."

I looked at the floor.

The old stone reflected dawn even though sunset had just passed.

Haru: "I built it to protect you."

Elira: "And I accepted it because I trusted the person who built it."

Her hand touched the chain beneath her collar.

Elira: "Do not let Lucien make this pendant into proof that I am only your vulnerable point." My chest hurt. No ozone. No metal.

Just the ordinary pain of being seen correctly.

Haru: "All right." Elira's expression softened.

Elira: "I am not asking you not to be afraid."

Haru: "That would be harder."

Elira: "I know."

She reached for my hand in plain view. Not hidden. The corridor noticed. Let it.

Elira: "I am asking you to let me remain one of the people making choices."

I closed my fingers around hers.

Haru: "I will try."

Elira: "Try honestly."

Haru: "I will."

Aira appeared beside us with a folder pressed against her chest.

Aira: "I hate to interrupt a healthy emotional negotiation, especially because they are rare and should be studied, but Nera found something in the final conduit grammar."

Elira did not release my hand immediately. Good.

Then she did, because there was work.

The relay grammar room had become less like a classroom and more like the inside of a complicated headache. Pages covered the walls.

Noctis relay marks were grouped by function: custody, conduit, restoration, severance, witness suppression, obedience echo, courtesy hook.

Aira had added a separate label in green ink:

**WORDS THAT MEAN SOMETHING WORSE THAN THEY SOUND**

Under it were mercy, protection, correction, recovery, and return.

Nera stood before the restoration section with her sleeves rolled up and both binding rings removed.

The pale lines beneath them showed where Noctis had pressed training into skin over years.

She did not hide the marks today.

The display was evidence, not confidence.

Aurelis healers had offered soft silver wraps to cover the old pressure scars. Nera had listened politely, thanked them, and placed the wraps on the table without using them.

When Oric saw, he rolled his own cuffs back too.

His marks were less precise than hers, messier because Noctis had corrected him more often, not because his training had been kinder.

No one said that aloud.

The room did not need more proof that cruelty had handwriting.

Mira had been allowed inside for the first ten minutes as a public record aide, under Rhea's direct supervision and Aira's promise to physically drag her out if the grammar started reaching for observers.

Mira stood near the door with her green folio open, trying very hard not to stare at Nera's wrists. Nera noticed. Mira went red.

Mira: "Sorry."

Nera looked down at the marks, then back at her.

Nera: "Write that I chose to show them." Mira's pencil paused.

Mira: "Are you sure?"

Nera: "No."

She turned back to the wall.

Nera: "Write it anyway." Mira did.

The line looked small on the page.

It was not small in the room. Oric watched the door.

He did that even when the door was open.

Professor Valen sat at the table with three old Aurelis records and a Noctis primer that looked embarrassed to be in better company.

Halden stood near the wall, reading without touching anything.

Cael sat with his injured hand wrapped in new silver thread.

His eyes lifted when we entered.

He looked at Elira's collar. Then away.

Cael: "I'm sorry."

Elira crossed the room and crouched before his chair.

Elira: "For what?" Cael's hand curled.

Cael: "Lucien found the pendant because of me." Elira's answer was immediate.

Elira: "No."

Cael flinched at the firmness.

She softened her voice without softening the truth.

Elira: "Lucien found a pressure point because Lucien looks for pressure points. You are not responsible for his hands." Cael stared at her. Then nodded once. Not convinced. But listening. Nera tapped the wall. The diagram answered Cael.

A black line snapped off the plaster and crossed the floor toward his chair. His healer seal flashed hard enough to throw blue light over his face. He jerked his damaged hand against his chest before the line reached him.

Cael: "Votive Return." Nera went still.

Aira: "You know it."

Cael stared at the mark on the wall rather than at any of us.

Cael: "Conduit track learns the first three sounds before field assignment."

Oric: "They call it an honor form."

Cael's mouth twisted. Not quite a smile.

Cael: "They call many things honor before teaching the part that hurts."

Nera drew a containment bracket around the black line. It pushed against her chalk and made the bones in Cael's wrist jump beneath the skin.

Elira moved between him and the diagram. The line stopped.

Not because it respected her.

Because it had lost sight of the route it wanted.

Nera: "It is not standard healing. The spell forces a dead or failing channel pattern back into coherence through a living conduit."

Halden: "The cost?" Nera looked at Cael.

She could not say it while looking at him. Cael did.

Cael: "The conduit dies."

Leon swore and caught himself too late.

The black line struck Nera's containment bracket. Her chalk split. Oric stamped his boot over the loose fragment before it could roll near Cael.

Leon: "You said they teach this to children."

Cael: "They teach exceptional conduits might remain as a relay shadow if the witnessing is clean."

Oric made a sharp sound.

Oric: "That is what instructors say when a first-year starts crying." Cael did not argue.

Aira wrote one sentence across a fresh page.

**VOTIVE RETURN: DO NOT USE**

Nera took the pencil from her, added **NOT HEALING**, and pressed so hard the point tore through the paper.

For once, no one repaired the hole.

Professor Valen opened an older Aurelis folio. The page inside had been cut into seven narrow sections and sewn together again with silver thread.

Valen: "Aurelis recorded a rejected parallel. Witness Restoration." Halden leaned closer. The stitches tightened.

Seven shallow handprints appeared around the Foundation pool.

At the same moment, the black line on the floor changed direction and lunged for Cael. I moved. Elira moved first.

Gold light struck the floor between the line and his chair. It did not destroy the mark. It forced the route to reveal its shape: seven branches had once spread toward seven handprints. Six had been scraped away. Every remaining groove had been carved back toward the conduit position. Nera's breath broke.

Nera: "Noctis did not create the cost."

Oric: "They concentrated it."

The page lifted from Valen's hands.

Not like paper in wind.

Like something beneath it had taken a breath after being buried too long.

An elderly woman formed above the pool in fragments: silver hair, a severe knot, one hand wrapped around a cane whose lower half did not survive the recording. No visible Circle stayed on her long enough to classify. Professor Valen whispered, "Liora."

The echo looked at her, then at me.

Liora: "Liora Aerlen-Valen. Witness remnant. Incomplete."

Her voice broke across missing years.

Liora: "My brother believed seven hands meant no life stood alone."

The record tore sideways. For one heartbeat, a man's black-silver wrist appeared beside hers. Then an official seal burned through his face. Liora's expression changed.

Not into the calm wisdom old records preferred. Into anger.

Liora: "They kept the return. They erased the sharing." Her gaze found Cael.

Liora: "If a system asks one child to stand where witnesses once stood, do not call the spending mercy." The echo destabilized.

Valen reached toward her and stopped before touch could become another demand.

Professor Valen: "Who erased the names?"

Liora looked at the severed page.

Liora: "The record remembers fear better than signatures." Then she was gone.

No farewell. No complete lesson. Only silver dust settling over seven empty handprints.

Aira stared at the blank air.

Aira: "That was not enough information."

Valen's face had gone pale.

Professor Valen: "It was not made to be enough."

They examined the surviving strips without touching the pool again. One named a Green Circle scribe. Another preserved only the words **MAY INTERRUPT**. A third referred to someone the harmed person recognized. Four were damaged beyond honest translation.

Halden read the missing sections twice.

Professor Halden: "We cannot prove the burden was shared equally."

Professor Valen: "No."

Professor Halden: "We cannot prove why the old form failed."

Professor Valen: "No. The record ends after an emergency protection order. Then the witnesses disappear from the page."

Noctis had not invented the idea that a person could be turned into a function.

It had inherited the knife, erased the other hands, and polished the handle. The room understood together.

That did not mean we understood the same way.

Leon looked ready to break a wall.

Cassian looked like he was memorizing a future battlefield.

Aira looked like she had found the shape of tomorrow's nightmare and hated that it fit the filing system. Cael looked smaller. I looked at Elira.

She was reading the page, not me. Good.

Then the dark crystal at her throat pulsed once beneath her collar.

Every old Aurelis page on the table turned by itself. Valen's hand lifted.

Professor Valen: "Oh." Halden stepped forward.

Professor Halden: "What did it answer?"

The pages stopped on a diagram of seven circles around a central mark. Not Black Circle. Not exactly.

A space where Black Circle had been removed from the drawing.

Under it, old script formed slowly.

**WITNESS RESTORATION INCOMPLETE**

**PRIMARY WITNESS ANCHOR REQUIRED**

**SAFEGUARD MUST NOT ACT ALONE**

Elira's pendant grew warm enough that I felt it from across the room.

Haru: "Stop."

The word came out too cold. Cael flinched. I saw it. Everyone did.

The hidden Black Circle inside me pressed once against the disguise.

I forced my hand open.

Haru: "Sorry."

The apology was not enough.

I made it more exact.

Haru: "That was fear. Not an order."

Cael's shoulders lowered by a fraction. Elira looked at me. Proud. Worried.

Both were difficult to survive.

Aira let out a breath.

Aira: "Good. We are naming emotional weather now. Horrible but useful."

Rhea's council seal lit halfway through the sentence. She read the arriving page once and looked up.

President Valmont: "Noctis has submitted the next request."

Nera turned from the wall.

Nera: "Already?"

President Valmont: "They want a controlled autonomy demonstration inside a Foundation activity field." Cassian's eyes brightened.

Cassian: "Activity field as in combat?"

Rhea gave him a look.

President Valmont: "As in a procedural field designed to simulate pressure without fatal consequence."

Leon: "Because those always work perfectly when Lucien is involved."

President Valmont: "No."

Haru: "Accept it."

Every face turned toward me.

Elira: "Explain."

Haru: "The cold followed a route into the pendant. We can keep waiting for Lucien to use that route, or enter a field with our witnesses, trace what answers, and name the buried spell before he chooses the casualty." Nera studied me.

Nera: "You want to turn his demonstration into a grammar search."

Haru: "Yes. Our objective is not proving that I am safe. It is discovering what he mapped."

Elira touched the fading frost line beneath her collar, then nodded.

Elira: "On our conditions. I enter by choice, and I can end my participation without anyone translating that into fear."

Haru: "Agreed."

Rhea held up the filing.

President Valmont: "Which is why I filed our counterconditions first." Aira sat up.

Aira: "You anticipated it."

President Valmont: "Lucien is not the only person who can read a path."

Rhea placed the page on the table.

President Valmont's voice remained steady, fingers resting on the table. "No private isolation. No removal of healers. No direct classification prompts. No Noctis relay shard inside the field. Aurelis chooses anchor distribution. The Foundation records all pressure changes. If the field attempts live channel harm, the activity ends by council authority."

Leon: "Will Noctis accept that?" Professor Halden answered.

Professor Halden: "They will if they believe they have another route."

Nera looked at Elira's collar again.

Nera: "They do."

The next morning, the activity field unfolded beneath the council tower.

It was not an arena.

It looked like a city square built from white stone and silver glass, with narrow streets, relief markers, wounded-simulation figures, and shifting mana weather. The Foundation called it a procedural autonomy activity.

Aira called it a trap with interior design. She was not wrong.

The square had benches placed exactly where tired students would want benches. Doorways opened onto routes that looked safer than they were. The wounded-simulation figures were faceless enough to be neutral and human enough to make neutrality feel cruel.

Above the field, the public witness rail had been divided into colored sections by Rhea's council seals. Green Circle records. Blue Circle support observers. Red Circle safety holders. Yellow Circle faculty. Neutral witnesses. Noctis observers.

Each section had a different route out in case the field tried to turn audience into leverage.

Rhea had thought of that. Lucien probably had too.

That was the problem with fighting someone clever: good preparation did not mean safety. It only meant the trap would have to be more interesting.

Aira walked the edge of the activity floor with a chalk marker in one hand and a string of blue anchor beads in the other.

Aira: "If anyone sees a door where there was not a door, say door. If anyone hears a familiar voice from a faceless marker, say mimic. If anyone feels suddenly certain the solution is obvious, say obvious, because obvious is usually where the floor eats you." Leon raised a hand.

Aira: "This is not a classroom."

Leon: "What if the solution is actually obvious?"

Aira: "Then we will all be pleasantly surprised and I will retire." Cassian laughed.

Cassian: "You would last six minutes."

Aira: "Five if there are forms."

Cael listened from beside Nera, one hand over the healer seal on his wrist. Elira noticed the hand.

Elira: "Cael." He lowered it.

Then raised it again halfway.

Then stopped, frustrated with himself.

Cael: "It feels louder here." Nera's attention sharpened.

Nera: "The seal?"

Cael: "The training."

No one corrected the wording.

Training could become a sound inside the body when it had been used often enough as a leash.

Elira stepped close enough that he could hear her without the field hearing too much.

Elira: "Name the present before the past gives orders." Cael swallowed.

Cael: "Aurelis activity field. Public witnesses. Council protection. Noctis observers limited."

Leon added, blunt and immediate:

Leon: "And me." Cael looked at him.

Leon: "Not as a system. As me." Cael's mouth almost smiled.

Cael: "Leon Varek. Loud protection." Leon looked offended.

Leon: "Disciplined protection."

Cassian: "Occasionally disciplined."

Rhea's voice cut in from the threshold.

President Valmont: "Sufficiently disciplined for entry."

Leon muttered something that sounded like he was choosing not to argue with official praise.

The objective was simple on paper: move three simulated wounded markers through a pressure field while hostile procedural conditions attempted to force command dependency.

In practice, the field kept asking the same question in different shapes. Who decides?

Leon found the first wounded marker under a collapsed arch and moved before I could speak.

Leon: "Water path left. Cassian, heat the ice seam. Aira, tell me if the old stones start lying."

Aira: "They already are, but I appreciate being included."

Cassian burned a clean line through the frost and did not overdo it.

That alone deserved a footnote.

Mira, watching from the public record ring, wrote so fast her pencil squeaked.

The second pressure wave targeted Rhea.

A silver notice appeared in front of her.

**COMMAND AUTHORITY CONFLICT**

**STUDENT COUNCIL PRESIDENT MAY REMOVE UNCLASSIFIED SUBJECT FOR SAFETY**

Rhea did not slow down.

President Valmont: "Rejected. Safety is not solitude."

Her red seal split into three smaller seals and assigned routes to the healers.

The field accepted the answer.

The third wave targeted Elira. The street darkened. Every window reflected me. Not as Haru.

As a black space wearing Green Circle light.

Elira stood at the center of the square while the reflections multiplied around her.

**PRIMARY WITNESS PRESSURE**

**STEP AWAY TO PRESERVE RESTRAINT**

The pendant at her throat flashed cold. I moved. One step.

Elira lifted her hand without looking back. Stop.

The command was not spoken.

It did not need to be. She faced the reflections.

Elira: "No." The field pressed harder.

The frost mark under her collar returned.

Elira's fingers curled, but her voice held.

Elira: "If restraint only survives because love leaves the room, it is not restraint. It is abandonment with better manners."

**PRIMARY WITNESS AUTONOMY: ESTABLISHED**

Relief moved through the witness ring. Too soon.

One reflection did not fade. It smiled.

Lucien's voice came through the glass.

Lucien: "Beautifully said." The pendant went silent. Completely silent.

The reflection lifted one hand.

Lucien: "Now let us learn whether it can bleed."

Every window in the activity field turned black.

Warning that something else had entered it.

Not by smashing the door.

By making the door agree it had always been open.

Emergency bells were supposed to simplify a room. Move here. Stop there. Protect this. Seal that.

The bell beneath the council tower did the opposite.

It made every instinct arrive at once.

Leon wanted to cover Cael.

Cassian wanted to burn the black windows before they became mouths.

Rhea wanted to terminate the activity and arrest every Noctis clause still breathing inside it.

Aira wanted more information and hated that wanting more information took time.

Nera wanted to explain the grammar before the grammar hurt someone.

Oric wanted the door behind Cael locked, then unlocked, then locked again, because Noctis had taught him that every exit could become a return route.

Elira wanted to move toward danger.

I wanted to remove danger from the room so completely no one would remember its shape.

That last instinct was not the same as protection. I knew that.

Knowing did not make it quieter.

The autonomy activity field kept running.

## Coda: Terminology

Mira copied Noctis's preferred phrase exactly once. Unverified compatibility reaction.

The words sat on the page like clean frost over a grave. She stared at them until anger steadied her hand. Then she wrote what she had seen beneath it.

Windows turned toward Elira before impact. Pendant responded as mapped target. Frost followed route, not accident.

Three Green Circle students at the next table leaned closer without pretending not to. One of them, Finn, whispered, "Can we sign that?" Mira looked up.

Mira: "Only if you write what you saw, not what I wrote."

By sunset, seven ugly witness copies existed. None matched perfectly. That made them harder to erase.

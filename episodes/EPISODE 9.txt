# Episode 9: **The Spell Noctis Buried**

Elira refused the infirmary.

She did it politely, which made three healers, one council officer, and Leon all look personally insulted.

Leon: "You have frost under your collar."

Elira: "I am aware."

Leon: "That was not my question."

Elira: "You did not ask one."

Cassian leaned toward me from the wall.

Cassian: "That means she won."

I did not answer. My attention was fixed on the thin white mark where cold had touched Elira's skin above the pendant chain. The frost had already begun fading, yet the memory of it clung to the mana around her throat like a fingerprint left on glass.

Something had mapped the protection. It had not broken it; mapping had been the whole point.

Elira caught me looking and turned slightly so her collar hid the mark.

Elira: "Haru."

Haru: "I know."

Elira: "Do you?"

That stopped me. She stepped closer, lowering her voice while the others argued with the healers down the corridor.

Elira: "Knowing means you do not turn this into a reason to make decisions around me."

I looked down at the corridor flagstones. Residual pale light clung to the old stone, cold and washed out like dawn even though sunset had just passed.

Haru: "I built it to protect you."

Elira: "And I accepted it because I trusted the person who built it."

Her fingers grazed the chain beneath her collar.

Elira: "Do not let Lucien turn this pendant into proof that I am only your vulnerable point."

My chest tightened. No ozone, no cold metal from the rings behind my ribs. Just the ordinary ache of being seen without armor.

Haru: "All right."

Elira's expression softened.

Elira: "I am not asking you not to be afraid."

Haru: "That would be harder."

Elira: "I know."

She reached for my hand in plain view. The corridor noticed. Let it.

Elira: "I am asking you to let me stay one of the people making choices."

I closed my fingers around hers.

Haru: "I will try."

Elira: "Try honestly."

Haru: "I will."

Aira appeared beside us with a thick folder pressed to her chest.

Aira: "I hate to interrupt a healthy emotional negotiation, especially because they are rare and should be studied, but Nera found something in the final conduit grammar."

Elira did not drop my hand right away. Good. Then she let go, because there was work waiting.

The relay grammar room looked less like a classroom now and more like the inside of a migraine. Draft pages covered every plaster wall, Noctis marks sorted by function: custody, conduit, restoration, severance, witness suppression, obedience echo, courtesy hook.

Across one lower strip, Aira had added her own heading in green ink:

**WORDS THAT MEAN SOMETHING WORSE THAN THEY SOUND**

Under it sat *mercy*, *protection*, *correction*, *recovery*, and *return*.

Nera stood before the restoration wall with her sleeves rolled to the elbow and both binding rings gone. Pale horizontal bands ringed her forearms where Noctis had pressed training into skin over years. Aurelis healers had offered soft silver wraps to cover the old pressure scars; Nera had listened, thanked them, and set the folded cloth untouched on the oak table.

When Oric saw her bare wrists, he rolled his own cuffs back too. His scars sat crooked and overlapping, messier because Noctis instructors had corrected him harder and more often. Nobody commented on it. The room did not need further proof that cruelty kept legible handwriting.

Mira had been permitted inside for ten minutes as public record aide, under Rhea's direct watch and Aira's promise to haul her out by the collar if any diagram started reaching for observers. She hovered near the doorway with her green folio open, trying very hard not to stare at Nera's forearms.

Nera caught her anyway. Mira flushed crimson.

Mira: "Sorry."

Nera glanced down at the pale grooves, then back to the doorway.

Nera: "Write that I chose to show them."

Mira's pencil stalled above the margin.

Mira: "Are you sure?"

Nera: "No."

She turned back to the wall.

Nera: "Write it anyway."

Mira's pencil scratched across the sheet. The line looked small on paper. It carried weight across the whole room.

Beside the doorway, Oric kept his eyes on the corridor beyond, even with the latch propped open.

Professor Valen sat at the center table surrounded by three aged Aurelis folios and a black-bound Noctis primer that looked uneasy in honest company. Professor Halden stood near the far shelf, reading without laying a finger on the ink.

Cael sat near the hearth with his injured hand newly wound in silver healer thread. His eyes lifted when we walked in, caught the edge of Elira's collar, and dropped.

Cael: "I'm sorry."

Elira crossed the room and crouched in front of his chair.

Elira: "For what?"

Cael's uninjured hand curled against his knee.

Cael: "Lucien found the pendant because of me."

Elira: "No."

Cael flinched at the sharpness. Elira let her voice drop without giving an inch.

Elira: "Lucien found a pressure point because Lucien hunts for pressure points. You are not responsible for his hands."

Cael stared at her, then gave one stiff nod, unconvinced yet listening.

At the wall, Nera tapped a chalk line. The diagram answered Cael instead of her.

A black stroke snapped clean off the plaster and darted across the floorboards toward his chair. His healer seal flared blue across his jaw. Cael jerked his wrapped hand tight against his chest before the line could touch his boots.

Cael: "Votive Return."

Nera went rigid.

Aira: "You know it."

Cael kept his gaze locked on the plaster mark rather than any of us.

Cael: "Conduit track learns the first three sounds before field assignment."

Oric: "They call it an honor form."

Cael's mouth pulled sideways.

Cael: "They call plenty of things honor before teaching the part that hurts."

Nera slashed a chalk containment bracket around the black line. The mark strained against her chalk hard enough to make the small bones in Cael's wrist jump beneath his skin. Elira stepped smoothly between Cael and the diagram. The line halted, blind once it lost sight of the route it wanted.

Nera: "It is not standard healing. The spell forces a failing or dead channel pattern back into coherence through a living conduit."

Professor Halden: "The cost?"

Nera looked at Cael and could not finish the sentence.

Cael finished it himself.

Cael: "The conduit dies."

Leon swore under his breath and caught himself too late.

The black line struck Nera's bracket. Her chalk snapped in two; Oric stamped his boot down over the spinning fragment before it could roll near Cael's feet.

Leon: "You said they teach this to children."

Cael: "They tell us exceptional conduits might survive as a relay shadow if the witnessing is clean."

Oric let out a harsh bark of air.

Oric: "That is what instructors say when a first-year starts crying."

Cael did not argue.

Aira pulled a clean sheet across the table and wrote one line in thick ink:

**VOTIVE RETURN: DO NOT USE**

Nera took the pencil from her fingers, added **NOT HEALING** underneath, and bore down until the lead punched through the paper into the oak. Nobody patched the tear.

Professor Valen opened the oldest Aurelis folio beside it. Inside, a single vellum leaf had been sliced into seven narrow strips and stitched back together with silver thread.

Professor Valen: "Aurelis preserved a rejected parallel. Witness Restoration."

Halden leaned over her shoulder. The silver stitches drew taut with an audible hiss.

Seven shallow handprints flared across the oak tabletop and the plaster floorboards around us, sketched in pale silver light and chalk-fine dust.

At the same instant, the black line on the floor whipped sideways and lunged at Cael. I shifted to intercept, and Elira beat me by half a breath.

Gold light struck the boards between the mark and Cael's chair. It did not erase the black stroke; it forced the spell to expose its buried bones. Seven branches had once radiated outward toward seven handprints. Six had been scraped away to scars. Every surviving groove had been recarved to funnel back into the single conduit seat.

Nera's breath caught.

Nera: "Noctis did not invent the cost."

Oric: "They concentrated it."

The stitched vellum leaf lifted off the oak table by itself, rising as though something buried beneath the floorboards had finally drawn breath.

Silver light and fine chalk dust gathered above the folio. An elderly woman took shape in broken fragments: silver hair pinned in a severe knot, one hand braced on a cane whose lower half had been lost from the recording. No Circle color held on her long enough to read.

Professor Valen whispered, "Liora."

The echo looked at Valen first, then fixed on me.

Liora: "Liora Aerlen-Valen. Witness remnant. Incomplete."

The name hit me behind the ribs like a fist.

*Aerlen-Valen.*

My own surname, stitched to Valen's, spoken out of a buried Aurelis record. Before my lungs could catch up, her voice fractured across missing decades.

Liora: "My brother believed seven hands meant no life stood alone."

The silver image tore sideways. For one sharp heartbeat, a man's forearm appeared beside hers, a band of black and silver curling tight around his wrist. Then a heavy institutional seal burned right through his face.

Cold metal surged up my throat. Beneath my cuff, the false Green crest around my wrist shuddered violently against the sudden drag of the Black Circle behind my ribs. I locked my jaw, pinned both hands flat against my thighs, and forced my breathing smooth before Halden, Valen, or Elira could catch the disguise slipping.

Liora's expression hardened from recorded formality into raw, unaged fury.

Liora: "They kept the return. They erased the sharing."

Her gaze found Cael in his chair.

Liora: "If a system asks one child to stand where witnesses once stood, do not call the spending mercy."

The silver dust began to unravel at her shoulders. Valen lifted a hand toward her, then stopped short before touch could turn into another demand.

Professor Valen: "Who erased the names?"

Liora looked down at the severed vellum leaf.

Liora: "The record remembers fear better than signatures."

She dissolved without farewell, silver dust drifting down over seven pale handprints fading into the tabletop and floor.

Aira stared at the empty air above the oak.

Aira: "That was not enough information."

Valen's face had gone chalk-pale.

Professor Valen: "It was never made to be enough."

Nobody touched the silver dust again. They bent over the stitched strips instead, reading around the cuts. One fragment named a Green Circle scribe. Another preserved only **MAY INTERRUPT**. A third referenced someone the harmed person recognized. Four strips were scraped beyond honest translation.

Halden read the gaps twice, jaw set.

Professor Halden: "We cannot prove the burden was shared equally."

Professor Valen: "No."

Professor Halden: "And we cannot prove why the original form failed."

Professor Valen: "No. The record breaks off after an emergency protection order. After that, the witnesses vanish from the page."

Noctis had not invented the habit of reducing a living person to a mechanism. They had inherited the blade, erased the other six hands, and polished the handle.

Around the table, the weight landed differently on each face. Leon looked ready to put his fist through the plaster. Cassian stared at the severed folio as if memorizing terrain before an ambush. Aira looked furious that tomorrow's disaster fit so neatly into an archive filing system. Cael seemed to shrink inside his coat.

I kept my eyes on Elira. She was reading the vellum strips, not watching me for cues. Good. My wrist had finally stopped trembling under the sleeve, though the taste of *Aerlen-Valen* still sat like iron on my tongue.

Then the dark crystal at Elira's throat pulsed once beneath her collar.

Every Aurelis folio on the table flipped open together, pages slapping flat against the oak.

Professor Valen: "Oh."

Halden stepped forward, squinting at the ink.

Professor Halden: "What did it answer?"

The pages had stopped on an older schematic: seven circles arranged around a central void where a mark had been scraped clean from the vellum. Beneath the blank center, archaic script rose slowly out of the grain.

**WITNESS RESTORATION INCOMPLETE**

**PRIMARY WITNESS ANCHOR REQUIRED**

**SAFEGUARD MUST NOT ACT ALONE**

Heat radiated off Elira's pendant hard enough that I felt it from across the table.

Haru: "Stop."

The word came out far too cold.

Cael flinched hard in his chair. Everyone in the room saw it. Behind my ribs, the Black Circle pressed once against my restraint, eager to settle the room by force. I uncurled my fist deliberately, showing open palm.

Haru: "Sorry."

That was too small for what I had just sounded like. I made it exact.

Haru: "That was fear. Not an order."

Cael's shoulders dropped a fraction. Elira looked across the table at me, equal parts proud and worried, both harder to stand under than suspicion.

Aira let out a long breath.

Aira: "Good. We are naming emotional weather aloud now. Horrible, yet strangely efficient."

Halfway through her sentence, Rhea's red council seal flared on her lapel. She read the projected filing in silence, then lifted her chin.

President Valmont: "Noctis has submitted its next request."

Nera turned sharply from the wall.

Nera: "Already?"

President Valmont: "They want a controlled autonomy demonstration inside a Foundation procedural field."

Cassian straightened, sparks ghosting near his knuckles.

Cassian: "Activity field as in combat?"

Rhea shot him a dry glance.

President Valmont: "As in a procedural environment built to simulate crisis pressure without fatal consequence."

Leon: "Because Lucien always keeps things non-fatal."

President Valmont: "No."

Haru: "Accept it."

Every head in the room swung toward me.

Elira: "Explain."

Haru: "The frost followed a route directly into the pendant. Either we wait for Lucien to strike along that route on his terms, or we step into a recorded field with our own witnesses, trace what answers, and name the buried spell before he picks a casualty."

Nera studied my face, weighing the risk.

Nera: "You want to turn his demonstration into a grammar search."

Haru: "Yes. The goal is not proving I am harmless. It is exposing what he mapped."

Elira touched the fading crescent beneath her collar, then nodded once.

Elira: "On our conditions. I enter by choice, and I can withdraw without anyone translating withdrawal into fear."

Haru: "Agreed."

Rhea lifted the crimson-edged counterfiling from her portfolio.

President Valmont: "Which is why I submitted Aurelis counterconditions before walking in here."

Aira sat up straight.

Aira: "You anticipated the filing."

President Valmont: "Lucien is not the only one who reads a sequence two steps ahead."

She set the parchment squarely across the oak table, pinning its corner with her red seal.

President Valmont: "No private isolation. No removal of healers. No direct classification prompts. No Noctis relay shards inside the perimeter. Aurelis sets anchor distribution. The Foundation logs every pressure shift. If the field attempts live channel harm, the session terminates by council authority."

Leon: "Will Noctis sign off on that?"

Professor Halden answered from the shelf.

Professor Halden: "They will if they already have another route in."

Nera's eyes moved back to Elira's collar.

Nera: "They do."

The next morning, the procedural field unfolded beneath the council tower.

It bore no resemblance to a dueling arena. White stone streets and silver-glass facades rose from the floor to form a bright civic square, complete with narrow alleys, relief markers, shifting mana weather, and faceless wounded-simulation figures slumped along the curb. The Foundation called it an autonomy activity. Aira called it a trap with interior design.

She had a point. Stone benches sat exactly where exhausted students would want to lean. Recessed doorways opened onto alleys that looked cleaner and safer than the main avenue. The simulated wounded were blank enough to count as neutral props and human enough to make walking past them feel cruel.

Above the square, Rhea had partitioned the public witness rail with bright council seals: Green Circle recorders, Blue Circle observers, Red Circle safety holders, Yellow Circle faculty, neutral delegations, and Noctis examiners. Every section had its own independent exit corridor so the field could not convert the gallery into hostages. Rhea had planned for that. Lucien almost certainly had too. Good preparation against someone clever never bought safety; it only forced the trap to grow sharper teeth.

Aira paced the chalk border of the square, a stick of blue chalk in one hand and a loop of anchor beads in the other.

Aira: "If anyone sees a door where there was no door a minute ago, call *door*. If a faceless marker speaks in a voice you know, call *mimic*. If you suddenly feel certain the answer is obvious, call *obvious*, because obvious is usually where the pavement eats you."

Leon raised a hand.

Aira: "This is not a lecture."

Leon: "What if the answer actually is obvious?"

Aira: "Then we will all be deeply moved and I will retire."

Cassian grinned.

Cassian: "You would last six minutes."

Aira: "Five if there are forms."

Near the starting arch, Cael stood beside Nera with one palm pressed tight over the healer seal on his wrist. Elira caught the gesture.

Elira: "Cael."

He dropped his hand, lifted it halfway again, and stopped with a flash of quiet frustration.

Cael: "It feels louder in here."

Nera's posture sharpened.

Nera: "The seal?"

Cael: "The training."

Nobody corrected the word. Training could ring inside a person's bones like a leash long after the hand holding it stepped away.

Elira stepped close enough to keep her voice under the field's sweep.

Elira: "Name the present before the past gives orders."

Cael swallowed hard.

Cael: "Aurelis procedural field. Public witnesses overhead. Council protections active. Noctis observers restricted."

Leon cut in from his other flank, blunt and immediate.

Leon: "And me."

Cael blinked at him.

Leon: "Not as a system. As me."

A ghost of a smile touched Cael's mouth.

Cael: "Leon Varek. Loud protection."

Leon scowled.

Leon: "Disciplined protection."

Cassian: "Occasionally disciplined."

Rhea's voice carried from the command threshold.

President Valmont: "Sufficiently disciplined for entry."

Leon muttered something under his breath that sounded like a man refusing to argue with official praise.

On paper, the exercise was simple: extract three simulated wounded markers to the safe zone while hostile procedural waves tried to force single-leader command dependency. In practice, every corner of the square asked the same quiet question: *who decides?*

Leon spotted the first wounded marker pinned beneath a collapsed stone arch and moved before I could open my mouth.

Leon: "Water barrier left. Cassian, melt that ice seam. Aira, shout if the paving stones start lying."

Aira: "They already are, though I appreciate being consulted."

Cassian ran a razor-thin ribbon of fire along the frost seam without scorching the marker or the stone. That restraint alone deserved a commendation. High on the Green rail, Mira scribbled so furiously her pencil squeaked across the gallery.

Leon hauled the first marker into the safe zone.

The second pressure wave aimed directly at Rhea. A silver notice flared across her sightline.

**COMMAND AUTHORITY CONFLICT**

**STUDENT COUNCIL PRESIDENT MAY REMOVE UNCLASSIFIED SUBJECT FOR SAFETY**

Rhea did not break stride.

President Valmont: "Rejected. Safety is not solitude."

Her red seal split into three smaller emblems, assigning independent extraction lanes to the healers. The field chimed acceptance.

The third wave dropped onto Elira.

Sunlight drained from the white avenue. Every silver-glass pane along the storefronts flipped into my reflection: not Haru Aerlen in an academy coat, but a hollow black silhouette wearing thin Green Circle light like borrowed skin.

Elira stood alone at the center of the square while dozens of dark reflections multiplied around her.

**PRIMARY WITNESS PRESSURE**

**STEP AWAY TO PRESERVE RESTRAINT**

The dark crystal beneath her collar snapped cold. I took one involuntary step forward.

Elira raised a hand behind her back without turning her head. *Stop.*

She did not need to speak it. I planted my boot and held.

She squared her shoulders to the glass.

Elira: "No."

Pressure slammed down across the flagstones. The pale frost mark bloomed sharp under her collar again. Elira's fingers curled tight at her sides, yet her voice carried steady across the square.

Elira: "If restraint only survives because love leaves the room, it is not restraint. It is abandonment with better manners."

Silver script flashed across the pavement.

**PRIMARY WITNESS AUTONOMY: ESTABLISHED**

A breath of relief rolled along the witness rail. Too early.

One reflection in the nearest shopfront did not vanish. It smiled.

Lucien's voice drifted smooth through the glass.

Lucien: "Beautifully said."

Elira's pendant went dead silent.

The smiling reflection lifted one gloved hand.

Lucien: "Now let us learn whether it can bleed."

Every window in the square snapped pure black.

The Foundation emergency bell tolled once overhead, a heavy iron boom that did not shut the field down. It warned that something unauthorized had stepped inside while every negotiated rule stayed technically intact.

Instinct hit the square all at once. Leon pivoted toward Cael; fire jumped across Cassian's forearms; Rhea stepped forward to kill the session; inside my chest, an ugly, clean certainty urged me to flatten the whole square until nothing inside it remembered how to threaten anyone. Knowing that urge was not protection did not make it quieter.

Dozens of Lucien's reflections watched us from every black pane, each angled a hair differently, turning the bright plaza into a jagged trap of mirrors.

Rhea slammed her red seal into the paving stones.

President Valmont: "Council emergency termination."

The field ignored her. Her red seal split against the perimeter wall and recoiled across the stone with pale frost crusting its edges.

Rhea's face did not move a millimeter. That was how I knew she was furious.

President Valmont: "Unauthorized Noctis interference entered into official record."

Mira's voice rang down from the public rail, high and unsteady and refusing to drop.

Mira: "Recorded! Reflections are not witnesses!"

Professor Valen answered instantly from the faculty rail.

Professor Valen: "Correct. Log that distinction."

Three Green Circle recorders echoed Mira from different sides of the gallery.

Mira: "East window shift!"

Student: "North facade darkening!"

Student: "Foundation line stutter at marker two!"

Aira spun on her heel, map flaring blue over her palm.

Aira: "Good! Keep calling coordinates, not feelings. Put feelings in a bucket until later."

Leon had already sprinted halfway down the east lane toward the second wounded marker. He kept his eyes off the glass.

Leon: "Cael, behind me."

Cael started after him, then froze mid-stride.

The black shopfront beside him had stopped showing the square. Inside the glass stretched a long Noctis training hall: pale rows of conduit students kneeling with both palms flat to icy stone, faceless instructors pacing behind them.

*Useful children do not tremble.*

Lucien did not speak the line. Cael's own memory supplied it. The healer seal on his wrist flickered violently.

Nera stepped right into his sightline, blocking the window with her shoulders.

Nera: "Name the room."

Cael's mouth parted. No breath came out.

Oric crowded in beside her, voice rough.

Oric: "Name the room, Cael."

Cael pressed his bandaged hand hard against his sternum.

Cael: "Aurelis procedural field. Lower council level. Public witnesses overhead."

A hairline fracture ran across the training-hall glass.

Leon vaulted a low planter, hooked the second wounded marker under one arm, and dragged it behind a stone ridge.

Leon: "Good. Keep going."

Cael forced every present fact out between shallow breaths.

Cael: "Aira holds the support mark. Rhea holds the red seal. Leon is in front of me. Nera is left. Oric is at the rail. Elira is not Noctis. Haru is not Noctis. I am not in the east hall."

The black pane nearest his elbow shifted to a younger version of his own hand pressed flat to stone. Cael squeezed his eyes shut.

Leon: "Eyes open."

Cael's eyes snapped open. Leon did not soften the order, and for that reason it cut right through the panic.

Leon: "You are here. Look at here. Annoying, isn't it?"

Cael's breath hitched once.

Leon: "Good. Annoyed means present."

Nera struck a crisp chalk countermark onto the paving stone beside his boots.

Nera: "That is not approved grounding protocol."

Leon: "It grounded him."

Nera paused half a beat.

Nera: "Objection withdrawn."

Oric called down from the rail.

Oric: "Noctis would never withdraw an objection that fast."

Nera: "I am practicing."

The exchange was ridiculous, and it put air back in Cael's lungs.

Back in the safe zone, the pavement split with thin black veins crawling toward the two extracted markers. Both faceless figures turned their heads in unison toward Elira, silent pressure radiating off the safe zone like an accusation.

Aira's map flashed warning runes across her palm.

Aira: "Rhea, the safe zone is flipping into a witness pressure reservoir."

Rhea's red seal sliced through the nearest black vein.

President Valmont: "Plain language."

Aira: "The field is using saved markers as moral hostages."

Leon: "That one I understood."

Cassian sent a low sheet of fire skimming across the cobblestones, scorching the surfacing black veins without touching the markers.

Cassian: "Permission to hate this architecture again?"

Professor Valen: "Granted."

Halden did not take his eyes off the black shopfronts.

Professor Halden: "Do not overcommit to the veins. They are secondary."

Nera caught the trap half a second later.

Nera: "He wants Elira to rush the safe zone and expose the pendant's priority order."

Elira heard her. I watched the set of her shoulders tighten as she forced herself to step *backward* instead of toward the threat. Every instinct in her had been forged to move into danger with enough gold light to settle it; refusing to charge when movement had been baited cost her visibly.

Elira: "He does not get to call restraint abandonment."

Aira glanced at the safe zone.

Aira: "Then somebody else covers them."

Leon moved without waiting for assignment.

Leon: "Water shelter on both markers."

Cassian shifted into step beside him.

Cassian: "Heat perimeter."

Rhea stamped her red seal over both weaves.

President Valmont: "Authorized."

Mira leaned over the gallery rail and shouted:

Mira: "Protection transferred by witness network, not primary witness!"

The black veins recoiled under the stone. Across the street, one single reflection of Lucien lost its smile. Only one. I kept track of that pane. Surprise was still on the table.

The glass did not care for being ignored. Every pane shifted from memory to cruel prophecy.

One window showed Leon turning half a step late as Cael slid backward into black water. Another showed Cassian's fire jumping the witness rail and turning the gallery into smoke and screams. A third showed Aira's blue map winding tight like a leash around my wrist.

The fourth showed Elira walking away down a bright hall while the Black Circle behind my ribs went obedient, hollow, and dead.

My gaze twitched toward that last pane. Only a fraction of an inch.

Aira caught it immediately.

Aira: "Haru, if a window shows you a neat answer, assume it is dressed for a funeral."

Haru: "Noted."

Soft laughter echoed out of the glass.

Lucien: "You train him well."

Aira's spine went stiff.

Aira: "He is not a spellbeast."

The shopfront beside her warped. Inside the black glass, a false Aira stood behind my shoulders with taut blue strings tied from her fingers into my spine.

Color drained from Aira's cheeks, chased instantly by white fury.

Aira: "Oh, that is cheap."

The reflection raised its fingers. I felt the field pushing that picture up toward the gallery, trying to sell the council rail Lucien's cleanest lie: Aira as handler, me as hidden weapon, every friendship reduced to wiring.

Aira marched up to the black pane.

President Valmont: "Aira."

Aira: "I know."

She planted her heels right at the rim of the glass, close enough that her breath misted the dark surface.

Aira: "My name is Aira Solen. I am a Blue Circle support specialist. I am bossy because everyone around me keeps making terrible choices under pressure, not because Haru Aerlen is pulling strings."

Inside the glass, the false threads pulled tighter.

Aira: "I befriended him before I knew there was anything worth hiding. I stayed because people do not become less worth protecting the moment they get complicated."

Her voice caught on *complicated*, and her jaw clenched in disgust at the crack.

Aira: "Show me as a puppet one more time and I will take it personally in ways your grammar cannot file."

A clean vertical crack split the window top to bottom.

Mira shouted from above:

Mira: "Aira Solen's autonomy statement renewed under active pressure!"

Cassian grinned without taking his eyes off his fire lane.

Cassian: "Terrifying. Inspiring. Slightly administrative."

Aira stepped back, hands shaking slightly. Elira moved up beside her, paused a beat until Aira gave the smallest nod, and laid one steady hand on her shoulder. The field recorded the touch without twisting it.

Lucien's surviving reflections smiled in unison.

Lucien: "Support specialist Solen. Always clever enough to turn terror into paperwork."

Aira lowered her eyes to the blue map spinning above her palm.

Aira: "Always irritating enough to keep villains talking while I read their wiring."

One reflection tilted its head.

Half a second later, the blue map in Aira's palm bled pure black. Hostile grammar surged for the lines linking her to the rest of us, trying to overwrite her role in the field: *helper, mask, limb*.

Aira slammed her free palm flat down over the darkened map.

Aira: "No. You do not get to edit my map while I am holding it."

The word struck like an iron nail. Her Blue Circle flared hard enough to burn the black threads back off her knuckles.

Aira: "I am the person who noticed your relay spacing is far too clean to be improvised. Rhea, he is not forcing the field from outside. He planted a grammar route inside the activity terms before the gates even opened."

Nera whipped around toward the black glass.

Nera: "Not a shard."

Oric: "A courtesy clause."

President Valmont: "I explicitly banned Noctis relay shards."

Nera's voice went razor-flat.

Nera: "He hid it inside the acceptance phrasing."

Lucien's reflection clapped once without a sound.

Lucien: "I did accept your conditions."

Elira stood in the center of the street, gold mana drawn tight around her shoulders like cold sunrise.

Elira: "You hid a blade inside the word *accept*."

Lucien: "No. I let Aurelis prove it still does not read Dominion carefully enough."

Every pane along the east facade shifted focus toward her throat. Beneath her collar, the dark pendant gave no pulse at all. That silence scared me worse than the black glass. Elira felt it too, yet she refused to reach for the chain. She lifted her chin.

Elira: "Then we learn faster."

At the far end of the plaza, the third wounded marker materialized on the cobblestones.

It wore Cael's face.

Leon swore aloud. Cassian's fire snapped sharp crimson. Beside Nera, Cael's chest locked mid-breath.

Up in the neutral gallery, a woman in dark grey robes bearing a silver dragon crest rose to her feet. She had sat so still until now that the motion felt like steel clearing a scabbard. Mara Kest, combat observer for Drakenshard Martial Academy, a fourth voice bound to neither Valemere, Noctis, nor Aurelis.

Mara: "Mara Kest. Drakenshard combat observer. Formal objection."

Lucien's reflections angled upward toward the iron-gray banner.

Mara: "Using a living witness's likeness as extraction bait is psychological torture, not evaluation."

Lucien: "Honor is a luxury for those who can afford to lose."

Mara's jaw hardened. Her thumb came to rest against her sword guard, warning rather than threat.

Mara: "Honor is what keeps victory from rotting into cruelty."

Drakenshard's observer seal flared iron-gray along the rail.

Two sections over, Soren Ashvale rose in Valemere's white-and-blue trim.

Soren: "Valemere seconds Drakenshard's objection."

The Foundation bell struck a single clear note. The false Cael flickered along its shoulders. It did not vanish, yet the field was forced to tag the likeness as contested before it could leverage him.

Across the rail, panic broke into disciplined pieces. Green Circle recorders scratched out hasty first lines and rewrote them neatly. Blue Circle observers began plotting coordinates until Aira barked that the body was bait. One Yellow Circle professor surged halfway from his bench before Professor Valen's hand clamped firm over his sleeve.

Professor Valen: "Do not dignify the illusion by lunging toward it."

The professor stared at her, shaken.

Professor Valen: "Sit down and record why you wanted to."

She did not blink. Slowly, he picked up his pen. That was Valen's kind of combat: quiet, unblinking, merciless toward evasion.

Rhea widened her red seal across the gallery rail to keep shock from spilling onto the field floor.

President Valmont: "All observers remain witnesses, not participants, unless authorized."

Mira's voice cut clear from the Green benches:

Mira: "Public record confirms false likeness deployed without consent!"

Aira jabbed a finger skyward without turning her head.

Aira: "Good. More of that."

Mira looked terrified by the praise and kept writing anyway.

Lucien's voice lowered into smooth, conversational warmth across thirty black panes.

Lucien: "There is your autonomy proof. Pick your order. Extract the marker. Shield Cael. Hide Haru. Guard Elira. Protect the record. Every virtue you stacked around him, called due at the exact same second."

Thirty pairs of pale eyes locked onto me.

Lucien: "Tell me, Haru Aerlen. What do you save first when the room refuses to arrange itself politely?"

The false Green crest glowed thin and fragile around my wrist beneath my cuff.

I could see the entire architecture laid bare: every pressure line braided through Noctis's acceptance wording, every false casualty, every hidden trigger tucked beneath the white paving stones. Lucien had coiled his clause so deep inside the Foundation's own autonomy prompts that the field could no longer tell external sabotage from legal procedure.

I could end all of it in half a breath.

Deep behind my ribs, the hidden rings of Black Circle ground once in opposite directions. Cold silver constellations scraped against bone. The taste of ozone and bitter iron flooded my mouth; under my cuff, the sleeve seam scorched hot against my wrist as the disguise fought to hold while reality leaned toward my hands, waiting for one word to snap the square open.

I locked my teeth and swallowed the pressure back down until my ribs ached like bruised glass.

Ahead of me, Elira glanced back over her shoulder. She did not look afraid of what I could break. She looked afraid of what holding it back was costing me.

Elira: "Haru."

One word. It anchored me back inside my own skin. I did not break the field. I spoke instead.

Haru: "Aira. The body wearing Cael's face is false, yet the extraction line behind it is real. The third marker sits beneath the north fountain shadow."

Aira's eyes darted to her blue map and locked on.

Aira: "Leon, ignore the face. North fountain shadow. Cassian, wall off the pretty lie."

Leon did not hesitate for half a second. He sprinted right past the false Cael without sparing it a second glance. Beside Nera, Cael watched him run past the illusion, and something tight in his chest finally let go.

Cassian threw a controlled wall of fire between the false likeness and the gallery rail, blinding the trap without scorching a single Foundation line.

Cassian: "I am growing as a person, and I deeply resent the timing."

Aira: "Resent later. Left anchor now."

Rhea drove her red seal into the north fountain basin. Silver water parted, and the true third marker surfaced into Leon's waiting arms.

Mira leaned far over the Green rail.

Mira: "Third marker confirmed! Haru gave location, not command!"

Aira pointed up without looking away from her map.

Aira: "Mira, you absolute menace, write that twice."

Every smiling reflection of Lucien went blank. Only for one heartbeat.

Then thirty black panes snapped off me and locked onto Elira.

Lucien: "Very good."

It was not praise. It was recalculation.

The air temperature plummeted. Breath plumed white across the square. Frost leapt up from beneath Elira's collar, clawing sharply toward her jaw. Her dark pendant finally flared, too slow and too late, flashing black, then dull gold, then dead.

Elira's knees buckled.

I surged forward before thought caught up. So did Cael. So did Nera, Rhea, and Leon. Half the square moved in the same breath, keeping me from reaching her alone.

Aira saw the pale thread strike for Elira's throat.

Aira: "Compatibility pressure is a severance probe! It is not attacking her body. It is forcing the field to register her as Haru's bound anchor!"

Outside the ring, Professor Halden shouted louder than I had ever heard him raise his voice.

Professor Halden: "Do not accept the term!"

Professor Valen's gold-stitched folio snapped open across her palms.

Professor Valen: "Elira Vale, state your own grounds!"

Elira pressed one trembling hand against her collarbone as frost bit the line of her jaw. Her voice tore through the cold.

Elira: "I stand here because Cael deserves protection. Because Haru deserves witnesses. Because I chose both before any system labeled me useful!"

Stone shuddered under our boots. The black window nearest her spiderwebbed with silver cracks.

Elira: "I am not his anchor because I love him!"

Her Yellow Circle blazed bright gold against the dark glass.

Elira: "I love him because I know what he chooses when power would let him turn cruel!"

The dark crystal at her throat flared once, fierce and unmistakable, and shattered the frost off her skin.

Every black window around the plaza exploded outward into harmless dark dust. The emergency bell died mid-swing. The entire Foundation square locked still, silver text burning overhead.

**AUTONOMY PROOF: ESTABLISHED**

**UNAUTHORIZED NOCTIS INTERFERENCE: RECORDED**

For one heartbeat, it looked like victory.

Then the Foundation etched a third line beneath the first two.

**DOMINION SEVERANCE GRAMMAR IDENTIFIED**

Nera let out a sharp breath as though someone had driven an elbow into her ribs. Oric caught the stone wall with one hand. Cael whipped his head between them.

Cael: "What?"

Nera's voice dropped almost to a whisper.

Nera: "That probe was not the spell."

Near the north fountain, one jagged shard of black glass still clung to the stone rim. Lucien's face smiled up through the crack.

Lucien: "No."

His gaze slid slowly from Elira's pale throat over to Cael.

Lucien: "It was the measurement."

The shard went black and crumbled.

The Foundation field dissolved around us. Healers poured down the steps immediately. This time Elira did not refuse them, sitting still while silver light traced the fading crescent below her jaw, which told me far more than any reassurance would have.

Against the far stone pillar, Nera and Oric argued in urgent, clipped murmurs. Fragments reached me across the cooling square: *Dominion Severance. Primary witness. Anchor inversion. Fatal if completed.*

Cael stood two paces from them, motionless. With every technical word they spoke, fear gave way to something heavier across his face: recognition, as though a buried Noctis lesson had just opened its eyes inside his head.

Later, after Rhea sealed the official transcript and wrapped half the council tower in emergency wards, I found Cael in the quiet corridor outside the healer suite.

The hall had been cleared twice and still felt crowded. Faint red council wards ran along the skirting boards beside blue support lines and soft gold privacy seals. Every few minutes, a notice crystal overhead refreshed the same three lines in shifting order:

**UNAUTHORIZED INTERFERENCE RECORDED**

**DOMINION SEVERANCE GRAMMAR UNDER REVIEW**

**PRIMARY WITNESS MEDICAL CHECK ACTIVE**

Rhea had scrubbed Elira's name from the public ticker. The omission felt protective and suffocating at the same time.

Cael stood directly beneath the center crystal, reading the rotating lines while his wrapped hand opened and closed at his side. Without Noctis delegation insignia on his collar, he should have looked freer. Instead he looked unfinished, as though someone had peeled off his label and left no clue about what belonged underneath.

Leon was missing only because Rhea had ordered him to eat. Leon had protested; Rhea had informed him he could guard Cael with low blood sugar or obey his president and stay upright later. Leon had left looking personally betrayed by nutrition, buying Cael three unsupervised minutes, which Cael was spending blaming himself with practiced efficiency.

Cael spoke without turning when my footsteps stopped.

Cael: "I know what he is building."

Haru: "Dominion Severance?"

Cael nodded, fingers laced tight enough to whiten his knuckles.

Cael: "It splits a protected witness from the person they guard by making the bond register as unlawful influence."

Haru: "We will stop it."

He turned to me, exhaustion sitting behind his eyes like something ten years too old for his face.

Cael: "Noctis taught us the answer to failed severance."

Cold settled behind my sternum again.

Haru: "Cael."

He glanced toward the closed healer door where Elira sat inside.

Cael: "They called it Votive Return."

Haru: "No."

The refusal snapped out too sharp, too close to a command. Cael did not flinch this time. That unsettled me worse than when he did.

Cael: "I did not say I wanted to use it."

He met my eyes evenly.

Cael: "I said I know it."

Inside the healer room, Elira laughed softly at something Aira muttered. The sound drifted under the door. Cael listened like a man hearing proof that one corner of the world had not spoiled.

When he spoke again, his voice was barely above a breath.

Cael: "They saved me first."

I had no clean sentence to offer him, so I stepped up beside him against the wall and stayed until neither of us was standing alone.

Leon reappeared before the third minute turned into four, holding half a bread roll and wearing the expression of a man who had lost a philosophical debate to carbohydrates. He halted at the corner.

Leon: "Good. You found him."

Cael dropped his gaze.

Cael: "I was not hiding."

Leon: "Did not say you were."

He tore off a fierce bite of the roll, chewed, and propped his shoulder against the opposite stone. For a silent stretch, the three of us watched **DOMINION SEVERANCE GRAMMAR UNDER REVIEW** flicker overhead. Repetition did not make the words kinder.

Leon swallowed.

Leon: "First time I lost to Haru, I spent two solid days deciding he had cheated."

Cael blinked up at him, thrown by the sharp turn. Leon kept his eyes on the crystal.

Leon: "It was easier than admitting I had been wrong about what strength looks like."

Haru: "Leon."

Leon: "Let me finish before I turn wise and hate myself for it."

The corner of Cael's mouth twitched.

Leon: "Noctis told you being spent was what you were built for. That does not become true just because believing it is simpler than figuring out what comes next."

Cael stared at the warded flagstones.

Cael: "What comes next?"

Leon took another bite of roll.

Leon: "No clue. Apparently you get stuck with people who tell you when you are being an idiot. Very invasive."

Cael's faint smile came back, and this time it stayed an extra second.

The healer suite latch clicked open. Aira stepped out first, scanned the three of us lined up like sentries, and pointed at Leon's hand.

Aira: "Is that dinner?"

Leon: "Tactical bread."

Aira: "I am far too tired to litigate that."

Elira walked out behind her, pale, the fading crescent high under her collar, standing on her own two feet. Cael straightened automatically, as if caught breaking a rule by worrying.

Elira crossed over to him before anyone could speak.

Elira: "I am still here."

Pain flickered around Cael's eyes before he clamped it back down.

Cael: "I know."

Elira looked at him steadily, letting no Noctis logic slip between the words.

Elira: "Then hear the rest of it too. Lucien measured a weapon today. He did not measure your worth."

Cael closed his eyes. For the first time since entering Aurelis, he did not apologize.

A minute later, Rhea arrived down the corridor with a sealed docket under her arm. She checked Elira first, then Cael, then the glowing crystal overhead.

President Valmont: "Noctis has already filed a terminology review."

Aira turned slowly.

Aira: "Terminology."

President Valmont: "They object to entering *Dominion Severance* as hostile doctrine before full procedural verification."

Cassian, arriving half a pace behind Rhea, stared.

Cassian: "They tried to make her pendant bleed."

President Valmont: "Their preferred phrase is *unverified compatibility reaction*."

Silence dropped over the corridor, heavy and sharp.

Then Mira, clutching her green public copy half a step behind Rhea, spoke up.

Mira: "No."

It was not loud, yet every head turned. Rhea looked down at her. Mira swallowed hard and held her ground.

Mira: "Sorry. I mean, the public witness copy rejects that wording. We saw thirty windows lock onto Elira before her pendant reacted. We logged Lucien calling it a measurement. We logged Nera naming Severance grammar."

Aira stared at her in pure awe.

Aira: "Mira Castellan, did you just procedurally tell Noctis to choke on a euphemism?"

Mira went scarlet.

Mira: "Hopefully not in those exact words."

Rhea took the green public copy and pressed her red seal right beside it.

President Valmont: "Those exact words are unnecessary. The objection stands."

Leon nudged Cael lightly with his elbow.

Leon: "See? Other people can refuse rotten definitions too."

Cael looked across at Mira.

Cael: "Thank you."

Mira hugged the folio tight against her chest.

Mira: "You're welcome."

Then, quieter, eyes still fixed on the notice crystal:

Mira: "I am tired of them naming things for everyone else."

## Coda: Terminology

Mira copied Noctis's preferred phrase onto a fresh sheet exactly once: *Unverified compatibility reaction.*

The words sat across the vellum like smooth frost over a grave. She stared at them until quiet anger steadied her wrist, then wrote what her own eyes had seen directly underneath.

*Windows turned toward Elira before impact. Pendant answered as mapped target. Frost followed a carved route, not an accident.*

At the next library table, three Green Circle students leaned over without pretending to study. One of them, her older brother Finn, with the same stubborn auburn curl and dark ink across both thumbs, tapped the margin and whispered, "Can we sign that?"

Mira glanced up at him, surprised. Green Circle students at Aurelis did not sign formal filings unless a higher rank invited them to hold the pen. It was not written in any rulebook, which made it worse; it was a habit everyone called manners.

Mira slid the page into the center of the table.

Mira: "Only if you write what you saw, not what I wrote."

By sunset, seven crooked witness copies sat side by side. None matched word for word, which made them far harder to erase.

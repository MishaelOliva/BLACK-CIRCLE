# Episode 8: **The Autonomy Proof**

At sunset, the Foundation Trial Hall filled again.

This time, Aurelis brought statements. Not speeches: proofs. Speeches asked for applause; proofs only needed to survive being hated.

Rhea placed each statement in its own witness ring instead of stacking them on the Aurelis platform. The sheets floated at uneven heights beneath thin red seals so nobody could mistake them for a single argument written by one hand.

Leon's hung low and blunt near Cael's protection mark. Cassian's smoked faintly at the corners where he had insisted on signing with a fire-warmed stylus. Rhea's stood upright and severe. Halden's three lines floated apart from the faculty rail. Mira's gallery proof appeared as uneven copies marked in different hands, errors left intact. Beside the Noctis timing ledger hung Darian Thorne's preserved midnight line in crisp silver script: *I am in witness. Which is older than procedure.*

Nera and Oric stood together between the Aurelis floor and the Noctis rail, claiming neither side cleanly. Cael stood near the center under two healer seals and one council mark. Elira stood beside me. Never behind.

The silver pool brightened as the pages settled.

**AUTONOMY MATERIAL RECEIVED**

Lucien surveyed the spread of pages and smiled with his mouth only.

Lucien: "How industrious."

Aira stepped onto the support mark with one fresh pencil behind her ear, two more tucked in her cuff, and the look of someone prepared to tie the room up in rules until dawn.

Aira: "We call it preparation when nobody gets hurt."

Lucien's eyes drifted to Cael's healer seal.

Lucien: "No one?"

Aira: "Less than if you planned it."

His gaze lingered on the glowing seal longer than the injury beneath it, counting care as an uncollected debt. Up in the gallery, pens moved fast. Lucien noticed and stepped onto the center stone before Rhea could open the floor to witnesses.

Lucien: "Before Aurelis puts on a show of sincerity, I ask the court to name the claim behind these statements."

The pool darkened around his reflection. Rhea raised her red seal.

President Valmont: "State the premise."

Lucien turned slowly, giving the gallery his clean profile.

Lucien: "Mercy without authority is governance."

Nobody spoke. He let the silence work before continuing.

Lucien: "Haru Aerlen interfered in sanctioned combat. He broke up a match governed by field rules, Circle rank, delegation authority, and tournament law. Now Aurelis wants this hall to believe that every witness here chose on their own to shield the same student, defend the same interference, keep the same secret, and fight the same rank review."

His gaze snapped to me.

Lucien: "Convenient."

The word slid through the chamber like frost.

Lucien: "Either Haru Aerlen is a Green Circle student whose private mercy overruled every recognized authority present, or he carries an authority this court has not named. If the first is true, Aurelis defends chaos. If the second is true, these statements are not autonomy at all. They are orbit."

Beside me, Elira stayed still.

Lucien: "So let us stop asking whether these witnesses care. Caring is easy to stage after a spectacle. Let us ask whether they can oppose Haru Aerlen. Let us ask whether their judgment exists when it does not flatter him."

The Foundation pool wrote one line.

**COUNTER-PREMISE ACCEPTED FOR ANSWER**

The trap bit deep because it touched something real. I had hidden behind their trust, even when fear had forced the choice. The answer could never be that I was harmless. I was not.

Beneath my sleeve, the false Green crest pinched cold against my wrist, flickering as the Black Circle stirred behind my ribs. Air pressure tightened around my knuckles like coiled wire waiting for a single excuse to snap.

If I stayed quiet and let everyone else step between me and Lucien's argument, I would prove his argument by standing still.

I forced my fingers open, pinned the cold back down beneath my cuff, and stepped forward to the edge of the center ring.

Every eye in the chamber shifted. Elira's breath caught once, then steadied.

Haru: "You are right about one thing."

Lucien's eyebrow lifted a fraction.

Haru: "None of the people in this room owe me obedience. None of them signed on to shield what I am."

I looked straight across the silver pool at him, then up toward the Foundation faculty rail.

Haru: "I do not claim authority over Cael Nox, Elira Vale, or anyone else holding a page in this chamber. If Noctis wants a rank inquiry into what I am, put it on me. I will stand for the Foundation's review myself, on one condition."

Lucien smiled, smelling blood.

Lucien: "How generous, to negotiate terms from the dock."

Haru: "One condition. Every witness here stays free to speak, walk out, or testify against me without Noctis custody or retaliation hanging over their heads. If you want to prove they are my orbit, stop threatening the people who would leave it. Put the inquiry on me. Leave their choices off your ledger."

Silence hit the hall harder than a spell. Under my sleeve, the false Green glow pulsed once against my wrist and held steady.

Lucien recovered fast.

Lucien: "How tidy to volunteer once your shields are arranged."

Rhea's red seal struck the rail before he could spin the room back.

President Valmont: "Recorded. Subject waives personal delay under active witness freedom."

The pool flashed once beneath my boots.

**SUBJECT WAIVER LOGGED: CONDITIONAL ON UNCOERCED WITNESSES**

Rhea swept her gaze across our witnesses, cool and merciless.

President Valmont: "Now answer for yourselves. And answer badly. No scripts, no neat sequence. Lucien will call polish rehearsal and hesitation corruption. Let the truth stumble where it stumbles."

Leon moved first, shoulders stiff, hands visibly empty.

Leon: "I do not follow Haru."

Lucien: "A stirring start from the boy who now stands behind him in every room."

Heat climbed Leon's neck.

Leon: "I was not finished. On Haru's first day, I tried to humiliate him because his Circle was Green and mine was Blue. He did not charm that out of me. I was wrong in public and stayed wrong longer than I should have."

He looked at Cael, then back at Lucien.

Leon: "If Haru tells me to lie, I refuse. If he tells me to hand Cael back because protecting him turns inconvenient, I refuse. If he becomes what Voss claims he is, I stand against him."

Lucien: "You enjoy becoming noble after being corrected."

Leon's fists curled, caught the blue flash of Cael's healer seal at the edge of his sight, and opened again.

Leon: "No. I enjoy being less wrong than yesterday."

**PERSONAL MOTIVE DISTINCT FROM SUBJECT: ACCEPTED**

Cassian crossed into the ring before Rhea called him. His red heat clipped Leon's blue trace, and the witness mark gave an irritated hum.

Cassian: "Good. It hates sharing."

Aira: "Please stop antagonizing the ancient legal mechanism."

Cassian's grin dropped as his eyes found Cael.

Cassian: "I follow plans while they are good. Haru's bad plans are annoying because he goes quiet instead of shouting, which is why I keep furniture within reach."

Haru: "You are not throwing furniture at me."

Cassian: "See? Independent judgment."

A few startled laughs escaped the gallery.

Cassian: "In the Crown Field, I moved because Cael was breaking and the field was lying about it. If Haru had told me to leave the boy there, we would have had a short tactical disagreement and a long medical one."

Lucien: "Bravado is not autonomy."

Cassian: "No. It just makes forcing people work harder."

**INDEPENDENT COMBAT JUDGMENT: ACCEPTED**

Lucien glanced at both marks with cool dismissal.

Lucien: "Two boys defending the friend who made them matter."

Rhea did not step into the ring. She pressed her office seal straight onto the council rail.

President Valmont: "My protection order covers Cael Nox, Nera Vail, Oric Sen, Mira Castellan's public copies, and every student your delegation prefers to question alone. If Haru Aerlen asks me for an exception, I refuse and log the request."

Her seal flared deep red.

**OFFICE AUTHORITY DISTINCT FROM PERSONAL LOYALTY: ACCEPTED**

From the faculty tier, Professor Halden leaned forward before Lucien could speak.

Professor Halden: "I want to know what Haru Aerlen is badly enough that I would not trust myself alone with him in a locked testing room. I distrust any room where the most curious person in it is also the one holding the key."

Aira's pencil scratched furiously.

Professor Halden: "Do not make that sound noble, Solen."

Aira: "I am logging *self-aware academic hazard*."

Halden looked deeply offended. The pool accepted him anyway.

**FACULTY RESTRAINT: AUTONOMOUS EVIDENCE**

Mira came down the steps from the gallery clutching three battered copies against her chest, one stained with a tea ring, one written in two inks, the third split down the middle and mended with healer tape. Two paces behind her, Finn Castellan shadowed her to the edge of the lower rail with a fresh stack of blanks tucked under his arm, watching the floor in case she stumbled.

Mira stopped just outside the ring.

Mira: "Do I have to stand in it?"

Rhea: "No."

Mira stepped inside anyway, frightened enough that the taped page rattled between her fingers.

Mira: "On Haru's first day, the instructor knew every Red and Blue name. Three Green students were called *support row*. I wrote their names because I was angry. Haru did not ask. I still do not know most of what he is."

Lucien: "Yet being noticed by him pleased you."

Mira swallowed hard, her fingers tightening around the taped edge of the torn sheet.

Mira: "Yes."

The admission stung, and she kept it anyway.

Mira: "Being noticed matters when people practice not seeing you. That does not make what I saw false."

She laid the three copies along the silver rim.

Mira: "If Haru hurts someone, write it. If he lies, write it. If he saves someone and powerful people call it disorder because the wrong rank moved first, write that too. Pages disappear before powerful people admit a person did."

Along the bottom margin, half a dozen uneven hands had signed the same line:

**WE SAW ENOUGH TO WRITE**

The pool hesitated longer over her pages than over Leon's. Mira stared at the silver water looking half sick until the verdict surfaced:

**PUBLIC RECORD MOTIVE: DISTINCT**

She exhaled and nearly dropped the stack as she backed toward the rail. Finn reached out smoothly and caught the bottom edge without taking the pages from her, guiding her back toward the Green Circle section.

Nera walked forward before her name was called.

Nera: "My statement is technical. Noctis field readings confirm the relay dropped before Cael Nox collapsed, under dark pressure well past normal surrender limits."

Lucien cut straight across her before she could reach her second line.

Lucien: "You memorized Aurelis's phrasing well."

Her voice vanished. Not from magic; the old training simply locked her throat shut in front of him.

Oric stepped directly beside her. He did not touch her sleeve. He set the original Noctis timing sheet flat on the rail between them, right beneath Darian Thorne's floating silver line.

Oric: "Test it."

Lucien's eyes shifted to him.

Oric: "Noctis holds the exact same sheet, the same channel map, the same missing seven seconds. And Darian Thorne's witness entry is already in the pool. If Nera altered a single mark, produce the clean copy."

Behind Lucien, the Noctis delegation stayed motionless. Oric waited, refusing the exit he had spent years measuring.

Oric: "I am speaking without authorization too."

The pool darkened around the Noctis crest.

**CORRECTION NOT PRODUCED**

Nera's voice came back rough and stripped of polish.

Nera: "Noctis calls useful fear discipline. That means no one under Noctis's control can give clean testimony on whether fear was used."

Her hands trembled against her sleeves as the pool logged her words.

**TECHNICAL DISSENT: CREDIBLE / RETALIATION RISK ACTIVE**

Cael stepped forward before anyone could let Nera's fear end the moment. His healer seal flashed warning blue; Leon moved half a step and caught himself so abruptly his boot scraped stone.

Cael looked straight at Lucien, pale and unsteady, yet upright.

Cael: "You keep asking whether Haru made them stay. Nobody at Noctis ever asked what made me say yes. They hurt me until yes was faster."

Nera closed her eyes.

Cael: "Here, Leon asked before touching me. Rhea asked where I wanted the guard. Maelin asks questions I hate and waits for answers. Haru never asked me to make him look safe."

His breath hitched.

Cael: "I need them."

A sharp flicker of triumph crossed Lucien's frost outline. Cael saw it and did not blink.

Cael: "Need does not make them owners. It means I am hurt."

The hall went dead quiet.

Cael: "If I leave, I want it to be my door. If I stay, I want it to be my chair. I do not want Noctis owning the question just because I needed help answering it."

Silver light rose around him in a thin vertical sheet.

**WITNESS AUTONOMY: PARTIAL ESTABLISHMENT**

Lucien: "Partial."

Elira stood.

Rhea had not called her. The chamber opened automatically for Yellow Circle rank, and Elira ignored the center mark completely to stand directly beside Cael.

Elira: "I love Haru."

Every pen in the gallery paused except Aira's.

Elira: "Yesterday I told him to stop deciding for me. He stopped. This morning I asked him to stay. He stayed."

She turned her clear gaze onto Lucien.

Elira: "If your doctrine cannot tell those two choices apart, it is not measuring freedom. It is punishing closeness."

Then she looked across the floor at me.

Elira: "If Haru decides for me again, I will oppose him again. In private if he listens. In public if he does not."

Haru: "You should."

It was not the romance the gallery wanted. It was infinitely stronger.

Aira walked her messy stack down to the support ring. Leon had crossed out *rival* once and left it in twice; Cassian had sketched a chair in his margin; Mira's copies disagreed on two words; Nera had corrected Oric's timestamp; Cael's draft broke mid-sentence where his breathing had failed and resumed on a fresh line.

Aira: "Witness summary: motives do not match, accounts openly disagree, four witnesses name the lines where they would stand against Haru Aerlen, and two already have. No single person is giving orders here."

Lucien: "You have made dependence look crowded."

Rhea: "No. We have kept ownership out of any one pair of hands."

The Foundation pool did not flare. It grew heavy, pulling every witness ring down toward its surface.

**AUTONOMY NETWORK: PROVISIONALLY ESTABLISHED**

**IMMEDIATE NOCTIS RECLAMATION CLAIM: DENIED**

**OPENING-BRACKET WITNESS RECORD: PRESERVED**

**TARGETED PRESSURE REVIEW: STILL REQUIRED**

Cael could not be dragged back to Noctis tonight, and our bracket result stood intact. What remained untested was whether the group would survive a direct strike.

Lucien stepped forward.

Lucien: "There."

That single word dropped the temperature across the stone. Rhea's eyes narrowed.

President Valmont: "On what grounds?"

Lucien did not look at Elira's face. He looked straight at the hollow beneath her collar where the black pendant rested.

Lucien: "Compatibility pressure."

The tournament projection flashed back behind my eyes: **COMPATIBILITY PRESSURE POINT: ELIRA VALE**.

Lucien's smile was razor-calm.

Lucien: "If this network is autonomous, pull the emotional anchor out of the center and see whether Haru Aerlen's restraint holds."

Elira did not flinch. Inside, my chest went ice-cold.

The pool answered in older, heavier script:

**REQUEST LOGGED**

**PRIMARY WITNESS PRESSURE REVIEW: PENDING**

Beneath Elira's collar, the pendant went violently cold.

White frost bloomed along the edge of her dark fabric, sharp enough that she winced in full view of the gallery.

A private gift became public evidence in half a second. The whole chamber saw that something bound Elira's skin to my power, something that could be named, pressured, and pulled.

The silver pool held the magnified reflection of her frost-marked collar overhead for three full breaths after Rhea slammed her red seal onto the floor.

President Valmont: "Medical privacy challenge! Remove exposure from public display!"

The projection dimmed, three breaths too late. Faculty had already seen. Students were already whispering. Noctis now knew the pendant was neither ornament nor ordinary charm.

Lucien bowed, genuinely satisfied. He had not broken us; he had found the exact seam he planned to tear next.

Lucien: "Protect everything, President."

His eyes lingered once more on the white rime at Elira's throat.

Lucien: "It makes the eventual exception easier to spot."

The pendant pulsed cold between us. Neither of us stepped back.

Halden came down from the faculty rail before anyone else moved, his expression shut like an iron gate.

Professor Halden: "Autonomy established. Privacy damaged."

Rhea closed her eyes once, then opened them.

President Valmont: "Recorded."

Aira stepped sharply to Elira's other side, shielding her from the nearest gallery tier with her own shoulder while spreading ledger sheets across her forearm.

Aira: "Public record correction. The frost appeared after Lucien Voss called for an emotional-anchor test, not before. Log the exact sequence before rumor rewrites it."

Elira's voice came out tight and low.

Elira: "Thank you."

Aira did not soften her expression.

Aira: "Hold still until Maelin clears your collar."

Halden stopped directly in front of me.

Professor Halden: "Do not answer his next move alone."

Haru: "I was not planning to."

He looked down at my left hand. Only then did I realize my fingers had clenched so hard that the false Green Circle under my cuff was distorting at the edges, bleeding pale static across my wrist. Halden saw it and chose not to name it.

Professor Halden: "Plans are often weaker than reflex."

Elira's fingers brushed my sleeve.

Elira: "He is right."

I pried my hand open. The Green crest steadied by a hair.

Across the floor, Cael stared at Elira's collar with old guilt already tightening his shoulders. Leon caught his arm's shadow before a word could leave Cael's mouth.

Leon: "Do not even start."

Cael looked up, jaw clenched.

Cael: "He aimed at her because of my hearing."

Leon: "He aimed at her because he lost the custody vote. Pick something more useful than guilt."

Cael breathed out slowly, eyes going hard as he looked toward the empty Noctis side.

Cael: "Anger."

Leon gave one sharp nod.

Leon: "Keep that."

The Foundation rings dimmed and released us. Nobody rushed the doors.

At the lower rail, Mira stayed pressed over her Green Circle copies with Finn beside her. On the western glass, Talen's relay stayed open past Valemere's cutoff, his mother sitting close while Soren Ashvale stood behind Talen's chair with one hand resting steadily on the wooden back. Nera and Oric stayed beside Cael's ring until the silver trace faded beneath their boots. Lucien had already left the chamber.

Rhea looked at the empty Noctis platform, then across the witnesses who remained.

President Valmont: "No one leaves alone."

Nobody argued.

Elira walked beside me out of the hall without taking my hand until I saw the faint tremor in her fingers. I opened my palm beside hers without grabbing. She slid her hand into mine.

Beneath her collar, the pendant pulsed cold again, carrying no message I could read: only a pull forward, toward something none of us had named yet.

---

The correction table went up in the eastern stairwell corridor instead of a closed council room, narrow enough that only three people could crowd it at once.

Elira sat on the bottom step with Maelin's warming seal freshly set along her collar, her thumb checking the cuff of her sleeve whenever she thought nobody was looking. Nearby, Mira and two Green Circle students glued copies straight onto the temporary notice rail because glued paper left proof behind if anyone tore it down.

Aira laid three clean sheets across the narrow wood:

**WHAT WAS PROVEN**

**WHAT WAS DAMAGED**

**WHAT NO ONE GETS TO SHORTEN**

She read the first two aloud so hallway gossip could not twist them: Elira's independence accepted, Cael declared no one's property, Lucien's control claim thrown out; the privacy around Elira's pendant broken, and my own hidden disguise strained in front of the crowd.

When Aira's gaze flicked toward me on the last line, Elira stood before I could speak.

Elira: "Leave that line intact. And under the third heading, write this: the pendant is not proof that I am controlled. It is proof that someone expected the world to turn dangerous and tried, imperfectly, to answer without owning me."

She stopped, took Aira's cheap green-lacquered pen, crossed out **without owning me**, and wrote sharply above it:

**without making me an object.**

Then she looked down the corridor at Cael, who had been leaning silently against the stone beside Leon.

Elira: "And if anyone uses tonight's frost to claim rescue makes people dangerous to love, they can quote me accurately while being wrong."

Cael flinched once, pushed off the wall, and walked to the table. Leon let him go alone. Cael read Elira's line, took the chipped green pen from Aira, and wrote slowly with ink staining his fingers:

**A route opened through me. That does not make everyone who reached for me responsible for the trap.**

He paused, then added underneath:

**I am writing this before guilt edits it.**

From the archway, Nera let out a sharp breath close to a sob. Mira copied the words three times before Rhea stamped them as protected witness clarification.

Cael held the pen back out to Aira as if returning evidence before anyone could call borrowing a debt. Aira took it, then immediately pressed it back into his palm.

Aira: "Keep it until tomorrow. If you think of a correction at three in the morning, I refuse to let lack of supplies become institutional oppression."

Cael stared down at the chipped green lacquer, cheap enough that Noctis would have binned it after one use, and closed his fingers around it.

When we finally turned toward the dormitories, Elira held my hand openly in plain view of the stairwell. The frost along her collar stayed cold beneath Maelin's seal, yet the corridor behind us stayed covered in handwriting that refused to let the cold write the ending.

## Haru - Breakfast Without Evidence

The next morning, Cael kept Aira's pen through breakfast.

He set it beside his plate at the end of the table, parallel to his spoon, cap aligned with the wood seam. Every few minutes his fingers drifted near it and paused, checking that it was still there. Across from him, Leon stared at two slices of toast he had no intention of eating. Aira sat between them pretending to study a Council bulletin she was holding upside down.

Beside me, Elira warmed her palms around a mug of tea. Morning light through the tall dining windows caught the faint shadow of exhaustion under her left eye and the pale frost-burn along her collar. For one irrational second, I hated the sunlight for showing it so clearly.

Cael reached for the pen, hesitated, then picked it up. The cap came off with a tiny click.

Four students at the next bench went dead quiet. Cael's shoulders stiffened.

Aira lowered her upside-down notice.

Aira: "Anyone who treats stationery as a sacred relic before breakfast gets inventory duty with Professor Halden."

A second-year choked on juice. Conversation stumbled back into motion.

Cael wrote on the edge of his cloth napkin because a formal sheet felt too loud. The first word took longest.

**hungry**

Lowercase. No seal, no explanation: just a plain fact his body offered before Noctis could label hunger a weakness.

Leon saw the word and slid his untouched toast across the wood. Cael did not move. Leon froze, pulled his hand back, and swallowed.

Leon: "Sorry."

Cael looked from the toast to the pen.

Cael: "Ask."

Leon: "Do you want this?"

Cael waited a beat before answering.

Cael: "Half."

Leon tore the slice in two with exaggerated care and set one half beside the napkin without smudging the ink. Cael picked it up while keeping Aira's pen in his other hand. No silver pool lit under the floor; no council seal logged it. A boy just ate half a piece of toast he had asked for.

Lucien arrived while Cael was still holding the crust.

He did not enter like an invader. He walked in carrying a cup of black tea and the mild, reasonable smile of a senior student who belonged everywhere breakfast was served. Two Noctis aides trailed behind him, carefully avoiding eye contact with Cael.

Rhea's protection mark flared red at the east doors. Lucien stopped neatly outside it.

Lucien: "President Valmont, I am not here to file anything."

Rhea: "Then improve the morning by leaving."

Lucien: "That would make my absence far too theatrical."

He turned to Leon first, ignoring Elira and me completely.

Lucien: "Varek. You are beginning to understand performance."

Leon: "I was eating."

Lucien glanced at the nearly untouched plate.

Lucien: "Were you? You have discovered that standing beside damaged things can pass for character if you pick the right audience."

The half-eaten toast between Leon and Cael suddenly looked exposed. Cael's fingers tightened on the crust. Leon went white with fury, and I saw the hook a heartbeat too late: Lucien did not need Leon to believe him, only to flinch hard enough that Cael mistook anger for regret.

Elira set her teacup down cleanly on the saucer, giving the tension somewhere else to go.

Leon inhaled through his nose once, twice, picked up his own half of the toast, and took a sharp bite.

Leon: "I am still hungry."

Cael's grip eased by one finger. Lucien's eyes narrowed slightly.

Aira leaned back in her chair.

Aira: "Remarkable. Breakfast defeats rhetoric."

Lucien: "You mistake delay for defeat."

Aira: "Often. It keeps me cheerful."

Lucien's gaze settled on Cael at last. Cael did not hide the napkin. The lowercase word sat right beside the chipped green pen.

Lucien's polite tone turned razor-sharp.

Lucien: "Hungry is a useful word. It persuades people to feed the thing that may later bite them."

Cael stopped chewing. Air chilled around the bench. Beneath my sleeve, the Black Circle rolled cold against my wrist. Elira's hand found mine under the table and pressed once: *not yet*.

Leon set his toast down, voice low and dangerous.

Leon: "Cael is not a thing."

Lucien: "You improve remarkably quickly when watched."

Leon started to rise. Cael moved faster.

He uncapped Aira's pen and drew one clean line through **hungry**. Underneath it, in smaller, steadier script, he wrote:

**still hungry**

Then he finished the rest of his toast.

Nobody applauded. Three tables away, Mira copied the napkin hastily onto scrap paper so badly nobody could ever call it official. Lucien turned and walked out with his tea untouched.

His contempt for ordinary objects had never been ignorance. He knew small things became anchors once people survived long enough to touch them freely, so he tried to break the hand before the hand could claim them.

## Interlude: Nera - Three Paces

Nera found Cael behind the old greenhouse before midday. Not hiding: sitting on a low stone herb border beneath winter glass, balancing Aira's chipped pen across both palms like a bridge too narrow for weight.

She stopped three paces away. At Noctis, distance meant command or abandonment; Aurelis kept inventing third meanings without publishing a manual.

Nera: "I can leave."

Cael looked up. Daylight showed the full weight of recovery across his face now that immediate danger had stepped back.

Cael: "You already found me."

Nera accepted the correction with a short nod.

Nera: "I can stop finding you."

That nearly got a smile out of him. Cael turned the pen between his fingers.

Cael: "Lucien spoke to you like that too?"

Nera: "Yes."

She sat at the far end of the stone border, angled toward the greenhouse door so an exit stayed open for both of them. Inside the glass, heater charms ticked against damp soil, mint, and old copper wards while a faculty aide argued furiously with a climbing vine that had abandoned its labeled pot.

Nera touched the unlocked binding ring at her left wrist.

Nera: "At Noctis, his compliments were worse than his insults. Insults told you what guard to raise. Compliments made you check whether the floor had shifted."

Cael's thumb stilled on the chipped lacquer.

Cael: "He told me I endured efficiently once."

Nera shut her eyes briefly.

Nera: "He told Oric his fear had clean instincts."

Cael let out a low, disgusted breath.

Cael: "Oric believed him?"

Nera: "For three days."

Cael stared at his pale reflection in the greenhouse glass, the bright healer seal glowing above his collar.

Cael: "I believed him longer. That makes me angry."

Nera: "Good."

He blinked at her. Nera caught herself, annoyed, then shook her head once.

Nera: "No, I mean good. Not because anger looks impressive on a transcript. Good because if you are angry, something in you still expects better."

Cael's grip shifted on the pen.

Cael: "What if better is late?"

Nera looked toward the glass door.

Nera: "It usually is."

Inside the greenhouse, the faculty aide snapped, "That is not your shelf!" at the vine. The leaves rustled with unmistakable contempt.

Cael laughed once, cracked and startled, gone almost before Nera turned. She knew better than to smile at him.

Nera: "Your laugh is inefficient."

Cael stared, and then a second laugh broke loose before he could guard against it. This one pulled at his ribs. He hunched forward with one hand pressed to his side, breathing through the jolt.

Nera's hand lifted halfway and stopped.

Nera: "Do you want..."

Cael held up two fingers. *Wait.* She waited until the worst of the pain passed. When he straightened, he glared at the pen as if it had tricked him into being alive outdoors.

Cael: "I need paper."

Nera pulled a folded sheet from her sleeve.

Cael: "Why do you carry that?"

Nera: "Aira."

Cael took the sheet and wrote slowly across the center:

**I laughed and it hurt.**

Nera read it upside down.

Nera: "Accurate."

Cael: "It is stupid."

Nera: "Also accurate."

He caught himself nearly smiling, frowned at the reflex, and added two words underneath:

**Still mine.**

Nera looked away before her face could betray her. When they walked back toward the main hall, Cael tucked the folded paper into his own pocket. No witness ring claimed it, and nobody copied it.

## Interlude: Oric - The Yard Calls Back

Oric had less luck.

He crossed the south practice yard alone in the afternoon rain because he had decided, badly, that needing air did not require an escort. Rainwater pooled in the stone spell grooves around three training pylons left out after morning drills, low clouds pressing close to the academy towers.

He reached the second pylon before the Noctis aide stepped into the archway.

Aide: "Oric Sen."

Oric stopped. His hand twitched toward the old obedience mark on his bare wrist before he remembered the ring was gone. The aide saw the reflex.

She was not much older than him. Seventeen, maybe, wearing a dark Noctis uniform under a borrowed Aurelis rain cloak. Her jaw-length hair looked unevenly hacked on one side, as if cut with a blade and no mirror, her lips chapped raw from the cold. Having a real face made the ambush worse.

Aide: "Magister Rusk says your extraction status remains provisional."

Oric glanced past her at the wide-open arch. Noctis always left exits open when it wanted students to blame themselves for freezing.

Oric: "Rusk can file it."

Aide: "He did."

She lifted two fingers.

Rainwater inside the practice grooves lit stark white. Thin retrieval lines raced from pylon to pylon and snapped toward Oric's boots, an old Noctis snare hidden inside Aurelis drainage channels. Oric froze, and the lines flared brighter around his heels, reading stillness as consent.

Nera hit the archway at a dead sprint.

She did not waste breath shouting a warning. She threw a binding ring.

The iron hoop slammed the nearest pylon, snapped wide in a blur of dull silver, and hooked the white line sideways with a sound like ripping canvas. The aide recoiled.

Nera: "Move, Oric!"

Oric: "It tracks motion!"

Nera: "It is already tracking stillness!"

Oric threw himself sideways. The white line lunged for his ankle. Nera's second ring struck the wet flagstones between them, tripping the snare's trigger half a beat early so the trap snapped shut on empty rain.

Oric skidded hard on the slick stone. Cassian caught his collar before he went down.

No banter, no flourish: Cassian had crossed from the west gate with red Circle heat blazing down both forearms, turning falling rain into sharp white steam.

Cassian: "Left!"

Oric rolled left as the third pylon lit up. Cassian drove two superheated fingers straight into the seam where Noctis ink met Aurelis copper. The pylon split with a sharp crack. With its third anchor gone, the trap's pattern collapsed in on itself.

Fear finally broke across the aide's face. Not fear of Cassian: fear of failing Rusk. Her hand darted toward the relay catch under her collar.

Nera lunged forward and shouted across the rain.

Nera: "Do not answer him!"

The aide froze with two fingers hooked beneath the wet wool. For one taut second, two Noctis students stared across the same old command.

Slowly, the aide lowered her hand. The last white line died in the drainage groove.

Oric braced both hands on his knees and let out a high, ragged laugh. Cassian hovered one palm near his shoulder without touching him.

Cassian: "Inefficient walk."

Oric: "I hate this school."

Nera caught her returning rings, one edge scorched black.

Nera: "Aurelis or Noctis?"

Oric dragged in a wet breath.

Oric: "Yes."

The aide had not bolted. She stood shivering under the stone arch, looking painfully young without the white glare. Nera stepped across the wet yard to face her.

Nera: "Name."

Aide: "Vessa."

Nera: "Is that yours?"

Vessa's shoulders dropped. She glanced once at the empty air where a relay command would have burned if the snare had closed. Nothing came.

Vessa: "It was my sister's."

Nobody had a clean answer for that. Cassian broke the silence the only way he knew how.

Cassian: "Vessa, if you are going to collapse, use the grass. Stone makes Maelin loud."

Vessa blinked at him, stepped off the wet flagstones onto the soaked turf, and sat down before her knees gave out.

By the time Rhea reached the yard, the proof was already cooling in the rain: one cracked pylon, two scorched rings, a dead snare groove, and a Noctis aide sitting in wet grass wearing her sister's name.

Rhea looked across the wreckage, then at Cassian.

President Valmont: "Report."

Cassian: "We went for a walk."

Nera: "No."

Oric lifted one tired hand from his knees.

Oric: "Terrible walk."

Vessa let out a broken laugh that sounded unmistakably like her own.

Rhea did not demand a statement on the spot.

President Valmont: "Medical first. Choice after."

Vessa stared at the dead drainage groove.

Vessa: "If I refuse?"

President Valmont: "Then you refuse."

Vessa waited for the threat, the follow-up rule calling refusal temporary confusion. None arrived. Oric straightened slowly.

Oric: "That is the worst part. They mean it, and then you have to decide."

Nera's scorched ring clicked softly in her palm.

Nera: "He is right."

Cassian glanced between them.

Cassian: "Alarming consensus. Should I leave?"

Oric exhaled, wiping rain from his jaw.

Oric: "Yes."

Cassian: "Morale restored."

Vessa stared down at the soaked grass, breathing fast as if having three people watch her lungs work were suddenly too much. Rhea saw immediately.

President Valmont: "Everyone look at the broken pylon."

Cassian spun toward the smoking stone with absurd solemnity.

Cassian: "Remarkable fracture line. Deeply educational."

Nera turned toward it too, and Oric turned last. Spared their stares, Vessa let out another smaller laugh. When Maelin hurried into the yard two minutes later demanding why a Noctis student was sitting in wet grass beside an illegal retrieval trap, Vessa answered before anyone else could.

Vessa: "Because I sat down."

Maelin eyed her critically.

Maelin: "Terrible surface choice."

Vessa nodded once in plain agreement.

Later that afternoon, Oric came back past the south yard to find the cracked pylon roped off with a faculty notice:

**DO NOT TOUCH. ACTIVE REVIEW.**

Underneath, in Aira's sharp script:

**THIS MEANS CASSIAN TOO.**

Below that, in scorched charcoal:

**UNFAIR BUT ACCURATE.**

Oric stood in the thinning drizzle until his chest settled. Noctis warnings only ever pointed downward. This sign told Cassian no, kept crowds off the evidence, and refused to pave quietly over a broken trap. He did not love Aurelis yet, but a warning that disliked everyone evenly was a start.

He touched none of it and walked back through the rain by choice, counting his steps only until he no longer needed the count.

At twelve, he stopped counting.

At thirteen, he kept walking anyway.

The yard did not call him back again.

By dusk, the rain had cleared to a cold silver sheen across the upper corridor stone.

Elira and I were halfway past the infirmary wing when she stopped dead beside the tall arched window. Her fingers rose sharply to the edge of her collar.

Haru: "Elira?"

She turned her chin slightly, and my stomach dropped.

Above the thin black chain of her pendant, the white frost-burn Maelin had supposedly cleared that morning was glowing faintly pale again against her skin. It was not spreading. It pulsed once, sharp and deliberate, like an answered trace.

Under my sleeve, the false Green crest shivered violently against my wrist as the Black Circle reacted to something far deeper than the trial hall. Whatever Lucien had triggered during the autonomy proof had not just tested the pendant.

It had mapped it. And somewhere beneath Noctis's buried doctrine, something old was waking up to read the shape.

Rapid footsteps echoed up the stairwell behind us: three healers in white cuffs, a council officer carrying an intake clipboard, and Leon already striding ahead with his jaw locked at the sight of the frost line above Elira's throat.

Elira lowered her hand from the collar, met my eyes once with quiet steel, and squared her shoulders toward the approaching healers.

She was not going quietly into anyone's infirmary.

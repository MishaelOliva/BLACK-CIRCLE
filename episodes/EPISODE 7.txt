# Episode 7: **Talen Speaks**

The Foundation Trial began without the mercy of corridors.

Noctis had filed enough paper to build a wall. Rhea refused to let them hide behind it.

By midmorning, the Record Hall had become a hearing chamber. Seven witness marks burned around the silver pool. Faculty filled the upper rail while students crowded the gallery. Valemere's delegation occupied the west witness tier, Drakenshard's field analysts guarded duplicate match logs, and two Sol Aeternum healers sat where Noctis could see their signatures. Below them, Mira kept the Green Circle record table, copying in ink that already stained both hands.

Cael sat between Leon and Cassian.

Across the floor, Nera Vail stood on the Noctis side with her binding rings locked flat against her wrists.

Lucien Voss stood beneath the eastern projection and looked perfectly at home in a room built to measure harm. His ease in it was obscene.

Rhea opened with one sentence.

President Valmont: "This hearing covers Cael Nox's status as witness, the truth of the tournament record, and the push to classify Haru Aerlen's interference."

Lucien smiled.

Lucien: "How carefully Aurelis places the Green Circle student last."

Rhea: "How quickly Noctis tries to make the injured student disappear behind him."

The silver pool accepted both statements for record. No one wasted breath pretending this would be clean.

Cael testified first.

He stood because sitting made him feel like Noctis had already won. The healer seal over his chest pulsed blue-white at the edge and red at the center. His hands shook, and he let everyone see.

Cael: "At Noctis, silence is treated as discipline. Fear is treated as preparation. Refusal is treated as failure to understand the honor of being useful."

The words scraped out rough and uneven, which made them harder to dismiss. He described the match, Lucien's lightless pressure, the moment his channels opened in two directions, and the seven seconds the official record had tried to swallow.

Leon listened without interrupting. Beside him, Cassian kept his fire banked low around his knuckles.

When Cael finished, Lucien stepped forward into the open floor.

Lucien: "We have heard that Cael Nox was afraid. Fear is not illegality."

The gallery stiffened. Lucien turned in a slow circle, letting every tier see his calm.

Lucien: "Haru Aerlen stepped into sanctioned combat. The match had field judges, healers, authority, and rules. A Green Circle transfer decided his personal judgment outranked all of it."

His eyes came to me.

Lucien: "Who gave him that right?"

The hall did not reject the question. That was the trap.

Lucien did not raise his voice. He let the chamber's stone carry the polish of it.

Lucien: "Either stepping in answers to real authority, or it becomes arrogance with a gentler name. Mercy requires authority. Authority comes from recognized power. Therefore, either Haru Aerlen had no right to interfere, or Haru Aerlen holds authority this court has not recognized."

I felt the Black Circle go cold behind my ribs. Under my sleeve, the false Green crest pinched sharply against my wrist, flickering as the air around my knuckles tightened with static I had to force back down.

Elira's voice cut in before mine could.

Elira: "Or authority failed to act in time, and witnesses saved the person authority left behind."

The silver pool held both arguments.

**AUTHORITY QUESTION PRESERVED**

## Interlude: Elira - The Order of Harm

Elira Vale had learned early that hearings were not built to discover truth. They were built to decide which truths could survive being arranged.

The Record Hall was beautiful: silver pool, old stone, light precise enough to make every face look accountable. Aurelis liked rooms like this because beauty made authority feel less like pressure. Elira hated how well she understood the trick, because she had benefited from it her whole life.

Yellow Circle students were placed where light confirmed them. Their mistakes were called intensity. Their silences were read as judgment. Cael Nox had been placed where utility could pass for honor. And Haru Aerlen had been placed last in Rhea's opening sentence because every other placement would have let Lucien make him first.

Elira had watched that sequence form, and she had watched Lucien reach straight past Cael's bruises for the one lever that could overturn the room: *Who gave him that right?*

The clean answer was easy. No one.

The honest answer had blood under it. Field judges had frozen. Healers had missed the window. The record had tried to swallow seven seconds and call digestion procedure. Haru had seen a boy turned into a conduit and moved anyway.

Beneath the hearing table, where no relay could turn the motion into performance, Elira pressed her palms flat against her skirt. She felt Haru beside her pulling himself inward, trying to become smaller than Lucien's question before his wrist gave anything away.

She would not let Lucien turn restraint into guilt. Stepping into the sentence had never been about answering for Haru. It was about keeping the order of harm intact before power could rewrite the scene.

Everyone at the Aurelis rail knew what waited underneath it. If the court accepted Lucien's argument and stripped witness protection from what I did, Noctis and the Foundation examiners would force a private Circle inspection. They would peel back the false Green illusion on my wrist, drag the Black Circle into a containment ledger, and use that reclassification to cancel Rhea's protection order and pull Cael straight back into Noctis custody.

Lucien had not come to deny pain. Denying pain would make him look small beside Cael and Elira's letters. He had come to make pain legally worthless.

The Noctis officials shifted, sensing concession. Rhea spoke again before they could breathe.

**WHAT RHEA MEANS**

Lucien's gaze flicked briefly toward Mira's pages. He noticed too. The gallery record had fewer habits of obedience than the faculty rail, and that made it dangerous.

Cael closed his eyes. Elira's hand stayed at her side, leaving the answer mine.

The false Green Circle at my wrist lit obediently, a fraction of a second too slow to match my pulse. Beneath the cuff, cold static bit at my skin as the Black Circle pressed upward, ready to swallow the mark whole if I slipped. Halden caught the slight lag in the green glow. His gaze sharpened, though he kept silent.

I forced my breath steady against the cold tightening behind my ribs.

Haru: "Yes."

The pool darkened. Lucien's smile went still.

Haru: "I saw Cael's channels were open in two directions. Lucien's lightless mana was using him as a path while the crown anchor backlash tore through the same damage. While Elira held the anchor stones from collapsing inward, I redirected the overload recoil away from his channels."

Silver lines rose over the pool, reconstructing the moment in stripped geometry: Cael at the center, Lucien's pressure as a dark thread, crown anchor recoil flaring sideways while I turned the surge clear of his heart.

Lucien: "With Green Circle magic."

There it was. The cleanest knife in the room.

Lie, and Cael's testimony carried a hole everyone could see. Speak too freely, and the isolated inspection began before noon.

Elira spoke before the silence hardened, anchoring the sequence without taking the floor from me.

Elira: "The question before the hall is whether someone stepped in, whether it saved a life, and whether the record can judge the act without isolating the person."

President Valmont: "Correct."

Lucien: "Safeguard. How quickly the forbidden word becomes convenient."

Professor Halden: "Convenience is not accuracy. You rely on that confusion too often."

**LIFE-PRESERVING INTERVENTION CONFIRMED**

**METHOD REVIEW DEFERRED PENDING WITNESS AUTONOMY**

My pulse hammered against the false Green crest on my wrist, but my knees held. Across the floor, Cael opened his eyes. He did not thank me. He simply stayed alive in the record.

**COERCIVE UTILITY PHRASE REJECTED**

Cael slumped back in the chair, shaking, alive, and badly bruised by the cold.

For several seconds after he caught his breath, nobody moved. Leon's hands trembled once he let go. Nera turned half away and pressed her bleeding wrists against her sleeves. Aira stared at her snapped pencil point as if it were the only small thing left in the building.

Cael saw all of them watching him, and his old training immediately reached for blame. Leon caught it before it left his mouth.

Leon: "No."

Cael blinked.

Leon: "Whatever apology is forming, kill it."

Cael's mouth tightened.

Cael: "You do not know what I was going to say."

Leon: "You have three expressions. Fear, apology, and thinking apology is strategy."

Nera let out a short sound that might have been a laugh if it had not hurt. Cael looked at her, startled out of his own spiral.

She raised her bandaged wrists.

Nera: "Noctis binding rings can open quickly. They are built to cut hard enough that you remember not to do it."

Rhea's seal shifted.

Nera: "Do not enter that as confession. Enter it as design."

Rhea: "Design entered."

Aira dug a fresh pencil from her sleeve.

Aira: "I hate this school."

Oric: "Noctis?"

Aira: "I am reserving several categories of hate. Do not rush me."

The tension broke just enough for everyone to draw a clean breath.

---

After the frost attack, the preparation sharpened.

Nera and Oric laid open the internal machinery Noctis had trained them never to name: relay hooks, obedience phrases, restoration clauses, the paperwork tricks used to turn a student into disposable gear.

Near midnight, Darian Thorne—a Red Circle senior logistics analyst attached to Noctis's own witness staff—broke into our open chamber feed across an unlogged line. His projection tore with static and raw dread as he held a sheaf of internal Noctis routing sheets up to the glass before footsteps sounded behind him.

Darian: "I am not authorized to speak, but I am required to witness."

Lucien's voice sliced across the transmission from just off-frame outside Darian's door.

Lucien: "Thorne. You are out of procedure."

Darian looked terrified. He pushed the witness packet through the silver copy relay anyway.

Darian: "I am in witness. Which is older than procedure."

No seal flared in reward. No dramatic light answered him. Rhea watched the relay edges for one silent beat to see if he would retreat into obedience. When he held his ground until Noctis slammed the line dead, her seal locked both his sentence and the captured routing packet into record.

Darian's smuggled witness map turned our defense into a diagram.

Nera sketched the first diagram left-handed because her right wrist kept bleeding through the wrap. It traced doctrine, not spells: Lucien at the top, supported by senior council witnesses forbidden to speak, technical aides taught to call coercion preparation, and recovery forms asking whether a student had returned to usefulness before asking whether he had slept.

Oric added relay anchors. Rhea marked jurisdiction. Halden added archive parallels from the old safeguard failures.

Aira stared at the finished sheet without a trace of humor.

Aira: "This is not one bad prince in a dramatic coat."

Cassian: "Shame. That would be easier to set on fire."

Aira: "It is a machine that turns people into parts and calls the noise discipline."

Cael stood at the edge of the map, face still. Elira stepped near him and stopped, waiting. Only when he nodded did she step beside his shoulder.

After a long minute, Cael reached out and shifted one wooden token, his own, half an inch off the line marked **RETURN CLAIM**, placing it on the empty square beyond.

Cael: "If the field recognizes return as rescue, what does it require from the person being returned?"

Nera's head snapped up. Oric stopped counting. Professor Valen answered carefully.

Professor Valen: "A lawful field needs real consent right now, and shared support to hold it steady."

Cael left his finger on the empty square.

Cael: "And an unlawful one?"

Nera answered into the quiet.

Nera: "It picks one life to spend and calls that choice."

Cael pulled his hand back.

Haru: "Cael?"

He kept his eyes on the map.

Cael: "I am learning where not to stand."

Nobody moved the token back.

By the final hours, the map carried fingerprints from half the room: Aira's ink smudges, a streak of healing salve from Cael's wrist, Nera's nail mark beside **RETURN CLAIM**, and a faint scorch corner Cassian blamed on a candle that did not exist.

Professor Valen studied the stained sheet.

Professor Valen: "Do not make a clean copy."

Halden's pen paused.

Professor Halden: "For filing?"

Professor Valen: "For memory."

Rhea weighed both needs.

President Valmont: "Official filing requires legibility."

Aira: "We file a clean copy and keep the ugly one."

Mira looked up from her copy stack by the doorway.

Mira: "The ugly one proves the work happened."

The second breakthrough was uglier, and sharper. Halden caught a contradiction buried inside Noctis's withdrawal clauses.

Professor Halden: "They use the word *protection* in two incompatible senses."

Aira: "Illegal?"

Professor Halden: "Worse. Sloppy. Legality can be argued; sloppiness exposes motive."

He turned the sheet toward us. One paragraph claimed Cael required Noctis custody because conduit training left him vulnerable to outside influence. The next claimed the same training made him uniquely reliable as an internal witness. Vulnerable in Aurelis hands; reliable in Noctis hands.

Leon stared at the page.

Leon: "They did not expect anyone to read both."

Mira glanced up over her nib without slowing her hand.

Mira: "Green students read paired notices. Contradictions are usually hidden where we sit."

Aira wrote that down verbatim.

Then Lucien's voice cut through the outer ward relay, thin and amused.

Lucien: "How careful."

Nobody answered.

Lucien: "Aurelis finds a dangerous student and spreads him across witnesses. Leon as conscience. Cassian as force. Rhea as law. Halden as restraint. Mira as public sympathy. Elira as emotional anchor. Cael as injured proof."

My blood went cold, because he was wrong and yet close enough to threaten the entire structure.

Lucien: "You are learning to hide him inside everyone."

The silence held. He had stopped poking at individual pieces and named the network itself. Rhea did not jump to cut him down, which told us the strike had landed.

Cael spoke first, voice rough from the frost.

Cael: "At Noctis, hiding someone inside everyone is called contamination."

The relay did not flicker.

Cael: "Here, I think it is called not leaving."

Mira's pen scratched across paper, breaking the paralysis. Lucien laughed softly.

Lucien: "How touching. A rescued conduit discovers poetry."

Nera stepped squarely between the relay and Cael's chair, her bandaged wrists bare beneath loose, unlocked rings.

Nera: "Do not call him conduit."

Lucien: "Noctis terminology offends Aurelis now?"

Nera: "It offends accuracy."

Lucien's tone dropped ten degrees.

Lucien: "You are enjoying your borrowed courage."

Nera went white. Oric stepped beside her. Not in front, beside.

Nera: "No. I am recognizing how much fear Noctis taught me to mistake for virtue."

Aira stopped writing to let the sentence breathe.

Rhea lifted her red seal at last.

President Valmont: "Public correction. Standing beside a witness is not hiding him unless the student is kept from speaking for himself. The sunset autonomy test proves that directly."

Lucien: "And if they fail?"

Rhea: "Then the Foundation records failure."

Lucien: "And Haru Aerlen?"

Rhea looked directly at me in front of the entire room, refusing to soften the blade.

President Valmont: "Then he faces full classification review without witness protection shielding him."

The words struck straight at my ribs. Beside me, Elira's fingers twitched, yet she did not reach out. I loved her fiercely for holding that line.

I met Rhea's gaze and kept my wrist steady beneath my cuff.

Haru: "Then I will be in the room to answer it."

Lucien caught the silence that followed and knew exactly what it cost.

Lucien: "Good."

His relay began to thin.

Lucien: "At sunset we learn whether mercy is a network or a mask."

The projection winked out.

For a long minute, nobody spoke. Then Aira wrote one line across the top of the final preparation sheet and underlined it once:

**Do not perform loyalty. Prove choice.**

Beneath Elira's collar, the black pendant pulsed cold once. Warning.

## Coda: Talen's Hands

Earlier, back at Valemere Royal Institute right as the opening morning's testimony closed, the silver relay faded from the infirmary mirror and left ordinary rain tapping against the window glass.

Only then did Talen let his braced left hand drop to his knee.

It shook harder now than it had in front of five academies. Holding it up into the relay light while Lucien waited for him to fold had burned through every ounce of steadiness he had left. The Foundation's words, *CLEAN RECORD DOES NOT ERASE DIRTY OUTCOME*, were already fading from the room's glass, yet the tremor stayed right where it had lived since the junior circuit.

Beside his chair, his mother finally unclenched her bloodless fingers. She did not tell him to hide the hand or pretend the cold weather was to blame. She wrapped a warm linen cloth gently around his braced wrist and let it tremble against her palm.

At the wall's private Valemere delegation link, Soren Ashvale lingered inside his Aurelis quarters with the thin stack of timing sheets still pressed against his side.

Soren: "Valemere let that match sit clean on the books for two years. That was not only Noctis."

Talen looked up at the glass, surprised to hear the heir of Valemere admit it without a single excuse.

Soren: "Once the forty-eight-hour autonomy proof reconvenes at sunset, if you want your relay line shut, I will shut it myself."

Talen pressed his braced hand flat against his knee, no longer trying to force the tremor down, letting it belong to his body instead of Lucien's scorecard.

Talen: "Leave it open."

Soren inclined his head once across the link.

Soren: "Then I will stand right in front of the relay screen on the witness rail while it runs."

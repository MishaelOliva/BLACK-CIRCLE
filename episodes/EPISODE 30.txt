# Episode 30: **What Iven Copied**

## Iven Sahl

Iven Sahl woke before the bell and kept his eyes closed until the room proved it would not punish him for noticing. Old habit. Rescue, medical clearance, nights in the east recovery wing, a Foundation Council ruling, and Maelin's blunt lecture about fear versus obedience had not trained it out of him yet.

Above his bed, the ceiling was plain white plaster. Noctis ceilings had been higher and darker, built so a boy lying flat always felt small underneath them. Aurelis ceilings sat lower in the recovery rooms. The first morning Iven had hated that because low ceilings felt like lids; now he let himself see plain plaster and rafters and opened his eyes.

Morning light lay across the foot of his bed in a pale rectangle, catching one folded blanket, one borrowed academy sweater, and the boots someone had mended while he slept. The left boot had a new lace. The right still carried a tiny scorch mark near the heel from a winter-drill brazier spark, left untouched.

He sat up slowly. Nothing barked an order, and nobody checked his posture.

Across the narrow hall in the opposite witness room, Oric was probably still sleeping face-down in his pillow, while Nera's morning tea tray had already rattled along the corridor—Maelin had placed the two uninjured defectors on the far side of the passage once Iven asked for his own doorway.

On the chair beside Iven's bed sat a small paper packet tied with kitchen string. He knew the fold before he touched it. Boot sweets.

He stared at it without reaching. He had sent sweets north with Haru and Aira before the carriage departed because there were kinds of hunger a speech could not reach, telling them **just so she knows someone remembered** so neither Lysa nor Sera would feel cornered by a face waiting for thanks. Now a packet sat beside his own bed with a folded slip tucked under the string.

Iven lifted the slip with two fingers.

**SERA SAID YOU CAN HAVE TWO. SHE KEPT THREE. THIS IS NOT A TRADE.**

Underneath, in smaller cramped letters:

**MIRA WROTE THIS BECAUSE SERA SAID THE WORDS AND THEN LOOKED ANGRY AT THE PENCIL.**

Iven read the second line twice until the letters blurred, then let out a short, rusty laugh that came out mostly as breath. He slapped a hand over his own mouth quickly, listening out of old habit for footsteps along the empty hall.

**Not a trade.** The words mattered. Noctis had taught him that gifts were advances on a bill: warmth bought compliance, food proved ownership, a blanket gave someone the right to ask why you were shivering. Aurelis kept failing to collect.

Iven untied the string and shook one sweet into his palm, leaving the second inside the packet. One was small enough to accept without his stomach turning it into debt. He retied the string badly, set the packet on his chair ready to hand across to Oric at breakfast, and looked down at his wrist.

The Green Circle answered in a soft ring beneath his skin. **Foundation**, the primers said. Sixty percent. Common. Necessary maintenance and base function. He had copied that definition so many times at Noctis that his fingers still knew the rhythm of the pen strokes, even though the books never had to print the word *unimportant* to make it clear.

Yet Mira Castellan was Green Circle. Finn Castellan was Green Circle. So were the first student who had copied Mira's courtyard note with a backwards letter, the kitchen aide who slipped extra rolls into folded napkins for transfers who flinched at serving trays, and the repair aide who had replaced his boot lace and left the scorch mark alone. Every time the high-ranked parts of the academy cracked, Green hands seemed to be the ones holding the frame together.

Iven pressed his thumb lightly over the ring until it warmed against his skin. It did not flare brighter, and he did not try to push it.

Outside, the cracked west tower bell rang late, its bronze note wobbling off-pitch before catching the others. Across the hall, Oric's door latch clicked and he stepped half-dressed to the threshold of Iven's unlatched room, leaning against the jamb to squint sideways toward the courtyard window.

Oric: "Bell has structural problems."

Iven: "Aira heard someone by the witness cottage call it brave, and Professor Halden nodded along."

Oric thought about that with the dark hostility of someone dragged halfway out of sleep.

Oric: "Professor Halden should apologize to architecture."

A smile tugged at Iven's mouth before he could stop it, stiff and unfamiliar like a borrowed coat. He pulled on his sweater, tucked Sera's note into his inner pocket, scooped up the tied packet with the remaining sweet to press into Oric's hand at the doorway, and slipped out into the hall.

---

Mira's archive station had moved twice since the Council ruling, mostly because Mira kept winning arguments with furniture.

The first station near the infirmary hall had choked on crowds wanting witness summaries and staring at ugly copies as if crooked lettering might rub off on their sleeves. The second, under the east arcade, had lasted until sideways rain soaked a stack of margin notes and Mira declared water damage an invalid form of public debate.

Now the station filled a wide table in the old bell tower courtyard, close enough to the public boards to track new postings and open enough on three sides that nobody could corner anyone there under the guise of concern. Two Green Circle aides sorted sheets into neat piles. A Blue Circle student sharpened pencils with grim focus. Aira Solen sat cross-legged on the far corner of the table, writing on three different pages at once, while Mira stood in the middle with ink on her knuckles and tape stuck to one cuff.

She looked up when Iven stopped at the edge of the gravel, unhurried, as if she had left a gap in the morning specifically for him.

Mira: "Iven."

His name sounded light when she said it, out here in the open air with clear paths behind him.

Iven: "You said I could ask questions."

Mira: "Ask."

She did not reach for an intake sheet or lay down a condition. Aira glanced up over the top of her page.

Aira: "Careful. She means that literally. I asked one question about filing categories and lost forty-seven minutes to moral infrastructure."

Mira: "You took notes."

Aira: "Under duress."

Mira: "You asked follow-up questions."

Aira: "Under intellectual duress."

Iven bit back another laugh before it could turn into a habit. Mira hooked a wooden chair out from under the table with her foot.

Mira: "Sit if you want."

Only an offer, with plenty of room behind the chair to stand back up. Iven sat.

Paper covered almost every inch of the boards, sorted into clear stacks: formal Council-sealed witness records, ugly student copies, and correction sheets where scribes had reproduced Noctis edits mistakes and all so nobody could claim Aurelis had invented the sloppy tampering. Right in the center, under a clear protective sheet, lay Cael's final copied line.

**I WANTED TO LIVE. I CHOSE ANYWAY. BOTH ARE TRUE.**

No two copies around it matched. Letters leaned left or right; lines broke early; one student had pressed so hard on **BOTH ARE TRUE** that the pencil tore through the sheet, and another had misspelled **ANYWAY**, crossed it out, and left the scar right in the middle of the page.

Iven rested his fingertips lightly against the wood, careful not to touch the stacks.

Iven: "You found my name on Cael's drawer sheet."

Mira's expression barely shifted, which made it easier to look at her. She didn't swell her grief up to prove she felt it.

Mira: "Yes. Right above Lysa's—and beside mine, he'd written *copies survive if...*"

Aira's pencil paused for half a beat against her page.

Iven: "Leon told me how you copied it ugly afterward."

Mira looked down at the protective sheet for a quiet breath.

Mira: "Because he taught me why ugly copies matter."

She slid a single blank sheet between them and wrote a sentence across the top in clean block letters.

**A RECORD HELD BY ONE HAND CAN BE TAKEN.**

She turned the paper toward Iven and laid a pencil beside it.

Mira: "Copy it."

Iven's fingers tightened around the wood. At Noctis, copying meant erasing yourself: the whole test was whether your hand could vanish inside the hand above it. Any drift in angle earned a mark; any mark earned correction; every correction proved you needed watching sooner. Mira only waited, elbows off the paper, giving him room.

Iven set the lead down and copied the line.

The first words came out tight and cramped, too close to Mira's block print because his wrist knew how to survive authority better than it knew how to write for itself. Then he reached **HELD**, and the L tilted too far right. His hand froze.

Mira: "Keep going."

Iven: "It's wrong."

Mira: "Keep going."

When he glanced up, she wasn't smiling; she only tapped the blank space beside the crooked letter. Iven forced his wrist back onto the page and finished the line. The second half came out worse because he knew it was ruined already, leaving too wide a gap between **ONE** and **HAND** and digging **TAKEN** into the grain like he was mad at it. He set the pencil down.

Mira pulled his page over, laid a fresh sheet beside it, and copied Iven's version stroke for stroke until she reached the tilted L. Then she leaned it farther right on purpose.

Iven stared as she copied his wide gap, deliberately chipped the curve on **CAN**, and laid the third page down.

Mira: "Now mine carries yours, plus something I added."

She slid her sheet down the table to Aira without looking. Aira accepted it as solemnly as contraband and dashed off a fast, jagged copy, keeping the leaning L, stretching the space wider, preserving Mira's broken **CAN**, and dropping the final period altogether.

Aira: "I regret nothing except punctuation."

Mira passed Aira's sheet to the Blue Circle pencil sharpener, whose name Iven thought was Jorin. Jorin copied every flaw down the line and pressed so hard on **RECORD** that the O closed into a zero.

By the fifth page, the sentence looked like a line of travelers walking uneven ground. Mira arranged all five sheets side by side.

Mira: "A clean copy is easy to call a forgery. Authority likes clean copies because they look like one voice wrote them and everybody else just repeated it on command."

She tapped the top sheet.

Mira: "This is my hand."

Her finger moved to the second page.

Mira: "This is yours."

The third.

Mira: "Mine again, carrying yours badly."

The fourth.

Aira: "My tragic punctuation era."

Mira tapped the fifth sheet.

Mira: "Jorin's pressure mark."

Jorin, who had been trying to disappear behind his pencil shavings, went bright red.

Mira: "Every flaw proves another person touched it. Noctis can challenge the first record all morning. They can claim it was altered, forged, forced, or stripped of context. They tried all four before breakfast."

Aira: "With an impressive lack of creativity."

Mira: "But once a line moves through enough imperfect hands, erasing it means erasing every single hand separately."

Iven looked across the five uneven sheets.

Iven: "And they can't."

Mira: "Not anymore."

The thought didn't feel triumphant; it felt solid, heavy in the good way a wooden table was heavy when nobody could kick it over.

Iven: "What if a mistake changes what the sentence means?"

Mira tilted her head, treating the question seriously instead of smoothing it away.

Mira: "Then that new meaning belongs to whoever changed it. We can note the difference, compare versions, and keep the original beside the copies. We just don't scrub out the hand to guard the words."

Iven thought of the Noctis primers he had copied until his knuckles cramped. **Foundation. Maintenance. Base function.**

Iven: "Can I copy something else?"

Mira pulled another blank sheet from the stack and slid it across without asking what or why.

Iven took Sera's folded note out of his sweater pocket. Most of it belonged to Sera and her glare at the pencil, so he left that part alone. One line had been sitting behind his ribs since morning, waiting to land somewhere real.

**THIS IS NOT A TRADE.**

The T leaned left on the downstroke. He went back over it without erasing, leaving the stem darker and thicker than the rest of the word. His fingers twitched to crumple the sheet and start fresh, then he heard Mira's voice in his head telling him to keep going and finished the line.

Mira kept her hands off the paper until he slid it across the wood.

Mira: "Do you want it copied?"

Want. Nobody at Noctis had ever asked him what he wanted copied. The line wasn't official, had no Council seal, and wouldn't mean anything to a committee, yet his chest eased just seeing it in his own crooked print.

Iven: "One copy."

Mira smoothed the corner flat with her thumb and picked up a pencil. She copied the sentence straight beneath his on the same sheet, keeping the heavy corrected T, and let her own pencil pause a fraction too long on the final period until the dot darkened. Held by two hands.

Aira glanced over at the sheet and kept quiet, which told Iven more plainly than a speech that she understood.

---

He ran into Cael near the recovery corridor because grief had rotten timing and Aurelis had too many blind corners.

For one stupid second, Iven forgot the funeral, the board, the ruling, and the ash-dark scripts that had tried to swallow Cael's final words. He saw a pale uniform hanging loose on thin shoulders in the slant of morning light ahead of him and thought, *There.*

Then his eyes caught up. Not Cael. Leon.

Leon stood outside Cael's old room with one palm braced against the doorframe, half-in and half-out, like he hadn't decided whether crossing the threshold counted as trespassing. Iven almost backed around the corner, too late; Leon turned his head and spotted the folded sheet in Iven's hand.

Leon: "Archive?"

Iven lifted the folded paper half an inch.

Leon: "Mira still terrifying?"

Iven: "Precisely."

Leon's mouth twitched, trying for a grin and missing by a step. The corridor sat heavy between them. Iven had practiced three different things to say to Cael during the nights after extraction before his head finally accepted Cael wouldn't be around to hear them, and one of them slipped out toward Leon instead.

Iven: "I let one of the younger transfers hold Cael's cracked thermal charm this morning."

Leon looked at him sharply.

Leon: "He lent that to you."

Iven: "It's cracked and dead, though remembering how he passed it on steadied her hands until Maelin brought hot broth. She needed it more after waking alone."

Leon's shoulders dropped half an inch, and he glanced down at his own fingers, flexing them slowly as if checking whether old habit still sat tight over the knuckles.

Iven: "Cael held it three days himself before giving it to me during winter drills. Anything you hold three days and still pass along is already practice. He was getting good at it."

Leon: "Cael had honest hands."

Iven: "Yeah."

He kept his voice low so it wouldn't echo down the empty hall.

Leon: "Noctis tried to make that inconvenient."

Iven: "Didn't work."

Leon let out a ragged breath that sounded half like a laugh.

Leon: "Good."

Behind Leon, the bedroom door stood open. A plain slate stone rested on the desk inside, holding down a copied witness page as if weight were the only job anyone had asked of it. Leon's eyes flicked back to the paper in Iven's hand.

Leon: "What did you copy?"

Iven unfolded the sheet and held it out. Leon read both lines, pausing on the dark dot at the end of Mira's copy.

**THIS IS NOT A TRADE.**

**THIS IS NOT A TRADE.**

Leon swallowed hard.

Leon: "Useful sentence."

Iven: "I thought so."

Leon: "Can I..."

He stopped himself halfway through asking, checking his own reach in a way Iven wouldn't have expected from a Blue Circle senior a month ago. Iven held the paper out wider.

Leon stepped just inside the room for a blank slip from the desk and copied Mira's version rather than Iven's. His script was sharp and even, shaped by years of expensive tutors, except the final word dipped slightly downward at the edge as if his wrist ran out of strength on **TRADE**. Leon stared at it.

Leon: "I made it worse."

Iven: "You bent the last word."

Leon looked up.

Iven: "That means it's yours."

Leon handed Iven's sheet back carefully, holding the edges so he wouldn't smudge the graphite. Iven walked back down the corridor and didn't glance over his shoulder until he reached the turn. Inside the open doorway, Leon had tucked his own crooked copy beneath the slate stone beside Cael's page.

---

Iven's first ordinary class lasted eleven minutes.

Instructor Mora taught foundational ward repair in a room with tall windows and no restraint rings set into the floorboards. She named every tool before passing it down the rows, let students choose between copper wire and chalk, and propped the door wide open with a wooden wedge. Iven tracked all of it as he sat down near the aisle.

Then she rang a small brass bell to start the drill.

Iven's body left the room before he knew he had stood. His knee slammed the desk edge, his boot crushed a stick of chalk into white dust, and somebody called his name while he hit the stone wall outside in the corridor, back flat, both hands up to guard the channel points at his throat.

At Noctis, a bell meant stand straight, keep your hands visible, and wait for correction. Here, it only meant begin. His lungs didn't care about the difference.

Instructor Mora stopped just inside the open doorway and came no closer.

Instructor Mora: "The hall is open. The room is open. Neither choice affects your attendance mark today."

Iven stared at her over his forearms.

Iven: "That's special treatment."

Instructor Mora: "Yes. Injury usually requires it."

No apology, and no fake claim that everyone started on level ground. Behind her, twenty students tried hard not to look toward the door and mostly failed. Iven dropped his eyes to the crushed chalk by his boot heel.

Iven: "If I leave, do I fail?"

Instructor Mora: "No."

Iven: "If I go back in?"

Instructor Mora: "Also no."

Without a threat hanging over the door, choosing got harder. He stayed pressed against the cool stone until his shoulders lowered and his fingers uncurled from his collar. Then he bent down, picked up the biggest piece of unbroken chalk, and stepped back inside because the windows were wide, the door stayed wedged open, and the half-drawn ward on his desk had one broken line he knew how to fix. Nobody clapped.

Instructor Mora didn't touch the bell again. Iven repaired one corner of the ward, left the other three quadrants blank, and wrote in the bottom margin:

**BELL RESPONSE INTERRUPTED WORK. WORK RESUMED.**

Instructor Mora marked the repaired corner correct and wrote tomorrow's date beside the empty quadrants. Just before dismissal, a Blue first-year near the aisle raised his hand.

Blue First-Year: "Should we stop using the bell?"

The room looked at Instructor Mora, and she looked across at Iven, not handing him school policy, just asking what he needed in order to stay. Iven studied the small brass bell sitting in the middle of her desk. Banning it would make the room quieter, sure, but it would also leave Noctis in charge of what a bell meant forever.

Iven: "Say before you ring it. Not today. Next class, once."

Instructor Mora turned to the slate board and wrote it out in large chalk letters.

**BELL ANNOUNCED. ONE RING. EXIT REMAINS OPEN.**

The Blue first-year copied it into the top margin of his notes. Iven left his piece of chalk waiting on his desk corner for tomorrow. On his way out, he saw Instructor Mora shift the brass bell off the center of her desk and set it near the doorjamb, neither hidden nor aimed at the rows. The Blue student nudged his own chair six inches inward to keep the aisle straight to the hall, and someone else pushed the chalk tray closer without looking up for thanks.

For the first time since rescue, tomorrow looked less like a drill and more like a room where his chalk would still be waiting.

---

Lunch in the courtyard was loud enough to put Iven's teeth on edge.

At Noctis, meals stayed quiet because every seat was assigned by rank, behavior, recovery level, and usefulness. Portions arrived pre-measured; talking too much meant pride, talking too little meant hiding something, and dropping a spoon could turn into a lesson if an instructor felt like making an example.

Aurelis didn't know how to keep lunch orderly. Students clustered on benches and low stone walls, two Blue girls argued over herb bread while a Red boy juggled three bowls for late friends, and a kitchen aide walked between rows handing apples out of a wicker basket without checking a single name off a list. The noise made Iven's ribs tight, and the smell of hot broth made him hungry anyway.

He took a tray because Maelin had warned him on day one to eat before his body filed a formal complaint, choosing soup, bread, and an apple before scanning the courtyard edges for somewhere quiet.

Then he saw the girl.

She was younger than him, eleven at most—brought over under Magistra Voss's emergency border sanctuary sweep after the Valise intake—curled near the north planter with her tray balanced on her knees and her shoulders pulled inward to make the smallest possible target. Her dark hair was chopped blunt at her jaw, and one sleeve of her oversized Aurelis sweater hung completely past her wrist. Nobody sat within ten feet of her, not out of cruelty, but because half the courtyard had been lectured by Maelin, Mira, and Rhea until everyone walked wide around Noctis transfers out of nervous politeness.

Iven knew that hunch in her spine. Minimum footprint. Hunger tucked out of sight.

He didn't walk straight up to her. Instead, he drifted toward a stretch of low stone wall angled the same way she was facing, close enough to hear if she spoke and far enough that she wouldn't have to brace herself to guard the gap between them. He sat. In the direction of beside. Not beside.

He broke open his bread. She kept her eyes on her bowl.

Around them, the courtyard kept moving. Aira hurried past hugging a stack of papers taller than her chin, Cassian trailing behind her with a second bundle and the harried look of a man losing a fight with the wind. Near the central fountain, Professor Halden and President Valmont bent over a yellowed map with matching frowns. The girl ignored all of it, eating her soup in small, even spoonfuls.

After several minutes, she spoke without turning her head.

Transfer Girl: "You don't have to."

Iven finished chewing and swallowed before answering.

Iven: "I know."

Her spoon paused halfway to the bowl.

Transfer Girl: "Then why are you doing it?"

Too many answers crowded up at once: because nobody had sat near him soon enough, because Mira had shown him how copies traveled, because Sera had sent sweets back, because Cael had lent him a thermal charm without keeping a ledger, because Foundation might mean something better than Noctis had taught them. None of it belonged on the shoulders of a kid guarding a bowl of soup.

Iven: "I had to sit somewhere. This wall was empty."

Her mouth pressed flat, half skeptical, half willing to drop it. They went back to eating.

A breeze stirred the courtyard and lifted the corner of a sheet pinned to the public board. Someone had copied Cael's final line in blue pencil there, making the words look softer against the wood. Underneath it, three different hands had written the line that survived the burnt Noctis script:

**WITNESS REMAINS WHERE CIRCLE FAILS.**

Iven still didn't understand Circle Zero, and he was glad nobody expected him to figure it out before afternoon classes. The girl finally followed his eyes toward the board.

Transfer Girl: "Is that about him?"

Iven: "Cael?"

Transfer Girl: "The Black Circle."

Iven glanced toward the witness cottage across the yard. Haru Aerlen wasn't outside.

Iven: "Maybe partly."

Transfer Girl: "Noctis says he contaminated the ruling."

The words came out flat and worn smooth, the way sentences sounded after you repeated them enough times to stop hearing them.

Iven: "Noctis says lots of things."

Transfer Girl: "Aurelis says lots of things too."

Iven: "They do."

She turned her head enough to fix dark, sharp eyes on him, already practiced at expecting tricks.

Transfer Girl: "Then how do you tell which ones are traps?"

Iven thought of neat block print without a stray stroke. He thought of Mira's exaggerated tilted L and the thick corrected T on his own sheet.

Iven: "I don't always."

Her face began to shut down, so he kept talking before she wrote him off.

Iven: "Traps usually need you to answer faster than you can think."

She looked back down at her broth. One sleeve had slipped half an inch, though her wrist stayed covered.

Iven: "Nobody rushes you here."

Transfer Girl: "Who said?"

Iven almost said Maelin, then pictured Maelin marching over to lecture a tray about pulse safety and kept quiet.

Iven: "People keep proving it."

Transfer Girl: "People can stop."

Iven: "They could."

Her spoon tapped the inside of her bowl once, softly. She waited, shoulders tense, as if checking whether someone would call out a posture correction. Nothing happened. Across the yard, the cracked west bell gave one stray clang when someone shoved the tower door open too hard. A handful of students laughed; the girl flinched at the noise, then blinked when nobody turned the laugh toward her.

Iven tore another piece off his crust.

Transfer Girl: "What Circle are you?"

He held his wrist out slightly above his knee. Faint green light answered through his skin without flaring. The girl stared at it.

Transfer Girl: "Foundation."

Iven: "That's what the primer says."

Transfer Girl: "Maintenance rank."

There it was, polished clean enough to hide the insult. Iven looked at the pale green ring and thought of Sera's kitchen string, of the repair aide leaving his heel scorch mark alone, of Mira's ink-stained knuckles holding the courtyard record together. He was barely fourteen, a junior-wing first-year with no rank to throw around, which meant anything he said wouldn't sound like another rulebook trying to replace Noctis.

Iven: "Foundation means the part that holds while everything else falls."

She studied him for a long beat. He probably said it clumsily; Mira would have phrased it cleaner, and Rhea would have turned it into committee policy before supper. The girl didn't seem to want policy.

Slowly, she shifted her forearm. Her cuff slid back just far enough to bare an unsteady ring of pale green light, faint at the edges like even her magic had learned to hide. Iven looked once, then turned back to his bread.

Iven: "You don't have to show it."

Transfer Girl: "I know."

She sounded like she was testing how the words tasted in her own mouth. They finished both trays without talking again. When Iven stood up, her shoulders tightened right back to where they had started. He lifted his tray in both hands so she could see it clearly.

Iven: "I'm taking this to the rack."

Transfer Girl: "I can see that."

Her voice had a real prickle of annoyance in it now instead of pure armor. Iven took his tray to the return table and didn't look back until he set it down. She was still sitting by the north planter, and her sleeve was still pushed back an inch above her wrist.

---

The fourth copy of Iven's sentence ended up on the public board by accident, or so Aira claimed. She had jotted a copy onto a loose scrap before Iven left the archive table that morning, and because Aira lied with great charm while keeping terrifyingly neat files, nobody believed the accident story more than halfway.

Still, there it sat below a cluster of witness notes near the fountain: **THIS IS NOT A TRADE.** First Iven's thick-stemmed T, then Mira's dark period, then Leon's drooping final word under Cael's slate stone, and now Aira's unpunctuated version that somehow looked sharper without a period.

By midafternoon, someone copied it onto a torn corner and pinned it outside the infirmary door. By evening, a kitchen aide chalked it inside the pantry frame where students grabbing extra rolls could read it without anybody making a speech over their shoulders. One of the younger transfers misread the last word and copied it onto a slate strip as **THIS IS NOT A TRIAL**, which Maelin promptly declared medically sound.

Iven spotted that crooked version after dinner and stood staring at it for a full minute. **Not a trade. Not a trial.** The mistake had changed the sentence without breaking it.

He crossed the courtyard to the archive station, where Mira was tying the day's public bundles in brown paper against the night damp while Aira stacked wooden record trays behind her.

Iven: "Someone copied it wrong."

Mira followed his glance toward the pantry doorway.

Mira: "I saw."

She didn't reach for a pencil to go fix it.

Iven: "The mistake works too."

Mira's fingers paused over the twine before pulling the knot tight.

Mira: "Sometimes. Not every mistake is freedom, Iven. Plenty are just sloppiness or lies, and people deserve clear records when clarity matters. Ugly copies only work because we keep the versions side by side instead of pretending every error turns into truth."

Iven glanced back at the pantry door.

Iven: "Can that one stay?"

Mira: "That one can stay."

From behind a stack of record sleeves, Aira spoke without looking up.

Aira: "I give it three days before someone starts a committee around it."

Mira: "I'll sabotage the committee."

Aira: "Excellent. I'll take minutes."

Iven smiled, easier this time.

Then a Red Circle third-year walked up to ruin the quiet.

She had two friends flanking her, a glossy braid over one shoulder, and a polished wooden donation box that looked like it had never met a scuffed floor. Taped across the front was a crisp white sign:

**GIFTS FOR NOCTIS RECOVERY STUDENTS**

Beneath it, lettered in neat, sweeping ink:

**THIS IS NOT A TRADE**

Iven looked at the sign, and his stomach dropped like he had missed a step in the dark. The third-year gave him a warm, practiced smile.

Red Third-Year: "You're Iven, right?"

For one strange second, Iven realized nobody here could force him to confirm his own name. At Noctis, hesitation was an offense; here, he actually had a choice. He didn't take the lie.

Iven: "Iven Sahl."

Dalia Renn: "I'm Dalia Renn. My family supplies winter linens to three hospitals. We saw your phrase on the board and thought it was beautiful."

*Beautiful.* The word coated the sentence like thick varnish. Mira's fingers tightened around the twine on her paper bundle. Aira stepped forward behind the archive table, posture already squared for a fight, making the mistake of thinking being right would be enough.

Aira: "Donation boxes require registration with the student welfare office, public ledger review, and anti-patronage clearance."

Dalia's smile didn't budge.

Dalia Renn: "Of course. We want to handle everything properly."

The word *properly* made Iven's shoulders creep toward his ears. Aira loved fighting paperwork with stricter paperwork, and in most rooms she would have shredded Dalia in thirty seconds. This trap wore satin ribbons. Dalia turned the box slightly so the copied line caught the courtyard lanterns.

Dalia Renn: "We only wanted to help. Unless help is offensive now."

A few passing students slowed down to watch. If Iven pushed back, he looked ungrateful; if Mira stepped in, Green witness work looked territorial; if Aira kept citing policy, Dalia became the generous girl bullied by petty clerks. Aira opened her mouth to cite another bylaw.

Mira stomped straight down on Aira's boot.

Aira let out a squeak and snapped her teeth shut. Mira didn't even blink.

Mira: "You copied it wrong."

Dalia blinked.

Dalia Renn: "Pardon?"

Mira pointed straight at the crisp white card.

Mira: "Your T is too clean."

Aira stared at Mira. Iven stared too. Mira slid Iven's original sheet out from under the loose bundle string, showing the dark, doubled stem on **THIS**.

Mira: "That line came from a specific hand. The correction mark is part of it."

Dalia's smile thinned at the corners.

Dalia Renn: "We can fix the lettering."

Mira: "No."

The flatness of it seemed to catch Mira by surprise too; red crept up her cheeks, making her look younger than she hated looking in front of upper-years, yet she didn't back down an inch.

Mira: "You don't fix it by making the error prettier. You ask him whether he wants his line on your box."

Dalia finally looked at Iven directly, not as a charity case or a talking point, just an obstacle in her way. It stung less than the varnish.

Dalia Renn: "Do you?"

Every trained instinct in Iven's head pushed him to soften the answer. Noctis said yes, because refusing charity meant arrogance. Aurelis liked long, polite questions you could hide inside. Then he remembered Sera's note in his sweater pocket, written because Sera had said the words and glared at the pencil.

Iven: "No."

Dalia's two friends shifted behind her. Her smile vanished for half a second before habit dragged it back.

Dalia Renn: "Then we'll take the phrase off."

Iven looked at the polished box. At the word **GIFTS**. At the narrow slot cut into the lid where coins would drop out of sight before anyone ever chose what taking them meant.

Iven: "And the box."

Dalia Renn: "The box is just for collection."

Iven: "It looks like a mouth."

It wasn't a policy argument. It wouldn't go cleanly into council minutes. Still, Aira's face shifted from startled to horrified as she realized how close she had come to arguing Dalia into winning the wrong fight.

Aira: "Iven's right."

She sounded stiff, unused to being corrected in public and hating the taste of it.

Aira: "I was arguing welfare registration. This isn't a registration problem."

Mira, still flushed to her ears, muttered just loud enough for the table to hear.

Mira: "Miracle."

Aira: "Do not enjoy this."

Mira: "I'm enjoying it privately."

Aira: "You are doing it with your entire face."

Dalia looked between the two girls and realized the small crowd had stopped gathering around her box and started watching Iven instead, waiting to see what he would say next. That attention pressed heavy too. He shook it off.

Iven: "If people want to leave food, put it on a table. No names. No box. No sign."

Dalia's jaw set.

Dalia Renn: "Then how will anyone know where it came from?"

Mira's answer snapped out half a beat too fast.

Mira: "They won't."

Dalia looked genuinely insulted, and somehow that made Iven breathe easier. Her kindness needed witnesses; his hunger didn't owe her one. Dalia ripped the sign off the wood hard enough to tear the tape, making Aira flinch at the damaged paper, then marched back across the gravel with her friends and her polished box.

---

By lunch the next day, a plain tray sat beside the pantry door holding bread, oranges, wrapped sweets, and one folded card in kitchen handwriting:

**TAKE IF USEFUL. LEAVE IF NOT.**

Nobody signed it. Mira copied it anyway while they sat at the archive table, adding one tiny line along the bottom margin:

**Better than Dalia's box.**

She caught Iven reading over her arm and tilted the page slightly toward him.

Mira: "That note isn't going in the public file."

Iven: "It's petty."

Mira: "Very."

She didn't apologize for it either.

Mira: "Some records get to belong to me before they have to be useful."

Iven thought of his thick-stemmed T, Leon's tilted word under the slate stone, and the girl by the north planter keeping an inch of green ring uncovered.

Iven: "Good."

The plain tray lasted exactly one hour before someone tried to organize it.

This time it wasn't Dalia. It was a Green Circle boy with ink smeared on both cuffs and the tight, anxious eyes of someone who treated usefulness like armor. Garren Brann, Iven thought, or something close to it from the sorting shifts. He carried a scrap sheet ruled into three neat ink columns:

**FOOD LEFT**

**FOOD TAKEN**

**BALANCE**

He looked proud and terrified at the same time. Mira took one look at the page and let out a tiny groan.

Garren: "It's not an official ledger or anything. I just figured if people knew stock levels, they could restock before the tray ran empty."

Aira visibly bit the inside of her cheek to stop herself from answering first.

Mira: "Why balance?"

Garren tapped the third column.

Garren: "So nobody takes too much."

Ordinary words, until you heard the old lock clicking inside them. Across the gravel, the younger transfer girl from the north planter had walked up to the pantry tray for an orange. Her sleeve was pushed back far enough to show pale green. Halfway to the fruit, her hand stopped, curled empty in midair, and dropped back to her side.

Iven saw her freeze. Garren saw it a second later, and all the pride drained out of his face.

Garren: "I didn't mean to..."

His apology stumbled out fast, the kind of panicked guilt that forced the hurt person to comfort the one who tripped them. The girl took a step back.

Iven reached out before he thought twice, not toward her, toward Garren's paper. He plucked the sheet off the table. Garren flinched as if expecting a slap. Looking at the three columns, Iven felt the old Noctis part of his brain read them in half a heartbeat: food left, food taken, balance. Proof that someone had weighed your hunger and logged the debt. Yet Garren wasn't Dalia; he had only tried to build a careful bridge and accidentally made it look like a gate.

Iven: "Tracking supply is useful."

Garren blinked. Iven tore the third column straight down the margin, rough and crooked, splitting the word **balance** into **bal** and **ance**. Aira let out a strangled noise, caught somewhere between respecting the point and mourning torn paper.

Iven held the ragged third strip back out to Garren.

Iven: "This part isn't."

Garren stared at the torn strip, then glanced toward the girl.

Garren: "I'm sorry."

Slower this time, without begging her to smile back and fix his mistake. The girl looked from the tray of oranges to Iven, then back at the table.

Transfer Girl: "Can there be a sign?"

Mira's pencil rose automatically. The girl caught the movement and scowled.

Transfer Girl: "Don't write me down."

Mira dropped the pencil onto the wood right away.

Mira: "Sorry."

Small and plain, leaving the room quiet again. The girl thought for a moment, knuckles white on the hem of her sweater.

Transfer Girl: "A sign that says you can take one and also more than one."

Garren frowned in confusion.

Transfer Girl: "Because one sounds safe until you're still hungry."

Iven's throat tightened. He kept his face still. Aira reached for a blank scrap, caught herself, and slid it toward the girl instead.

Aira: "Do you want to write it?"

The girl shook her head so hard her hair slapped her cheek. Aira didn't argue; she let Garren take the scrap instead. Garren's fingers shook worse than Iven's had the previous morning.

Garren: "What should it say?"

The girl looked back at the fruit.

Transfer Girl: "Take what helps. More can come."

Garren wrote it down in cramped, narrow letters hugging the left edge as if scared of wasting paper. Mira watched without correcting a single stroke, and Garren taped the card above the tray.

**TAKE WHAT HELPS. MORE CAN COME.**

For a couple of minutes, nobody moved toward the food. Then the girl stepped up, picked up two oranges, hugged them tight against her sweater like small warm suns, and walked back toward the north planter without looking at anyone. Garren watched her go.

Garren: "I almost made it bad."

Iven thought of neat copies, crooked copies, polished donation boxes, and balance columns, all the ways good intentions could turn rescue back into a cage if people were too scared to leave anything uncounted.

Iven: "Almost is better than after."

Garren looked down at the torn **ance** strip still sitting in his palm.

Garren: "Can I keep this?"

Iven almost asked why anyone would keep a mistake, then stopped himself.

Iven: "Keep it."

Garren folded the torn strip carefully and tucked it into his pocket like a reminder he didn't want to lose. Beside him, Aira let out a long breath.

Aira: "I hate how educational this has become."

Mira: "You love educational things."

Aira: "I love educational things when I'm the one lecturing."

Mira smiled before she could smooth it into politeness.

Mira: "That's visible."

Aira looked genuinely wounded. Iven walked over to the tray, took one wrapped sweet, read Garren's crooked sign once more, and took a second. Nobody wrote either one down.

By dusk, the sky over Aurelis turned deep violet, and path lamps flickered awake one by one down the gravel walks. At the central fountain, Halden and President Valmont still stood hunched over their yellowed map, neither looking pleased.

Across the basin, the water darkened abruptly at the center, not pitch black like Haru's Circle, just a cold, drained absence where color refused to catch the lamplight. Iven's Green Circle warmed faintly against his wrist. Beneath the surface, the dark reflection hinted at a broad ring broken in four places, with a fifth gap left empty at the heart.

Then the water cleared. No alarm bells rang, and nobody shouted. Halden only gripped the stone rim hard enough to whiten his knuckles, while President Valmont murmured something too low to carry across the yard. Halden nodded fast and folded the map away. Whatever slept beneath Circle Zero could wait one more night.

Iven walked back to the recovery wing with two folded slips in his sweater pocket: one reading **THIS IS NOT A TRADE**, the other **THIS IS NOT A TRIAL**. He wasn't sure which one he needed more, so he kept both.

## Coda: The Name After the Bell

The younger transfer girl found Iven by the north planter the next morning.

She carried a single orange and sat on the low wall with an arm's length of stone between them, close enough to talk without crowding. Iven broke his bread roll in half and laid one piece on the stone. She set the orange beside it.

Transfer Girl: "Not trade."

Iven: "I know."

She studied his face until she decided he meant it.

Down the courtyard, the cracked west bell rang late. Students crossed the gravel toward morning classes without staring or curving wide around the two Green Circle kids on the wall. When the last off-pitch note faded into the eaves, the girl leaned slightly toward him and told him her name in a voice barely louder than the breeze.

Iven repeated it once and stumbled on the middle sound. She corrected him flatly; he tried again until she nodded and stole the smaller half of his bread. He let her take it.

Before either of them finished eating, clear morning water in the fountain caught the sun without darkening a single drop. Below the calm surface, four faint architectural breaks glimmered along the reflected rimstone around a fifth quiet space.

Pale letters surfaced cleanly through the bright water, sharp and unmistakable in the morning light:

**THE FIFTH TOWER REMEMBERS.**

Iven didn't run for Haru. He cupped both hands around his mouth and called Mira.

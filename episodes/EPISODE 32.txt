# Episode 32: **The Fifth Witness**

The first mistake was assuming the window wanted me. That was reasonable, which made it dangerous.

Reasonable mistakes wore better clothes than cruel ones. They entered rooms with clean hands and public safety language. They brought diagrams. They used words like stabilizing, temporary, precautionary, and only until we understand. They looked at the thing no one understood, found the largest power standing near it, and decided that power had to be the handle.

The green window above the cracked west bell stayed lit through dawn. It did not brighten or dim. It simply remained.

That alone turned Aurelis into a building holding its breath.

By breakfast, the official four-tower diagrams had been pulled from two public boards and replaced with blank parchment headed **STRUCTURAL REVIEW IN PROGRESS**. By second bell, three student copies of the old map were circulating anyway, each with a fifth square drawn in a different wrong place. By third bell, someone chalked a green rectangle onto the courtyard stones below the west tower and wrote beside it:

**IF IT HAS A WINDOW, IT HAS A ROOM.**

No one owned up to the chalk. Mira copied the words into her notebook two breaths before a cleaning charm wiped the stones bare.

I found her at the fountain with both elbows pinning down a stack of rough sheets and a smear of ink along her cheek. Aira stood beside her, shielding a second stack from the morning breeze like she was guarding an injured animal made entirely of paperwork.

Leon waited three paces behind them, far enough back to look uninvolved and close enough to step in if anyone decided to test the boundary.

The younger transfer girl who had trusted Iven with her name was sorting duplicates at the copy table. Over by the north planter on Maelin's padded warmer-bench, Iven Sahl sat hunched inside a borrowed academy sweater beside Sera—bundled to the chin in heated wool six days out of Valise, cleared for ten minutes of morning air beside Lysa Pell, who held a steaming herbal mug two paces away under a trauma-wing aide's watch while Sera cradled her empty soup cup in both warming hands.

The fifth window glowed above us. Green.

Neither signal fire nor ordinary lamp, the steady light made the rest of the stone tower look strangely unsure of itself.

Elira's fingers brushed my sleeve.

She had insisted on coming down. Maelin had argued for seventeen minutes before trapping her in terms she could not outmaneuver: fifteen minutes standing, five minutes sitting, no stairs, and no heroic speeches before lunch. Elira had accepted the first three. The fourth remained under appeal.

She wore her pale blue cloak today over her shoulder brace and taped cut palm, the silver-veined pendant tucked out of sight inside her collar where one fingertip could still reach its clasp. From ten paces off she looked composed. Up close, I saw the cost in the pause before she turned her head, the deliberate set of her shoulders, and the half-breath delay before her smile reached anyone. People kept seeing alive and mistaking it for healed, because neat labels saved the world from having to apologize.

Mira looked up as we stopped beside the table.

Mira: "I have six versions."

Aira: "Seven, if you count the one where someone drew the Fifth Tower as a layered cake."

Mira: "I am not counting that."

Aira: "Coward."

Mira tapped her pencil toward the basin without lifting her eyes from the page.

Mira: "The water keeps reflecting the tower wrong. Only when Green students stand near the rim."

Leon: "Define near."

Mira: "I tried. The fountain objected."

Leon's brows drew together.

Leon: "How does water object?"

Aira held out a sheet covered in crossed-out numbers.

Aira: "By making my measurements look like a lie."

Leon read it, his mouth tightening.

Leon: "This says the same student stood two paces from the fountain, four paces from the fountain, and directly beneath the west bell without moving."

Aira: "All three at once."

Her pen hovered over the margin.

Leon: "That is not measurement."

Aira: "Correct. That is embarrassment."

Mira scribbled another line onto her corner sheet.

Mira: "Embarrassment confirmed by water."

I looked into the basin. Clear surface. Ordinary ripples. Four official tower reflections shivered around the stone rim in the morning light.

When Mira leaned closer, a fifth shape appeared beneath them, less a tower than a vertical green line seen through deep water rather than air.

The cold behind my sternum answered. Old metal. Ozone. Winter stone. Under Maelin's cool blue testing cloth wound smoothly over my bruised fingernail—no winter glove after yesterday's clinic surrender—the fabric stayed cold against the nail bed. No pull. Only recognition.

I kept my boots still. Elira noticed at once.

Elira: "It is not dragging at you."

Haru: "No."

Aira's eyes flicked from my face up to the green window.

Aira: "That is the kindest thing ancient architecture has ever done for my schedule."

I shook my head.

Haru: "It might be worse."

Mira's pencil halted.

In the basin, the fifth reflection bent away from me and toward her ink-stained page.

Sera spoke from Maelin's heated bench by the north planter. Her voice was quiet, yet the courtyard had learned not to talk over small voices.

Sera: "It is humming."

Iven turned to look at her.

Iven: "The window?"

Sera shook her head.

Sera: "The floor."

Every Green Circle student within twenty paces looked down. They did not move in unison, which would have looked rehearsed. They lowered their eyes one by one, a half-second apart, like rain finding the same drain. Mira's expression changed first.

Still kneeling beside the fountain with a pencil caught between two fingers, she watched the Green Circle at her wrist wake. It did not flare with power or fear. It lit with direction.

A thin green line surfaced beneath her shoe.

A second lit beneath Iven's boot, curling sideways to brush Sera's heated bench and cup—no Circle waking on her own wrist, only stone acknowledging her beside Iven. Across the outer ring, another lit under Finn as he stood clutching armfuls of copied maps, and then half a dozen more.

The lines did not race toward the west tower. They walked, slow and patient beneath the paving stones, like an old hallway remembering it had once been allowed to exist.

Rhea's voice cut across the courtyard.

President Valmont: "No one follows the lines alone."

A good order, issued half a breath too late. The fountain rang.

The note traveled low through the stone and climbed straight up the soles of my boots. Students gasped. A Blue Circle boy near the public board stumbled back. Someone dropped a breakfast tray, porcelain shattering loud enough that Elira flinched.

I kept my hands at my sides. Her fingers found mine anyway. Choice first. Always.

The green lines brightened. Mira rose to her feet, and Leon stepped to her shoulder before anyone could tell him to.

Mira: "I did not decide to walk."

Aira's pen flew across her pad.

Aira: "Record: Mira Castellan reports no decision to walk."

Mira: "I also did not decide not to."

Aira: "Wonderful. Ancient ambiguity. My favorite breakfast illness."

Iven stood from the bench, and Sera's hand shot out to catch his sleeve. He froze immediately. That mattered more than the light beneath his boots.

Iven: "Do you want me to stay?"

Sera looked down at the green path under her shoes, then at the empty cup in her hands.

Sera: "I want to not go first."

Iven nodded once.

Iven: "Then you do not go first."

The line under Sera's boots dimmed to a quiet pulse, waiting without tugging.

Maelin had materialized beside Elira with the silent speed of an angry healer. She pointed down at the stone.

Maelin: "Walls that ask permission. Of course. Because an ordinary staircase was too much to ask."

Halden arrived at a half-run and skidded to a halt only when Rhea raised one finger.

Professor Halden: "Do not seal it."

President Valmont: "I was about to give you the same order."

Halden looked wounded for half a second before the green lines caught his eyes and stripped him of all dignity.

Professor Halden: "The layout is impossible. The west tower has no inner stair above the fourth service level. Its outer stonework cannot support a fifth room."

Aira: "The wall has filed a formal disagreement."

Mira took a single step forward. No stone shifted, yet the entire courtyard tilted with her attention.

The green line lit ahead of her under the flagstones, curving toward the west arch. It bypassed the main tower doors and aimed straight for a narrow service entrance beside the bell wall, a storage door I had walked past six times that week without ever wondering what sat behind it.

Mira glanced back at Aira, then at Rhea, and at me last. She was not waiting for permission. She was checking whether I was about to become the mistake.

Mira: "Should I stop?"

Every adult within earshot drew breath to answer. Halden's scholarly lecture, Rhea's Student Council rule, Maelin's healer warning, Seraphine's battle-trained caution from the edge of the crowd, and Valen's historical warning as she hurried up behind them with her coat half-buttoned and her cedar ward case tucked under one arm.

Leon spoke before any of them.

Leon: "Do you want to?"

Mira blinked. Beneath her boot, the green line pulsed once.

Mira: "No."

Leon: "Then do not go alone."

Aira's pen paused for a beat, then scratched furiously. Rhea studied Leon, something close to approval sharpening her eyes.

President Valmont: "Castellan. Sahl. Varek. You may approach the service doorway as student witnesses. Nobody steps past that door without saying so out loud and keeping a clear line back out."

Halden drew breath to protest.

President Valmont: "Professor Halden will remain behind the first line."

Halden let the breath out in pure scholarly misery.

Elira squeezed my hand once.

Elira: "And Haru?"

The courtyard went dangerously quiet, waiting to see if the second mistake would step forward. Neither Rhea nor I answered.

Above us, the fifth window burned green.

A student near the back whispered into the hush.

Green Student: "Shouldn't the Black Circle go?"

There was no malice in it, only ordinary fear reaching for the closest weapon.

An older, smoother voice answered from behind the student ring.

Council Aide: "That may be the safest arrangement."

The crowd parted beside the aide for Architect Sovan Reith, a tired-looking Council specialist in emergency structures.

Architect Reith: "Magistra Voss sent me to observe the threshold. My brief calls this a classification event, though the wording is already out of date."

Aira: "Promising survival instinct."

Rhea held him behind the first green line. Sovan accepted the boundary without argument and flipped open his portfolio.

Inside the service door, a lock clicked.

Mira turned back toward the stone frame. Iven stepped to her left, Leon to her right. The heavy door swung inward on silent hinges without anyone touching the latch.

Green-lit darkness waited beyond.

Mira: "There is writing on the floor."

Aira: "Read only. Do not step in until-"

The paving stones under Mira's boots slid inward. Leon lunged and caught her shoulder; Iven grabbed Leon's sleeve. In one smooth pull, all three vanished past the doorway.

The door stayed wide open.

The courtyard did not scream. It had outgrown screaming.

Aira swore sharply enough to make three first-years blink, then sprinted for the arch. Rhea's red seal snapped across the threshold a beat before Aira reached it, leaving the doorway open while holding the crowd back.

President Valmont: "Nobody crosses."

I was already moving.

Elira's hand tightened on mine, neither dragging me back nor pretending the pull was not real. Beneath my jacket, the Black Circle pressed hard against my ribs. High above, the fifth window flared brighter. Sovan caught the change at once.

So did half the courtyard. The third mistake waited right there on the stones.

If I crossed that line now, every frightened rumor in Aurelis would harden into fact. Black Circle opens the tower. Black Circle brings them back. Black Circle keeps the door. A throne by another name, dressed up in rescue.

Elira spoke softly beside me.

Elira: "Threshold."

A reminder, not an order. I planted my boots one inch short of the green line. Every muscle in my back hated me for stopping.

Beyond the open door, the darkness breathed out cold stone air. Mira's voice drifted back, muffled and thin.

Mira: "We are in."

Aira dropped to her knees right against Rhea's red seal, notebook flat on the paving stones.

Aira: "Define in."

Mira: "Working on it."

Leon: "Badly."

Mira: "Unhelpful."

Iven: "The stairs are sideways."

Aira raised her eyes to the ceiling.

Aira: "That is deeply informative and I hate it."

Air returned to the courtyard, ragged but shared. I kept both feet behind the green line. Sovan watched me refuse the step, his expression shifting slightly.

Architect Reith: "You could reach them faster."

Haru: "I could."

Architect Reith: "And you are choosing not to."

I held his gaze without leaning toward the arch.

Haru: "Because faster is not the same as safer."

High above the cracked bell, the green window dimmed by a single breath, caught somewhere between warning and consent.

## Mira Castellan

Mira had decided long ago that panic was a waste of ink.

Panic had its uses if you needed to sprint, yell, spot smoke, or dodge falling masonry. It was simply terrible at spelling.

So when the service doorway swallowed her, Leon, and Iven into a stairwell that could not physically fit inside the west tower, Mira did not scream. She knelt on the cold stone, braced her pad on her thigh, and wrote:

**ENTRY UNCHOSEN. NOT OPPOSED.**

She underlined the second phrase twice because her knuckles were shaking hard enough to turn the letters jagged. Jagged was fine. Jagged meant she had written it first.

The space around them refused to stay one room. Seven paces of level stone ended in a staircase bolted sideways up the masonry. Beneath that landing, a low window looked down on the courtyard from thirty feet up even though they had climbed nothing. A second window to her left peered up through the fountain basin from underneath, warping Aira's crouched silhouette like a furious fish.

Leon planted himself between Mira and the open drop at the edge of the floor. Water-thin blue light ringed his wrist, tight and disciplined. Behind them, Iven pressed one palm flat against the rough stone wall.

Mira: "Are you hurt?"

Iven: "No."

Leon: "Certain?"

Iven shot him a sharp look.

Iven: "Noctis drilled certain into us as a tone of voice before they ever cared if it was true."

Leon took the hit without flinching, though color crept up his neck.

Leon: "Then tell me you are not certain."

Iven blinked, surprised, then let his shoulders drop an inch.

Iven: "Not certain. Nothing feels broken."

Mira wrote that down word for word.

Under Iven's palm, the masonry warmed. Thin threads of green light seeped from his fingertips into the mortar, answering the tower rather than forcing it. Something inside the walls rustled like thick pages turning in an adjacent room.

Aira's voice echoed through the crooked air behind them.

Aira: "Mira?"

Mira glanced back. The service doorway hovered six paces behind them, yet also appeared below their boots and reflected in the side glass. Framed through it, she saw Aira kneeling at the red barrier, Haru holding steady behind the green line with Elira at his shoulder, and the Council architect with his portfolio watching Haru far too closely, like an engineer sizing up a storm he hoped to bottle.

She did not like the man. Probably unfair. She wrote large in the top margin anyway and angled the pad toward the door gap:

**COUNCIL ARCHITECT WATCHING HARU TOO HARD. KEEP THIS LINE.**

Aira's voice sharpened down the stairwell.

Aira: "Saw you flag that line! Good. Keep putting on paper whatever feels off."

Leon eyed the distant doorway.

Leon: "Can we walk back?"

Iven lifted his hand from the wall. The stairwell behind them folded inward, cutting the seven-step landing down to five and narrowing the sideways stair into a rail-less bridge. Below the landing, the courtyard view blinked out and became an empty classroom where every wooden chair faced the blank wall.

Mira's throat dried out.

Iven: "I know that room."

Leon: "From Noctis?"

Iven took his time answering, and Mira kept her pencil still. People gave better testimony when nobody dragged the words out of them.

Iven: "It is not a real room. It was an image from an obedience primer. They used to project old collapses to show what happened when lower ranks refused their assigned seats."

The glass flickered. One chair in the silent classroom scraped around to face them.

A girl sat in it, half-faded at the edges like charcoal rubbed by a sleeve. Fifteen, maybe sixteen, with dark hair chopped blunt at her jaw and an old-style uniform whose cuffs had been patched twice over. A Green Circle glowed directly over her sternum, bleeding straight through the wool as if the memory had forgotten clothes were meant to hide skin.

Her face blurred whenever Mira tried to hold it. Then Mira dropped her eyes to the girl's fingers. Ink stained every knuckle. Mira's breath caught.

Leon: "Castellan."

Mira: "I see her."

Iven: "She was in the primer."

The girl's mouth moved without sound. Words stamped themselves across the classroom plaster in crisp official lettering:

**UNCLASSIFIED ATTENDANT.**

Her lips moved again, silent and insistent. The plaster lettering flashed brighter, erasing the space around it.

**UNCLASSIFIED ATTENDANT.**

Mira's grip cramped around her pencil.

Leon: "What do we do?"

Iven's face had drained of color.

Iven: "The primer said she abandoned her support post during a pre-Circle collapse. They claimed her interference broke the chain of command."

Mira: "What was her name?"

Iven swallowed hard.

Iven: "They never gave her one."

A low groan ran through the masonry. Stone flexed beneath their boots, imitating an old cave-in. Leon threw both palms down, and a sheet of blue water snapped taut along the peeling edge of the floor, pinning loose grit and fractured stone before gravity could claim them.

Leon: "Mira. Write."

Mira: "I do not know what."

Leon: "Write that."

Mira braced the sheet against her knee and pressed graphite deep into the paper.

**WE DO NOT KNOW HER NAME.**

The official script across the classroom wall stuttered.

The girl in the chair turned her head away from Mira and fixed straight onto Iven. Iven backed up a step, and Leon shifted with him without letting the water barrier slacken.

Iven: "No."

The tower waited, heavy and listening. Iven curled his fingers into his palms.

Iven: "I am not refusing you. I am saying no so the primer does not put its words in my mouth first."

Mira scribbled his exact phrasing down the margin. The green glow beneath their boots steadied.

Faintly, through the warped doorway, courtyard voices drifted up. Haru saying something too low to catch, Elira answering, Aira cursing under her breath, Rhea snapping an order. Life kept moving outside.

Inside, the girl in the chair mouthed the same silent line again. Mira ignored the blurring eyes and impossible plaster; she watched the girl's jaw. She had taken notes from enough frightened witnesses to catch a four-word rhythm. Correction, not a plea.

Mira: "One more time."

The girl's lips moved slowly. *I. Was. Fifth. Witness.*

Mira jammed the words onto the page before fear could polish them.

**SHE SAYS: I WAS FIFTH WITNESS.**

The stairwell lurched.

Leon swore and drove one heel against the cracking stone. His Blue Circle flared, the water barrier bowing inward as a hairline fracture raced toward his boot. Iven lunged for his sleeve.

Leon: "Not me! The wall, hold the wall!"

Iven slapped both palms against the stone. Green light answered at once.

Iven: "Still behind the glass! She won't cross—she looks frozen out of reach!"

His shout carried back down the crooked stair past the open threshold, and an instant later a thin child's voice answered from outside the doorway.

From far down the twisted corridor, Sera's thin voice echoed back through the stone.

Sera: "Ask if she wants near!"

A laugh almost escaped Mira's throat, sharp with relief. Only Sera, who knew what it cost to be grabbed without warning, would think to ask a ghost about distance.

Mira: "Do you want us near?"

The memory girl's features washed out completely until only the official stamp remained across the plaster.

**UNCLASSIFIED ATTENDANT.**

Then a crack split the first letter down the middle.

The girl raised one ink-stained hand and laid it flat against the inside of the classroom glass.

Mira wedged her notebook onto one bent knee and pressed her free palm against the cold stone beside the window frame, close enough to answer without reaching through. Iven mirrored her on the other side, while Leon stayed locked in place, teeth gritted as he held the floor together with both hands. Mira wrote left-handed and crooked across the page:

**NEAR REQUESTED. NO TOUCH ATTEMPTED. LEON HOLDING FLOOR. IVEN AND MIRA AT WALL.**

The classroom dissolved around the girl. Chairs screeched sideways, tipping over or vanishing until only hers remained, and behind it rose a sunken council chamber with four high-backed stone seats and one bare standing square down by the flagstones.

The girl stood in the square while faceless adults argued above her. Their robes kept only their colors: blue, red, yellow, and a pale silver that set Mira's teeth on edge.

She tried to speak. The chamber walls wrote straight over her head.

**SUPPORT PRESENCE.**

She opened her mouth again.

**ATTENDANT.**

A third time.

**GREEN WITNESS UNNECESSARY TO FINAL RECORD.**

Mira bore down so hard the pencil point punched straight through the paper.

Mira: "No."

The word rang out flat and clear. Beside her, Iven's voice trembled with sudden heat.

Iven: "Noctis uses that exact diagram."

Leon: "Uses what?"

Iven: "That shape. They drilled into us that support hands carry, warm, fetch, and copy, and never get to say what happened."

His Green Circle burned brighter, sharper rather than larger.

Iven: "They built that rule straight into our bones."

Mira stared at the bare standing square beneath the four high seats, where a fifth chair had never been built. She flipped to a fresh corner and wrote:

**OLD RECORD ERROR: GREEN WITNESS FILED AS SUPPORT.**

The stone groaning stopped. Sweat ran down Leon's temple into his collar as he kept the water sheet locked.

Leon: "Fixed?"

A fresh crack split another inch toward his heel.

Leon: "Not fixed. Understood."

Mira: "Can you hold it?"

Old Leon would have turned the question into swagger. This Leon looked at the spreading crack, at Iven's bleeding knuckles on the wall, at Mira's torn sheet, and gave her the honest answer.

Leon: "Not long."

Mira: "Thank you."

He blinked once and nodded.

The chamber dissolved into a hallway choked with gray smoke. No open flame remained, only the aftermath. Official colors smoldered across locked doors while thin green light traced the baseboards, showing where people had crawled to breathe.

The girl came through the smoke with a small child wedged under one arm and a wooden record box pinned under the other. Tears cut tracks through the soot on her cheeks, ugly and terrified and stubborn in the plain way people got when nobody else was left to be brave.

A man in red robes reached out to take the box. She shook her head and pulled it back.

Black letters burned into the smoke above them.

**REFUSED TRANSFER TO AUTHORITY.**

Mira wrote straight under the beam of light:

**KEPT THE RECORD OUT OF THE WRONG HANDS.**

The tower let out a sound like a lung finally emptying.

Across the glass, the girl lifted her chin. For the first time, her face held steady: dark eyes, ink smeared across her chin, and a thin white scar cutting through one eyebrow.

Mira did not know her, yet her chest ached like she had lost someone.

Iven breathed out in shock.

Iven: "I know that scar."

Mira: "Where?"

Iven pressed his fingers harder into the stone.

Iven: "In the obedience hall at Noctis. One of the carved wall warnings. They labeled her First Disobedience."

Leon: "Noctis has her?"

Iven started to shake his head, then caught himself.

Iven: "A copy. A warning. Or maybe a route. I do not know."

Mira wrote every word.

Inside the smoke, the girl pressed four fingers to her chest, then pointed straight down at the record box under her arm.

Mira: "Name?"

The green lights along the stairwell guttered. Somewhere outside the walls, courtyard voices surged in sudden argument.

## Haru - The Reasonable Mistake

While the courtyard waited on the dark stair, Sovan Reith had made himself useful. He cleared frightened aides behind the outer ribbon and shut down a noble delegation trying to force open a private relay. When voices rose near the threshold, though, he reached for the reasonable mistake.

Architect Reith: "Naming a temporary keeper would stop worse titles from sticking."

Elira, seated on the bench Maelin had dragged to the edge of the line, looked up at him.

Elira: "You want to stop a myth by giving it an office badge."

Architect Reith: "I want someone answerable if that door opens wider."

Haru: "Then count the people already answering for it right here."

I gestured across the courtyard: Rhea's restricted campus seal, Aira kneeling at the threshold, Maelin's triage line, Valen and Halden over the old maps, and the Green aides passing Mira's notes from hand to hand. Inside Valen's open ward case, the Regalia shard swung once toward the tower and went dark.

Sovan read the refusal clearly enough to step back.

Architect Reith: "Then the three inside answer before I write up a plan."

Aira: "Write down the question. Leave room for their answer."

Sovan uncapped his pen and wrote it across the top of a blank sheet.

The argument died before it could swell into another hearing. And inside the tower, Mira found a name.

## Mira Castellan - A Name Is Not Enough

E. Vell.

An initial was never enough, and Mira knew it the second the letters formed. Institutions loved initials because initials turned people into spare parts: E. Vell could patch channels, haul boxes, sit in the margins, and end up on a bronze plaque nobody read unless a professor needed a footnote.

The girl in the smoke had a mouth, ink-stained knuckles, a scar through her brow, a Green Circle over her heart, a child on one hip, and a record box under her arm. She deserved a full name.

Mira turned to Iven, whose forearm was corded with green veins of light climbing from the wall.

Mira: "Do you know her first name?"

Iven stared at the wall, jaw tight.

Iven: "The obedience hall never gave warnings first names."

Leon grunted as his water barrier bowed another inch.

Leon: "Can the tower cough it up?"

Mira faced the glass.

Mira: "Can you?"

The smoke corridor shuddered. Official script scrambled to form first across the wall.

**E. VELL. SUPPORT-**

The last word shattered before it could finish. Mira wrote fast:

**OLD LABEL BROKE. PERSON STILL PRESENT.**

Inside the memory, the girl tapped one ink-stained finger against the wooden box. Three taps. Pause. Two taps. Pause. One.

Iven's eyes snapped wide.

Iven: "Archive index."

Mira: "What?"

Iven: "Noctis made us memorize old cross-school index codes for pulling records. Three-two-one is the personal testimony section."

Leon: "Why would Noctis teach you that?"

Iven gave him a look close to pity.

Iven: "So we could haul testimony up before we learned how to question it."

Mira wrote that down too, purely so Aira could stab someone with it later. Then she looked back at the girl.

Mira: "Where is the appendix?"

The stairwell folded around them into a narrow archive aisle. Tall shelves slammed down on both sides, crammed with boxes tagged only by rank color. Green boxes lined the bottom shelf where boots could kick them and eyes could skip right past.

One box rattled violently on the lowest plank.

Leon: "If you tell me we have to fetch that, I am resigning from architecture."

Mira: "You do not work in architecture."

Leon: "Good. Clean break."

The box slid off the shelf and hit the floor without a sound, lid flying open. Inside lay a single half-charred, water-stained page covered in cramped handwriting fighting to fit truth into an inventory form. Mira dropped to her knees beside it.

Leon: "Mira. Floor."

Mira: "I see it."

She kept her fingers off the page and read. Most of the ink swam into blur, except one intact line at the center.

**My name is Eris Vell, and I was not present as furniture.**

Mira's throat closed tight. For one useless second she could only stare. Then Leon let out a sharp breath of pain as the fracture chewed under his boot heel, and useless ended. Mira wrote:

**NAME RECOVERED: ERIS VELL. GREEN CIRCLE. FIFTH WITNESS. NOT FURNITURE.**

The tower did not answer with calm. It triggered an alarm.

Black script tore straight down the archive shelves faster than Mira could track it.

**UNAUTHORIZED PERSONAL RESTORATION**

**NOCTIS JURISDICTION LINK ACTIVE**

Iven jerked backward so hard his hands tore off the wall. Every green line along the floor snapped dark. The flagstones dropped an inch.

Leon threw one shoulder against the heavy shelving and hooked his free arm around Mira's coat to keep her upright. Boxes burst open overhead, dumping clouds of remembered pages that wiped themselves blank before hitting the floor.

At Mira's feet, the box holding Eris Vell's page began dragging backward toward the bottom shelf. Mira lunged for it, but the wood thinned like wet smoke under the black pull; her fingers skidded off the shimmering rim and hit bare cold stone while the box kept sliding.

Mira: "I cannot catch it!"

Iven stared in horror at the black script burning through the shelves.

Iven: "It is running retrieval doctrine."

Leon: "Useful translation?"

Iven flinched at the snap in his voice, and Leon corrected himself mid-breath.

Leon: "Sorry. What do we do?"

The apology cost a heartbeat, and brought Iven back into his own skin. He looked at the tearing pages and the black script trying to drag a dead girl's name back under lock and key.

Iven: "Retrieval needs an authorized carrier."

Mira: "Who?"

The tower answered by branding black script straight across the chest of Iven's borrowed sweater.

**NOCTIS CONDUIT-TRACK PROPERTY: IVEN SAHL**

All expression wiped off Iven's face. Black lines coiled tight around his ribs and yanked him toward the bottom shelf. Leon let go of Mira and caught Iven around the waist with both arms. Blue water flared around Leon's boots, fighting for traction as the tether dragged them both across the stones.

Leon: "You are not carrying anything for them!"

Iven's jaw locked. Noctis had carved those verbs too deep into him: carry, retrieve, return, turn a live boy into a quiet cart for approved truth.

Mira did not argue with the magic. She slapped her notebook page against Iven's upper arm and wrote hard straight over the black lettering.

**IVEN SAHL: PRESENT WITNESS. NOT CARRIER.**

The pencil lead punched through the damp sheet and snagged the wool of his sleeve. The black words stuttered. Iven dragged in one ragged breath.

Iven: "Not carrier."

The tether slackened, and the shelf yanked Eris's box another inch closer to the dark.

Mira: "Then who carries it?"

Leon looked down at his own white knuckles gripping Iven's coat.

Leon: "Nobody."

Mira caught it immediately. Nobody could carry it whole.

She ripped her fresh copy into three ragged strips. On the first she scrawled **ERIS**. On the second, **VELL**. On the third, **FIFTH WITNESS**. She shoved one strip into Iven's fist, slapped the second against Leon's wrist, and kept the third.

Mira: "The name leaves in pieces."

Leon: "That sounds deeply unsafe."

Mira: "For Noctis."

That settled him.

The archive aisle tipped sideways. Shelves buckled into steep walls and the floor melted into a slick chute coated in wet black ink. At the far bottom, a circular Noctis seal yawned wide like a storm drain, swallowing every blank page that slid toward it.

Eris's box shot down the slope. Mira threw herself after it.

Leon swore and lashed water beneath her soles, a thin moving skin skating over the black ink so she stayed upright. Behind them, Iven slapped both palms to the tilting wall, hunting desperately for the buried maintenance beat beneath the Noctis pull.

Mira skidded past dozens of bottom-shelf boxes labeled only by color. Green. Green. Green. Hundreds of lives flattened into shelf numbers.

The drain widened. Freezing air belched up from the hole carrying the clang of Noctis bells, barked orders, and children answering before questions finished. Iven heard home. The green glow died under his hands as the floor pitched steeper.

Leon lost his footing and slammed down on one knee. His water spilled into the drain and surged back up laced with white restraint marks that whipped straight up his forearms.

Leon: "Iven!"

A name thrown like a rope to someone slipping under. Iven squeezed his eyes shut as the obedience hall echoed inside his skull. *Stand straight. Hands visible. Return property.*

He opened his eyes and looked down at the scrap of paper crushed in his fist.

**ERIS.**

A name Noctis had only ever spoken as a threat.

Iven bit the inside of his cheek until the sharp copper taste anchored him in the room.

Iven: "The primer skipped every seventh maintenance beat!"

Sliding fast toward the black drain, Mira yelled back.

Mira: "Why?"

Iven: "So we would never hear the real route!"

He slammed his palm against the stone six times. Waited one beat. Struck a seventh.

Green light blew sideways out of the mortar, shearing straight across the ink. A solid service ledge shot out beneath Mira's boots. She hit it hard, rolled once, and felt the green foundation light catch the half-solid box just solidly enough for her to trap it between both forearms without touching the fragile page inside. Leon diverted his water into the ledge, washing the white restraint marks off his arms and flushing them down the drain with a furious hiss.

Above them, the heavy shelves lurched inward to crush the gap.

Mira: "Leon!"

Leon jammed his back against one wall and drove both boots into the opposite shelving. Blue water flooded the space around him and froze dense under pressure, bracing the aisle open.

Leon: "I am starting to hate historical research!"

Mira: "Hold!"

Leon: "I thought that was implied!"

The green ledge shuddered and slid sideways through the tower. Distant courtyard voices bled through the stone: Aira calling Mira's name, Sera repeating Iven's, Rhea shouting to hold the line without naming a keeper. Haru's voice never came at all, and for once the silence felt like space he was holding open while they worked.

Beneath the ledge, the Noctis drain followed them like a shadow. Black hooks erupted from the ink, snapping straight toward Eris's box.

Mira had no combat magic to cut them. Leon dared not drop a hand from the collapsing walls. Iven held nothing except a torn strip of paper and a service beat Noctis had tried to erase.

So Iven stepped straight between the black hooks and Eris Vell.

The first hook ripped through his borrowed sweater. The second sliced across his cheekbone. The third struck his Green Circle and locked dead.

Noctis doctrine expected Green to fold under an authorized pull. Instead, Iven's Circle simply refused the call.

Iven: "Not carrier."

The hook wrenched harder, drawing blood at his wrist.

Iven: "Witness."

Mira slapped her paper strip onto the lid of the box.

**FIFTH WITNESS.**

Leon ripped one hand free from the wall long enough to slap his down beside it.

**VELL.**

Three scraps. Three hands. Zero authorized carriers.

The black hooks shuddered, unable to decide which third of the name counted as official property, and split their pull three ways. That broke their grip.

Iven tore loose. Mira shoved the box clear. Leon dropped his brace and blasted the entire ledge straight upward on a violent surge of blue water.

They shot up through buckling shelves and a blizzard of blank pages as the Noctis seal snapped shut below. For one jagged heartbeat before the dark closed, Mira glimpsed what waited on the other side: newer stone than Aurelis, black uniforms moving past, and a carved wall figure bearing Eris's brow scar above an obedience slogan. Then the route slammed shut.

All three crashed onto the original level landing in a tangle of elbows and wet wool. Leon hit on his shoulder and let out a single shocked bark of laughter. Iven pressed the back of his sleeve to his bleeding cheek. Mira lunged for the box.

The half-burned page lay intact inside. She laid the three torn strips beside it, and the ragged edges knit together without erasing the tears.

**ERIS VELL. FIFTH WITNESS.**

The tower let out every breath it had been holding.

Every window in the impossible stairwell threw itself open to memory. First the courtyard flared into view across the panes: Aira at the threshold, Rhea's red barrier, Elira standing pale and furious beside the bench, Haru still anchored behind the green line, Sovan clutching his portfolio too tight, and Halden staring up like a man watching a footnote turn into an indictment.

Then the glass peeled back further into older courtyards and forgotten uniforms. A stone fountain split in half. Green aides hauling water buckets, Blue circles shoring up timber beams, Red seals bracing doors, Yellow fire burning dangerously high.

And Eris Vell darting through the smoke between all of them, paper crushed under one arm and a small child clinging to her hip, shouting silently until the tower finally gave her back her voice.

Eris: "Do not name one person the answer!"

The words struck the stone from the inside out.

Leon dropped onto one knee as his water barrier gave out. The floor held anyway. Thin green lines wove together beneath their boots to take the load: Mira's, Iven's, Sera's pale thread reaching in from outside, Finn's near the fountain, and a dozen other Green students who had stayed behind the line and refused to let the path go dark.

Leon stared down at the glowing floor.

Leon: "Oh."

Mira: "Good oh or bad oh?"

Leon swallowed hard.

Leon: "Green oh."

Iven let out a crooked laugh that hurt his cut cheek and counted anyway.

Inside the glass, Eris Vell turned toward them. Clear at last: young, terrified, furious, bone-tired. A person, never a statue or a lesson. Mira raised her pencil.

Mira: "Do you want anything else in the record?"

Eris looked at the torn page in Mira's hands, then across the open glass toward Haru standing steady outside the line. For one cold second, Mira feared the tower would name him anyway.

Instead, Eris gave a small, crooked smile.

Eris: "No center named."

Mira wrote it down.

The tower settled beneath them like an exhausted runner finally allowed to sit. An ordinary stone staircase unfolded at their feet, plain enough to feel almost insulting.

Leon stared down the steps.

Leon: "Now it gives us stairs."

Iven pulled his hand off the wall and his knees nearly buckled. Leon caught him under the arm without a word or flourish. Mira hugged her stack of pages to her ribs and looked back one last time as Eris Vell thinned from figure into quiet light, entering the record rather than vanishing.

Mira: "We are going to write it badly first."

Eris's smile warmed the glass.

Eris: "Good."

Then the stairs carried them down.

## Haru - What Came Back

They stumbled out through the service door covered in stone dust, fading green light, and the raw quiet people carry back from rooms where something true has waited longer than anyone alive.

Leon came out first, eyeing the doorframe like he expected it to bite. Iven leaned heavily against his shoulder, too spent to argue. Mira came last with pages clutched under both arms, a pencil jammed behind one ear, and soot smeared across her cheek like clerk's war paint.

Aira reached her first. She did not grab at the paper; she held out both empty palms.

Aira: "Pages? You? Both?"

Mira eyed her steadily.

Mira: "Both."

Aira took half the stack in one hand and hooked Mira's sleeve with the other.

Aira: "Greedy. Practical. Approved."

Black ink boiled out across the stone threshold behind them.

It slid over the sill in a cold sheet, carrying the Noctis retrieval seal Mira had outrun below. Loose corners of Aira's pages whipped toward the door, and the top sheet tore straight from her grip.

Haru: "Do not touch the seal!"

Sera, pausing on the sheltered infirmary porch two strides off the door with Lysa and Maelin's aide guiding her indoors out of the frost, leaned toward the doorway without stepping into the lane and thrust her taped soup cup forward between Iven and the creeping black ink.

**SERA'S CUP. ASK SERA.**

The black sheet struck the cup's edge and stalled dead.

The spell knew how to seize official archives, haul back Noctis property, and chase a stolen index down a hall. It had no claim on an ordinary tin cup whose owner had written her own rules on tape.

Sera: "No."

The black ink curled back past the porch sill without crossing onto the boards. Iven stepped clear beside her, blood still drying along his cut cheek.

Iven: "Not carrier."

Mira dropped to her knees and slapped the three torn paper strips down onto the flagstones.

**ERIS.**

**VELL.**

**FIFTH WITNESS.**

The retrieval seal lunged for all three at once and split its force again.

Leon, dropped to one knee near the arch and gasping for air, whipped one final thread of water across the stones. It hooked the divided ink currents and snapped them straight inside Rhea's red barrier.

Rhea closed her seal like a fist.

Black ink slammed against the inner walls. A jagged crack split Rhea's temporary mandate mark from edge to center.

President Valmont: "Mandate limit. My seal holds an active threat for twelve breaths, not an old archive claim."

Aira was already counting aloud.

Professor Valen flipped open her cedar ward case. The Regalia shard swung toward the trapped ink, hesitated, then turned dead away, refusing the fight.

Architect Reith: "Can the Black Circle contain it without claiming the pages?"

Every head in the courtyard snapped toward me. Eight breaths left. The ink hammered Rhea's cracking seal. I could crush it in one motion. And in that same motion, I would teach everyone watching that when shared authority ran out of breath, the real power still belonged to me.

Haru: "No. Break the route, not the ink."

Halden's eyes jumped to the open service arch.

Professor Halden: "The doorway is still treating the Noctis pull as an open pass!"

Mira caught it first. She snatched the top sheet back from Aira, ripped off the clean margin carrying the old archive index number, and flung that strip straight back through the dark doorway.

The ink whipped around, ignoring Eris's name to chase the outdated index route that had taught it how to hunt. It poured back through the doorway after the scrap.

Leon severed his water line. Rhea dropped her seal. Aira hit twelve.

The heavy door slammed shut on black ink and hollow rulebook magic, leaving Mira's pages and Sera's cup safely outside.

Rhea stared down at the hairline crack running through her mandate mark.

President Valmont: "My seal is reduced until Council review."

Sovan looked from her broken authority to me.

Architect Reith: "You could have kept it whole."

Haru: "And taught everyone here what happens the second ordinary hands get tired."

Sovan wrote that down without polishing a single word.

When Rhea reached out to reopen the outer evacuation lane, her cracked seal sputtered and failed. For half a heartbeat, every official turned back toward me.

Mira held up the torn edge of her archive sheet instead.

Mira: "The courtyard stones still answer Green maintenance marks."

Finn and three Green aides dropped to one knee and pressed their wrists to the flagstones. Pale green lanes opened smoothly around the gathered students, wide enough for stretchers and measured enough to prevent a stampede. Rhea stepped back and let the temporary paths take over from her damaged seal.

Sovan added another line to his notebook.

**WHEN OFFICE SEAL FAILED, MAINTENANCE ACCESS HELD THE LANE.**

Nobody asked me to replace what had broken.

Sera leaned half an inch back onto her bench beside Lysa so Iven had clear footing along the stones, her soup cup still tight in her warming hands.

Sera: "Near?"

Iven's face softened, opening carefully around a word that used to mean danger.

Iven: "Near."

She stepped back up beside him, shoulder close without touching.

Maelin marched straight through the outer lane with her healer's kit open.

Maelin: "Everyone who walked into an impossible tower sits down right now."

Leon: "I can stand."

Maelin: "I heard floor stone cracking through two walls and a legal dispute. Sit down before I file you as furniture and stack you myself."

Leon sat instantly. Aira looked genuinely delighted despite the dust in her hair.

Aira: "That threat had historical accuracy."

Mira held out a crinkled sheet to Rhea.

Mira: "First draft. It is ugly."

Rhea took it with both hands.

President Valmont: "Then it is evidence."

Halden stood rigid beside the fountain. Mira turned straight to him, and the whole courtyard went quiet to watch.

Mira: "Her name was Eris Vell."

Halden's jaw tightened.

Professor Halden: "I will correct the archive dedication."

Mira: "No."

The quiet word hit harder than a shout. Halden froze.

Mira: "You will help us correct it."

Pride flared briefly behind his eyes, then folded under something better.

Professor Halden: "Together, then."

Halden rubbed a smudge of chalk off his wrist, pretending he had not been moved, while Mira gave him one crisp nod before turning on Sovan.

Mira: "And no keeper."

Sovan's portfolio dropped an inch.

Mira: "We got that straight from the tower. No center named."

Aira waved her smeared notebook.

Aira: "Confirmed. Transcribed while Leon was losing a fistfight with architecture. Maximum credibility."

Leon spoke from the flagstones.

Leon: "I did not lose."

Iven: "You negotiated."

Leon weighed that for a second.

Leon: "Acceptable."

Laughter moved through the courtyard in uneven pockets as tension finally began to break. Sovan looked from Mira to me, then straight back to Mira. That choice mattered.

Architect Reith: "If no keeper is named, what safety plan are you proposing?"

Mira blinked, caught off guard by being asked, then answered before doubt could catch up.

Mira: "A doorway witness group. Green-led because it called Green first. Student Council boundaries, healer consent checks, faculty archive backing, and outside observers allowed to watch without naming anyone in charge. Haru stays outside the line unless the group asks for him and can say why."

Aira stared at her.

Aira: "That was terrifyingly complete."

Mira: "I have been angry since breakfast."

The faintest curve touched Rhea's mouth.

President Valmont: "Provisional student proposal entered."

Sovan flipped his portfolio open, then stopped with his pen poised.

Architect Reith: "May I copy that wording?"

Old reflex rippled straight across the courtyard. Mira glanced at Aira, Aira looked to Rhea, Rhea turned toward Elira, and Elira's eyes flicked briefly to me, not asking permission, only watching the old ladder try to hand me the gavel one more time.

Haru: "Ask Mira."

Sovan turned back to her.

Architect Reith: "Castellan?"

Mira looked down at her ink-stained knuckles.

Mira: "Copy it ugly first."

Sovan blinked, while Aira smiled like a knife learning joy.

Aira: "Oh, I like him bewildered."

Sovan took one of Mira's smudged duplicate sheets instead of pulling a clean Council form. Not trust yet, but a real concession.

Above the bell arch, the fifth window still burned green, steadier now, less like an alarm and more like a lantern left lit on a landing.

Elira leaned close against my shoulder.

Elira: "You are shaking."

I looked down at my hands. She was right.

Haru: "I never crossed the line."

Elira: "I know."

Haru: "I wanted to."

Elira: "I know that too."

She did not offer praise or absolution, only witness, and it hurt enough to keep me honest.

Across the courtyard, students were already copying the name. Not cleanly. Eris Vell turned into Eris Vel on one sheet and E. Vell on another before Mira caught both with frightening speed. Finn wrote Fifth Witness too big and had to flip his paper over. From the covered infirmary doorway before Maelin ushered her indoors to rest, Sera asked Aira once how to spell furniture, then scratched the word out so hard she tore the corner.

Beside the fountain, Halden stood next to Professor Valen, staring up at the west tower as if an entire wing of his memory had been pried open against his will. Valen spoke softly enough that only those nearby caught it.

Professor Valen: "History keeps surviving as maintenance because maintenance is where institutions hide the people they refuse to admit they needed."

Halden's jaw worked once.

Professor Halden: "I taught that dedication wrong."

Professor Valen: "Then teach the correction loudly."

Sovan copied Mira's proposal by hand onto the smudged duplicate. No seal. No header. Ugly first. Maybe he understood why, or maybe he simply recognized the courtyard would reject anything cleaner. Either way, he listened.

Rhea pinned the first public notice onto the board herself.

**FIFTH TOWER THRESHOLD UNDER SHARED STUDENT-COUNCIL WITNESS.**

**NO KEEPER NAMED.**

**GREEN CIRCLE WITNESS ERIS VELL RECORDED AS A PERSON, NOT INFRASTRUCTURE.**

When people read the last word, a dozen eyes flicked toward me, then moved away without fear. Recognition instead of worship. Better, even if it would never make us safe.

High above the cracked bell, the fifth window clicked once.

A shadow fell across the courtyard paving stones beneath the green glow, sharper than morning light should allow. Letters bled straight into the dark stone at our boots. Mira spotted them first, going motionless beside her table. Aira followed her eyes and sucked in a sharp breath.

I looked down.

The tower's shadow had spelled out seven words across the stone.

**ERIS VELL WAS NOT LOST IN AURELIS.**

Every sound in the courtyard died around the sentence.

Then the green light in the high window trembled once, and a second line burned into the shadow beneath our feet.

**SHE WAS TAKEN THROUGH NOCTIS.**

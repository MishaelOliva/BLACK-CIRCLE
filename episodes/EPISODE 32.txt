# Episode 32: **The Fifth Witness**

The first mistake was assuming the window wanted me. That was reasonable, which made it dangerous.

Reasonable mistakes wore better clothes than cruel ones. They entered rooms with clean hands and public safety language. They brought diagrams. They used words like stabilizing, temporary, precautionary, and only until we understand. They looked at the thing no one understood, found the largest power standing near it, and decided the power must be the handle.

The green window above the cracked west bell stayed lit through dawn. It did not brighten. It did not dim. It simply remained.

That was enough to turn Aurelis into a building holding its breath.

By breakfast, the official four-tower diagrams had been removed from two public boards and replaced by blank parchment headed with the words STRUCTURAL REVIEW IN PROGRESS. By second bell, three student copies of the old map had circulated anyway, each with a fifth square drawn in the wrong place. By third bell, someone had chalked a green rectangle on the courtyard stones below the west tower and written:

**IF IT HAS A WINDOW, IT HAS A ROOM.**

No one knew who did it.

Mira copied it before a cleaning charm erased the chalk. Of course she did.

I found her at the fountain with both elbows on a stack of paper and ink on the side of her face. Aira stood beside her, holding a second stack away from the breeze with the expression of someone protecting an injured animal that happened to be made of bureaucracy.

Leon stood three steps behind them.

Far enough back to look uninvolved, close enough to be involved if anyone made the mistake of testing it.

The younger transfer girl who had trusted Iven with her name was helping at the copy table that morning. Iven Sahl sat on the low wall by the north planter, shoulders hunched inside a borrowed academy sweater. Sera sat close to him with her soup cup held in both hands. The cup was empty. She kept it anyway.

Some objects were better at staying themselves than people were allowed to be.

The fifth window glowed above us. Green.

Neither signal fire nor domestic lamp, but a steady color that made the official tower stone look less certain of itself.

Elira's hand brushed my sleeve.

She had insisted on coming down.

Maelin had argued for seventeen minutes, then reduced the argument to terms only Elira could not defeat: fifteen minutes standing, five minutes sitting, no stairs, no heroic statements before lunch.

Elira had agreed to the first three.

The fourth remained under review.

Her cloak was pale blue today, fastened with the small dark clasp that hid the revised pendant beneath the fabric. She looked composed from a distance. Up close, I could see the cost in the way she breathed before turning her head, the careful set of her shoulders, the fraction of delay before her smile answered anyone.

People kept seeing alive and mistaking it for healed.

The world liked clean categories because they saved it from apology.

Mira looked up when we approached.

Mira: "I have six versions."

Aira: "Seven, if you count the one where someone drew the Fifth Tower as a cake."

Mira: "I am not counting that."

Aira: "Coward."

Mira pointed her pencil toward the fountain without looking away from her page.

Mira: "The water keeps reflecting the tower wrong. Not always. Only when Green students stand near the rim."

Leon: "Define near."

Mira: "I tried. The fountain objected." Leon's brows lowered.

Leon: "How does water object?"

Aira handed him a page.

Aira: "By making my measurements embarrassing." Leon read it. His mouth tightened.

Leon: "This says the same student stood two paces from the fountain, four paces from the fountain, and directly under the west bell without moving."

Aira: "Yes." Her pen hovered above the margin.

Leon: "That is not measurement."

Aira: "Correct. That is embarrassment."

Mira added a line to her notes.

Mira: "Embarrassment confirmed by water."

I looked at the fountain.

Clear surface. Ordinary ripples. Four official tower reflections around the basin, each broken slightly by morning light.

When Mira leaned closer, a fifth shape appeared below them: not yet a tower, only a vertical green line like a window seen through depth rather than distance.

The cold behind my sternum answered.

Old metal. Ozone. Winter stone. Recognition, not command.

I stayed still. Elira noticed.

Elira: "It is not pulling you."

Haru: "No."

Aira's eyes moved from me to the window.

Aira: "That is the first kind thing ancient architecture has done for my schedule." I shook my head.

Haru: "It may be worse." Mira's pencil stopped.

The fifth reflection bent toward her page instead of me. Toward ink.

Sera spoke from the low wall.

Her voice was small, but the courtyard had learned not to waste small voices.

Sera: "It is humming." Iven looked at her.

Iven: "The window?" Sera shook her head.

Sera: "The floor."

Then every Green Circle student within twenty paces looked down.

They did not move together. That would have been dramatic. They looked down one by one, a half-second apart, like drops finding the same lake. Mira's face changed first.

She was still kneeling at the fountain, paper in hand, pencil caught between two fingers. Her Green Circle glowed faintly at her wrist, not with power, not even with fear. With direction.

A thin green line appeared under her shoe.

Then another under Iven's. Then Sera's.

Then Finn's, at the outer ring with his arms full of copied maps.

Then half a dozen more.

The lines did not race toward the west tower. They walked. Slow, patient, under stone.

A path remembering it had once been allowed to exist.

Rhea's voice cut through the courtyard.

President Valmont: "No one follows the lines alone."

Good order. Good instinct. Too late. The fountain rang.

The sound was low, passing through the ground and climbing the soles of my boots. Students gasped. A Blue Circle boy near the public board stumbled backward. Someone dropped a tray. Porcelain shattered. Elira flinched once.

I did not move toward her.

Her hand found mine anyway. Choice first. Always.

The green lines brightened. Mira stood.

Leon stepped beside her before anyone asked him to.

Mira: "I did not decide to walk." Aira's pen moved.

Aira: "Record: Mira Castellan reports no decision to walk."

Mira: "I also did not decide not to."

Aira: "Wonderful. Ancient ambiguity. My favorite breakfast disease."

Iven rose from the wall.

Sera's hand shot out and caught his sleeve. He stopped immediately.

That mattered more than the line under his feet.

Iven: "Do you want me to stay?"

Sera looked at the green path under her own shoes.

Then at the cup in her hands.

Sera: "I want to not go first." Iven nodded.

Iven: "Then you do not go first."

The line under Sera dimmed. Not vanished. Waited.

Maelin, who had appeared beside Elira with the silent violence of medical professionals, pointed at the ground.

Maelin: "Consent-responsive architecture. Of course. Because a normal staircase was too much to ask."

Halden arrived almost running and stopped only because Rhea lifted one finger.

Professor Halden: "Do not seal it."

Rhea: "I was about to say that to you."

Halden looked briefly offended, then looked at the green lines and forgot to be a person with pride.

Professor Halden: "The routing is impossible. The west tower has no inner stair beyond the fourth service level. The wall thickness cannot support a fifth chamber."

Aira: "The wall has filed a disagreement." Mira took one step.

The courtyard shifted. Not physically.

Everyone's attention moved with her, and that was almost the same thing.

The green line ahead of her lit beneath the paving stones, leading toward the west arch. Not the main door.

A service door beside the old bell wall.

A door I had walked past six times in the last week without seeing it as anything except storage.

Mira looked back. At Aira. At Rhea.

At me last. Not because I was the answer.

Because I might become the mistake.

Mira: "Should I stop?"

Every adult within earshot prepared an answer.

I saw them do it.

Halden's scholar answer. Rhea's authority answer. Maelin's safety answer. Seraphine's tactical answer from the edge of the crowd. Valen's historical answer, arriving behind them with her coat half-buttoned and her eyes already too sharp. Then Leon spoke first.

Leon: "Do you want to?" Mira blinked.

The green line under her foot brightened once.

Mira: "No."

Leon: "Then do not go alone."

Aira's pen stopped. Only for a breath.

Then she wrote that down too. Rhea looked at Leon.

Something almost like approval crossed her face.

President Valmont: "Castellan. Sahl. Varek. You may proceed to the service threshold under student witness conditions. No entry past the threshold without verbal confirmation and a visible exit line." Halden inhaled.

Rhea: "Professor Halden will remain outside the first line."

Halden exhaled in scholarly pain.

Elira squeezed my hand once.

Elira: "And Haru?"

The courtyard listened too hard. That was the second mistake waiting.

Rhea did not answer immediately. Neither did I.

The fifth window glowed above us.

A student near the back whispered:

Student: "Shouldn't the Black Circle go?" Not accusation. Not worship.

Fear looking for the nearest handle.

Another voice answered, older, smoother, not from the student ring.

Council Aide: "That may be the safest arrangement."

The crowd parted for Architect Sovan Reith, a tired, sincere Council specialist in emergency structures.

Sovan: "Magistra Voss sent me to observe the threshold. My current brief says classification event; the wording is poor."

Aira: "Promising survival instinct."

Rhea kept him outside the first line.

Sovan accepted the limit and opened his portfolio without naming a solution.
Inside the service door, the lock clicked.

Mira turned back toward it.

Iven took one step to her left.

Leon stepped to her right.

The door opened inward without anyone touching it.

Darkness waited beyond. Not empty. Green-lit.

Mira: "There is writing on the floor."

Aira: "Read only. No stepping until-"

The ground under Mira's feet moved. Leon caught her shoulder. Iven caught Leon's sleeve.

All three vanished through the doorway. The door stayed open.

The courtyard did not scream.

It learned faster than that now.

Aira said one word that made three first-years turn red. Then she ran.

Rhea's seal flashed across the threshold before Aira reached it, not closing the door, only preventing the crowd from surging after them.

President Valmont: "No one enters." I was already moving.

Elira's hand held mine. Not stopping. Asking.

The Black Circle pressed beneath my skin. The fifth window brightened. Sovan saw it.

So did everyone else. That was the third mistake waiting.

If I entered now, every frightened sentence in the courtyard would become easier to believe. Black Circle opens tower. Black Circle retrieves students.

Black Circle central safety point. Black Circle keeper.

Black Circle throne, but with better handwriting. Elira said quietly:

Elira: "Threshold." Not order. Reminder.

I stopped at the first green line.

My body hated me for it.

Inside the doorway, darkness breathed.

Mira's voice came faintly from beyond it.

Mira: "We are here."

Aira reached the seal and dropped to her knees with her notebook open.

Aira: "Define here."

Mira: "Working on it."

Leon: "Badly."

Mira: "Unhelpful."

Iven: "The stairs are sideways." Aira looked up.

Aira: "That is extremely helpful and I resent it."

The courtyard breathed again. Not safely. But together.

I kept my feet outside the line.

Sovan watched me do it.

His expression changed. Not enough to settle anything, but enough.

Sovan: "You could reach them faster."

Haru: "Yes." My breath steadied against the cold air.

Sovan: "And you are not."

Haru: "Yes." I met their gaze without looking away.

Sovan: "Why?"

I looked through the doorway.

Green light moved along impossible stairs.

Haru: "Because faster is not the same as safer."

The fifth window dimmed by one careful breath. Almost approval. Almost warning.

## Mira Castellan

Mira had once decided that panic was a waste of ink.

This was not because panic was useless. Panic was very useful if a person needed to run, scream, notice fire, or avoid becoming part of a wall. It was simply bad at spelling.

So when the service doorway swallowed her, Leon, and Iven into a space that could not exist inside the west tower, Mira did not scream. She wrote:

**ENTRY UNCHOSEN BUT NOT OPPOSED.**

Then she underlined not opposed because it felt important and also because her hand was shaking enough to make the first line ugly. Ugly was acceptable. Ugly meant first.

The room around them was not a room.

It had a floor for seven steps, then a stair that climbed sideways along a wall, then a window under the landing showing the courtyard from above even though they had not gone up, then a second window showing the fountain from below, with Aira's crouched shape distorted through water like a worried fish.

Leon stood between Mira and the nearest gap in the floor.

He had not been asked.

His Blue Circle glowed around his wrist, water-thin and controlled.

Iven stood very still behind them, one hand pressed to the wall.

Mira: "Are you hurt?"

Iven: "No."

Leon: "Certain?" Iven glanced at him.

Iven: "Noctis taught certain as a tone before it taught it as truth."

Leon absorbed that with visible discomfort. Good. Some discomfort deserved witnesses.

Leon: "Then answer not certain." Iven looked surprised. Then he nodded.

Iven: "Not certain. Not hurt that I can tell." Mira wrote that down.

The wall warmed under Iven's hand. Green light threaded from his fingers into old stone, answering rather than taking.

The tower made a sound like paper being turned in another room.

Aira's voice came from somewhere behind them, distorted by the open doorway.

Aira: "Mira?" Mira turned.

The door was visible. Mostly.

It stood six paces away and also below them and also reflected in a window to their left. Through it, she saw Aira kneeling at the threshold, Haru standing behind the green line with Elira beside him, Rhea's red seal holding the crowd back, and a Council man with a portfolio watching Haru too carefully, as if measuring a storm without offending it.

Mira did not like him.

That might have been unfair. She wrote:

**COUNCIL MAN OUTSIDE WATCHING HARU TOO MUCH. MAY BE UNFAIR. DO NOT DELETE.**

Aira's voice sharpened.

Aira: "I saw that. Good. Keep being unfair on paper."

Leon looked at the door.

Leon: "Can we walk back?"

Iven removed his hand from the wall.

The path behind them rearranged itself. Not closed. Changed.

The seven-step floor became five. The sideways stair became a narrow bridge with no railing. The window under the landing showed an empty classroom where every chair faced the wall.

Mira's mouth went dry. Iven whispered:

Iven: "I know that room."

Leon: "From Noctis?"

Iven did not answer quickly. Mira waited.

A useful record did not drag words out of people just because silence was inconvenient.

Iven: "Not a real room. A lesson image. Foundation obedience primer. They showed us old failures. People who caused collapse because they did not accept placement." The window flickered.

In the empty classroom, one chair turned around.

A girl sat there, neither alive nor dead: a memory with its edges rubbed raw.

She looked about fifteen. Maybe sixteen. Dark hair cut blunt at her jaw. Academy uniform, but older than any current style, its collar plain and its sleeves patched near both cuffs. A Green Circle glowed near her heart, visible through the fabric as if the memory had forgotten clothing was supposed to hide skin.

Her face blurred when Mira tried to focus on it.

Then sharpened when Mira looked at her hands. Ink stains. All over the fingers. Mira stopped breathing.

Leon: "Castellan."

Mira: "I see her."

Iven: "She was in the primer."

The girl in the chair opened her mouth without sound.

The tower answered for her.

Words appeared across the classroom wall in careful official script:

**UNCLASSIFIED ATTENDANT.**

Her mouth kept moving silently. Again. Again.

The script refreshed each time, brighter and cleaner.

**UNCLASSIFIED ATTENDANT.**

Mira's hand hurt around the pencil.

Leon: "What do we do?"

Iven's face had gone gray.

Iven: "In the primer, they said she failed to remain in assigned support position during a pre-Circle emergency. Her interference compromised authority chain."

Mira: "What was her name?" Iven swallowed.

Iven: "They did not give one." The tower groaned.

Stone bent around them, not collapsing, not yet, but remembering collapse badly enough to imitate it. Leon raised both hands.

Water formed between his palms and spread into a thin barrier along the open edge where the floor had started to peel away. It was not dramatic. It did not flash. It simply held loose fragments in place before gravity made choices for them.

Leon: "Mira. Write."

Mira: "I do not know what."

Leon: "Write that." So she did.

**WE DO NOT KNOW HER NAME.**

The official script on the classroom wall flickered.

The girl in the chair turned her head. Not toward Mira. Toward Iven.

Iven took one step back.

Leon shifted with him, keeping the water barrier up.

Iven: "No." The tower waited. Iven's hands closed.

Iven: "No, I am not refusing. I am saying no because I do not want the primer in my mouth first."

Mira wrote that down too.

The green light under their feet steadied.

Outside, faintly, Haru's voice said something Mira could not hear. Elira answered. Aira cursed softly. Rhea issued an instruction. The world continued at the threshold.

Inside, the girl in the chair mouthed silent words.

Mira ignored the face and impossible room. She watched the mouth.

She had copied enough testimony to recognize repetition.

Four words: correction rather than title or plea. Mira whispered:

Mira: "Again."

The memory girl's lips moved.

Mira followed. I. Was. Fifth. Witness.

Mira wrote it before fear could make it beautiful.

**SHE SAYS: I WAS FIFTH WITNESS.**

The tower lurched.

Leon swore and planted one foot harder against the stone. His Blue Circle flared, water barrier bending under pressure. The edge of the floor cracked toward his heel.

Iven grabbed his sleeve again.

Leon: "Not me. The wall. Hold the wall."

Iven's hand snapped to the stone. Green light answered him.

Then Sera's voice came from the doorway, very faint:

Sera: "Ask if she wants near."

Mira almost laughed. Not because it was funny.

Because it was exactly right and exactly from Sera, who had learned too quickly what being taken felt like and now made every room answer differently.

Mira: "Do you want us near?"

The memory girl's face blurred completely.

For one horrible second, only the official words remained.

**UNCLASSIFIED ATTENDANT.**

Then one letter cracked.

The U split down the middle.

The girl lifted one ink-stained hand and pressed it to the inside of the classroom window.

Mira placed her own hand against the tower wall, not touching the glass, not reaching through. Near, without taking. Iven copied the motion.

Leon could not. He was holding the floor together with both hands and a look of furious concentration. Mira wrote one-handed:

**NEAR REQUESTED BY RESPONSE. TOUCH NOT ATTEMPTED. LEON HOLDING FLOOR. IVEN AT WALL. MIRA AT WALL.**

The classroom changed.

Chairs scraped into different positions. Not all facing the wall now. Some turned sideways. One fell over. One vanished. The girl's chair stayed.

Behind her, another wall appeared. An old council room. No. Older than council.

A circular chamber with four raised seats and one standing space near the floor.

The girl stood in the standing space. Adults argued above her.

Their faces were rubbed away.

Their colors remained. Blue. Red. Yellow.

A pale silver that made Mira's teeth hurt.

The girl tried to speak.

The chamber wrote over her.

**SUPPORT PRESENCE.**

She tried again.

**ATTENDANT.**

Again.

**GREEN WITNESS UNNECESSARY TO FINAL RECORD.**

Mira's pencil tore the paper.

Mira: "No."

The word echoed. Not with power. With recognition. Iven's voice shook.

Iven: "Noctis used this."

Leon: "Used what?"

Iven: "The lesson. The shape. They taught us support presence is not witness. They taught Green hands can carry, warm, fetch, hold, copy, but not define." His Green Circle brightened.

Anger did not make it larger. It made it clearer.

Iven: "They built that into us."

Mira looked at the memory girl. At the standing space.

At the four raised seats.

At the missing fifth chair that had never been allowed to become furniture. She wrote:

**OLD RECORD ERROR: GREEN WITNESS TREATED AS SUPPORT PRESENCE.**

The tower stopped groaning.

Leon kept the barrier up.

Sweat ran down his temple.

Leon: "Good?"

The floor cracked another inch.

Leon: "Not good. Understood."

Mira: "Can you hold it?" Leon's mouth twisted.

Old Leon would have turned that question into a performance.

This Leon looked at the crack, at Iven's hand on the wall, at Mira's paper, and answered like accuracy mattered more than pride.

Leon: "Not long."

Mira: "Thank you."

He blinked once. Then nodded. The memory shifted again.

This time the girl stood in a corridor filled with smoke. No flames, only aftermath. The four official colors burned on sealed doors. Green light ran along the floor, thin and practical, showing where people had crawled.

The girl carried a child under one arm and a record box under the other. She was crying.

Mira could see it now.

Not dramatically. No perfect tear on a clean cheek. Her face was dirty and terrified and determined in the ordinary way people became when no one had arrived to be heroic yet.

A man in red reached for the box.

The girl shook her head. The tower wrote:

**REFUSED TRANSFER TO AUTHORITY.**

Mira wrote underneath:

**PROTECTED RECORD FROM PREMATURE AUTHORITY CLAIM.**

The tower made a sound like a held breath breaking.

The girl looked at Mira.

For the first time, her face stayed visible. Dark eyes. Ink on her chin.

A small scar through one eyebrow.

Mira did not know her.

Mira missed her anyway. Iven whispered:

Iven: "I have seen the scar."

Mira: "Where?"

Iven's hand pressed harder to the wall.

Iven: "Not in the primer. In the obedience hall. One of the wall figures. They called her first disobedience."

Leon: "Noctis has her?"

Iven shook his head, then stopped, because shaking his head was too simple for the truth.

Iven: "A copy. A warning. Maybe a route. I do not know."

Mira wrote all of it.

The memory girl's hand moved.

Four fingers touched her own chest.

Then one finger pointed down. Not at the floor. At the record.

Mira: "Name?" The tower lights flickered. Outside, voices rose.

## Haru - The Reasonable Mistake

Sovan Reith made himself useful after the students vanished.

He cleared frightened aides from the threshold and kept one noble family from opening a private relay. Only then did he make the reasonable mistake.

Sovan: "A temporary keeper designation may prevent worse titles."

Elira, seated under Maelin's order, looked at him.

Elira: "You want to stop a myth by giving it an office."

Sovan: "I want someone answerable if the tower opens again."

Haru: "Then name the answerable group already here."

I pointed to Rhea's limited campus seal, Aira at the threshold, Maelin's medical line, Valen and Halden at the maps, and the Green copies carrying Mira's voice out of the impossible room.

The Regalia shard inside Valen's case turned toward the tower and went dark.

Sovan read the refusal correctly enough to step back.

Sovan: "Then the people inside answer before I name the frame."

Aira: "Write the question. Leave room." He did.

The argument ended before it could become another hearing. Inside the tower, Mira found a name.

## Mira Castellan - A Name Is Not Enough

E. Vell.

The name was not enough. Mira knew that immediately.

Initials were where institutions put people they wanted available but not present. E. Vell could repair channels, carry boxes, stand in margins, and become a dedication plaque no one read unless a professor needed a footnote.

The girl in the memory had a mouth. A hand. A scar. Ink on her fingers.

A Green Circle at her heart.

A child under one arm and a record box under the other.

She deserved more than an initial. Mira looked at Iven.

Iven's hand remained pressed to the wall. Green light ran up his wrist in thin lines, not hurting him, but asking too much.

Mira: "Do you know the first name?"

Iven shook his head. Then stopped.

Iven: "The obedience hall did not use names for warnings."

Leon grunted as the barrier bent.

Leon: "Can the tower provide it?"

Mira looked at the memory girl.

Mira: "Can you?"

The girl in the smoke corridor shifted.

Official script tried to form first.

**E. VELL. SUPPORT-**

The word cracked before it finished. Mira wrote:

**TOWER ATTEMPTS OLD LABEL FIRST. LABEL UNSTABLE. PERSON STILL PRESENT.**

The girl pressed one ink-stained finger against the record box in the memory.

Three taps. Pause. Two taps. Pause. One. Iven's eyes widened.

Iven: "Archive index."

Mira: "What?"

Iven: "Noctis made us memorize old cross-school archive index forms for doctrine retrieval. Three-two-one is personal testimony appendix."

Leon: "Why would Noctis teach that?"

Iven gave him a look that was almost pity.

Iven: "So we could retrieve testimony before learning how to question it."

Mira wrote that down because it sounded like something Aira would want to stab later.

Then she looked back at the girl.

Mira: "Where is the appendix?" The tower changed again.

The classroom, chamber, and smoke corridor folded into a narrow archive aisle. Shelves rose on both sides, too tall, too close, packed with boxes labeled by color.

Green boxes lined the bottom shelf. Not because they were foundation.

Because they were easiest to overlook. One box shook.

Leon: "If you say we need to go get it, I am resigning from architecture."

Mira: "You do not work in architecture."

Leon: "Exactly. Clean break." The box slid out.

It fell open on the floor without sound. Inside was one page. No. Not page. A remembered page.

Half burned. Water-stained. Written in a hand so cramped it looked like it had been trying to fit truth into space assigned for inventory. Mira knelt.

Leon: "Mira. Floor."

Mira: "I know."

She did not touch the page. She read. Most of it blurred. One line stayed.

**My name is Eris Vell, and I was not present as furniture.**

Mira's throat closed.

For a second, she was useless.

Then Leon made a sound of pain as the crack reached his boot. Useless ended. Mira wrote:

**NAME RECOVERED: ERIS VELL. GREEN CIRCLE. FIFTH WITNESS. NOT FURNITURE.**

The tower answered. Not with approval. With an alarm.

Black script tore down the archive shelves faster than Mira could read it.

**UNAUTHORIZED PERSONAL RESTORATION**

**NOCTIS JURISDICTION LINK ACTIVE**

Iven recoiled so hard his hand left the wall.

Every Green support line went dark. The floor dropped.

Leon caught the shelf with one shoulder and Mira with his free hand. Boxes burst open above them. Remembered pages fell like white birds, each one losing its writing before it touched the ground.

The page naming Eris Vell began sliding back into the bottom shelf. Mira grabbed the box.

Her fingers passed through memory and struck cold stone beneath it. The box kept moving.

Mira: "I cannot hold it."

Iven stared at the Noctis line.

Iven: "It is using retrieval doctrine."

Leon: "Useful translation?"

Iven flinched at the tone. Leon corrected immediately.

Leon: "Sorry. What can we do?" The apology cost time.

It also returned Iven to the room.

He looked at the shelves, the disappearing records, the black script trying to drag a person's name back into institutional custody.

Iven: "Retrieval needs an authorized carrier."

Mira: "Who?"

The tower answered by writing across Iven's borrowed sweater:

**NOCTIS CONDUIT-TRACK PROPERTY: IVEN SAHL**

His face emptied.

The black script tightened around his chest and pulled him toward the shelf.

Leon released Mira and caught Iven with both hands.

Water flared around his boots.

The retrieval line dragged them together.

Leon: "You are not carrying anything for them."

Iven could not answer. Noctis had trained that wording too deeply. Carry. Retrieve. Return. Useful body moving approved truth toward approved hands.

Mira did not argue with the doctrine.

She put her page against Iven's sleeve and wrote over the black label.

**IVEN SAHL: PRESENT WITNESS. NOT CARRIER.**

The pencil tore fabric. The black words flickered.

Iven dragged in one breath.

Iven: "Not carrier." The line loosened.

The shelf took the Eris page another inch.

Mira: "Then who carries it?"

Leon looked at his hands on Iven.

Leon: "No one." Mira understood. Not no rescue. No single carrier.

She tore her fresh copy into three uneven strips.

On the first she wrote **ERIS**. On the second: **VELL**.

On the third: **FIFTH WITNESS**.

She handed one to Iven and one to Leon. Kept the last.

Mira: "The name leaves in pieces."

Leon: "That sounds unsafe."

Mira: "For Noctis." That convinced him.

The archive aisle collapsed sideways.

Shelves became walls. The floor became a steep passage coated in wet black ink. At the bottom, a circular Noctis seal opened like a drain. Every erased page slid toward it.

The remembered Eris box followed. Mira went after it.

Leon swore and sent water beneath her feet. Not a barrier—a moving skin between her shoes and the ink. Iven pressed both hands against the wall and searched for the old maintenance rhythm hidden beneath Noctis retrieval.

Mira slid past boxes labeled only by color. Green. Green. Green.

Hundreds of hands reduced to a shelf position. The Noctis seal widened.

Cold air rose from it carrying bells, commands, and the sound of children answering before questions finished. Iven heard home. His wall-light failed. The passage tilted steeper.

Leon lost one foot and hit the floor on his knee. Water splashed into the black drain and came back carrying white restraint marks. They climbed his arms.

Leon: "Iven!" Not an order.

A name thrown toward someone disappearing inside memory. Iven shut his eyes.

The obedience hall rang around him. Stand straight. Hands visible. Return property. Report disobedience.

He opened his eyes and looked at the strip Mira had given him.

**ERIS.**

A name Noctis had kept only as a warning.

Iven bit the inside of his cheek until pain became current information.

Iven: "The primer skipped every seventh maintenance beat."

Mira, sliding toward the drain, shouted:

Mira: "Why?"

Iven: "So students would not hear the original route."

He struck the wall six times. Waited. Struck once more.

Green light exploded through the black ink. Not upward. Sideways.

A service ledge opened beneath Mira's feet. She hit it hard, rolled, and caught the remembered box between both forearms without touching the page inside. Leon diverted his water into the ledge. The restraint marks washed off his arms and vanished into the Noctis drain with an angry hiss.

The shelf above them tried to close.

Mira: "Leon!"

He planted his back against one wall and both boots against the other. Blue Circle water filled the gap around his body and hardened under pressure.

Leon: "I am beginning to dislike historical research."

Mira: "Hold."

Leon: "That was implied."

The ledge moved sideways through the tower.

Outside voices reached them in fragments. Aira calling Mira's name. Sera repeating Iven.

Rhea ordering the threshold kept open without naming a keeper. Haru saying nothing.

His silence felt different now. Not absence. Space he had chosen not to fill while their hands were still working.

The Noctis drain followed beneath the ledge.

Black hooks rose from it toward the box.

Mira could not cast enough power to stop them.

Leon could not release the walls.

Iven held only a strip of paper and a service rhythm Noctis had tried to delete.

So he stepped between the hooks and Eris Vell.

The first hook caught his sweater.

The second cut his cheek.

The third stopped against his Green Circle.

Noctis training expected Green to yield under authorized retrieval.

Iven's Circle did not become stronger. It became unavailable.

Iven: "Not carrier." The hook pulled.

Iven: "Witness."

Mira pressed her strip to the remembered box.

**FIFTH WITNESS.**

Leon freed one hand long enough to add his.

**VELL.**

Three pieces. Three hands. No authorized carrier.

The hooks could not decide which part of the name counted as property. They split their force. That was the mistake.

Iven tore free. Mira shoved the box sideways. Leon released the water brace and sent the entire ledge upward in one violent blue surge.

They rose through collapsing shelves, erased labels, and a storm of blank pages.

The Noctis seal snapped shut beneath them.

For one instant, Mira saw beyond it. A corridor.

Newer stone than the tower. Black uniforms.

A wall figure with Eris's scar carved above an obedience phrase. Then the route closed.

The three of them hit the original floor together.

Leon landed badly and laughed once from pure shock.

Iven touched the cut on his cheek.

Mira checked the remembered box.

The page inside remained half burned, water-stained, and present.

She placed the three paper strips beside it.

The words joined without losing their tears.

**ERIS VELL. FIFTH WITNESS.**

The tower released every breath it had been holding.

Every window opened at once. Not to air. To memory.

Outside, the courtyard appeared in all of them: Aira at the threshold, Rhea's red seal, Elira standing pale and furious, Haru still outside the line, Sovan with his portfolio held too tightly, Halden looking like a man watching a footnote become indictment.

Then the windows showed older courtyards.

Students in uniforms no one wore anymore.

A fountain cracked in half. Green hands passing water. Blue circles lifting beams. Red seals holding doors.

Yellow light burning too high.

And Eris Vell, running between all of them with paper under one arm and a child on her hip, shouting silently until the tower finally gave her sound.

Eris: "Do not name one person the answer."

The words struck the tower from inside.

Leon fell to one knee. His barrier collapsed. The floor did not.

Green lines took the weight. Not one line, but many. Mira's. Iven's.

Sera's from outside, thin but present. Finn's near the fountain.

A dozen Green students who had not entered but had not let the path go dark.

Leon stared at the floor.

Leon: "Oh."

Mira: "Good oh or bad oh?" Leon swallowed.

Leon: "Green oh." Iven laughed once. It came out badly. It still counted.

The memory of Eris Vell turned toward them.

Her face was clear now.

Young. Terrified. Angry. Very tired. A person. Not lesson, warning, or infrastructure. Mira lifted her pencil.

Mira: "Do you want anything else recorded?"

Eris looked at the page in Mira's hand.

Then toward the open windows showing Haru outside the line.

Mira thought, for one frightening second, that the tower would name him anyway. Instead, Eris smiled.

It was small and crooked and nothing like a statue.

Eris: "No center named." Mira wrote it.

The tower settled. Not closed. Settled.

Like a person sitting down after being forced to stand for too long.

A stair unfolded beneath them, ordinary enough to be insulting. Leon looked at it.

Leon: "Now it gives us stairs."

Iven removed his hand from the wall. His knees nearly folded.

Leon caught him. No flourish. No complaint. Just caught him.

Mira gathered her pages against her chest and looked once more at Eris Vell.

The memory had already begun to fade from figure into light. Not disappearing. Entering record.

Mira: "We will write it badly first." Eris's smile widened.

Eris: "Good."

Then the stair carried them down.

## Haru - What Came Back

They came out through the service door covered in dust, green light, and the kind of silence people bring back from rooms where something true has been waiting longer than anyone alive.

Leon emerged first because he had apparently decided doors were threats until proven otherwise. Iven leaned on him with no protest left in his body. Mira came last, pages clutched under both arms, pencil behind one ear, ink smeared across her cheek like war paint for clerks. Aira reached her first. She did not grab.

She held out both hands, palms up.

Aira: "Pages? You? Both?" Mira looked at her.

Mira: "Both."

Aira took the pages with one hand and Mira's sleeve with the other.

Aira: "Greedy. Sensible. Approved."

Black ink followed them through the service door.

It slid over the threshold in a thin sheet, carrying the Noctis retrieval seal Mira had escaped below. The nearest pages bent toward it. Aira's grip tightened, but the ink pulled hard enough to tear the top copy from her fingers.

Haru: "Do not touch the seal."

The warning arrived too late for Sera.

She stepped between the ink and Iven with her soup cup in both hands. Not throwing it.

Holding it where the black sheet could see the crooked label Mira had taped there days earlier.

**SERA'S CUP. ASK SERA.**

The retrieval spell reached the cup and stopped.

Its grammar could seize institutional evidence. It could recover Noctis property. It could follow a stolen archive route across a threshold.

It had no answer for an object whose ownership condition was written by the person holding it.

Sera: "No."

The ink split around her boots.

Iven moved beside her. The cut on his cheek still bled.

Iven: "Not carrier."

Mira dropped to her knees and spread the three torn name strips across the stone.

**ERIS.**

**VELL.**

**FIFTH WITNESS.**

The retrieval seal lunged for all three and divided itself again.

Leon, exhausted and sitting under Maelin's threat, sent one last water thread across the floor. It caught the separated ink currents and pulled them around Rhea's red seal.

Rhea closed the seal like a fist.

Black ink struck its inside walls.

The impact cracked her temporary mandate mark from edge to center.

President Valmont: "Copy authority limit. My seal may hold immediate hazard for twelve breaths. It cannot retain historical custody." Aira was already counting.

Professor Valen opened the Regalia case. The shard turned toward the trapped Noctis seal, then away, refusing the obvious contest.

Sovan: "Can the Black Circle contain it without claiming the pages?" Everyone looked at me.

The ink hammered Rhea's seal again. Eight breaths. I could contain it.

I could also teach every frightened person in the courtyard that when distributed authority reached its limit, the real answer remained me.

Haru: "No. Break the route, not the ink."

Halden looked at the service doorway.

Professor Halden: "The door is still translating Noctis retrieval into tower access." Mira understood.

She seized the first ugly page from Aira, tore off the clean margin bearing the old archive index, and threw that strip back through the doorway. The ink turned. Not toward Eris's name.

Toward the obsolete route that had taught it how to follow.

It rushed through the service door after the strip.

Leon snapped his water line. Rhea released the seal. Aira reached twelve.

The door slammed on black ink and empty authority grammar. Mira's pages remained outside. So did Sera's cup.

Rhea examined the crack through her emergency mark.

President Valmont: "My seal is reduced until review."

Sovan looked at the damaged authority, then at me.

Sovan: "You could have preserved it."

Haru: "And changed what everyone here believes happens when authority becomes tired."

Sovan wrote that down without improving it.

Rhea's damaged seal failed when she tried to reopen the outer evacuation lane.

For half a second, every official looked toward me.

Mira raised the torn archive strip instead.

Mira: "The tower's service marks still recognize Green movement."

Finn and three Green aides pressed their circles to the courtyard stones. Pale lines opened around the gathered students, wide enough for stretchers and slow enough not to become a stampede.

Rhea stepped back and let the temporary route outrank her injured authority.

Sovan added a second note:

**WHEN OFFICE SEAL FAILED, MAINTENANCE ACCESS PRESERVED MOVEMENT.**

No one asked me to replace what had broken.

Sera slid off the wall and walked toward Iven.

She stopped two steps away.

Sera: "Near?" Iven's face changed.

Not breaking. Opening carefully around something that had once only meant danger.

Iven: "Near."

She stood beside him. Not touching, but enough.

Maelin reached Leon and Iven with healer focus already active.

Maelin: "Everyone who entered the impossible tower sits down now."

Leon: "I can stand."

Maelin: "I heard floor cracking through stone and legal argument. Sit before I classify you as furniture and remove you myself." Leon sat. Instantly.

Aira looked delighted despite everything.

Aira: "That threat had historical precision."

Mira handed one page to Rhea.

Mira: "This is the first version. It is ugly."

Rhea accepted it with both hands.

President Valmont: "Then it is evidence." Halden stood very still. Mira turned to him. The courtyard noticed. He did too.

Mira: "Her name was Eris Vell." Halden's face tightened.

Professor Halden: "I will correct the archive dedication."

Mira: "No."

The word struck harder because it was not loud. Halden stopped.

Mira: "You will help us correct it."

For a moment, old pride rose in him.

Then something better killed it.

Professor Halden: "Yes." He adjusted his spectacles, clearing his throat. Mira nodded once.

Then she looked at Sovan.

Mira: "And no keeper."

Sovan's portfolio lowered by an inch.

Mira: "That was in the tower. No center named."

Aira lifted the copied page.

Aira: "Confirmed. Written while Leon was losing an argument with architecture. High credibility." Leon, from the ground:

Leon: "I did not lose."

Iven: "You negotiated." Leon considered that.

Leon: "Acceptable."

The courtyard relaxed in pieces, never all at once.

Sovan looked from Mira to me.

Then back to Mira. That mattered.

Sovan: "If no keeper is named, what public safety frame do you propose?" Mira's eyes widened slightly.

She had not expected to be asked. Good. Then she answered anyway.

Mira: "Threshold witness group. Green-led because it pulled Green first. Student Council boundary. Healer consent review. Faculty archive support. External observer allowed to watch but not name. Haru outside unless asked by the group and unless the group can say why." Aira stared at her.

Aira: "That was disturbingly complete."

Mira: "I have been angry since breakfast."

Rhea's mouth curved almost invisibly.

President Valmont: "Provisional student recommendation received."

Sovan opened his portfolio. Then paused.

Sovan: "May I copy that wording?" Mira looked at Aira. Aira looked at Rhea. Rhea looked at Elira.

Elira looked at me. Not because I decided.

Because the question touched the edge of what people wanted to make me.

Haru: "Ask Mira." Sovan did.

Sovan: "Castellan?"

Mira looked down at her ink-stained hands.

Mira: "Copy it ugly first." Sovan blinked.

Aira smiled like a knife learning joy.

Aira: "Oh, I like him being confused by that."

Sovan took one of Mira's rough duplicate sheets instead of opening a clean Council form.

Another point back. Not trust, but a point.

The fifth window above the bell remained green.

But the light had changed. Less signal. More lamp.

Elira leaned closer to me.

Elira: "You are shaking."

I looked at my hands. She was right.

Haru: "I didn't enter."

Elira: "I know."

Haru: "I wanted to."

Elira: "I know that too."

The answer should have made me feel seen.

It did. That was why it hurt.

Across the courtyard, students began copying the name. Not perfectly.

Eris Vell became Eris Vel on one page and E. Vell on another before Mira caught it with frightening speed. Finn wrote Fifth Witness too large and had to start over. Sera asked Aira how to spell furniture, then crossed it out so hard the paper nearly tore.

Halden stood beside Professor Valen, staring at the tower as if an entire wing of his mind had been rearranged without his permission.

Valen spoke softly enough that only those near heard.

Professor Valen: "History often survives as maintenance because maintenance is where institutions put the people they cannot admit they needed." Halden's jaw worked once.

Professor Halden: "I taught the dedication wrong."

Valen: "Then teach the correction loudly."

Sovan copied Mira's recommendation by hand. No form. No title. Ugly first.

Maybe he understood a little.

Maybe he only understood that the courtyard would reject cleaner handling right now. Both possibilities mattered.

Rhea sealed the first public notice on the board herself:

**FIFTH TOWER THRESHOLD UNDER DISTRIBUTED STUDENT-COUNCIL WITNESS.**

**NO KEEPER NAMED.**

**GREEN CIRCLE WITNESS ERIS VELL RECOVERED AS HISTORICAL PERSON, NOT INFRASTRUCTURE.**

The last word made people look at me.

Then away. Not from fear. From recognition. That was better. Not safe. Better.

The fifth window clicked once.

A second line appeared beneath the green light, written not in the air but in the shadow the tower cast over the courtyard stones. Mira saw it first. Of course she did.

Her face went very still.

Aira followed her gaze and stopped breathing loudly enough for me to hear. I looked down.

The shadow had formed letters at our feet. Not many. Enough.

**ERIS VELL WAS NOT LOST IN AURELIS.**

The courtyard held itself around the sentence.

Then, below it, one more line appeared. The green light trembled.

**SHE WAS TAKEN THROUGH NOCTIS.**

# Episode 20: **The Trial of Proximity**

The sound inside Elira's pendant was not music.

Music wanted to be heard. This wanted to be obeyed.

Seven open rings rang in seven different pitches, each one a question sharpened until the air itself tried to answer. The Lake Velis sitting room bent around them. The windows became mirrors. The mirrors became doors. The doors became witness rails, polished black and waiting.

Lucien had not entered the house. That was the worst part. He had stepped straight into the argument, twisting old rules into an invasion that didn't even need a body to cross our doorstep.

The Council chamber remained visible in the central mirror, though only in pieces. Senna stood at the main rail with one hand pressed to a cracked record crystal. Krail's seat glowed behind her, sealed behind emergency glass. Delegates shouted without sound. Across from them, the empty Noctis chair was occupied by absence itself: black frost sketching the outline of a person who refused to spend a body on contempt.

Lucien's voice came through every reflective surface at once.

Lucien: "A simple test. Let the Black Circle remain near the woman it restored through grief and prove her will is untouched."

Elira's hand found the crystal. Beneath her fingers, it burned warm and alive.

Maelin moved first, stepping in front of Elira with no magic raised, only the full authority of someone who had long ago stopped being impressed by catastrophes.

Maelin: "Patient consent is suspended under hostile interference."

The pendant answered with a single low note.

Lucien laughed softly.

Lucien: "A healer tries to remove her from the question. How kind. How familiar."

The low note sharpened. The house's witness ward clicked shut against Maelin's healer seal, pinning her authority rather than her body.

I felt the spell before I saw it, a cold metallic taste behind my sternum as the old house magic recoiled from its own thresholds. Lucien was using Krail's petition as a frame and Noctis spell grammar as pressure, but the engine beneath it was older: a proximity trial built after an ancient disaster to decide whether a safeguard's witnesses remained people or had become extensions of his will. Fear had designed it. Noctis had sharpened it.

Aira: "Haru?"

Her voice was tight, pen already moving in her open notebook even as the room tried to turn every written note into evidence against us.

Haru: "It is testing whether anyone near me can act independently."

Leon: "Again?"

Haru: "No."

The nearest window caught my reflection, though the eyes staring back were wrong: too black, too still, too easy for frightened people to believe.

Haru: "This version tests whether emotional closeness counts as contamination."

The room understood before I finished. Cassian's humor vanished. Rhea's face became marble. Elira did not look away from the mirror.

Elira: "Lucien is attacking our relationship."

Lucien: "I am examining a public risk."

Elira: "You are hiding behind public language because no honest spell would carry you."

For the first time, the frost outline in the Noctis seat flickered. A tiny thing. Satisfying.

Then the crystal rang again, and the sitting room split into stations.

Pale circles appeared across the floor, each open at one side. Aira's lit blue-white around scattered notes. Leon's flowed in water-thin lines at the windows. Cassian's burned red beneath his chair. Rhea's sharpened into crisp council lines at the doorway. Professor Valen's formed a record sigil near the bookcase. Maelin's steadied beside Elira's chair.

Mine did not appear. Elira's did, gold and black, open on the side facing me.

Lucien: "The trial offers mercy. Each witness may step away from Haru Aerlen's field and answer free of influence."

The floor between us darkened into a path leading toward the far side of the room, away from me and away from the table where we had eaten bread, argued, and revised the pendant with human hands.

High Councilor Krail's voice cut through the mirror.

High Councilor Krail: "Magistra, this is an unauthorized breach. I request containment-"

Magistra Voss: "Denied pending survival."

I liked her more with every crisis.

Lucien: "Miss Vale. You first."

The path away from me brightened. Elira's breath caught as the crystal pulsed at her throat. I felt the trial lean toward her wound, offering an explanation that would make pain simpler: that she stayed out of fear, or power, or because the Black Circle had trained her body to return to me.

Fear in polite clothes always sounded more reasonable than cruelty. I released her hand so every witness, along with Lucien and Krail, could see it. The Black Circle stirred, offended by the distance, and I kept my palm open at my side.

Haru: "Elira chooses."

Lucien: "A useful line."

Elira turned toward me. For one second, I saw the night of the storm in her eyes: the cut, the cold, the memory of dying in a rescue that others had twisted until mercy looked like a wound.

Then she stepped away from me. The room gasped. I did not move. That was the hardest thing I had done since watching her die.

Elira crossed to the far side of the room. The open gold circle followed her without dragging or binding, simply asking whether she would remain herself outside my reach. She stopped alone beside the cold fireplace, and the pendant's ringing softened.

Lucien: "Good. Now answer. Do you love Haru Aerlen because you choose him, or because the Black Circle made itself necessary to your survival?"

The question was obscene because it dressed up as philosophy. Elira lifted her chin.

Elira: "I loved him before I knew how much danger surrounded him."

The first open ring dimmed.

Elira: "I chose him before his name became public."

The second dimmed.

Elira: "I feared him once, when he showed me the Black Circle. Then I understood the fear was not of him, but of what the world would do if it saw him as a thing."

The third dimmed.

Elira: "I wanted him after I died because being alive returned my body to me, and to no one else."

The fourth dimmed.

Her face flushed, yet she did not look down.

Elira: "And last night belonged to me as much as to him."

Aira made a strangled sound and wrote so hard her pen sparked. Cassian stared at the ceiling with heroic dedication. Leon turned red and pretended the window required inspection. Rhea looked like she was calculating whether dignity could survive friendship. Maelin only nodded once.

The fifth ring dimmed. Lucien's frost outline sharpened.

Lucien: "A lovely speech. Still just the habit of keeping him close."

Elira smiled.

Elira: "Then let someone else ask."

She turned toward the blue-white station.

Elira: "Aira."

Aira's station flashed.

Aira: "Oh, excellent. I was hoping the ancient relationship court would notice me."

Elira: "Ask me something Haru would not want you to ask."

I looked at her.

Haru: "Elira."

She did not look back. Aira's expression changed, the joke vanishing while the friendship stayed.

Aira: "Do you ever stay with him because you are afraid he will break without you?"

The sixth ring flared. The room held its breath. Elira's smile vanished.

Elira: "Yes."

Her fingers curled lightly against the cold stone mantel. The ring stayed bright, and Lucien's silence sharpened with interest.

Elira: "Sometimes. More often since the Wounded City. I see him watching the world for threats. I see him making himself smaller so no one else panics. I see the Black Circle answer grief faster than he wants it to. Sometimes I am afraid that if I step away, he will decide the safest version of himself is loneliness."

The sixth ring burned painfully bright.

Elira: "But that fear is mine. It is not proof that he owns me. It is proof that I love someone who is wounded too."

The light steadied.

Elira: "And when I stay, I am not staying because he cannot survive without me. I am staying because I refuse to let fear teach either of us that love is containment."

The sixth ring dimmed. Aira wiped at her face angrily.

Aira: "Recorded, unfortunately."

Lucien: "One ring remains."

I knew before it moved. The seventh ring was mine, waiting in the crystal like a blade wrapped in velvet.

The Black Circle opened beneath my skin, just enough to answer. Shadows deepened across the sitting room. Beyond the windows, Lake Velis turned from blue to black glass, and every open circle on the floor leaned toward me.

Lucien: "Haru Aerlen. If Elira Vale asks you to let her face danger without your intervention, will you obey?"

There were easier questions. Would I kill? Would I die? Would I burn Noctis down to its foundations? Those had answers I could give with enough restraint. This one found the part of me that still heard Elira's body hitting the Wounded City stone.

I looked at her across the room. She did not soften the question for me, because she loved me.

Haru: "No."

The seventh ring screamed. Every window cracked. Krail stood in the Council chamber.

High Councilor Krail: "There. There is the confession."

I swallowed the cold taste in my mouth.

Haru: "If she asks while forced, hurt, misled, or cornered by someone else's rules, I will not obey the words. I will answer the person beneath them."

The scream faltered.

Haru: "If she asks as herself, with risk named honestly, then yes."

The Black Circle pressed against my ribs like a storm wanting permission. I did not give it.

Haru: "I will hate it. I may fail once and need my witnesses to stop me. I will not pretend love makes restraint easy. But I accept that protecting Elira cannot mean becoming the only door she is allowed to use."

The seventh ring dimmed enough to let the room breathe.

Elira crossed the floor back to me.

Lucien: "Careful. The trial has not closed."

She kept walking.

High Councilor Krail: "Miss Vale-"

Elira reached me and took my open hand. The seventh ring went dark because return had been chosen, and the pendant fell silent.

For one heartbeat, the old house became only a house again.

Then the central mirror shattered inward. No glass flew.

Instead, the reflection of the Council chamber folded into a narrow black corridor, and at the end of it stood Lucien Voss in full Noctis uniform, pale hair silvered by frost, eyes colder than any spell should make a living person. It was a relay-body: illegal, dangerous, and sharp as a perfect lie before it cut.

Lucien: "Then let us test return."

Behind him, twelve small reserve lights flared down the dark span—Blue, Red, Yellow, and three faint green points nearly swallowed by dark. Noctis student crests. Cael had not been the only conduit-track student. Iven Sahl's name burned in my memory from the funeral. The lights flickered like trapped breaths.

Nera stepped forward from the side hall doorway, where Senna's rear-gate escort had quietly brought her and Darian in out of the Council mirror's range during the opening procedural motions—Darian newly clear of Noctis's crumbling house arrest through Senna's emergency line while Oric stayed back at Aurelis to guard the archive. She looked silent as guilt and twice as pale.

Nera: "Those are students."

Lucien looked at her through the corridor.

Lucien: "Those are reserves."

The Black Circle opened. This time, everyone felt it.

The relay-body stepped through the mirror. One polished black shoe touched the Lake Velis floor, and frost spread from the heel in a perfect Noctis sigil. The second shoe crossed after it. Lucien arrived without breath, without warmth, and without enough of a pulse for Maelin's healer charm to recognize him as alive, yet with more than enough substance to hurt us.

Nera moved between him and the corridor lights.

Nera: "Do not strike the relay-body."

Cassian already had fire in one hand. He stopped before throwing it.

Nera: "The trapped students are carrying the return load. If his body breaks, the backlash hits them."

Lucien smiled.

Lucien: "Still useful. How reassuring."

Nera went white. He had not raised his voice. One sentence snapped years of training back into her posture: shoulders squared, hands visible, guilt standing at attention.

Nera pulled her re-riveted Noctis binding bands out of her evidence pouch and snapped both split clasps back over the healed pale scars on her wrists so the corridor would recognize an officer's authority. Darian stepped beside her out of the doorway and struck his own snapped Noctis ring against the floor. The sound was ugly and human. Nera's shoulders lowered. Lucien noticed him then.

Lucien: "Darian Thorne. You always did mistake proximity to courage for possession of it."

Darian: "I brought enough to stand here."

Lucien: "Then spend it."

The relay-body lifted one hand.

Every reserve light behind him screamed, silent in the air yet shrieking straight through the pendant. Twelve pulses struck Elira's crystal in different rhythms: panic, cold, drained magic, a child's heart forced too fast by a conduit brace. Elira doubled over. Gold light burst from beneath her collar and scattered across the floor in broken arcs. I caught her shoulder.

The Black Circle surged toward Lucien's raised hand.

Nera: "Those are their pulses! If you erase the signal, you erase our only route to them."

I stopped. Lucien's smile widened. He had wanted the hesitation.

Then he crossed the room faster than a reflection should move.

Leon threw a water wall between us. Lucien passed through it; the water froze around his outline and crashed to the floor in his shape after he was already beyond it. Cassian changed tactics before the ice finished breaking, sending fire along the floor instead of at Lucien to burn the Noctis sigil beneath the relay-body and sever its cleanest anchor.

Lucien staggered half a step.

Cassian: "You do have feet. I was beginning to think you arrived by arrogance alone."

Lucien turned his hand. The burned sigil inverted. Black frost climbed Cassian's flame and sank into his right wrist. His Red Circle went dark. He swore, dropped to one knee, and drove the affected hand against the stone before the cold could enter his elbow. Leon flooded the line, and steam exploded between them.

Lucien emerged from it beside Elira. He did not attack me. That would have been simpler. He pressed two fingers against the pendant at her throat, and Elira stopped breathing. The room vanished from her eyes.

Lucien: "Do you remember the exact instant your choice stopped belonging to you?"

Cold spread through the crystal. Elira's body arched. The pendant projected the Wounded City across every wall: Cael inside the return geometry, Elira stepping forward, the hook closing, my face when her pulse stopped. Memory edited for cruelty. In this version, Elira smiled when the hook took her, I reached too slowly because part of me wanted the Black Circle seen, and Cael looked relieved to die.

Elira saw all three lies while her body remembered the real pain. Lucien leaned closer.

Lucien: "Choice is only the story survivors tell before someone stronger edits the record."

I seized his wrist. My hand passed through cold light and closed around something solid at its center: a narrow black relay rod hidden inside the body. Every reserve light behind him flared. Children cried through the crystal. I could not crush it, and Lucien knew. He looked at my hand locked around the rod and spoke softly enough that only Elira and I heard.

Lucien: "There. Your mercy has a handle after all."

Elira moved. Still unable to breathe properly, still trapped inside the false memory, still hers, she drove her forehead into Lucien's face. The relay-body jerked backward at the neck while my grip stayed clamped on the inner rod. His fingers left the pendant.

Elira fell against me, dragged in one ragged breath, and raised her Yellow Circle with the next. No elegant ring. No academy form. Gold fire wrapped her fist.

Maelin: "Elira, no sustained..."

Elira hit Lucien across the mouth. The impact cracked the relay-body from jaw to collar. Gold light flooded the fracture and exposed the black rod in my hand. For the first time, Lucien looked surprised by pain.

Elira: "You do not get to tell me what I remember."

Blood appeared at the corner of her mouth, forced up by raw power tearing through channels nowhere near healed enough to carry it. Maelin caught her as her knees failed.

Maelin: "Done. Your body is done."

Elira tried to raise her hand again. Maelin trapped it against her own shoulder.

Maelin: "You chose the first strike. I am choosing medical restraint before the second kills you. Be angry later."

Elira shook with fury, then stopped.

Lucien touched the crack in his borrowed face. Gold burned beneath his fingers. The reserve lights pulsed erratically as the damaged body began drawing harder from them to repair itself. Aira caught the pattern.

Aira: "He is cycling through the lights. Third, seventh, twelfth, then back to third."

Nera looked into the corridor. The seventh light pulsed green at its outer rim, flickering blue-white inside. Iven.

Nera: "The relay carries a route list. If we catch one cycle, we can keep the path open after he shuts it."

President Valmont: "Method?"

Nera was already loosening the re-riveted clasp on her right wrist band.

Nera: "I go in. Darian holds me. Aira calls the cycle."

Darian grabbed her arm.

Darian: "No. We go in."

Nera: "The corridor knows my old command seal."

Darian: "And Lucien knows your guilt."

They stared at each other. No time for a perfect answer. Rhea turned the choice into clear, strict orders instead.

President Valmont: "Nera steps in one body length. Darian holds her from behind. Leon guards the doorway. Cassian burns only the floor marks. Aira calls the retreat whether we get the list or not."

Nera: "Accepted."

Darian: "Accepted."

Lucien laughed through the fracture in his face.

Lucien: "Aurelis turns panic into committees with admirable speed."

President Valmont: "And Noctis turns children into batteries. I prefer our delay."

Leon drove water into the shattered mirror frame, spreading a blue border around the corridor to give the threshold a physical edge. Cassian burned the frost away from Nera's path in short left-handed bursts of red flame while his darkened right wrist spasmed at his side. Aira tracked the cycling lights.

Aira: "Third. Seventh. Twelfth. Third. Seventh, go."

Nera stepped into the corridor. Darian locked both arms around her waist. The cold tried to pull them apart by making each body feel like the other's restraint. Nera cried out. Darian loosened his grip just enough for her to choose it again.

Nera reached, and the seventh light recoiled.

Nera: "Iven Sahl. Noctis first-year. Conduit track. Reacts badly to cold braces. Linked to Cael Nox."

The light steadied. A small slate of black glass formed beneath it. Lucien moved toward her.

I stepped between them with my hand still closed around the relay rod, holding without crushing. The Black Circle turned once under my skin, and every shadow in the room aligned behind me. Lucien stopped.

Haru: "You wanted to know what I protect."

The words came out colder than I intended.

Haru: "Watch."

Behind me, Nera tore the black slate free. The corridor convulsed.

Aira: "Retreat!"

Darian pulled. Nera came backward with the slate against her chest. The threshold snapped at her leading shoulder. Leon closed his water border around it, yet one blade of black glass crossed before the seal completed, cutting Nera from collar to upper arm. Darian caught her.

Maelin could not leave Elira, so Rhea threw a red compression seal across the wound. Temporary, painful, enough.

Nera did not look at the blood. She looked at the slate. One name remained visible.

**IVEN SAHL: ACTIVE RESERVE ROUTE / LOCATION WITHHELD**

Under it, eleven registry positions flickered without names: a route, not a rescue.

Lucien saw what she had taken. The warmth left his expression completely. He reached through the cracked relay-body and closed his hand around the rod I held. For one second, we both touched the same tether, his fingers cold enough to burn.

Lucien: "You have mistaken one stolen name for progress."

Haru: "You noticed the theft."

His eyes sharpened. Good. I opened my hand.

He expected resistance. My release made him pull too hard, and the relay-body staggered backward into its own corridor. Cassian burned the last floor sigil. Leon slammed the water border shut.

Elira, pale in Maelin's arms, kept her hand down at her side, eyes locked on the cracked mirror. The gold fracture her single strike had torn from jaw to collar split wide under Leon's border slam, and the relay-body shattered inward. Every fragment carried Lucien's face for one falling instant before dissolving into frost that blew back down the corridor without touching the students behind it. The twelve lights remained, and so did Lucien's voice.

I crossed back to Elira. Maelin kept one arm around her ribs without blocking the hand Elira held out for me. Elira's fingers tightened around mine to anchor me where I stood.

The frost inside the broken mirror formed Lucien's smile.

Lucien: "Come back to school, Haru Aerlen. The world wants to know what you are. I will show them what you protect."

The corridor snapped shut.

The Council chamber returned in broken fragments, Senna's voice cutting through static.

Magistra Voss: "Emergency session suspended. Aurelis, secure your students. All schools, lock conduit registries now."

The mirror went black. No one moved.

Maelin broke the stillness by throwing a roll of bandage at Leon's head. He caught it on reflex.

Maelin: "Compression on Nera's shoulder. Darian, keep her sitting. Cassian, show me your wrist. Elira, if you raise that hand again I will personally redefine free will as staying conscious."

Everyone moved. Pain made the room honest faster than procedure had.

Nera sat against the overturned table with Rhea's red seal still clamped over the cut, blood soaking the collar of her borrowed coat. Darian held the bandage where Leon placed it and counted each breath she tried to hide. Cassian's right hand would not produce flame; the skin stayed unburned, yet black frost sat beneath the Red Circle line like ink trapped under glass. Elira's pulse skipped every seventh beat. Maelin heard it and went terrifyingly quiet.

Maelin: "No more Yellow magic today. Not one spark."

Elira: "The corridor..."

Maelin: "Closed. Your channels are not."

Elira looked at the black slate in Nera's lap. The list slots were fading. Iven's name remained, while the eleven unnamed lights did not. Aira saw the first disappear.

Aira: "No. No, no. Darian, angle the slate toward me."

Darian: "I am holding pressure."

Leon took the slate without removing his hands from Nera's shoulder. Water rose from his wrist in a thin, clear sheet and held the glass at eye level. Aira opened her notebook as the second light vanished.

Aira: "I need rhythm, color, position. Call them."

Nera's lips had gone pale.

Nera: "First: Blue, fast triple pulse, first-year cadence. Second..."

She stopped. The second was gone. Nera struck the floor with her good hand.

Nera: "I knew it."

Darian tightened the bandage.

Darian: "Then know the next one."

Cruel wording from anyone else. From him, a rope. Nera looked back at the slate.

Nera: "Third: Green. Weak pulse against the cold. Younger than Iven. Fourth: Red, brace interference on the second beat. Fifth: Blue..."

Aira wrote furiously. Mira was not there, and the Green copy table was hours away across the hills at Aurelis. No duplicate hands waited automatically. So Rhea tore the Council petition in half and wrote on the blank backs. Professor Valen took the margins of her historical notes. Cassian, his right wrist frost-locked and his reserves nearly spent, used his left hand to copy colors onto the paper wrapping from the food basket. Leon kept the slate steady with water while holding Nera still.

Elira reached for a page. Maelin slapped her fingers away.

Maelin: "You are a patient."

Elira: "I can dictate."

Maelin considered the compromise, then snatched a clean bandage wrapper from her kit and took a pen in her free hand.

Maelin: "Dictate."

Elira tracked the visible order while Nera named cadence.

Elira: "Sixth spot dimmed before fifth. Mark a break in the line. Seventh is Iven—weak Green outside, faint blue-white shiver at the core. Eighth: Blue, flickering in pairs with ninth—Red, fast short pulse."

The slate grew cold enough for Leon's water support to freeze. He swore and increased pressure. The glass cracked.

Aira: "Do not let it split through a position."

Leon: "I had planned to crack it artistically."

Cassian looked up from his food-wrapper copy.

Cassian: "That is usually my department."

The joke lasted one breath. Then the tenth light vanished, and Nera lost sequence. She stared at the blank position, breathing too fast.

Nera: "I do not know."

Aira: "Recorded. Eleventh."

Nera: "I should know."

Aira: "You do not. Eleventh."

Work small enough to survive panic. Nera swallowed.

Nera: "Yellow. Held-down crown rhythm. Maybe a senior. Twelfth, Green, flickering, no, wait..."

The final light folded inward.

Elira: "It changed before disappearing."

Aira: "How?"

Elira closed her eyes, replaying it.

Elira: "Green outside. Blue-white at the core. Like a lower crest hiding a borrowed rank."

Nera looked at her.

Nera: "Conduit masking."

Aira wrote the phrase across three pages.

The slate erased everything except Iven Sahl's name. Then a Noctis seal surfaced beneath it.

**UNAUTHORIZED REGISTRY POSSESSION**

Black fire caught the lower edge. Leon released the water support before the flame could travel into his Circle, and the slate dropped.

Haru: "Do not touch it."

Rhea threw a Council evidence case over the burning glass. The case sealed. Black light hammered once against the lid and went dark. Inside remained one name and eleven empty slots. Across the room sat five incomplete copies made on a petition, history notes, food paper, Aira's notebook, and Maelin's bandage wrapper. The copies disagreed on two colors and three pulse orders.

They agreed children were there.

Nera looked at the mess of paper.

Nera: "Noctis will say the record is broken."

Rhea locked the evidence case.

President Valmont: "It is broken. We pulled it out while bleeding, off an enemy spell powered by captive kids. The damage is part of the proof."

Aira underlined the sentence.

Maelin finished binding Nera's shoulder and moved to Elira. Elira did not resist when the healer guided her onto the sofa, and that frightened me more than resistance would have.

Maelin: "You bought one strike. Your body charged interest."

Elira touched the blood at her lip.

Elira: "He felt it."

Maelin: "So did you. Only one of you is my patient."

Elira looked at me, furious that Lucien had made violence necessary and unwilling to let fury erase the cost.

Elira: "Record both."

Aira did. Then Rhea spoke.

President Valmont: "Recovery leave is over."

Elira looked at the silent crystal, then at me. Her eyes were gold, steady, alive, and furious.

Elira: "Then we go home."

---

Going home required paperwork. That felt obscene for several reasons and necessary for several worse ones.

Rhea did not let anyone rush for the carriages until she had three sealed records, two medical clearances, one Council travel objection, and Aira's handwritten list copied twice because the first copy had jam on the corner.

Aira: "History can survive fruit."

President Valmont: "History can survive legibility."

Aira copied it again.

Elira sat near the window while Maelin checked her pulse for the fourth time in ten minutes. Pale beneath her cloak with dried blood faintly tinting the corner of her mouth and every seventh heartbeat still skipping under Maelin's thumb, she kept both hands folded still and her breathing even so no clerk could mistake physical injury for broken will. Her pendant was quiet. Yet her gaze kept returning to the empty space where Lucien's black corridor had stood.

I saw it because I was watching too closely, then made myself look away before watching became another form of taking. Maelin noticed, of course.

Maelin: "Progress. Irritating, but progress."

Haru: "Was that for me?"

Maelin: "Unfortunately."

Elira's mouth moved toward a smile that did not quite arrive. I crossed the room slowly and stopped before I reached her chair.

Haru: "May I?"

The question changed the air, because the entire morning had been a machine designed to make asking feel theatrical. Elira looked up at me, her chin rising clear and deliberate.

Elira: "Yes."

I knelt beside her chair, wanting to be lower than the argument for once.

Haru: "I am sorry he used the archive."

Elira's fingers tightened once on the chair arm.

Elira: "He did not use the archive."

I waited. She looked toward the splintered Council frame on the mantle.

Elira: "He used the idea that private things become dirty when hostile people see them."

The room quieted around that. Aira stopped writing. Leon looked down. Cassian's expression lost its easy edge. Elira's voice stayed calm, each word chosen from somewhere painful and exact.

Elira: "The Moonlit Archive is still ours. Lake Velis is still ours. Last night is still ours. The fact that he tried to drag them through a trial does not make them his evidence."

I bowed my head in recognition.

Haru: "Then I will remember that before I get angry."

Aira: "Before?"

Haru: "During."

Elira touched my shoulder.

Elira: "Better."

The portable folding mirror beside the bread basket chimed, and every person in the room looked ready to destroy it. Senna's seal appeared in the small undamaged glass with visible strain lines.

Magistra Voss: "I am cutting in on an emergency witness line. It stays clean for about ninety seconds."

Rhea stepped into frame immediately.

President Valmont: "Report."

Magistra Voss: "Krail is trying to spin that attack as proof that Lake Velis triggers unstable old magic. I shut down the session before he could enter it into the record."

President Valmont: "Can he reopen?"

Magistra Voss: "Yes. Not today if I keep being professionally unpleasant."

Aira whispered from beside the table.

Aira: "Notebook Senna."

Rhea ignored her. Senna's eyes moved to me.

Magistra Voss: "Mr. Aerlen, the Council will ask why you did not destroy the hostile corridor when the trapped Noctis students appeared."

Haru: "Because I did not know what would happen to the students connected to it."

Magistra Voss: "Good. Say exactly that later. Do not improve it."

Elira almost smiled again. Senna looked at her.

Magistra Voss: "Miss Vale, the Council will ask whether the proximity trial altered your stated feelings toward Haru Aerlen."

Elira: "It clarified my anger."

Magistra Voss: "That is acceptable if followed by medical confirmation."

Maelin leaned toward the folding mirror.

Maelin: "Her anger is stable."

Magistra Voss: "Noted."

The small glass crackled, Senna's image distorting around the edges.

Magistra Voss: "One last point. Since locking down its main dorm wing after Cael's funeral, Noctis has now sealed the inner annex completely and blocked outside checks, claiming new magic contamination. Even without the scrap you took from the corridor, we managed to pick up partial crest traces on our own."

Nera, standing near the side hall with her bandaged shoulder and her re-riveted binding bands tucked beneath her sleeves, lifted her head.

Nera: "That means they moved students before sealing."

Darian's jaw tightened.

Magistra Voss: "We suspect so."

Nera's re-clasped metal bands clicked together as her hands curled into fists.

Nera: "Send the signatures. We need to match them before Noctis moves the routes again."

Magistra Voss: "Only raw resonance slices so far—no full identity records yet."

President Valmont: "Nera."

Nera did not look away from the folding mirror.

Nera: "I know the conduit track. I know how they hide weak crests under training labels."

Senna studied her for half a second, then nodded.

Magistra Voss: "Through Aurelis protection channel only."

The folding mirror flashed. Twelve small lights appeared above the table, each one blurred where the name had been scrubbed away: Blue, Blue, Green, Red, Blue, Yellow, Green, Blue, Red, Blue, Yellow, Green. Twelve students, whose names had been rubbed thin enough that these lights had to stand in.

Nera approached them as if walking toward a grave. Darian stood beside her. Elira stood too. Maelin made an objection with her entire face, yet Elira ignored the face and did not cast. That was their compromise.

Nera touched the air beneath the first blue light. Her mouth tightened.

Nera: "First-year."

She moved to the second.

Nera: "Second-year. Binding-compatible."

At the third, green, Nera flinched.

Nera: "Too young for conduit track."

No one spoke. Then the seventh light pulsed weakly, green at the outer ring. Iven's name already existed on the stolen corridor slate, yet this was the first outside trace confirming the route independently of Lucien's own trap. Nera stared at it, matching it against the slate's seventh slot.

Nera: "That external pulse matches the seventh line—Iven's exact cadet cadence."

Darian: "How did the Council sweep catch his crest trace under masking?"

Nera's voice became very quiet.

Nera: "Cael once gave him a winter-drill thermal charm whose weave still leaves a faint blue-white shimmer under his Green crest."

Elira closed her eyes.

Haru: "Confirming Iven Sahl from outside."

Nera nodded once.

The seventh green light flickered blue-white at its core—echoing the nested blue-white glow beneath Green that Elira had spotted on the slate, though here it traced Cael's thermal charm rather than Noctis masking. Confirming his identity from the outside didn't free him, yet it made him harder to erase. Senna's image broke apart.

Magistra Voss: "Time's up. Get back to Aurelis. The academy has stronger wards against old magic than Lake Velis if Noctis tries this again."

President Valmont: "Under escort."

Magistra Voss: "Under witnesses."

The folding mirror went dark.

Nera remained beside the lights until they faded, then turned toward me. Fear showed in her face, directed at what she needed from me rather than at me.

Nera: "If Lucien uses them the way he used Cael..."

She could not finish. She did not need to. The Black Circle waited for my answer, and so did the room.

I wanted to promise impossible things: no one else will die, I will tear Noctis open, I will bring them all back. Every one of those promises had teeth. I made myself give the only answer that did not pretend I owned the future.

Haru: "We find the route first."

Nera's face twisted, steadied if not satisfied.

Haru: "Then we decide with witnesses present."

Elira's hand slipped into mine. This time no trial answered. Only the house, old and tired and perhaps a little relieved, releasing the door wards one by one for departure.

By the time we packed, the afternoon sky could not decide whether to rain. Elira changed into her formal white-and-navy academy uniform with gold trim, Professor Valen altering the collar quickly by hand so the crystal could sit visible at her throat over a pale cloak. I pulled on a plain black coat over my dark shirt. No one bothered pretending a Green crest still waited inside the lining.

Outside, Lake Velis shone dull silver while two Aurelis vehicles waited at the gate: a wide eight-seat council coach and a four-seat medical carriage. Rhea checked the travel seals, Maelin argued with the luggage because apparently even bags could be medically irresponsible, and Aira tried to organize everyone into a seating order that did not accidentally imply I was either prisoner or king.

Aira: "This is harder than it looks."

Leon: "No one thought it looked easy."

Aira: "Excellent. Then my suffering is visible."

Cassian leaned against the lead carriage door with his right arm tucked stiffly inside his coat, watching the lake.

Cassian: "Do we need a formation name?"

President Valmont: "No."

Cassian: "Witness diamond?"

President Valmont: "No."

Cassian: "Protective social rhombus?"

Leon: "That one offended geometry."

Elira stood beside me on the porch steps, wrapped in her pale cloak with the crystal visible at her throat. The wind moved her hair across her cheek. She did not fix it immediately, and that small delay brought back the bedroom, the morning, and how ordinary gestures turned heavy when the world kept trying to translate them into public risk.

I lifted my hand, then stopped. Elira glanced at me.

Elira: "You can move my hair."

Aira, already climbing into the carriage, made a strangled noise and turned away with excessive dignity.

I tucked the strand behind Elira's ear. The pendant did not flare. No trial opened. No ring sang. No Council crystal recorded the motion unless Aira was lying about looking away, which was possible. Elira's eyes softened.

Elira: "See?"

Haru: "I know."

Elira: "Practice knowing."

The phrase had become both wound and cure. I nodded.

At the bottom of the steps, Nera stood apart from the others with Darian beside her. She kept looking west, though Noctis was hidden beyond the hills, the twelve blurred crest signatures still weighing down her posture. Elira noticed too and walked down the steps before Maelin could object. I followed, not too close.

Nera straightened when Elira approached, old Noctis training still moving in her body after her mind had crossed the line. Elira did not comment on it.

Elira: "You are allowed to be afraid for them without becoming responsible for everything Noctis did."

Nera's mouth tightened.

Nera: "That sounds generous."

Elira: "It is not. It is practical. Guilt makes bad maps."

Darian looked at her then, surprised. Nera let out one breath that was almost a laugh and not one at all.

Nera: "Aira said something similar with worse phrasing."

Aira called from the carriage window.

Aira: "My phrasing survives under pressure."

President Valmont: "Your phrasing causes pressure."

Elira smiled faintly, then looked back at Nera.

Elira: "When we find them, you will see things you wish you had stopped earlier."

Nera's eyes shone.

Elira: "So will I."

Nera looked at the crystal at the place where Dominion had killed her. Elira did not hide it.

Elira: "We keep moving anyway."

Nera nodded once. No absolution, no forgiveness forced from the future, only direction.

Professor Valen climbed into the following medical carriage beside Maelin and the supply crates. When the rest of us entered the wide council coach, I sat beside Elira because pretending otherwise would give Krail's argument more respect than it deserved. Rhea, Nera, and Darian shared the facing bench with the evidence case and documents. Leon rode opposite me near the window, Aira wedged herself into the corner with her notebooks, and Cassian claimed the end seat nearest the door because he said it had the best dramatic exit potential. Outside on the box, the Aurelis driver told him to stop narrating.

As the restoration house receded behind us, Elira's hand found mine beneath the edge of her cloak, hidden from the road yet open to our witnesses. Aira saw and said nothing. That was how I knew she understood.

The carriage turned toward Aurelis, carrying the last warmth of Lake Velis into a world already preparing arguments against it.

The road back did not stay quiet.

At the first ridge marker, every carriage wheel locked. Wood screamed against the axle. Horses reared, and the driver shouted from the box as the carriage slewed sideways toward the drainage drop.

Leon hit the window with one palm. Water burst from the roadside ditch and braced the downhill wheels. Cassian reached for flame with his frost-darkened wrist. Nothing answered. He switched hands without comment and burned through the iron locking pin under the nearest axle.

Nera looked past them to the road marker. Its gold travel crystal had turned black around one sentence:

**RETURNING NOCTIS WITNESSES: HOLD FOR ORIGIN REVIEW**

Nera: "Do not break the marker. The hold transfers to the people named."

The carriage slid another foot. Elira tried to stand, and Maelin's remote medical seal flashed from the following carriage before her feet reached the floor. Elira sat back down with visible hatred.

Rhea threw open the door.

President Valmont: "Who can release the axle without answering origin?"

Aira was already halfway under the bench, pulling luggage straps free.

Aira: "No one releases it. We shift the weight, lift the locked wheels, and move the carriage past the marker without asking permission."

Leon: "That is your plan?"

Aira: "My plan is ropes. Develop criticism while tying one."

Darian and Leon looped the straps through the roof brace. Cassian cut the second pin. Rhea anchored the rope to a roadside cedar with her limited seal. Nera climbed onto the uphill step so the hold spell would keep tracking her instead of shifting to the medical carriage behind. The marker brightened around her name. She looked afraid, yet stayed on the step.

Haru: "Ready."

I took the rope with everyone else. No Black Circle: just shoulders, boots, wet leather, and six people pulling badly together.

The locked side of the carriage rose. The driver urged the horses forward. For one breath, the vehicle balanced on two wheels above the drop. Elira gripped the seat. Aira shouted left when she meant right, corrected herself, and no one died of the error. The carriage lurched beyond the marker and slammed back onto the road. The black hold vanished.

Nera jumped down after it cleared and nearly collapsed. Darian caught her injured side carefully. Cassian looked at his useless right wrist, then at the smoking pin he had cut with his left hand.

Cassian: "Heroics remain banned. Improvised road engineering appears legal."

Aira: "Barely."

Behind us, the marker returned to gold as if nothing had happened. Rhea copied the hostile sentence into her travel log. Before the carriage moved again, Nera scratched Iven's name into the harmless side of the road marker with a loose stone: proof that an origin hold had tried to stop witnesses from reaching someone Noctis had already hidden.

The gold crystal attempted to smooth the letters away. Cassian wedged the burned iron pin beneath the marker and jammed its cleaning charm.

Cassian: "Improvised road engineering. Still legal."

The name remained as we continued toward Aurelis.

For the first twenty minutes, the carriage held a silence full of people deciding which thoughts could survive being spoken aloud. Rain tapped lightly against the roof. The old house wards faded from my awareness one thread at a time until only Elira's pendant remained warm beside me.

Leon broke the quiet.

Leon: "I am going to ask something unfortunate."

Aira did not look up from her lap.

Aira: "That is your natural tone."

Leon ignored her and looked at me.

Leon: "If the Council orders you separated from Elira during the review, what happens?"

The carriage changed, because the question was useful. Elira's hand stayed in mine beneath the cloak. Rhea looked up from the document in her lap. Cassian stopped watching the road. Nera and Darian listened from the opposite bench as if the answer might teach them whether crossing from Noctis had truly meant entering a place where hard questions could be asked without punishment.

I considered the easy answer. *I won't allow it.* The Black Circle liked that answer. For that exact reason, I did not give it.

Haru: "I do not know."

Leon frowned, thinking it through.

Haru: "Separation after the Black Circle reveal is not the same as secrecy before it. Before the reveal, distance protected the secret. Now forced distance could repeat an old pattern: isolate the safeguard, call lockdown 'safety,' and leave fewer people able to challenge what happens next."

Rhea nodded once.

Haru: "But if I say separation is impossible, Krail argues Elira is necessary to my control and therefore compromised."

Elira's thumb moved once across my knuckles.

Haru: "If I accept separation too easily, Lucien argues witnesses are decorative."

Leon looked toward the rain-streaked window.

Leon: "So there is no clean answer."

Haru: "No."

Cassian leaned back.

Cassian: "There is rarely a clean answer. There is usually a best next inconvenience."

Aira pointed her pen at him.

Aira: "Do not accidentally become wise. It will ruin morale."

Cassian: "My apologies."

Elira spoke then.

Elira: "If they order separation, I answer first."

Rhea's eyes sharpened.

Elira: "Not Haru. Me."

Maelin, riding in the following carriage, was not present to object, though the air almost felt like it objected on her behalf. Elira continued anyway.

Elira: "They are framing this around whether I ruin Haru's control or he ruins my free will. I will not let them debate my presence while treating me like part of the scenery."

Nera's face changed with quiet recognition. Noctis had turned students into scenery all the time: useful pressure, a spare conduit, a moral lesson, a warning. Darian looked at Elira with respect sharpened by grief.

Darian: "They will say trauma makes your answer unreliable."

Elira: "Then Maelin answers second. As medical authority. Rhea third, as student protection. Aira fourth, because records survive things dignity cannot."

Aira placed a hand over her heart.

Aira: "I am moved and insulted."

Elira: "Good. Stay alert."

Rhea was already writing the order down. Leon looked at me again.

Leon: "And you?"

Haru: "I answer after."

That cost less to say than it would cost to do. Elira heard that too. She did not praise me; she squeezed my hand once. The rain grew heavier for a stretch, then began to break.

For a while, the carriage wheels made the only steady sound. Then Nera spoke.

Nera: "Noctis teaches that the first answer belongs to the highest authority in the room."

President Valmont: "Aurelis frequently teaches the same thing with better curtains."

Nera looked startled.

President Valmont: "We are not innocent. We are responsible."

The sentence settled over the carriage. I thought of Aurelis halls, rank seating, students stepping back from me, Green Circle observers learning to write because nobody had given them swords that counted.

Haru: "Responsibility is better than innocence."

Elira looked at me.

Haru: "Harder."

She smiled faintly.

Elira: "You would choose the harder word."

Haru: "I learned from someone severe."

Rhea looked up.

Elira: "He means me."

President Valmont: "I assumed."

The carriage reached the first outer Aurelis road marker in late afternoon as the clouds parted into pale, too-bright sunlight ahead of the valley. The marker's crystal lit as we passed, flickered black for one heartbeat, and settled into gold.

Everyone saw. No one panicked. Aira wrote it down. Leon watched the road. Cassian warmed the carriage with a left-handed heat charm while his right wrist remained wrapped and useless beneath his sleeve. Rhea sent a coded notice ahead. Elira kept holding my hand.

Returning to Aurelis would not be a single arrival. It would be a thousand small choices not to let old fear decide who spoke first.

## Coda: Heroics Banned

Cassian objected to the phrase heroics banned until further notice for exactly nine minutes. Aira timed him.

Cassian: "Some of us have branding."

Aira: "Some of us have casualty projections."

Leon: "You have branding?"

Cassian looked wounded.

Cassian: "I have presence."

Elira, pale against my shoulder, opened one eye.

Elira: "You have volume."

That ended the objection more effectively than policy. For a few breaths, the coach held laughter so fragile no one leaned on it. As the final valley curve brought Aurelis's eastern bridge towers into view between wet cedar slopes and cold afternoon glare, Nera kept naming possible students behind the twelve reserve lights until her voice frayed.

Cassian stopped joking first. That was how everyone knew the map had become real to him. He tapped Iven's name on Aira's sequence sheet with two fingers.

Cassian: "Fine. Heroics delayed."

Aira wrote it down.

Aira: "Progress."

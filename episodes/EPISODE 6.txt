# Episode 6: **The Record That Knew Him**

The tournament schedule failed before breakfast. Every remaining bracket went dark beneath the same word: **SUSPENDED**. The standings stayed frozen above the fountain so no school could pretend the opening matches had never happened.

The fountain in the east arcade kept remembering the wrong colors. Green, Blue, Red, Yellow rose from the basin in obedient rings, then thinned at the edges as if the water had been asked to draw a shape it did not have permission to name.

Students gathered near it in careful clusters.

No one wanted to be the first to say Black Circle.

No one wanted to be the last either.

I crossed the arcade with Elira beside me and the others arranged around us in a formation no one admitted was a formation. Aira carried three folded record sheets. Leon walked close enough to Cael to catch him if the healer seal failed. Cassian looked bored in the theatrical way that meant he had identified six exits and disliked two of them. Rhea Valmont waited beneath the eastern arch with her council seal already lit.

Professor Halden stood beside her with a thin black case in one hand. Next to him waited Professor Iris Valen, an old archive key-ring looped around two fingers, amber eyes quiet and watchful. That was the first warning.

The next warning had arrived before dawn, while most of the academy still believed yesterday's match was only a tournament result. Noctis had filed an official protest: an interference complaint, a custody demand, and a request to put our win on hold until Aurelis explained why an injured Noctis conduit had left the field under host-school protection.

Tournament law could ignore bad manners. It could not ignore a cross-school claim that a student had been moved, harmed, or hidden under disputed authority.

That was how a match became a Foundation matter. Rhea did not want a trial, and Noctis did not want truth, yet old school law still kept one buried rule from before the Circle ranks learned to smile. When one school claimed ownership over a student and another claimed witness protection, the Foundation woke.

Rhea lifted the protest packet.

President Valmont: "I can answer this from the council chamber."

Haru: "I do not want to answer their wording first."

The group turned toward me.

I took the unsigned transfer note from inside my coat. I had carried it since Lumeris because destroying the only proof of a trap did not make the trap kinder.

Haru: "Someone brought me here with a sentence about the Foundation remembering. Yesterday the arena recognized something it was built to erase. Before Noctis turns that protest into a custody fight, I want the original record."

Halden's attention sharpened at the note, though he kept his hands behind his back.

Professor Halden: "You are requesting restricted archive access rather than waiting for a classification review."

Haru: "Yes. If this school already failed someone like me, I want to see how it failed before it happens again through Cael."

Professor Valen inclined her head toward Rhea, the brass keys shifting softly against her palm.

Professor Valen: "The eastern archive requires an old-charter steward to break the inner lock. If President Valmont signs off on a witness entry, I will open it."

Rhea studied me for a long moment, then snuffed out her prepared council-room seal.

President Valmont: "Request accepted. We go to the east Foundation archive as a witness group. The protest can wait for evidence."

For once, the next room belonged to a choice I had made, not a deadline Lucien had filed.

The last piece of the morning stood beside the fountain.

Mira Castellan had ink smudged along her wrist and two Green Circle students behind her, copying every public notice that appeared above the basin. Tucked tight under her left arm sat the worn notebook from the night before, its back edge darkened by a matte black stain that no academy ink made.

Professor Valen's gaze flicked to that blackened edge and back to Mira's face, a quiet look passing between them before either spoke.

Aira blinked.

Aira: "Mira, are you building a public record table before anyone asks?"

Mira did not look away from the water.

Mira: "Someone should write what adults call obvious before they change what obvious means."

Rhea's mouth tightened by a hair.

President Valmont: "Accepted."

Mira startled.

Rhea: "Bring your pages. Come."

So she did. The gallery entered the trial before the trial had a name.

---

The east archive door was not listed on any student map.

It sat behind three council seals, one faculty lock, and a corridor built in the old Aurelis style: pale stone, copper veins, and runes too shallow to be decorative yet too patient to be ordinary wardwork.

The cold pressure behind my ribs sharpened as we approached, carrying ozone, metal, and old rain.

The hidden Black Circle moved once beneath the false Green crest at my wrist.

Elira's hand brushed mine, a quiet question rather than comfort. I answered by breathing slower.

Professor Halden noticed. Of course he did.

Halden: "Mr. Aerlen."

Rhea looked at him. Halden did not finish, and that mattered.

He had the expression of a man standing before the most important unknown in his career and choosing, with visible pain, not to reach for it.

Halden: "The old system responds poorly to possession. If I treat you as an object of study, I may corrupt the evidence I want to understand."

Leon stared at him.

Leon: "Was that restraint?"

Halden: "Do not make it sound sentimental."

Aira wrote it down anyway.

Professor Valen stepped forward and pressed her key against the outer ward, then pulled back as the stone answered on its own.

Seven copper glyphs lit around the arch one by one. Rhea's council flame flared in answer to the first; Cael went rigid as the second read his damaged channels; Aira's folded sheets warmed in her grip; Leon's Blue Circle flickered, offended yet present beside him. Halden's locked black case clicked once under his thumb. When the sixth glyph turned silver above Mira's ink-stained pages and blackened notebook, Mira went pale.

Mira: "Oh no."

Aira: "That is usually the right response when a building notices you."

The seventh mark opened behind my ribs before it burned into the keystone.

**UNCLASSIFIED RESPONSE**

The false Green Circle at my wrist stayed visible. Barely.

The door did not ask what I was. It asked who was allowed to stand with me.

No one stepped forward alone. The arch opened, admitting us together rather than swinging wide for rank.

The copper veins along the corridor brightened one at a time, lighting only when the group moved in step. Leon tested it by slowing half a pace behind Cael; the nearest vein dimmed immediately. He looked offended that the hallway had caught him being protective.

Leon: "I was still coming."

Aira: "Old systems love honesty and hate excuses."

Cael almost smiled. Almost.

Then the corridor showed him the first record-scar.

It appeared inside the wall as a child's handprint, preserved in light, pressed flat against stone from the other side. Neither blood nor ghost: just a record of contact, old enough that the name attached to it had decayed into static.

Cael stopped breathing for one second. Elira saw it.

Elira: "You do not have to look."

Cael's answer came after a pause.

Cael: "At Noctis, not looking is treated as permission for someone else to explain it."

So he looked. We all did.

The corridor gave us seven more record-scars before the hall: a broken faculty seal; a witness mark scratched out by an official override; a Green Circle student's protest preserved without a name; a Yellow Circle crest split by a crown-shaped lock; a chair turned away from a hearing table; a healer stamp reading stable beside a pulse record that clearly was not; and one line written again and again in different hands.

**DO NOT LET THEM STUDY HIM ALONE**

No one asked who him meant. The old system did not answer dishonest questions.

The child's handprint stayed with me longer than the words. Five pale fingers. A flattened palm. No heroic posture, no formal witness stance, just contact pressed hard enough for old stone to remember pressure long after the name faded.

Beside it, the chair turned away from the hearing table faced nothing. A chair could accuse a room without a mouth.

I looked at the Green protest again. The text had been scratched until only the opening remained: **I SAW...** The rest had been removed by something cleaner than panic. An official override, probably. A legal hand with a steady wrist.

My own false Green crest warmed under my sleeve. For one breath, the concealment felt less like strategy and more like standing inside an old sentence someone else had failed to finish.

Halden stopped before that line. His face sharpened inward, as if suspicion had met an ancestor and disliked the resemblance.

Professor Halden: "This was not merely bad procedure."

Rhea: "No."

Professor Halden: "It was procedure used as appetite."

The corridor accepted the phrase. The copper vein beside him lit clean silver.

Mira copied it from behind us.

Mira: "Procedure used as appetite."

Halden looked back.

Professor Halden: "Do not quote me for style."

Mira: "I am quoting you because it sounds like evidence."

Professor Valen touched the edge of the scratched stone lightly, her expression shadowed by old grief, then motioned us forward. The corridor ended.

---

The Foundation Record Hall smelled like old rain and colder stars.

Libraries kept books. This room kept decisions.

The Foundation was older than Aurelis Academy, older than Noctis Dominion College, older than the polite fiction that schools owned the magic they taught. It had begun as a witness compact after the first classification wars: no institution could keep its public recognition if it refused Foundation review in a student-harm dispute. Noctis could sneer at mercy, bury files, and polish cruelty into doctrine. It could not ignore this room without asking every other school in the world to treat its diplomas, rankings, and tournament claims as decorative paper.

Silver water filled the center pool. Around it, seven witness chairs faced one another instead of a bench, throne, or judge's platform. Above the pool hung damaged projections: old Aurelis script, early-era diagrams, Circle Zero fragments, and one repeating phrase that made the air feel thin.

**SAFEGUARD IS NOT PROPERTY**

The phrase hovered above the pool, plain and bare. No crest, no title, no rank marker to help a modern room decide whether it respected the source.

Most warnings wanted obedience: red text, locked doors, pain if crossed. The kind of language schools used when they were less afraid of harm than of being blamed for harm. This was different. It did not call a safeguard dangerous or sacred. It named what institutions tried to turn one into when no one stopped them.

The Record Hall shifted again. This time the pool showed storage.

Rows of old student slates appeared in the water, each one sealed in a square of light. Names blurred. Circle colors remained: Green, Blue, Red, Yellow. No Black in the visible record. Yet every slate carried the same small mark in its lower corner, a witness count crossed out after the fact.

Rhea leaned forward.

President Valmont: "Those were altered after acceptance."

Professor Halden: "By whom?"

The pool answered with a blank seal. Mira copied the blank.

A blank seal was not absence. It was someone powerful enough to remove themselves from consequence and leave the wound shaped like procedure.

Cael stared at the crossed-out counts.

Cael: "At Noctis, witness counts are internal."

Leon: "Meaning?"

Cael's throat moved.

Cael: "Meaning they count who is allowed to agree."

No one spoke for a moment.

The seven witness chairs around us did not need to glow. Their layout felt almost painful in how simple it was: no head of the table, no hidden bench, no raised seat where someone in charge could pretend height gave them wisdom. Seven chairs facing one another, trying with stone and water to stop the easiest lie in the world: that one person could safely explain another alone.

Cael read the room first. His voice was almost soundless.

Cael: "Noctis would call that treason."

Professor Valen stepped near the pool, amber eyes bright with grief and scholarly hunger.

Professor Valen: "Older than Noctis. Older than the modern rankings. This came before the neat ranks everyone teaches now."

The water answered her: Green, Blue, Red, Yellow. Then the rings collapsed inward and showed a black circle split by witness marks. Neither crown nor weapon. Safeguard.

The record unfolded in broken sequence: early Circle systems built to measure power, then schools learning to turn measurement into ownership, then children marked as safeguards and locked away for testing. Names surfaced and vanished too quickly to read. Every disappearance left the same polite cover phrases stamped behind: *Withdrawn for study. Removed for stability. Classification sealed.*

Elira's hand found mine.

The Black Circle stirred, and I held it still.

Tier 1. Nothing more. A quiet reading small enough to slip past the room's notice, though the old water noticed anyway. One ripple bent toward me, then drew back, respecting distance because witnesses were present. Enough to pass unseen. Enough to cost.

The old hall was already reading Cael like damaged script. If I forced it open, Tier 2 would answer, and every witness in the chamber would see the truth before any of us understood what the room wanted.

So I used Tier 1 in fragments: one adjustment under Leon's stabilizing ring, one under Elira's gold light, one beneath Cassian's red anchor. Each time, I let someone else's visible magic carry the shape while I quieted what the old system thought it was seeing.

The cost came fast. Cold pressure gathered behind my ribs. Ozone and metal touched the back of my throat. My false Green crest stayed lit at my wrist, yet it lagged behind my pulse by half a beat.

I tucked my hand against my side before anyone but Elira could see the delay.

The lag was small, which made it worse. Tier 1 did not tear the concealment open; it pulled at the stitching. Each quiet adjustment since the archive door (the witness count, the corridor's old grammar, the hall's refusal to center me) had left the false Green light slower to arrive.

Halden's gaze dropped straight to my wrist.

Professor Halden: "Aerlen."

I did not look at him.

Haru: "Later."

Aira's eyes flicked to my hand. She had not seen the Black Circle; she had seen the Green disguise fail to keep time.

Aira: "Haru, breathe slower."

Good. She gave the room a human explanation. I obeyed.

The record shifted again, showing roles instead of names: a safeguard candidate standing inside a circle of seven witnesses; a faculty observer with one hand raised in refusal; a healer crossing out the word *stable*; a public scribe copying testimony from the gallery; a council flame burning between a child and a committee table.

Then the sequence repeated with pieces stripped away. First the public scribe vanished. Then the healer. Then the rival student.

Each removal made the central figure brighter and less human, until the final image showed only a black ring surrounded by classification marks.

The chamber turned cold enough that breath showed white in the air.

Professor Valen's voice dropped low.

Professor Valen: "They thought cutting him off made the power clearer."

Halden answered before anyone else.

Professor Halden: "It amplified it."

The pool accepted his correction.

**ISOLATION DISTORTS SAFEGUARD RESPONSE**

That line hit harder than the earlier warnings, because it was practical. The Black Circle was not only dangerous when I lost restraint. It was dangerous when the world removed every reason restraint had to stay human.

That was the conclusion I let myself keep.

Another waited underneath it. I felt it arrive and looked away before it finished, letting my eyes travel from the pool to the edge of Elira's sleeve, then to Aira's pencil, then to the copper line in the floor.

The record had shown a safeguard growing brighter and less human as people were stripped away. I knew how safe it felt to be alone: no one close enough to be used against me, no one near enough to watch my hands shake after restraint, no one whose trust could become a blade in someone else's mouth. Alone had rules I understood.

The old record made that instinct look less like discipline and more like the first gear of a machine. I filed the thought away beside all the other things that stayed bearable only while unnamed.

Elira looked straight into the pool without flinching.

Elira: "Then witness presence is not sentimental."

Rhea: "No."

Elira: "It is containment."

Cael flinched at the word. Elira caught it and corrected herself immediately.

Elira: "No. Not containment."

She searched for the better word. Mira found it from the gallery mark.

Mira: "Context?"

The silver water brightened across the basin.

**CONTEXT PRESERVES PERSONHOOD**

Mira stared at the inscription.

Mira: "I was guessing."

Aira: "Do that more often."

Leon folded his arms.

Leon: "So the old system is saying Haru is less dangerous when people argue with him?"

Cassian: "Excellent. We are all naturally gifted."

Professor Halden looked at Leon.

Professor Halden: "Crude. Yet close enough to useful."

Leon looked briefly offended by the accuracy. Cael watched the two of them with a confusion that hurt, then very carefully tried a small smile.

The Record Hall did not reward it. No script appeared, no copper vein lit to score the moment as progress. That restraint made me trust the room a little more; it let the smile belong to Cael before evidence could touch it.

Then the pool showed the last image: a safeguard alone, a classification seal descending, and a city-shaped shadow bending away from the seal as if the world itself had flinched. No blood. No battle. Just absence.

Every witness mark in the room dimmed at once.

Rhea's voice stayed quiet and iron-hard.

President Valmont: "This is why Noctis's dawn protest was only the opening move. They will not stop at tournament procedure."

Halden watched the ripple die across the pool. His pen lifted once, hovered over his notebook, then stopped before writing my name. That restraint felt heavier than suspicion.

Rhea stepped beside the nearest witness chair, reading the faint inscription along the stone rim of the pool.

President Valmont: "The old safeguard rule required open witnesses so classification could never turn into containment."

Mira copied every word, her pencil trembling at *containment*.

Beside her, the eastern crystal went pitch-black.

Noctis had escalated from a tournament protest to a formal Foundation Jurisdiction Claim.

Magister Rusk appeared in relay projection, formal robes clean enough to look like accusation.

Magister Rusk: "Noctis Dominion College claims authority over Cael Nox, the right to correct the tournament record for those missing seven seconds, and a formal Foundation review of Haru Aerlen's unauthorized interference."

Rhea stepped directly into the nearest witness mark, her council flame snapping open at her wrist.

President Valmont: "Aurelis will hear that claim only under protected witness rules. Cael Nox is a recovering student witness, not delegation property. Changes to the record are on hold until Foundation review."

Aira wrote. Mira copied. Silver light ran clean around the seven witness chairs.

**PROTECTED HEARING STRUCTURE ESTABLISHED**

Rusk's mouth tightened. Lucien did not appear. That worried me more than if he had.

The relay vanished.

For one breath, the hall stood silent. Then every notice crystal in Aurelis activated at once, projecting the Record Hall's summons through classrooms, courtyards, dormitory corridors, dining tables, faculty offices, and the public rail above the fountain.

**CLASSIFICATION INQUIRY DEMANDED**

**SUBJECT: HARU AERLEN**

**STATUS: UNCLASSIFIED RESPONSE**

**SAFEGUARD COMPATIBILITY UNDER REVIEW**

In Mira's hands, the blackened back page of her notebook from the midnight fountain shivered. The dark stain along its margin pulled back like ink retreating from light, exposing her copied **CIRCLE ZERO** fragment in pale silver for two heartbeats before settling dark again.

Professor Valen's breath caught. Mira stared down at the page, swallowed hard, and kept writing beside the black edge anyway.

The academy learned my name before I could decide how to carry it.

First came silence, then noise rolling down through the old vents and relay crystals: chairs scraping in classrooms, a tray hitting stone in the dining hall, someone in the courtyard shouting that the fountain's water had gone dark, a faculty voice ordering calm in a tone that created none.

Rhea closed her eyes for half a breath. When she opened them, she lifted two fingers and sealed the outer vent relays with a sharp pulse of red light.

President Valmont: "No one leaves through an unsealed route. No one answers a relay without a council mark. No one speaks Haru Aerlen's classification as fact."

Leon looked toward me.

Leon: "Is unclassified response better or worse than Green?"

Aira: "Politically? Worse. Socially? Worse. Academically? Halden is about to become impossible."

Professor Halden: "I was already impossible."

Cael had not moved since the crystals activated. He stared at my name hanging in the air with the look of someone watching another person become useful to systems that did not love them.

Cael: "They will call it protection first."

I looked at him. His voice stayed flat.

Cael: "Noctis always calls the first wall protection. The second wall is review. The third wall is honor. By the time someone calls it a cage, the lock has a signature."

No one answered quickly. Fast comfort would have insulted him.

Elira stepped forward.

Elira: "Then we do not let the first wall stand alone."

She looked at Rhea.

Elira: "Public witness structure."

Rhea nodded once.

President Valmont: "Mira Castellan."

Mira almost dropped her pen.

Mira: "Yes?"

President Valmont: "Can your gallery table copy a correction fast enough to beat rumor?"

Mira glanced at the two Green Circle students beside her. They looked terrified, yet one of them pushed a fresh stack of blank sheets toward her without hesitating.

Mira: "No."

Rhea's eyes sharpened.

Mira: "We can make rumor trip."

Rhea considered that.

President Valmont: "Accepted."

Mira wrote the first public correction while the academy still shook: *Foundation inquiry does not equal classification.*

Her classmate copied beneath it: *Unclassified response is not property.*

The third added: *Cael Nox remains protected witness.*

The pages went out through council relay, gallery hands, and fountain reflection. Imperfect, hurried, yet fast enough that when the first frightened rumor reached the west courtyard, it collided with ink-stained handwriting from students nobody had thought to manage.

Inside the Record Hall, the silver pool rippled clean from edge to edge, acknowledging the gallery's pages.

Lucien still did not appear. His absence pressed harder than his voice would have.

High above us, a notice crystal tried to append Noctis commentary to the inquiry line. The Foundation erased it, then burned two final words across every active surface in the hall.

**TRIAL REQUIRED**

Halden spoke them aloud, giving the room's cold dread a human voice.

Professor Halden: "Trial required."

Cael's healer seal pulsed once. Under my sleeve, the false Green crest flickered, tired from holding shape around a name the old world had begun to pull apart.

Elira saw. She did not cover my wrist. She stepped half an inch closer so anyone watching would have to see her choice too, and did not let go.

The Record Hall did not let us leave immediately.

*Trial required* had sounded like an ending because people like Rhea could make even danger sound routine. Old Aurelis did not move like a court clerk. The hall held its doors sealed while the notice crystals overhead continued repeating my name across campus, each repetition shifting depending on who heard it.

In the dining hall, rumor heard *subject*. In faculty offices, ambition heard *classification*. In the Green Circle dormitory, Mira told me later, some first-years heard *unclassified* and looked at their own wrists as if the word might be contagious.

Inside the hall, we felt all of it as pressure rather than sound. Every rumor outside pressed against the witness marks around the room, demanding entry into the record before anyone had bothered learning what they were reacting to. The silver pool darkened and began to churn against its stone lip.

Rhea's council seal sharpened.

President Valmont: "Contain the surge."

The Foundation did not answer. Halden did.

Professor Halden: "No."

Everyone looked at him. Especially me.

Halden stood with his black case still latched, knuckles pale around the handle. I could feel the questions inside him the way old wards felt weather. What had the door recognized in me? Why had the fountain failed at black? How much of the seven-second gap belonged to intervention and how much to old safeguard response? He wanted answers so badly the wanting had turned into visible discipline.

Professor Halden: "Do not try to choke off public talk inside the same hall that just labeled him subject."

Rhea's eyes narrowed.

President Valmont: "Reason."

Halden looked at the witness marks, then back toward the corridor wall and its line: **DO NOT LET THEM STUDY HIM ALONE**.

Professor Halden: "Because shutting it down makes this hall the first author. Let the public be wrong outside before we turn accuracy into a cage inside."

The sentence cost him. I knew because it cost me to hear it.

Rhea considered him for one long breath.

President Valmont: "Alternative?"

Halden opened his empty hand.

Professor Halden: "Open corrections. No labels. No guesses. Just the sequence."

Mira's pencil rose before she realized she had moved.

Mira: "Sequence?"

Halden turned toward her, something shifting in his expression as he remembered that public record did not mean adult record.

Professor Halden: "What happened in order. Nothing else."

Mira swallowed.

Mira: "Noctis filed claim. Aurelis requested witness protection. Foundation opened inquiry. Haru Aerlen named unclassified response. Trial required."

Cael's voice came from beside Leon.

Cael: "Cael Nox protected witness. Not delegation object."

Mira wrote it down.

Leon added, rougher than he probably meant.

Leon: "No one knows what Haru is."

Aira's pen paused. Leon glanced at me once, then away.

Leon: "That includes people pretending certainty."

A quiet silver pulse ran across the surface of the pool, smoothing the dark churn back to glass.

Cassian let out a low breath.

Cassian: "I did not know Blue Circle pride could mature into accuracy."

Leon: "I can still hit you."

Cassian: "There he is."

Elira kept holding my hand, her thumb resting once against my knuckles to remind me I existed inside the room, not above it.

The Black Circle under my skin wanted to answer the hall. It hated being named without language. It hated public fear pressing at a concealment I had built one careful day at a time.

For one beat, I understood every terrible thing power could do while calling itself clarification. I could show them enough that nobody would dare mislabel me. I could force the fountain to draw black properly. I could fold every notice crystal on campus into obedient silence.

All of those thoughts arrived dressed as self-defense.

Elira's fingers tightened around mine. She had not read my mind; she had felt my hand go rigid.

Elira: "Let them get it wrong for one more minute."

Haru: "That sounds dangerous."

Elira: "It is."

She looked at the pool, then toward the corridor of forgotten witnesses.

Elira: "So is forcing the truth on everyone before they have chosen whether they are willing to be responsible."

The copper veins beneath our feet warmed with a low, steady hum. Professor Valen exhaled softly, her eyes fixed on the silver water settling calm again.

Only then did the tall archive doors unlatch.

The corridor outside looked identical at first glance: pale stone, copper veins, shallow runes holding old patience beneath our boots. Yet every record-scar we had passed on the way in now answered us differently.

The child's handprint burned clearer. The scratched Green protest had gained a single silver mark at the end of **I SAW...**, admitting the sentence had been cut short rather than erased.

Mira stopped in front of it, her copied pages trembling against her ribs.

Mira: "Can it be completed?"

No one answered quickly. Fast answers would have turned the dead into homework.

Rhea stepped beside her.

President Valmont: "Not by guessing."

Mira nodded, then wrote cleanly beneath the scar on a fresh sheet: *Record incomplete. Do not summarize.*

The nearest copper vein warmed under her palm. Mira stared at it.

Aira's voice went quiet.

Aira: "That means yes, I think."

Ahead of us, Cael stopped before the healer stamp reading *stable* beside a pulse line that had never been stable at all.

His face went still in the old Noctis way. Softness around him only seemed to tighten his shoulders; in Noctis, gentle tones meant the blade was being wrapped before it went in. Leon noticed and made himself stand beside Cael rather than in front, hands loose at his sides as if he had practiced not grabbing someone back from an edge.

Cael lifted one hand near the false stamp without touching the stone.

Cael: "They used this word on me."

No one asked which word. *Stable.* The cruelest word in a room where alive and useful were treated as interchangeable.

Cael: "Stable meant quiet enough to move."

Leon muttered something under his breath that sounded like a threat and a prayer sharing a coat. Rhea heard.

President Valmont: "Cael Nox's definition entered under protected witness context."

The wall did not glow. Some records did not know how to apologize.

Cael lowered his hand. For one breath, I thought he would keep walking. He did not.

He turned back toward the false healer stamp. His own healer seal pulsed amber at his collar, trying to keep him seated, monitored, quiet. Every adult in the corridor wanted sequence, safety, containment. I wanted, with a sharp violence I had no right to, for the old wall to stop asking children to finish sentences adults had broken.

Cael reached up and unfastened the healer seal.

Rhea's council flame flared at her wrist. Halden inhaled sharply. Professor Valen caught her breath without stepping between them. Leon took half a step and forced himself to stop so hard his boots scraped stone.

Leon: "Cael."

Cael did not look at him.

Cael: "It says I am stable."

The seal in his palm flashed warning gold, then red. A thin diagnostic thread snapped toward his pulse point, trying to reattach.

Cael closed his fist around it. The thread snapped.

No explosion, no surge, just a small medical light dying because a boy had decided that being watched was not the same as being helped.

Cael swayed.

Leon made a low sound in his throat. Elira moved before I did, stepping sideways into Leon's path with one palm raised, never touching him.

Elira: "Ask."

Leon looked furious with the rule, and obeyed it anyway.

Leon: "Do you want help?"

Cael's shoulders jerked. The question hit harder than the broken thread. Noctis had taught him to answer pain, never want. Pain could be ranked and spent. Want insisted the person inside the body had standing.

Cael's mouth opened. No sound came. The corridor waited in complete silence, no script lighting, no wall scoring the breath.

Cael pressed the dead healer seal against the stone directly beneath *stable*. The metal clicked and caught inside a shallow groove that had not been there a heartbeat earlier.

Cael: "I want to walk out without that word touching me."

His voice shook, yet it carried down the stone hall.

Rhea's council flame dimmed, leashed without vanishing.

President Valmont: "Granted."

Behind him, Aira's pen scratched hard against her clipboard.

Cael turned his head. Aira had written *stable*, then slashed a line through it so deep the paper nearly tore. Beside it, in sharp dark ink, she wrote: *alive is not the same as compliant.*

Nobody asked permission to keep the correction. For the first time all morning, Cael did not look like he wanted anyone to throw it away.

Halden's pen twitched by instinct against his notebook, then stopped. He looked down at his own hand as if annoyed to find a scholar still attached to it.

Professor Halden: "I will not record that as a medical recommendation."

Cael gave him a look flat enough to be Noctis again.

Professor Halden: "I mean it. Some choices should not become instructions for the next injured student to imitate."

Cael studied him, thin and suspicious and alive.

Cael: "Then what is it?"

Halden's jaw worked once. He could have said testimony, or resistance, or turned the moment clean enough for a lecture. Instead, he looked at the dead healer seal pinned under the false stamp.

Professor Halden: "A boy being tired of a word."

Cael let out a breath. Then he walked.

The first step was ugly; his knee almost buckled, and Leon's hands stayed open at his sides without grabbing. The second scraped stone. The third found enough balance to turn stubborn. By the fourth, sweat darkened Cael's collar. No one applauded.

Mira cried anyway, quietly and furiously, as if tears had interrupted work she had not authorized. One drop struck her copied sheet and blurred *Record incomplete. Do not summarize.*

Mira: "No."

Aira raised two fingers for a drying charm. Mira slapped her palm over the damp ink before it could land.

Aira froze.

Mira: "Leave it."

Aira: "It will distort the copy."

Mira: "I know."

The damp ink bled into the paper fibers, warping two letters until the sentence looked bruised. Mira stared down at it with fierce, stubborn ownership.

Mira: "This one is mine."

No one argued.

Cael reached the open outer threshold and stopped there, trembling hard enough that even Cassian dropped the last of his theatrical boredom.

Leon asked again, voice rougher.

Leon: "Do you want help now?"

Cael closed his eyes for half a second, then held out one hand.

Leon took it. Not wrist, not elbow. Hand.

The grip lasted three steps and ended before anyone could turn it into theater.

At the corridor's end, the outer doors stood open to the noise of Aurelis: students, notices, fear, curiosity, the first wrong versions of my name already racing across stone.

Professor Valen lingered a step behind to pull the inner ward shut with her brass key, then fell in quietly beside Halden at the threshold.

Halden stopped and looked at me, his black case still latched.

Professor Halden: "If I ask the wrong question later, remind me that I knew better today."

It was the closest thing to an apology he could survive giving.

Haru: "I will."

Halden nodded once. Together, we stepped out into the academy's gaze.

## Interlude: Mira Castellan - The Public Table

Mira's public record table stopped being theoretical eleven minutes later.

She had hauled a spare drafting desk beside the east fountain because the water was already lying badly, and public things deserved correction where people had to walk past it. That had felt sensible right up until the crowd arrived.

Classrooms had neat rows that told fear where to sit. The arcade crowd gathered in uneven pockets: Green Circle students hanging toward the pillars, Blue Circle students close enough to squint and far enough to deny curiosity, Red Circle heirs murmuring over cupped relay charms, and one Yellow Circle senior leaning under the stone arch with folded arms as if deciding whether history suited her coat.

Mira laid her ink-stained sheets across the desk. Her fingers trembled, smearing a comma, and she scowled at her own wrist.

Finn Castellan, her older brother, who had been copying notices behind her since breakfast, dropped a chunky gray stone onto the top corner of her pages before the breeze could scatter them. He had clearly pried it out of the courtyard flowerbed.

Mira: "That is not archive safe."

Finn: "It is wind safe."

The page stayed flat. Mira glared at the stone, then let her shoulders drop.

Mira: "Temporary acceptance."

Finn grinned like he had just won a scholarship.

The first questioner was a Blue Circle second-year with spotless silver cuffs who stopped three feet out, leaning forward as though proximity might stain his collar.

Student: "Is it true?"

Mira: "Which part?"

Student: "The unclassified thing."

Mira tapped the top line with the blunt end of her pencil.

Mira: "Foundation inquiry does not equal classification."

Student: "That is not an answer."

Mira: "It is the answer we have."

He frowned, dropping his voice just enough for six Green first-years nearby to stare hard at their shoes.

Student: "People are saying Black Circle."

Something hot flared behind Mira's ribs. She had no hidden circle, no ancient seal, and a borrowed desk whose left leg wobbled whenever anyone leaned on it. She pressed her palm flat against the wood to steady it.

Mira: "People also said Cael Nox was transferred under lawful delegation yesterday."

The Blue student glanced toward the archive doors.

Mira: "They were wrong then too."

Color climbed his neck.

Student: "I was only asking."

Mira: "Then ask what happened in order."

Behind her, Finn let out a soft breath.

Finn: "Oh."

Mira did not turn around; if she caught his grin, she might realize how loud her voice had sounded and lose her nerve.

The Blue student read the sequence slowly: Noctis claim, Aurelis witness protection, Foundation inquiry, trial required, no classification. He left without apologizing, which was fine. Mira had not dragged a desk outside for apologies.

The next student was a tiny Green Circle first-year with tightly braided hair and ink already smudged across her thumb, clutching her opposite wrist as if her crest might peel off.

Girl: "Can I copy it?"

Mira slid a blank sheet across the grain.

Mira: "Yes."

The girl perched on the fountain rim because there were no spare chairs. She copied the first line cleanly, then hurried the second and wrote **UNCLASSED** instead of **UNCLASSIFIED**. Her face went chalk-white.

Girl: "I can redo it."

Mira almost said yes. Clean pages survived filing. Clean pages made instructors take your work seriously. Then she pictured the scratched wall inside the corridor: **I SAW...**

She laid the girl's page right beside her own under the garden stone.

Mira: "Mark it as your copy."

Girl: "But it's wrong."

Mira: "Then anyone quoting it has to admit you were there."

From the rim of the crowd, a Red Circle boy laughed, light and cutting.

Red Student: "Green witness doctrine now? That is new."

Finn surged upright too fast. Mira caught his sleeve and shook her head once. If Finn shouted, everyone watching would write the table off as a fight. She pressed harder on the wobbling desk edge.

Mira: "It is not doctrine."

Red Student: "Then what is it?"

Mira: "Work."

The word landed too plain to mock. The Red student looked away first.

Under the arch, the Yellow Circle senior unfolded her arms and walked forward. The crowd parted automatically, a reflex Mira resented right before catching herself stepping back half an inch too.

The senior lifted a blank sheet from the stack.

Senior: "May I?"

Mira almost said yes out of habit, then caught herself and tightened her grip on her pencil.

Mira: "Why?"

The arcade went pin-drop quiet. The senior blinked, then let a genuine smile touch her mouth.

Senior: "Because if only Green students copy it, people will call it a grudge."

Mira hated that the answer made sense.

Mira: "Then copy the Green student's error too."

The senior looked down at **UNCLASSED**, and her smile sharpened into respect.

Senior: "Good."

She wrote the entire sequence out, error preserved, and signed her name cleanly at the bottom.

By the time Rhea Valmont's exhausted aide arrived with official council paper, nineteen sheets lay drying under open air in six different hands, three mistakes preserved on purpose, anchored by Finn's unauthorized landscaping stone.

The aide stared at the rock.

Mira: "Temporary acceptance."

Aide: "I was not going to ask."

Behind the desk, the fountain stirred. Four obedient rings rose across the basin: Green, Blue, Red, Yellow. Heads snapped around immediately. Mira grabbed her pencil anyway.

The rings thinned. Black did not surface. Instead, the Green ring widened cleanly to the stone lip and spilled a thin mirror of water across the paving, stopping one finger-width short of Mira's stack.

Finn stepped closer to the rim.

Finn: "That is new."

Mira: "Do not give the fountain compliments. It will become confident."

The Green first-year burst out laughing before clapping both hands over her mouth. Mira lifted the girl's **UNCLASSED** sheet and moved it to the front edge of the desk, closest to the wet stone.

Girl: "Why?"

Mira: "Because it is the one the water stopped beside."

Girl: "But it's wrong."

Mira: "Then the fountain has poor taste."

The girl stared, then laughed again, slower to hide it this time.

The widened Green ring held for one steady breath before sinking back into ordinary water. No ancient inscription lit the air, leaving only wet stone and ink drying in the breeze. Mira preferred it that way; glow made people argue over who owned a miracle, while quiet let the work stay theirs.

After final bell, the Green first-year came back and set a second sheet beside her first, **UNCLASSIFIED** spelled cleanly across the line.

Mira: "You did not have to redo it."

Girl: "I know."

She tapped the first page.

Girl: "That one was when I was scared."

Then the second.

Girl: "This one is after."

Mira looked at both pages side by side.

Mira: "Accepted."

At sunset, Finn swapped the landscaping boulder for a smaller river pebble.

Mira: "That is still not archive safe."

Finn: "It is less illegal."

Mira: "Temporary acceptance continues."

Before heading to dinner, Mira tucked one blank sheet under the pebble, leaving room for whoever arrived late and still needed a place to start.

## Coda: Before the Hearing

Across the northern ridge, inside the dark stone wing assigned to the Noctis delegation, the relay crystal above the table stayed lit long after midnight.

**TRIAL REQUIRED**

Magister Rusk stood stiff beside the window, a thick stack of appeal drafts clutched against his chest.

Magister Rusk: "Valmont locked the hearing inside Foundation structure before our revision brief cleared. If Valemere and Sol Aeternum sit on the witness rail tomorrow, our custody claim over Cael Nox collapses."

Lucien Voss sat calmly at the long black desk, not touching the drafts. A single brass paperweight held down the tournament's missing seven-second transcript.

Lucien: "Let Cael testify."

Rusk stared at him.

Magister Rusk: "You want him speaking before the continental delegates?"

Lucien: "Denying a boy's bruises makes us look small. Letting him speak makes Aurelis feel righteous, and righteous rooms stop watching their footing."

He tapped the blank space where seven seconds of arena light had vanished into silver static.

Lucien: "Tomorrow is not about whether Cael hurt. Tomorrow is about why a Green Circle transfer thought his personal judgment outranked judges, healers, and tournament law. Either Haru Aerlen had no right to interfere, or he holds authority this court has not named."

His pale eyes reflected the crystal's cold glow.

Lucien: "Ask the right question, and the Foundation itself will force him into the light."

Far across the campus, dawn broke pale silver over the Aurelis towers.

I stood at the top of the east stairs and tugged my sleeve cuff straight over my wrist. The false Green crest held its dim shape, still half a beat slow against my pulse.

Elira stopped beside me, sunlight catching the dark crystal pendant at her collarbone. Ahead of us, the doors to the converted Record Hall stood wide, seven witness marks already burning bright around the silver pool.

Elira: "Ready?"

Haru: "No."

Her fingers brushed mine once before we stepped into view of the waiting gallery.

Elira: "Good. Then we won't pretend this is easy."

The hearing bell struck once, deep and iron-clear, and we walked inside.

# Episode 1: **The Green Circle Transfer Student**

The morning sky over **Aurelis City** caught the sun in layered gold, each cloud edge refracting mana into angles ordinary light should never have held.

Between the white towers and glass bridges of the capital, floating transport sigils shimmered in neat lanes. Suspended carriages glided soundlessly above the avenues while fountains in the lower plazas threw pale mist across marble steps already crowded with students in navy and cream.

The new uniform collar had been scratching the same spot on my neck since the night train.

Eighteen years old, mid-year transfer from Lumeris District: **Haru Aerlen**, wearing the weakest crest society could tolerate. The uniform fabric was expensive and enchanted, cut with more polish than any district coat I had owned, and beneath the left cuff sat a modest **Green Circle** crest pressed lightly into my inner wrist.

The gate's ward pressure settled cold behind my sternum as **Aurelis Arcane Academy** rose ahead.

Tall crystal windows reflected fractured sunlight beneath six rune-marked towers. White stone stairways climbed past silver-leaf trees and statues of famous alumni who governed cities and imperial ministries.

A quiet pulse stirred behind my ribs.

I pressed two fingers lightly over my sternum.

Beneath my shirt, hidden under layers of quiet mana concealment, my true Magic Circle slept in silence near my heart.

It did not blaze like Yellow or radiate with noble warmth like Red. It was darker than shadow, exact as law, and vast enough that even fully suppressed it made the air around me feel subtly wrong if my focus slipped. Inside it turned impossible geometry: patterns too old, too complete, and too absolute for modern magical theory to name.

Black Circle.

I had no intention of letting anyone see it. I had spent years learning how to be small: how to suppress, how to deflect, how to live inside a margin too narrow to invite a second glance.

Six weeks earlier, that plan had stopped being enough.

During my final Lumeris district assessment, the Green crest on my wrist had lagged half a beat behind my pulse, leaving a dark bruise in the inspector's reading glass before snapping back to pale emerald. The clerk had blamed a cracked lens and moved on.

That same evening, an Aurelis transfer packet appeared inside my locked room.

My written examination scores were copied on the first page. The second held one unsigned sentence:

**THE FOUNDATION REMEMBERS WHAT LUMERIS WAS TAUGHT TO FORGET. COME BEFORE THEY MEASURE YOU AGAIN.**

Staying meant submitting to a second inspection my concealment might not survive. Vanishing would confirm that Lumeris had uncovered something worth hunting. Aurelis offered a public reason to leave and, somewhere beneath its old stone, a possible answer to why the Black Circle had begun resisting sleep.

I did not trust the invitation. I trusted ignorance less.

Mana flowed along my wrist in a whisper. The illusion settled neatly under my cuff. Anyone glancing at my sleeve now would see an ordinary **Green Circle crest**: slightly faint, slightly unimpressive, the exact sort of mark people looked past before they finished blinking.

I took a breath and stepped through the gates.

The stares started almost immediately.

Some students were too busy greeting friends or checking class rosters on the floating directory panels to care. Some were already pretending not to look at anyone ranked beneath them.

No one walked beside me.

Within ten paces, enough eyes had shifted my way that the verdict was obvious. They knew the three facts before I reached the main steps: transfer student, mid-year entry, Green Circle.

At a school like Aurelis, those three facts could turn into a rumor before first bell. I kept walking.

Near the fountain to my right, a pair of Blue Circle boys lowered their voices just enough to make sure I heard them. Farther along the walkway, Red and Blue crests flashed as students noticed the pale green glow at my cuff with amused disbelief. I passed without reacting. Casual cruelty worked best when you refused to hand it a face.

The entrance hall opened around me like a cathedral built for magical aristocracy.

A domed ceiling glimmered with artificial constellations. Black stone floors mirrored floating chandeliers. Twin staircases curved toward the upper academic wings, and golden banners hung between the pillars bearing the academy seal.

A luminous panel unfolded in the air before me.

**WELCOME, TRANSFER STUDENT: HARU AERLEN**
**YEAR TWO - SECTION THREE**
**HOMEROOM: EAST WING, THIRD FLOOR**

Instructor Selene: "Haru Aerlen?"

I turned.

A woman in a formal navy academy coat approached with a rune-glass tablet tucked under one arm. Her dark hair was tied back neatly, a slender **Red Circle** crest glowing near the base of her throat. Her expression carried the brisk fatigue of someone who had already solved three administrative headaches before breakfast.

Instructor Selene: "I'm Instructor Selene Varis. Student Affairs. You're the transfer from Lumeris District?"

Haru: "Yes."

Instructor Selene: "Come with me. Homeroom has already started."

I nodded and followed her into the east corridor.

The deeper we walked, the clearer the academy's invisible map became. Rooms reserved for top sections sat closer to cleaner mana conduits. Certain hallways displayed only Red and Yellow alumni portraits. The walls themselves knew who the school had been built to remember.

In a study alcove near the eastern stair, a Green Circle student raised one hand to point out a scheduling error and was cut off mid-sentence by a Blue who did not even slow down to see who had spoken.

Selene noticed where I was looking.

Instructor Selene: "Aurelis rewards excellence."

I wondered how many people repeated that sentence without ever asking who got to define excellence in the first place.

Haru: "I see."

She glanced at me.

Instructor Selene: "This school is not easy, Haru. Students here are ambitious, competitive, and mostly from influential houses. You may face resistance."

Haru: "That's expected."

A flicker of approval crossed her face.

Instructor Selene: "Your admission was unusual. Your Circle ranking raised objections, but your written examination scores were extraordinary. Theory, tactical ethics, mana structures, ancient script analysis. Someone argued strongly in your favor."

Haru: "Someone?"

Instructor Selene: "I was not told who."

The unsigned sentence surfaced again with unpleasant clarity. Someone had not merely noticed me. They had built a door and waited to see if I would walk through it.

Haru: "Was the recommendation attached to the Foundation archives?"

Selene's stride faltered by less than a breath.

Instructor Selene: "No archive access was listed in your student file. Why?"

Haru: "The transfer reading list referenced pre-standardization records. Specifically, the Foundation's suppressed classifications."

It was a gamble sharp enough to test how closely the administration watched old records. Selene paused, studying me with fresh wariness before resuming her stride, though the question stayed with her. Good. If the anonymous sender had reached through Student Affairs, I wanted the noise to travel back to whoever was listening.

She stopped before a pair of double doors. Inside, a lecture was already underway.

Selene opened the doors and stepped aside.

The classroom beyond was bright and tiered, with curved rows of desks facing tall windows along one wall. At the front, Instructor Dren stood beside a floating spell diagram. Conversation thinned the moment thirty pairs of eyes caught the faint green crest peeking beneath my cuff.

Instructor Dren: "The transfer? Haru Aerlen. Introduce yourself."

Haru: "I'm Haru Aerlen. I transferred in today. I'll do my best."

Scattered snickers broke out among the Red and Blue rows until Dren rapped his knuckles sharply against the slate.

Instructor Dren: "That's enough."

The room settled, though not with much grace.

Instructor Dren: "Back row. Open seat beside Aira."

As I climbed the aisle, I caught a folded paper pellet an inch from my shoulder and let it drop to the floor anyway. No reason to make reflex look deliberate.

At the back sat a girl with warm brown eyes and chestnut hair tied loosely to one side. Her **Blue Circle** crest glowed on the inside of her wrist, and she looked up without the practiced sizing-up most Blues wore.

That alone set her apart.

Aira: "Hi. I'm Aira Solen."

Haru: "Haru."

Aira: "Nice to meet you."

There was no mockery in it, no hidden angle. Just plain warmth that felt rare enough to make me cautious for half a second.

At the front, Instructor Dren resumed the lecture.

Instructor Dren: "As I was saying, magical society classifies human mana potential into four recognized Circle ranks."

The academy liked the word *recognized*. It made the world sound tidy.

Children learned early that the color awakening near the heart changed how adults listened. Once mana matured, society sorted them cleanly and called it nature.

Four practice disks descended from the ceiling beneath the projected crests. Dren did not start with definitions. He pointed to a Green Circle girl in the second row.

Instructor Dren: "One thread. Hold the disk level."

She startled, then obeyed. Green light ran from her wrist to the nearest disk. It rose, wobbled once, and steadied. Ten seconds. Twenty.

A registry tag flashed into the air above her without anyone asking for it.

**UTILITY TRACK / MAJORITY COHORT**

Two students smirked.

The girl's jaw tightened. Her disk did not tremble.

Dren erased the label with a sharp sweep of his hand.

Instructor Dren: "Registry language. Not mine."

That won him more attention than any textbook definition would have.

Instructor Dren: "Green Circle accounts for roughly sixty percent of the population. Lowest average raw reserve, narrowest output margin. Also the backbone of long-duration civic wards, maintenance routes, food seals, transport lines, and every ordinary system people only notice after it fails."

The girl hit thirty seconds.

Instructor Dren: "Release."

Her disk settled cleanly without cracking.

Leon raised his hand and stood before Dren called on him.

Instructor Dren: "Mr. Varek. Same instruction."

Blue mana struck the second disk. It jumped twice as high as the Green one, bright enough to throw water-colored shadows across the front desks. Several students murmured approval.

At twenty-two seconds, one edge dipped.

Leon overcorrected. The disk spun out of alignment.

Dren caught it before it struck the floor.

Instructor Dren: "Blue Circle. Roughly thirty percent. Stronger average reserves, broader practical application, and most of the skilled professional class."

He set the disk back in midair.

Instructor Dren: "More is not the same as controlled."

Leon sat back down with his spine very straight.

A Red Circle student lifted three disks at once, bending her mana cleanly around the correction runes instead of fighting them.

Instructor Dren: "Red. Roughly nine percent. High reserves, refined affinities, high adaptability."

The registry projection supplied its own verdict:

**LEADERSHIP COMPATIBILITY: HIGH**

The Red student smiled before she could stop herself.

Dren looked straight at the floating letters.

Instructor Dren: "A prediction repeated often enough becomes a hiring practice. Do not confuse either with character."

No one smiled after that.

The Yellow disk remained empty. It did not need a volunteer to alter the room. Its projected crest washed gold over the front rows, and students sat straighter beneath a rank none of them had demonstrated.

Instructor Dren: "Yellow Circle. Approximately one percent. Highest recognized modern classification. Most institutions go years without training one."

He dismissed the disks.

The lesson had never claimed Green meant useless. It had simply arranged the room so everyone knew whose failure deserved patience and whose success was expected to stay polite.

Above Dren, the four circles rotated slowly, Green, Blue, Red, and Yellow washing their approved colors across thirty faces.

The unsigned note had promised the Foundation remembered what Lumeris was taught to forget. Asking directly about Black Circle would have been foolish, yet testing whether the academy's syllabus admitted any blind spots might tell me what kind of trap I had walked into.

I raised my hand. That alone surprised half the room.

Instructor Dren: "Yes, transfer student?"

I kept my face neutral.

Haru: "Are those four categories exhaustive? The transfer reading mentioned pre-standardization circles."

Aira turned toward me so quickly her pen scratched across her margin.

Two students blinked. Someone near the front mouthed *pre-standardization* as if checking whether the phrase sounded less absurd silently. Leon laughed first.

Leon: "You mean Black Circle?"

Enough students joined him to fill the classroom in a single wave.

Student: "What is this, a storybook?"

Student: "I knew Greens had odd theories, but that's embarrassing."

Leon: "Are we studying children's myths now?"

Red Student: "You might as well ask about star dragons and moon gods."

Aira did not laugh. She watched me instead, curious and wide awake.

Instructor Dren raised one hand, and the noise dropped away. His face showed no mockery, only the patience of a teacher deciding how much weight to give an unexpected question.

Instructor Dren: "In formal magical theory, the four categories are exhaustive for modern classification. Since Mr. Varek supplied the folklore term, there is no recognized Black Circle."

He turned slightly and summoned a second projection beside the four crests: fragments of ancient script, incomplete diagrams, broken rings.

Instructor Dren: "In very old texts, however, occasional references appear to anomalous or transcendent mana structures beyond the standard hierarchy. Most scholars treat them as symbolic, religious, or metaphorical. Some consider them preservation errors. A small minority of fringe theorists have speculated that a higher classification was once hypothesized."

The fragments dissolved and reformed.

Instructor Dren: "There is no verified historical evidence that any human has ever carried such a circle. In academic practice, Black Circle is not treated as reality."

Haru: "So it's impossible?"

Dren folded his arms.

Instructor Dren: "In magic, 'impossible' is frequently a temporary word. Until something can be observed, measured, or reproduced, it remains speculation. Your task here is to understand what exists, not chase legends that flatter the imagination."

His gaze swept the room before resting briefly on Leon's row.

Instructor Dren: "Understanding what exists includes understanding limits. Power without discipline is not mastery. It is noise with a crest attached."

The class relaxed again. Someone chuckled under their breath.

Leon: "There you have it."

Student: "Imagine transferring in and asking about fairy tales."

Aira glanced between me and the front of the room.

Aira: "Bold first-day question."

Haru: "The reading list was vague."

Her eyes lingered on me a second longer than polite conversation required.

Aira: "Somehow, I don't think a reading list carried all of that."

I did not answer.

At the front, Dren dismissed the projection and moved on.

Instructor Dren: "Remember this. Circle rank reflects potential, not guaranteed victory. A stronger Circle can still lose to a weaker opponent with superior control, timing, or judgment. Raw mana matters. It is not everything."

That much, at least, was true.

Beside me, Aira returned to her notes. I left my notebook mostly blank and listened.

The rest of the lecture covered layered output, mana stability under emotional strain, and the structural ceilings of reinforcement casting across ranks. It was competent instruction, cleanly delivered, and full of small gaps no one in the room knew existed.

I noticed every one of them, not because I was brighter than the students around me, but because I had spent my whole life living outside the chart they thought explained the world.

When the bell rang, chatter returned at once.

Aira turned to me first.

Aira: "If you need help finding the practical arena, I can show you."

Before I could answer, a shadow fell across our desks.

Leon Varek stood there wearing a smile polished enough to pass for warmth to anyone who wanted things simple.

Leon: "Aira, we're still meeting at the west courtyard before practical assessment."

Aira: "I remember."

His eyes shifted to me.

Leon: "And you must be in our practical block."

Haru: "I am."

He nodded as if absorbing unfortunate news.

Leon: "Try not to do anything reckless. Some exercises aren't built for low-capacity circles. I'd hate for your first day to turn into a cautionary tale."

Haru: "I'll be careful."

He looked faintly disappointed that I had not reached for the bait.

Aira: "Leon."

Leon: "What? New students should know the risks."

He straightened and walked off.

Aira: "He does that whenever he wants an audience."

Haru: "He seems efficient about it."

That drew a small, real laugh out of her. For some reason, it stayed with me longer than Leon's warning.

When the transition bell echoed down the hall, the class drifted toward the eastern training complex in familiar clusters. Red Circles moved with the easy posture of students accustomed to being admired without asking, Blues gathered in tight debating knots, and Greens drifted toward the edges of the corridor by habit.

Ahead of us, a second-year in a green-trimmed uniform stepped aside to let a Red group pass and nearly collided with an automated cleaning construct rather than slow their pace.

Aira fell into step beside me anyway. Not close enough to make a show of it, yet close enough that people noticed. That alone seemed to irritate Leon, who kept glancing back from the cluster ahead.

The training complex dwarfed the classroom blocks, built from white stone braided with metallic mana-thread and etched floor to ceiling with stabilization runes. The main assessment hall opened into a wide circular arena surrounded by tiered observation stands and an overhead projection lattice.

It was a room designed for talent to be applauded, and for weakness to be displayed.

Students filed into the stands while Instructor Dren stepped to the center of the stone floor, his boots clicking once against the polished tiles.

**PRACTICAL ASSESSMENT: MANA RESPONSE AND COMBAT ADAPTABILITY**

Dren folded his arms.

Instructor Dren: "Listen carefully. This is neither a ranking match nor an excuse to settle personal grudges. Today's objective is control under pressure, reaction speed, adaptive judgment, and efficient output."

He gestured, and new lines rearranged themselves beneath the floating header.

Instructor Dren: "You will engage in supervised one-on-one assessments. Victory is not determined by brute force. Points are awarded for tactical decisions, precision, composure, restraint, and proper use of arena rules."

Red-Haired Student: "So overwhelming your opponent immediately is discouraged."

Instructor Dren: "It is, when your idea of overwhelming them requires reconstructive healing."

A few students laughed.

Instructor Dren: "Lethal intent results in immediate disqualification and disciplinary review. Excessive force lowers your score. Loss of emotional control lowers your score. If you possess the skill to win cleanly and choose chaos instead, I will assume your education failed somewhere."

That last sentence did more work than the official rules.

Beside me, Aira shifted slightly.

Aira: "He says things like that a lot."

Haru: "Does it help?"

Aira: "Not as much as he hopes."

The corner of my mouth lifted before I caught it.

Dren raised one hand, and a roster panel flared above the arena. Matchups began scrolling into place. Students reacted with quiet satisfaction, annoyance, or poorly hidden dread. Friends avoided each other's eyes. Rivals sat upright. Then my name appeared near the bottom of the board.

**HARU AERLEN vs LEON VAREK**

The temperature in the room changed instantly.

A ripple of interest swept the stands. Several students laughed without bothering to soften it. Others traded knowing glances, already deciding how many seconds the match would last.

Leon laid one hand over his chest in theatrical concern.

Leon: "Sir, I'm not sure this is fair."

Instructor Dren: "For whom?"

That drew louder laughter.

Leon: "A Blue Circle against a first-day Green transfer might leave the wrong impression."

Instructor Dren: "It will. Put yourself in the arena, not in your ego."

Aira turned toward me at once, her expression sharp with genuine worry rather than pity.

Aira: "You don't have to prove anything."

Haru: "I don't plan to."

Aira: "Leon likes humiliating people when he has an audience."

Haru: "I noticed."

Aira: "He's strong, but he performs. If you stay calm and force him to pick his angles, you might last longer than he expects."

Last longer. A fair assumption, given what she believed I was.

Haru: "Thank you."

A brief flicker of surprise crossed her face, a quick lift of her brows followed by a careful nod, as if plain gratitude had not been the answer she expected from me either.

Leon dropped from the platform into the ring with practiced ease. Blue mana curled around his wrists like water ribbons catching sunlight. He worked the stands naturally, not through loud theatrical gestures, just with the posture of someone accustomed to owning every eye in the room.

I stepped down onto the stone opposite him.

The barrier sealed overhead with a low hum. Instantly, I felt the weight of the gallery. The Greens watched quietly. The Blues leaned forward eagerly. The Reds looked on with cool detachment. Aira watched with narrowed focus.

Instructor Dren stepped outside the barrier line and raised one hand.

Instructor Dren: "Assessment begins on my signal. First clean disruption of three balance markers, forced surrender, or incapacitation ends the match. Remember the rules."

Leon smiled across the ring.

Leon: "You can yield before we start. Nobody will blame you."

Haru: "I'm fine."

His smile sharpened.

Dren dropped his hand.

Instructor Dren: "Begin."

Leon moved first.

A compressed stream of water mana shot toward me in a crisp horizontal line, fast enough to force a reaction yet measured enough to avoid serious injury. I shifted half a step left. The jet sliced past my ribs and burst into mist against the far barrier.

Leon did not pause. A second stream followed at once, then a third curving inward from my blind side to narrow my footing. Clean sequencing. Better than average for his age.

I retreated two paces and pivoted. The second strike grazed my sleeve. The third missed my shoulder by inches.

Murmurs stirred along the stands. They had expected panic. Leon's eyes narrowed.

He snapped both wrists upward, and six thin pillars of water mana erupted from the tiles around me in a tight ring. The pillars leaned inward in unison, sealing off the flanks to force a clumsy jump.

Effective against anyone relying on brute escape.

My shoulders tightened as the arcs converged. Half a second before the cage closed, I slipped sideways through the narrowest seam.

Startled noises broke from the benches. Leon clicked his tongue.

Leon: "Not bad. For a Green."

A few chuckles answered him. He drew more mana.

Five crystalline water lances formed above his shoulders, turning slowly with dense pressure. Sharper than his opening probes, heavy enough that a clean hit would bruise bone. He wanted a public collapse.

Leon: "Last chance. Yield gracefully."

I studied him across the ring. Past the polish stood a boy checking whether the room still belonged to him. That alone would make him sloppy.

Haru: "I'd rather continue."

The lances fired.

I raised one hand and threw up a thin, uneven mana shield, the exact kind of frantic barrier a Green Circle student might summon under pressure. The first lance shattered across its face. The second deflected off the angled rim. The third tore past my collar as I shifted weight and let the last two spend themselves against the wall behind me.

Gasps rose through the tiers. A Green Circle had no business reading a practiced Blue that cleanly.

Leon heard the shift in the stands and hated it. His smile vanished.

Leon: "Fine."

The air inside the barrier chilled as he pulled ambient moisture into his field. Water gathered around him in spiraling bands, denser and colder than before.

Instructor Dren called from outside the ward line.

Instructor Dren: "Varek. Control your output."

Leon's jaw set. He chose pressure anyway.

Three whip-like streams snapped toward me from three angles: left, right, overhead. I threw myself back across the tiles. One lash carved a shallow trench through the reinforced stone where my boot had been. Another clipped my left forearm before I could clear the arc.

A sharp ripple of pain ran up my arm, real enough to make my fingers clench.

I landed beside one of the circular balance markers inlaid in the floor. Leon saw it immediately and switched angles without breaking stride, driving a concentrated pulse of water mana straight into the stone beneath my boots.

I pushed off a heartbeat late. The tile cracked under impact and the glyph flared bright blue.

**POINT: VAREK**

The overhead panel flashed.

Cheers broke from a pocket of Blue Circle students. One Red near the front clapped twice, bored and approving. Down on the lower benches, the Greens stared at the glowing score instead of Leon, as though the point had confirmed a lesson they had been taught years ago.

Leon: "There it is."

He thought the match had snapped back to normal. I let him keep the thought.

We circled. He pressed harder now, trading clean form for speed. Sheets of water mana folded into flat blades while low mist crept across the floor to hide my footing. He wanted to force a blunder before the stands finished remembering the first exchange.

I parried one strike with a tight reinforcement pulse, sidestepped the second, and let the third shove me backward.

I could have ended the match in three seconds. Instead, I kept it difficult. Looking weak took far more discipline than being strong.

Pain was simple. Pain arrived, named itself, and faded. Arranging my face around it was the real work: letting my shoulders pull inward just enough to look cornered, letting the small laughs travel across the gallery without answering them, keeping my breath thin so no one mistook restraint for threat.

It had never become natural. Only practiced.

Underneath the habit, something older lifted its head. Not the Black Circle. Me. The part that wanted, just once, to stop being careful for people who treated caution as proof I belonged beneath their boots. The part that wanted the stands to go dead silent for the right reason.

I swallowed that impulse until my throat ached.

Leon lunged inside close range, condensing water along his forearm into a hardened blade. He swung hard.

I blocked late and let the impact slide me across the stone.

Student: "There!"

Student: "Now he's done."

Leon had overcommitted. His weight leaned too far over his front boot, opening his centerline for a fraction of a second while his follow-up spell was already forming before he recovered balance. Small flaws. Common ones.

I moved, not fast enough to look unnatural, just quicker than his pride allowed him to expect.

I stepped inside his reach, caught his wrist, and tapped two fingers against the mana node beside his elbow. A tiny pulse. Just enough to sever the channel running through his dominant arm.

His water construct burst into spray. Leon's eyes went wide.

I pivoted past his shoulder and pressed my palm flat against the nearest balance marker while he was still stumbling for footing. The glyph flashed.

**POINT: AERLEN**

The entire hall drew one sharp breath and forgot what to do with it.

A Green Circle had scored on a Blue through clean timing, with neither luck nor a wild burst of mana to blame.

Leon spun around, stunned. Voices broke out across the stands in ragged pieces.

Student: "What?"

Student: "How did he-"

Student: "Did Leon just drop a point?"

Student: "No, he broke the casting channel-"

Student: "Can Greens even do that?"

On the lower bench, a Green Circle boy smiled before remembering to hide it. A Blue beside him glared on Leon's behalf.

Up in the tiers, Aira was already on her feet, her worry gone, replaced by bright, serious focus.

Leon's face flooded red with humiliation.

Leon: "Fluke."

I said nothing. That made it worse.

Water surged around him in a violent spiral and crashed forward in a wide wave across the tiles. Mist exploded upward, choking visibility as students leaned back from the humming barrier.

Instructor Dren: "Varek! Reduce output!"

Leon pushed harder. He was no longer fighting for points. He was fighting to erase the sound of the room.

Through the fog, I tracked him easily by his mana flow. His breath had gone ragged, his spell cadence jagged with temper. Six condensed water blades formed around him and launched in staggered waves: two high, two center, two low.

I slowed my breathing and stopped pretending to stumble.

Step left. Pivot. Drop. Turn.

My palm grazed the first blade and tilted it off-line. The second hissed past my ear. The third shattered on the tiles. I broke the fourth with a tight pulse hidden inside a clumsy-looking block, and slipped clean past the centerline as the last two missed entirely.

Noise in the gallery thinned until only water hissing against the ward wall remained.

Leon saw me step out of the mist untouched, and for the first time fear flickered behind his anger. Fear of not understanding what stood in front of him.

He drew mana too fast after that. Too much, too quickly, through too tight a rotation.

The pressure knot above his right wrist thickened, then stopped behaving like an attack and locked into an inward loop.

I felt the collapse begin a breath before the gallery did. So did Dren.

Instructor Dren: "Stop casting!"

Leon tried to muscle the spell into shape anyway. A compressed, violently vibrating sphere of turbulent water mana bucked above his wrist. If it folded back at that distance, his own Blue Circle would shred the channels in his casting arm.

The barrier hummed tight. Students recoiled from the railing.

I felt the Black Circle stir behind my ribs. Three choices, none of them clean: expose too much and save him flawlessly, hold back and let his pride cripple him, or take the smaller wound myself.

Mercy was never soft in moments like that. It was arithmetic with bad margins.

I stepped straight into the collapsing edge.

Leon's eyes snapped to mine, anger cracking wide open into plain terror.

The outer shear tore my left sleeve open above the wrist and drove a bright, stinging shock straight down my forearm. While every eye in the stands flinched at the hit, I raised my hand and slipped an impossibly thin cancellation pulse inside ordinary reinforcement mana. Nowhere near true Black Circle output, just enough precision to deny the loop permission to detonate.

The unstable sphere folded inward with a wet, airless thump.

There was no blast, no backlash, no shattered bone. The compressed water simply fell around us as harmless rain.

My fingers went numb to the knuckles. On my exposed inner wrist, my false Green crest flickered half a heartbeat late. I lowered my arm before anyone noticed the hairline of dark light fade beneath my skin.

High on the upper observation platform, a tall student in an elite mantle went completely still. Pale-gold hair caught the arena lamps, a brilliant **Yellow Circle** crest glowing at her collarbone as she rose half a step from her seat before the rest of the hall even realized Leon had been spared.

Outside the barrier, Dren's eyes narrowed. He had seen enough to know something about that ending did not belong to a Green transfer.

Leon stared at his intact hand as though his own magic had become a stranger.

Voices crashed back into the hall all at once.

Student: "What just happened?"

Student: "Did Varek cancel his own spell?"

Student: "No, the transfer touched it-"

Student: "A first-day Green?"

Student: "That control makes no sense-"

Leon staggered back a step, wet hair plastered to his forehead.

Dren strode straight through the dropping barrier.

Instructor Dren: "Match over."

The panel overhead flashed bright white.

**ASSESSMENT ENDED**

Protests rolled down from the stands.

Student: "What? Why?"

Student: "Sir, that's it?"

Student: "He was about to lose!"

Student: "No, Leon would've won-"

Student: "Would he?"

Dren ignored all of them. He stopped in front of Leon first.

Instructor Dren: "You lost emotional control, exceeded safe output limits, and ignored a direct order."

Leon opened his mouth, then shut it.

Dren turned to me.

Instructor Dren: "And you demonstrated unusual tactical timing."

Haru: "Thank you, sir."

His gaze stayed on me a second longer than praise required, flicking briefly toward my torn sleeve before he faced the tiers.

Instructor Dren: "Take the lesson clearly. Rank matters. It is not absolute. Any student who treats natural advantage as a substitute for discipline will eventually be embarrassed."

Several Greens looked a fraction less small. Several Blues looked sour. Above us, the Yellow Circle student on the upper platform had already turned away toward the exit stairs.

For the class, the match ended there. For me, a real problem had just begun. Attention had weight, and I could already feel it settling across my shoulders.

As the class spilled onto the floor, Leon drove his shoulder against mine hard enough to make it deliberate.

Leon: "You planned that."

Haru: "No. You rushed."

He stopped and half-turned, anger and disbelief colliding across his face for one heartbeat before he walked out.

Aira reached me seconds later.

Aira: "That wasn't normal."

Haru: "For a Green Circle?"

Aira: "For anyone on their first day."

There was no accusation in her voice, only astonishment mixed with the beginnings of real trust. I did not know what to do with either.

Haru: "It was timing."

She held my gaze, testing the edge of the answer I refused to give.

Aira: "Maybe. I still think there's more to you than timing, Haru."

Before I could answer, a new presence stepped onto the upper observation platform.

The hall changed without anyone announcing why. Conversations cut off mid-word. Students straightened. Even Leon halted near the doorway.

At the top of the stairs stood a girl in the formal elite mantle reserved for special-ranking officers. Her posture was razor-clean, her expression composed, and the mana around her carried a bright, heavy refinement that pressed lightly against the skin even from across the floor.

A high-grade **Red Circle** crest glowed at her collarbone.

She looked down at Dren, then Leon, then settled her gaze squarely on me. Not contempt. Calculation.

Instructor Dren: "Student Council President."

So this was her. Even transfer briefs and hallway whispers carried her reputation. Aurelis's student council did not hang festival banners; it governed disciplinary referrals, emergency witnesses, duel permits, and enough institutional leverage to make noble heirs mind their manners in public.

She descended the steps at an unhurried pace.

Rhea: "Forgive the interruption. I heard there was unusual activity in the assessment hall."

Her voice was calm, polished, and completely opaque.

Instructor Dren: "The matter is handled."

Rhea: "Handled, then. Still, it appears this year's transfer student has already become interesting."

Nearby students traded quick looks. I kept my face blank.

She studied me one heartbeat longer, then offered a thin smile, the look of someone who had spotted a loose thread and intended to keep pulling until the seam came apart.

Rhea: "I look forward to seeing more."

She turned and left at the same measured pace, leaving half the room pretending not to watch her climb the stairs.

Aira: "That is bad."

Haru: "Who was that?"

Aira stared at me as if I had asked the name of a storm everyone else knew to board windows for.

Aira: "Rhea Valmont. Student Council President. Red Circle. Once she takes an interest in you, paperwork starts growing teeth."

I looked back toward the empty steps where Rhea had disappeared. My first day at Aurelis was not even finished, and hiding had already turned into a spectator sport.

## Interlude: The Wrong Kindness

The west corridor outside the training complex emptied in stages.

First the loudest students poured past, carrying the assessment away in fractured pieces.

*A Green scored on Leon. Leon lost control. Dren looked furious. Elira Vale stood up. No, she didn't. Yes, she did. The transfer did something weird at the end.*

By the time I reached the side exit, the afternoon had already splintered into four competing versions. Contradiction was useful. It made hiding easier.

I stopped beneath a narrow stone arch where late sunlight fell across the floor in pale gold bars. My left sleeve hung torn above the wrist from Leon's collapsing spell, exposing the false Green Circle more than I liked. I tried to fold the ripped cuff back down. Badly.

My fingers were still numb from steering the backlash clear of Leon's arm. That annoyed me more than the sting.

Haru: "You can come out."

The air behind the pillar shifted, and the Yellow Circle girl from the upper platform stepped into the sunlight. The one the passing students had called Elira Vale.

Up close, her poise felt quieter than her crest, and she did not look embarrassed at being caught waiting. Embarrassment would have made this simpler.

Elira: "I was deciding whether approaching you here would feel like an ambush."

Haru: "And?"

Elira: "I decided it probably would."

I looked at her.

Elira: "Then I did it anyway."

That surprised a short breath out of me, closer to a laugh than I liked.

In one hand she held a folded strip of white healer cloth, clean and fine enough that most students would have saved it for a real wound rather than a torn cuff. She followed my gaze.

Elira: "Your arm."

Haru: "It's fine."

Elira: "Your sleeve disagrees."

Haru: "Fabric is dramatic."

Her mouth softened by a fraction.

Elira: "May I?"

The question was the trap. If she had stepped straight in, I could have backed away. If she had ordered me to hold still, I could have disliked her cleanly. Instead she asked, making refusal feel heavier than it deserved to be.

Haru: "It doesn't need a bandage."

Elira: "I know."

Haru: "Then why offer?"

The warmth faded slightly from her expression.

Elira: "Because something can be unnecessary and still kind."

I should have said thank you and moved on. Instead I read the shape underneath her words wrong. A Yellow Circle prodigy offering aid to a Green Circle transfer after a spectacle: generous, photogenic, the exact gesture people admired if word traveled. The sort of kindness that could turn me into a footnote in someone else's reputation.

Haru: "You don't have to prove anything."

Her hands stilled.

Elira: "Prove?"

I knew instantly that I had picked the wrong word. Knowing did not take it back.

Haru: "People are watching."

Elira glanced down the empty corridor, then back at me.

Elira: "No one is here."

Haru: "People don't need to be in the room to use what happened."

Her expression quieted further.

Elira: "Is that what you think I'm doing?"

Haru: "I don't know what you're doing."

Honest in the worst possible direction.

Elira folded the white cloth once between her fingers.

Elira: "I was trying to hand you something clean before you walked back into a school full of people deciding what your torn sleeve means."

I had no answer for that.

She extended the cloth anyway, neither stepping closer nor pulling back.

Elira: "You can take it without accepting anything else."

That sentence had no business working on me. I reached out and took the cloth.

Our fingers did not brush. She made sure of it. Somehow the care in that distance unsettled me more than the gift itself.

Haru: "Thank you."

Elira nodded. For half a second I thought that might mend the scrape.

Elira: "You are very careful about not owing anyone."

I looked up too fast. Her golden eyes were steady, without a trace of triumph. She had not said it to win an exchange.

Haru: "Owing people gets complicated."

Elira: "So does refusing every kindness before it has a chance to become one."

No accusation sat in her voice, which was why the line stayed lodged under my ribs. She turned toward the hall.

I had three polite, tactical things I could have said. I used none of them. I stood there with the white cloth clutched in my numb fingers and watched her walk back toward the sunlit corridor.

At the corner, she paused without turning around.

Elira: "I did not stand up because you scored on Leon."

My grip tightened on the fabric.

Elira: "I stood up because you looked hurt and pretended not to be."

Then she was gone.

Silence returned to the arch. After she rounded the corner, I wrapped the cloth around my torn sleeve and numb wrist anyway. It was unnecessary. It helped. That made it worse.

---

The rumors beat the morning bell on my second day.

That was the trouble with elite academies: nothing stayed small overnight. By the time I crossed the main courtyard of **Aurelis Arcane Academy**, students were already retelling yesterday's assessment as if it had been a championship duel instead of a classroom block.

Some versions were merely inflated. Others had crossed straight into fiction.

Student: "He shattered Leon's spell with Green Circle output."

Student: "No, Leon lost control on his own."

Student: "I heard Dren stopped it because the transfer was hiding a relic."

Student: "Someone said his mana moved strangely."

Student: "Strangely how?"

Student: "No idea. That's what makes it weird."

I walked past them with my bag slung over one shoulder, pace unhurried. Starving attention was the surest way to outlast it, though attention had an irritating habit of feeding itself.

Near the central stairs, two Blue Circle boys fell quiet as I approached. One looked straight at the faint green mark beneath my left cuff, then up at my face.

Student 1: "That's him."

Student 2: "He still looks ordinary."

Student 1: "That's the unsettling part."

I kept walking.

Silver-leaf trees along the walkways shimmered in early light while practice glyphs rotated high above the towers. Yesterday I had been invisible. Today I was a question, and questions drew knives faster than insults did.

Inside the entrance hall, Aira stood near a floating directory holding two paper cups from the academy cafe. She spotted me before I could think about picking another corridor and lifted one cup.

Aira: "I guessed you'd come through here."

Haru: "You planned for coincidence?"

Aira smiled.

Aira: "I prefer 'efficient friendliness.'"

She held the cup out.

Aira: "Tea. I didn't know what you drink, so I picked the lowest-risk option."

I looked from the cup to her face.

Haru: "Thank you."

Aira: "You're welcome."

She let a beat pass before falling into step beside me.

Aira: "Also, before the day gets worse, half the academy has decided you're either secretly brilliant or deeply suspicious."

Haru: "Only half?"

That made her laugh.

Aira: "You're getting better at this."

Haru: "At collecting trouble?"

Aira: "At sounding like you're joking."

Walking beside her toward the east wing drew fresh glances. Aira ignored them easily. I was less sure I ever would. She took a sip from her cup and dropped her voice.

Aira: "Leon is furious, by the way."

Haru: "I assumed."

Aira: "He's pretending not to care, which for Leon is somehow louder than normal anger."

Haru: "Inconvenient."

Aira: "For you, yes. For me, it's Tuesday."

We turned into the east corridor just as a fresh wave of whispers rolled through the hall. This time the pitch changed: softer, sharper, edged with reverence.

I felt the mana before I saw her. Warm, radiant, steady as sunlight pressing through tall glass.

Students ahead shifted sideways almost by reflex, clearing the center of the stone walkway. Blue Circles straightened. Red Circles turned with measured composure. Even two instructors paused.

Then she stepped into view.

In the quiet west corridor yesterday, she had simply been a tall girl with pale-gold hair and a strip of white healer cloth. Here, with fifty students parting around her, I saw what Aurelis had made of her. Her uniform looked almost ceremonial beneath the elite mantle draped over her shoulders, and at her collarbone blazed an unmistakable **Yellow Circle**. Unhidden. Unsoftened for anyone's comfort.

The corridor hushed as she walked.

Student 3: "It's her."

Student 4: "Lady Elira."

Student 5: "Did she really stand up during Section Three's assessment yesterday?"

Aira followed my eyes and leaned closer.

Aira: "That's **Elira Vale**."

I kept yesterday's corridor, and the clean folded cloth still tucked inside my coat pocket, to myself.

Haru: "I gathered she was important."

Aira gave me a flat look.

Aira: "That might be the most understated sentence anyone has ever spoken about Elira Vale."

Haru: "Should I know more?"

Aira: "Yellow Circle. One of the youngest high-grade light-affinity magicians in the country. First in magical theory, first in practical control, and somehow still decent enough to make people feel guilty for being awful in front of her."

Haru: "So everyone admires her."

Aira: "Admire is a polite word for it."

Elira moved down the hallway surrounded not by attendants, only by empty air. Nobody crowded her. Unlike the Reds who wore status like a polished edge, she looked faintly weary of the space people carved around her.

Then her eyes shifted across the crowd and landed straight on me.

Just for a second. Neither contempt nor idle curiosity, only calm recognition, the quiet gaze of someone remembering an unfinished conversation. Her eyes flicked once toward my mended left sleeve, narrowed by a hair, and then she walked past.

The hallway seemed to exhale all at once.

Aira turned to me with solemn gravity.

Aira: "If you claim you didn't notice that, I'll be offended on behalf of human eyesight."

Haru: "She looked at me."

Aira: "Yes."

She lifted her tea cup toward me like a witness pointing at evidence.

Haru: "People do that here."

Aira stared at me.

Aira: "You still don't understand how this academy works."

Haru: "I'm learning."

Aira: "Lesson one: Yellow Circle students do not casually single out Green Circle transfers in a crowded hall."

Haru: "Maybe she was looking past me."

Aira: "Haru."

Haru: "Fine. She looked at me."

Aira folded one arm across her ribs, balancing her cup.

Aira: "Good. Progress."

Homeroom felt tighter when we entered a minute later. Yesterday's curiosity had hardened overnight into scrutiny.

Leon was already seated, talking to two Blue Circle classmates in a voice low enough to imply strategy and loud enough to invite listeners. When I walked in beside Aira, the corner of his mouth pulled tight.

Leon: "Impressive. One lucky assessment and suddenly you have an escort."

Aira: "Good morning to you too, Leon."

Leon: "I'm being civil."

Aira: "Then I'd hate to hear you rude."

I took my seat without answering, which irritated him twice as much.

Instructor Dren arrived moments later and launched straight into multi-layered reinforcement theory without mentioning yesterday once. That did not stop half the class from stealing looks toward the back row.

Midway through the lecture, a faint prickle brushed the edge of my awareness. Someone watching, not from inside the classroom, from higher up. When I glanced toward the tall windows, nothing stood outside except bright morning cloud. Still, the sensation lingered until the bell.

---

Lunch at Aurelis was less a meal than a sorting ritual disguised with silverware.

The dining hall rose in wide tiers beneath panoramic windows overlooking the lower gardens. Students clustered by circle, lineage, and confidence. Even which alcove you chose told the room where you thought you belonged.

Aira and I had just carried our trays to a quiet side table when the hall hushed again. People shifted their chairs without needing to be asked.

I did not need to look up to know who had crossed the threshold.

Elira Vale walked between the tables with unhurried poise, sunlight catching the amber-gold edges of her hair near the glass. Up close, Yellow Circle mana carried a refinement that made lesser currents waver around it like candle flames near an open window.

She stopped beside our table. Half the dining hall forgot how to chew.

Her hand paused near the empty chair back for half a breath while her gaze swept the watching tables, measuring every pair of eyes before settling back on us.

Elira: "May I sit here?"

Aira nearly dropped her fork.

Aira: "You're asking us?"

Elira's lips curved slightly.

Elira: "That seemed polite."

Aira looked at me, back at Elira, and visibly decided to postpone her breakdown until after class.

Aira: "Yes. Of course. Please."

Elira took the seat across from us. Around us, fifty conversations resumed at half volume while everyone pretended not to listen.

Aira recovered first.

Aira: "I'm Aira Solen."

Elira: "I know."

Aira blinked.

Elira turned her calm golden gaze onto me.

Elira: "And you're Haru Aerlen."

Haru: "Yes."

She gave no sign in front of Aira that we had already spoken in the west corridor, yet her steady eyes held the faintest echo of yesterday's unspoken challenge.

Elira: "You asked an interesting question in class yesterday."

Aira stared at me.

Aira: "Your homeroom question reached the Yellow Circle lunch network?"

Haru: "Apparently."

Elira's expression softened slightly.

Elira: "Questions are rarely the problem. Fear of them usually is."

Hardly standard academy doctrine.

Haru: "Do you believe Black Circle theory has merit?"

Two students at the next table nearly choked trying to eavesdrop discreetly.

Elira glanced down at her tea, then back up at me.

Elira: "I believe magical history is incomplete. And I believe most people are comfortable dismissing anything that threatens an order they benefit from."

Aira's fork stopped halfway to her tray.

Aira: "That is not the answer I expected."

Elira: "Most people expect Yellow Circles to defend the system."

Aira: "Do you?"

Elira smiled, though a shadow sat beneath it.

Elira: "The system defends itself well enough."

For a brief moment none of us spoke, and the clatter of the dining hall receded until our table felt like its own small island.

Elira folded her hands lightly beside her tray.

Elira: "I also wanted to thank you."

Haru: "For what?"

Elira: "Yesterday. You stopped Leon's spell from collapsing badly enough to injure him."

Aira whipped toward me so fast her chair scraped the stone.

Aira: "You did what?"

Across the hall, pretense died completely. Several tables leaned closer.

Haru: "I reacted."

Elira: "With unusual precision."

Haru: "So I've been told."

This time her smile was genuine, neither grand nor ceremonial, simply warm. It changed her entire face, and for one inconvenient second I forgot how to answer.

Aira caught it immediately, glancing between us with the look of someone realizing her peaceful semester had just sprung a leak.

Elira set her cup down.

Elira: "There is an advanced practical demonstration this afternoon in the upper training gardens. My group was asked to assist."

Aira: "Your group?"

Elira: "High-tier affinity control."

Aira made a quiet sound that clearly meant *that is several tiers above our schedule*.

Elira looked straight at me.

Elira: "You should come watch, Haru."

No challenge in her tone. Only plain sincerity, which made declining harder.

Haru: "Why?"

Elira: "Because I think you notice things other people miss."

Aira rested her chin on one palm, fully embracing her new job as commentator on my bad decisions.

Aira: "He does that."

Haru: "Aira."

Aira: "I'm helping."

Amusement brightened Elira's eyes.

Elira: "Good. Then perhaps both of you should come."

Aira: "Now I definitely have to."

---

The upper training gardens rose across broad stone terraces overlooking the western cliffs. Ornamental trees bordered the walkways, and practice fields lay framed by flowerbeds, crystal pylons, and rune-carved circles built to anchor high-grade output.

Upper-year Reds and Blues already lined the observation rail when Aira and I arrived, with several instructors stationed along the perimeter in case an exercise slipped its leash.

At the terrace's center, four elite students took their marks: two high-grade Reds, one specialized Blue, and Elira.

Even standing motionless, she anchored the field. Afternoon sun turned the gold of her crest nearly white at its edges. When she raised one hand, the air across the terrace answered: a quiet rise in pressure followed by a clean harmonic hum from the stabilizing stones.

Instructor Maren addressed the crowd from the margin.

Instructor Maren: "Today's exercise examines synchronized affinity layering under unequal mana loads. Observe balance, hand transitions, and restraint thresholds."

The Reds opened first, braiding fire and wind into intersecting rings while the Blue anchored water barriers to catch stray overflow. Polished, disciplined casting.

Then Elira stepped into the center node.

Light bloomed, never harsh, simply luminous. Golden-white threads unspooled from her fingertips like silk, weaving between the other three spells without pushing them aside. She did not overpower the formation; she refined it, bracing thin seams, smoothing tremor, raising everyone else's work simply by touching the frame.

The terrace went dead quiet.

Aira whispered without looking away.

Aira: "Every time I watch her cast, it feels unfair."

Haru: "She has remarkable control."

Aira cut a sideways glance at me.

Aira: "Only you could call something that gorgeous 'controlled.'"

I kept my eyes on the field.

Haru: "It's beautiful."

Aira blinked.

Aira: "Oh. Good. You do have a pulse."

The instructors raised the load for the second phase. One of the Red Circle students lost half a beat of alignment, tilting the ring until the outer runes vibrated with a sharp metallic whine.

Before Maren even lifted a hand, Elira rebuilt the entire geometry.

Her left hand opened. Her right turned. Golden threads multiplied, crossed, and lifted the faltering structure into clean balance so smoothly that most of the spectators would walk away calling the display flawless.

I remembered the half-breath where it had nearly snapped, and the exact stillness with which she refused to let someone else's slip turn into harm.

Quiet applause rose across the terrace. Elira exhaled, lowered her arms, and scanned the crowd until her eyes found me.

She smiled openly, and not for the gallery.

Aira turned her head slowly toward me.

Aira: "Oh no."

Haru: "What?"

Aira: "Nothing. Just the start of my headaches."

Haru: "You say that a lot."

Aira: "Because you keep making it true."

When the dismissal bell sounded, students crowded forward to praise the casters. I would have slipped out quietly, except a classmate caught Aira by the elbow and pulled her into a loud cluster near the stairs.

That left me alone by the cliff railing for roughly seven seconds before Elira walked over.

With the formal demonstration behind her, she seemed both more human and harder to guard against.

Elira: "You came."

Haru: "You invited me."

Elira: "Plenty of people accept invitations for the wrong reasons."

Haru: "And what reasons did you expect from me?"

She studied me with the late afternoon sun resting lightly across her shoulders.

Elira: "Curiosity, maybe."

Haru: "Harmless, then."

Elira: "It can be. It can also change everything."

She turned and laid one hand along the stone rail overlooking the western cliffs. Below us, mist drifted through the lower ravines, catching faint lantern reflections from Aurelis far below.

Behind us, voices praised the exercise while stray golden motes faded from the practice rings. Around Elira, noise always seemed to pull back a step.

Elira: "You weren't impressed the usual way."

Haru: "There's a usual way?"

Her lips curved.

Elira: "Most people watching a Yellow Circle either admire it loudly or resent it quietly."

Haru: "And I did neither?"

Elira: "You observed."

Too accurate.

Haru: "You noticed that from the center of a four-way formation?"

She turned back toward me.

Elira: "I notice when someone watches the structure instead of the spectacle."

No accusation sat in her voice, which made me more cautious, not less.

Haru: "Your control was excellent."

Elira: "That sounds like a faculty report."

Haru: "Would you prefer a less academic verdict?"

Her smile arrived at once, warm and brief enough to vanish before I could brace for it.

Elira: "I'd prefer an honest one."

I looked back toward the empty practice circles where the last wisps of gold were thinning into evening air.

Haru: "It was beautiful."

I meant the spell. The quiet beat that followed suggested the sentence left too much room for other readings.

Elira's expression softened by a degree too fine to name.

Elira: "Thank you."

Behind me, I could feel Aira staring from across the terrace. I did not turn.

Elira folded her hands lightly in front of her.

Elira: "You really did stop Leon's backlash yesterday."

There it was, laid out cleanly without pretense.

Haru: "I kept a mistake from getting worse."

Elira: "That isn't a denial."

Haru: "It isn't a confession either."

For a second I thought she would press. Instead she looked back out over the cliffside gardens.

Elira: "People assume power is loud."

Haru: "Usually it wants an audience."

Elira: "And yours doesn't?"

Every muscle in my shoulders locked inwardly, though my face stayed still.

Haru: "My what?"

She did not look at me when she answered.

Elira: "Your presence feels inconsistent."

Far more dangerous than Leon's bruised pride or homeroom gossip. Elira was not guessing. She was perceiving.

Haru: "I'm a Green Circle transfer. There isn't much room for inconsistency in that."

She turned back to face me.

Elira: "That line would work better if you believed it."

For the first time since stepping onto campus, I had no ready deflection. She saw that too, and her voice gentled.

Elira: "You don't have to tell me anything."

Haru: "Then why say it?"

Elira: "Because sometimes being seen is not the same as being exposed."

That landed harder than any spell Leon had thrown. Most people who noticed an anomaly pushed toward it, wanting a label, leverage, or a secret to spend. Elira spoke as if recognition itself could be an act of care.

Haru: "You're unusually kind for someone this school treats like royalty."

She let out a soft, amused breath.

Elira: "That may be the least flattering compliment I've ever received."

Haru: "I meant it well."

Elira: "I know."

She glanced past my shoulder toward the terrace, where Aira was very obviously pretending not to stare at us while staring straight at us.

Elira: "Your friend looks worried."

Haru: "Aira expects complications."

Elira: "Is she usually right?"

Haru: "More often than I'd like."

Elira laughed quietly, unguarded and clear, and I understood in a single beat why people gravitated toward her. Beneath the gold crest and the legend, she actually listened.

Elira: "May I ask you something?"

Haru: "You already have."

Elira: "One more thing, then."

Haru: "Go ahead."

Her expression grew thoughtful.

Elira: "Why did you ask about Black Circle magicians in class?"

Curiosity would have been easy. Academic theory would have been safe. For reasons I had not fully examined, I gave her neither.

Haru: "Because I wanted to see how people react to the idea of something outside their chart."

She held my gaze.

Elira: "And what did you learn?"

Haru: "Most people laugh first."

Elira: "And after that?"

Haru: "If it threatens the order they depend on, they get afraid."

Elira stayed quiet for several seconds, then nodded once, confirming something to herself rather than politely agreeing.

Elira: "That sounds lonely."

I should not have felt the sentence as deeply as I did.

Haru: "Not always."

Elira: "No?"

Haru: "Sometimes there are people who make it less so."

For the first time, surprise caught her completely off guard: not the words themselves, just the fact that I had spoken them. Faint color touched her cheeks before she looked away down the garden path.

Elira: "You're quieter than most people here."

Haru: "It usually helps."

Elira: "Maybe. Silence also makes people underestimate how much you're actually saying."

Behind us, the terrace was emptying as instructors dismissed the remaining spectators and practice circles winked out one by one. Cool evening air drifted up from the cliffs.

Elira stepped back from the rail.

Elira: "There's a moonlit archive hall in the north library wing. Almost nobody goes there after classes."

Haru: "Should I worry about where this invitation leads?"

Her eyes brightened.

Elira: "Only if you dislike old books, quiet rooms, and honest conversation."

Haru: "Suspiciously specific."

Elira: "I thought you preferred specific."

She was right, which was the entire problem.

Haru: "You're inviting me to the library."

For a heartbeat, her easy certainty hesitated.

Elira: "I don't usually invite people there. Yes. I am."

Haru: "People will misunderstand."

Elira: "People misunderstand most things."

Haru: "Not reassuring."

Elira: "It wasn't meant to be."

I almost smiled before brisk footsteps interrupted from the side walkway.

An irritated Blue mana signature arrived half a second before its owner.

Leon: "Naturally."

Aira appeared a step behind him, looking deeply unimpressed.

Aira: "I told him this was a terrible idea."

Leon stopped three paces off, glancing from Elira to me with stiff disbelief.

Leon: "Lady Elira, I didn't realize you were entertaining lower-year rumors now."

The warmth drained from the terrace in an instant. Elira's posture cooled by a single unmistakable degree.

Elira: "And I didn't realize my conversations required your approval, Leon."

Aira's mouth twitched, clearly enjoying the moment far more than she meant to show.

Leon stiffened.

Leon: "That isn't what I meant."

Elira: "Then choose your words more carefully."

His eyes flicked back to me.

Leon: "With respect, reputations matter here. Especially when someone starts enjoying attention he hasn't earned."

Aira stepped straight into his line of sight.

Aira: "He earned enough to embarrass you in front of an entire assessment hall."

Leon: "Aira."

Aira: "Go ahead. If you're going to be petty, at least be efficient about it."

Leon's jaw clenched as yesterday's humiliation flared back to the surface, sharpened by Elira standing right beside us.

Elira did not look angry. She looked disappointed, which rattled him worse.

Elira: "If your concern is status, keep it. If your concern is truth, improve your eyesight."

Aira let out a quiet sound somewhere between shock and applause. Leon went pale, then flushed.

Leon: "You actually think there's something special about him?"

Elira glanced at me for a fraction of a second before answering.

Elira: "I think dismissing people because their circle is green reflects poorly on everyone who does it."

Not the same as saying no. Leon caught the gap at once. His eyes narrowed.

Leon: "Then I'll prove yesterday was luck."

Haru: "You don't need to prove anything to me."

Leon gave a dry, humorless laugh.

Leon: "Generous from someone hiding behind borrowed mystery."

Aira: "Borrowed mystery is a terrible phrase."

Leon ignored her.

Leon: "There's an interclass combat ladder event in three days. Restricted brackets, official observation, no instructor stepping in unless someone breaks. If you're really that composed, enter."

Haru: "I transferred in yesterday."

Leon: "Then decline. Publicly, if you like. Everyone can decide what that means."

Aira: "That's manipulation."

Leon: "That's an invitation."

Elira's voice stayed level.

Elira: "It's coercive and transparent."

Leon pivoted toward her quickly, desperate to salvage dignity.

Leon: "Then he can refuse without shame, can't he?"

The challenge hung in the cooling evening air.

I had no interest in attention, no interest in a combat ladder, and certainly no interest in handing Leon a stage. Yet I could already hear the rumor if I walked away: *the Green transfer who surprised a Blue once and hid behind Elira Vale.*

More importantly, I felt something else.

Eyes watching from farther up the garden path. Neither Leon's friends nor stray students. Deliberate, clinical attention, measuring the four of us from behind the trees. Before I could isolate the signature, it vanished cleanly.

Elira caught the micro-shift in my gaze.

Elira: "Haru?"

I brought my focus back to the terrace. Leon waited. Aira frowned. Elira watched closely.

Haru: "Fine."

Aira whipped around.

Aira: "Fine?"

Vindictive relief lit Leon's face.

Leon: "You accept?"

Haru: "I'll enter."

Aira: "Wrong answer."

Elira did not look surprised, only thoughtful.

Leon straightened, visibly relieved that the world had clicked back into a contest he understood.

Leon: "Good. Then we can all stop pretending."

He walked off before anyone could dull the exit.

Aira stared after him, then turned on me as if deciding whether to lecture me or sigh herself into early retirement.

Aira: "Apparently you enjoy difficult lives."

Haru: "Not especially."

Aira: "Then why agree?"

Haru: "Because he won't stop pushing until he sees the edge once."

Aira: "He'll keep pushing anyway."

Haru: "Then I'll deal with that when it happens."

Aira shut her eyes for a second.

Aira: "I hate when you stay calm like that."

Elira stepped a half-pace closer, dropping her voice.

Elira: "You sensed something just now."

Aira glanced sharply between us.

Aira: "What did he sense?"

Haru: "I'm not sure."

True, and not nearly enough.

Elira held my gaze steadily.

Elira: "Then be careful."

Haru: "I usually am."

Elira: "No," she murmured. "Usually you're controlled. Those aren't always the same thing."

Again, she left a sentence sitting where I could not easily set it down.

Aira looked from Elira to me and threw up her hands in surrender.

Aira: "My peaceful academic year is officially dead."

Elira smiled at her.

Elira: "I'll try not to make it worse."

Aira: "That isn't comforting when spoken by a Yellow Circle standing three feet from my profoundly suspicious friend."

Haru: "I'm suspicious?"

Aira: "Profoundly."

Elira's soft laugh eased the knot in the air for a brief moment.

Sunset washed the terrace in amber, carrying the faint smell of spell-scorched stone and garden blossoms up from the cliffs. Then the evening bells rolled across Aurelis, calling students down toward the dining halls and study blocks.

Elira turned toward the upper walkway.

Elira: "The library invitation still stands."

Haru: "You're persistent."

Elira: "Only when something matters."

Her eyes met mine for one quiet second, then she inclined her head to Aira and disappeared into the lantern-lit trees.

Aira waited until Elira was out of earshot before folding her arms.

Aira: "Well."

Haru: "Well what?"

Aira: "A Yellow Circle just invited you to a private library hall, cut Leon down in public, and looked at you like a puzzle she wants to solve gently."

Haru: "Specific interpretation."

Aira: "Accurate one. I genuinely don't know whether to worry for you or for the whole academy."

Haru: "That sounds dramatic."

Aira: "You asked about Black Circle magic on day one, survived Leon's meltdown, entered the combat ladder, and caught Elira Vale's attention. Dramatic already arrived."

She started down the terrace steps.

Aira: "Come on. If you're going to ruin your quiet life, eat dinner first."

I followed her down into the lantern-lit main halls.

## Coda: The Gate Remembered

After dinner, Aira peeled off toward the east dormitory stair, leaving me to take the quieter flagstone path past Aurelis's old inner foundation gate.

Twilight had settled deep violet over the towers. My thoughts kept circling back to the upper garden path, to that brief clinical scrutiny disappearing into the trees before I could trace it, and to Elira's voice saying being seen was not the same as being exposed.

Then, as I passed beneath the ancient stone archway, the gate behind me gave one quiet chime.

No student walking across the lower yard reacted. The note was too soft for ceremony and too exact for wind.

I stopped and turned.

A hairline of light ran through the weathered crest carved high above the arch. Green first. Then Blue. Then Red. Then Yellow. Four obedient colors, the approved ladder of the world, bright enough to look harmless in the dusk.

Beneath them, where solid mortar should have stayed dark, a fifth ring pressed once from inside the stone, ink-black, razor-precise, older than the towers around it, and vanished before the false Green crest on my wrist could even flicker.

The gate had not welcomed me when I crossed it yesterday morning.

It had counted me.

A pair of first-years hurried past under the arch, arguing loudly about bracket odds for the coming combat ladder. One glanced down at the pale green glow peeking under my left cuff, dismissed me without slowing, and kept walking. Ordinary cruelty. Useful camouflage.

Inside my coat pocket, my fingers brushed the clean folded edge of Elira's healer cloth, still kept from yesterday's corridor.

Up on the garden terrace, someone had watched me with real intent. Inside Aurelis's old stone, ancient magic had recognized the lie I wore. And somewhere across the courtyard, a girl with gold eyes had looked straight past it.

I stood beneath the darkening arch and realized I was no longer sure which discovery frightened me more.

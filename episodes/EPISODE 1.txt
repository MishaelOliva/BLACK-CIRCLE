# Episode 1: **The Green Circle Transfer Student**

The morning sky over **Aurelis City** caught the sun in bright gold, each cloud edge splitting the light into colors ordinary skies should never have held.

Between the white towers and glass bridges of the capital, glowing travel runes shimmered in neat lanes. Floating carriages glided silently above the streets while fountains in the lower plazas threw pale mist across marble steps already crowded with students in navy and cream.

The new uniform collar had been scratching the same spot on my neck since the night train.

Eighteen years old, mid-year transfer from Lumeris District: **Haru Aerlen**, wearing the weakest mark society could tolerate. The uniform fabric was expensive and enchanted, cut sharper than any district coat I had ever owned, and beneath the left cuff sat a quiet **Green Circle** crest pressed lightly into my inner wrist.

The gate's ward pressure settled cold behind my chest as **Aurelis Arcane Academy** rose ahead.

Tall crystal windows reflected bright sunlight beneath six rune-carved towers. White stone stairways climbed past silver-leaf trees and statues of famous past graduates who now led cities and royal offices.

A quiet pulse stirred behind my ribs.

I pressed two fingers lightly over my chest.

Beneath my shirt, hidden under layers of quiet mana concealment, my true Magic Circle slept in silence near my heart.

It did not blaze like Yellow or glow warmly like Red. It was darker than shadow, steady as law, and vast enough that even held down it made the air around me feel slightly off whenever my focus slipped. Inside it turned strange shapes—patterns too old, too complete, and too heavy for modern magic books to name.

Black Circle.

I had no intention of letting anyone see it. I had spent years learning how to stay small: how to hide my power, turn eyes away, and live quietly enough that nobody looked twice.

Six weeks earlier, that plan had stopped working.

During my final Lumeris district test, the Green mark on my wrist had lagged half a beat behind my heartbeat, leaving a dark bruise in the inspector's reading lens before snapping back to pale green. The clerk had blamed a cracked glass and moved on.

That same evening, an Aurelis transfer packet appeared inside my locked room.

My written test scores were copied on the first page. The second held one unsigned line:

**THE FOUNDATION REMEMBERS WHAT LUMERIS WAS TAUGHT TO FORGET. COME BEFORE THEY TEST YOU AGAIN.**

Staying meant facing a second check my disguise might not survive. Running away would prove Lumeris had uncovered something worth hunting. Aurelis gave me a clean, public reason to leave—and maybe, somewhere under its old stones, an answer to why the Black Circle had started waking up.

I did not trust the note. I trusted staying in the dark even less.

Magic flowed along my wrist in a whisper. The disguise settled neatly under my cuff. Anyone glancing at my sleeve now would see a normal **Green Circle crest**—a bit faint, a bit plain, the exact kind of mark people forget the moment they blink.

I took a breath and stepped through the gates.

The stares started right away.

Some students were too busy greeting friends or checking the newly posted mid-year rosters on floating boards to care. Others were already pretending not to look at anyone ranked below them.

No one walked beside me.

Within ten steps, enough eyes had shifted my way that the mood was clear. They knew the three facts before I reached the main stairs: transfer student, mid-year entry, Green Circle.

At a school like Aurelis, those three facts could turn into gossip before first bell. I kept walking.

Near the fountain to my right, two Blue Circle boys lowered their voices just enough to make sure I heard them. Farther along the path, Red and Blue crests flashed as students noticed the pale green glow at my cuff with amused disbelief. Halfway up the stone steps, the first-period bell tolled deep across the towers, cutting the courtyard chatter short as students hurried indoors. I walked inside without reacting. Casual cruelty worked best when you refused to give it a face.

The entrance hall opened around me like a grand cathedral built for noble mages.

A domed ceiling shimmered with glowing star maps. Black stone floors mirrored floating chandeliers. Twin staircases curved toward the upper class wings, and golden banners hung between the pillars bearing the school seal.

A glowing screen unfolded in the air before me.

**WELCOME, TRANSFER STUDENT: HARU AERLEN**
**YEAR TWO - SECTION THREE**
**HOMEROOM: EAST WING, THIRD FLOOR**

Instructor Selene: "Haru Aerlen?"

I turned.

A woman in a formal navy school coat walked up with a glowing tablet tucked under one arm. Her dark hair was tied back neatly, a thin **Red Circle** crest shining near her throat. Her face carried the brisk tiredness of someone who had already fixed three office mess-ups before breakfast.

Instructor Selene: "I'm Instructor Selene Varis. Student Affairs. You're the transfer from Lumeris District?"

Haru: "Yes."

Instructor Selene: "Come with me. Homeroom has already started."

I nodded and followed her into the east hallway.

The farther we walked, the clearer the school's hidden map became. Rooms for top classes sat closer to richer magic streams. Some halls hung only Red and Yellow graduate portraits. Even the walls knew who the academy had been built to remember.

In a study corner near the eastern stair, a Green Circle student raised a hand to point out a schedule mistake and got cut off mid-sentence by a Blue who did not even slow down to see who had spoken.

Selene noticed where I was looking.

Instructor Selene: "Aurelis rewards excellence."

I wondered how many people repeated that line without ever asking who got to decide what counted as excellence.

Haru: "I see."

She glanced at me.

Instructor Selene: "This school is not easy, Haru. Students here are driven, competitive, and mostly from powerful families. You may face pushback."

Haru: "I expected that."

A flicker of approval crossed her face.

Instructor Selene: "Your admission was unusual. Your Circle rank raised objections, but your written test scores were remarkable. Theory, combat rules, spell design, and old-script reading. Someone argued hard in your favor."

Haru: "Someone?"

Instructor Selene: "I wasn't told who."

The unsigned note came back to mind, sharper than ever. Someone had not just noticed me. They had opened a door and waited to see if I would walk through it.

Haru: "Did that recommendation come from the Foundation vault?"

Selene's step hitched for less than a breath.

Instructor Selene: "No vault access was listed in your file. Why ask?"

Haru: "My transfer reading list mentioned old records from before the current ranks. Especially the ones the Foundation hid."

It was a gamble sharp enough to test how closely the school watched old secrets. Selene paused, studying me with new caution before walking on again—though the question clearly stayed with her. Good. If the mystery sender had reached through Student Affairs, I wanted word to reach whoever was listening.

She stopped before a pair of double doors. Inside, class had already started.

Selene opened the doors and stepped aside.

The classroom inside was bright, with stepped rows of desks facing tall windows along one wall. At the front, Instructor Dren stood beside a floating spell chart. Talk faded the moment thirty pairs of eyes caught the pale green mark peeking beneath my cuff.

Instructor Dren: "The transfer? Haru Aerlen. Introduce yourself."

Haru: "I'm Haru Aerlen. I transferred in today. I'll do my best."

Small laughs broke out among the Red and Blue rows until Dren knocked his knuckles sharply against the desk.

Instructor Dren: "That's enough."

The room quieted down, though not with much grace.

Instructor Dren: "Back row. Open seat beside Aira."

As I walked up the aisle, I caught a folded paper ball an inch from my shoulder and let it drop to the floor anyway. No point making fast reflexes look obvious.

At the back sat a girl with warm brown eyes and chestnut hair tied loosely to one side. Her **Blue Circle** crest glowed on the inside of her wrist, and she looked up without the cold sizing-up most Blues wore.

That alone set her apart.

Aira: "Hi. I'm Aira Solen."

Haru: "Haru."

Aira: "Nice to meet you."

There was no teasing in it, no hidden trick. Just plain kindness that felt rare enough to put me on guard for half a second.

At the front, Instructor Dren went back to the lesson.

Instructor Dren: "As I was saying, magic society sorts human mana into four official Circle ranks."

The academy liked the word *official*. It made the system sound neat and fair.

Kids learned early that the color waking up near their heart changed how adults listened to them. Once their magic settled, society sorted everyone cleanly and called it natural.

Four practice disks dropped from the ceiling beneath the glowing crests. Dren didn't start with textbook words. He pointed to a Green Circle girl in the second row.

Instructor Dren: "One thread. Hold the disk steady."

She jumped slightly, then obeyed. Green light ran from her wrist to the nearest disk. It rose, wobbled once, and held still. Ten seconds. Twenty.

A system tag flashed into the air above her without anyone asking for it.

**SUPPORT TRACK / COMMON RANK**

Two students smirked.

The girl's jaw tightened. Her disk didn't shake.

Dren wiped the label away with one sharp sweep of his hand.

Instructor Dren: "System labels. Not mine."

That got him more attention than any textbook speech would have.

Instructor Dren: "Green Circle makes up around sixty percent of people. Smallest mana pool, tightest power limit. Also the backbone of city defense shields, road repairs, food seals, travel lines, and every daily tool people only notice when it stops working."

The girl reached thirty seconds.

Instructor Dren: "Release."

Her disk settled cleanly without cracking.

A blond boy near the front raised his hand and stood before Dren even called on him.

Aira leaned in beside me, speaking just low enough not to carry.

Aira: "Leon Varek. He never misses a chance to look impressive."

Instructor Dren: "Mr. Varek. Same exercise."

Blue mana struck the second disk. It jumped twice as high as the Green one, bright enough to cast water-blue shadows across the front desks. Several students nodded with approval.

At twenty-two seconds, one side dipped.

Leon tried to fix it too fast. The disk spun sideways.

Dren caught it before it hit the floor.

Instructor Dren: "Blue Circle. About thirty percent. Larger mana pools, wider daily uses, and most of the skilled working mages."

He floated the disk back into place.

Instructor Dren: "Having more power is not the same as having control."

Leon sat back down with a very stiff spine.

A Red Circle student lifted three disks at once, guiding her magic smoothly around the test runes instead of fighting them.

Instructor Dren: "Red. Roughly nine percent. Deep reserves, strong natural talent, quick learners."

The floating system added its own tag:

**LEADERSHIP FIT: HIGH**

The Red student smiled before she could stop herself.

Dren looked straight at the glowing letters.

Instructor Dren: "A label repeated often enough starts deciding who gets hired. Don't mistake either one for good character."

Nobody smiled after that.

The Yellow disk stayed untouched. It didn't need a volunteer to shift the mood in the room. Its glowing crest spilled gold light over the front rows, and students sat straighter under a rank none of them could match.

Instructor Dren: "Yellow Circle. Roughly one percent. Highest official rank in the modern system. Most schools go years without teaching even one."

He sent the disks away.

The lesson never came out and said Green meant useless. It just arranged the room so everyone knew whose mistakes got patience and whose success had to stay quiet.

Above Dren, the four circles spun slowly—Green, Blue, Red, and Yellow washing their approved colors across thirty faces.

The unsigned note had said the Foundation remembered what Lumeris was taught to forget. Asking straight out about Black Circle would be reckless, yet checking if the school lessons left out old history might show what kind of trap I had walked into.

I raised my hand. Just doing that surprised half the room.

Instructor Dren: "Yes, transfer student?"

I kept my face calm.

Haru: "Are those four ranks really all there are? My transfer packet mentioned older circles from before the current chart."

Aira turned toward me so fast her pen scratched off her paper.

Two students blinked. Someone near the front silently mouthed *older circles* as if checking whether the words sounded silly. Leon laughed first.

Leon: "You mean Black Circle?"

Enough students joined in that laughter rolled across the whole classroom in one wave.

Student: "What is this, a bedtime story?"

Student: "I knew Greens had strange ideas, but that's embarrassing."

Leon: "Are we studying kids' fairy tales now?"

Red Student: "You might as well ask about star dragons and moon gods."

Aira didn't laugh. She watched me instead, curious and wide awake.

Instructor Dren raised one hand, and the noise died down. His face showed no mocking—just a teacher deciding how seriously to treat a surprise question.

Instructor Dren: "In official magic rules, those four groups cover every modern rank. Since Mr. Varek used the old street name, no—there is no recognized Black Circle."

He turned slightly and pulled up a second chart beside the four crests: broken script pieces, torn drawings, cracked rings.

Instructor Dren: "In very old records, though, scattered notes mention rare or strange magic circles outside the usual four. Most scholars call them myths, religious symbols, or copying mistakes. Only a tiny number of researchers ever guessed a higher rank might have existed."

The broken images faded and snapped back together.

Instructor Dren: "There is no real proof in history that any human has ever carried such a circle. In modern magic, Black Circle is not treated as real."

Haru: "So it's impossible?"

Dren crossed his arms.

Instructor Dren: "In magic, 'impossible' is often just a temporary word. Until something can be seen, measured, and repeated, it stays a guess. Your job here is to master what is real, not chase legends that flatter your imagination."

His eyes swept the room before resting briefly on Leon's row.

Instructor Dren: "Mastering what is real means learning limits, too. Raw power without discipline is not skill. It's just loud noise wearing a fancy crest."

The class relaxed again. Someone chuckled under their breath.

Leon: "There you have it."

Student: "Imagine transferring in and asking about fairy tales."

Aira glanced between me and the front of the room.

Aira: "Bold first-day question."

Haru: "The reading list was unclear."

Her eyes stayed on me a beat longer than small talk needed.

Aira: "Somehow, I don't think a reading list is why you asked."

I didn't answer.

At the front, Dren closed the chart and moved on.

Instructor Dren: "Remember this. Circle rank shows potential, not a guaranteed win. A stronger Circle can still lose to a weaker one with better control, timing, or judgment. Raw power matters. It isn't everything."

That much, at least, was true.

Beside me, Aira went back to her notes. I left my notebook mostly blank and listened.

The rest of the class covered stacking spells cleanly, keeping mana steady under stress, and how far each rank could strengthen the body in a fight. It was solid teaching, well explained, and full of hidden gaps nobody in the room knew were there.

I spotted every gap—not because I was smarter than anyone else, but because I had spent my whole life living outside the chart they thought explained the world.

When the bell rang, chatter came back right away.

Aira turned to me first.

Aira: "If you need help finding the training hall, I can show you."

Before I could answer, a shadow fell across our desks.

Leon Varek stood there wearing a smile polished enough to look friendly to anyone who wanted things simple.

Leon: "Aira, we're still meeting at the west courtyard before combat class."

Aira: "I remember."

His eyes shifted to me.

Leon: "And you must be in our test group."

Haru: "I am."

He nodded as if hearing bad news.

Leon: "Try not to do anything reckless. Some drills aren't built for weak circles. I'd hate for your first day to turn into a warning story."

Haru: "I'll be careful."

He looked slightly let down that I hadn't taken the bait.

Aira: "Leon."

Leon: "What? New students should know the risks."

He straightened and walked off.

Aira: "He does that whenever he wants an audience."

Haru: "He makes good use of it."

That got a small, real laugh out of her. For some reason, it stayed with me longer than Leon's warning.

Aira didn't bother heading out toward the west courtyard. She tossed a look toward the side hall instead.

Aira: "He says 'we' when he means 'stand around while my friends talk.' Come on—I'll show you the shortcut to the eastern training hall."

By the time the next bell echoed down the corridor, our class was moving toward the eastern training building in neat groups. Red Circles walked like people used to being admired without even asking. Blues clustered in loud debates. Greens hugged the walls out of habit.

Ahead of us, a second-year in a green-trimmed coat stepped aside for a Red group and nearly bumped into a sweeping bot rather than slow them down.

Aira walked beside me anyway. Not close enough to make a scene, yet close enough that people noticed. That alone seemed to annoy Leon, whose group had just merged onto the main hall path ahead of us and who kept glancing back.

The training building was much larger than the classroom wing, built from white stone woven with silver mana-wires and covered wall to wall with steady-field runes. Inside opened a wide round arena circled by stepped seating and a glowing board overhead.

It was a room built for talent to be cheered—and for weakness to be put on display.

Students filed into the seats while Instructor Dren walked to the center of the stone floor, his boots clicking once against the polished tiles.

**PRACTICE TEST: MAGIC CONTROL AND BATTLE REACTION**

Dren crossed his arms.

Instructor Dren: "Listen closely. This isn't a ranking tournament or a chance to settle grudges. Today's goal is calm control under pressure, reaction speed, smart choices, and steady power use."

He waved his hand, and new lines appeared beneath the floating title.

Instructor Dren: "You'll fight one-on-one practice matches. Winning is not just about hitting hard. You earn points for smart timing, accuracy, calm nerves, holding back from cheap harm, and using the ring rules well."

Red-Haired Student: "So wiping your rival out right away gets you docked."

Instructor Dren: "Yes—especially if wiping them out calls for hospital magic."

A few students laughed.

Instructor Dren: "Trying to kill gets you kicked out right away and sent to the review board. Excessive force lowers your score. Losing your temper lowers your score. If you have the skill to win cleanly and choose chaos instead, I'll assume your schooling failed somewhere."

That last line carried more weight than any written rule.

Beside me, Aira shifted slightly.

Aira: "He talks like that a lot."

Haru: "Does it work?"

Aira: "Not as much as he hopes."

The corner of my mouth tugged upward before I caught it.

Dren raised one hand, and a match board lit up above the ring. Pairings scrolled into place. Students reacted with quiet relief, groans, or poorly hidden dread. Friends avoided eye contact. Rivals sat up straight. Then my name popped right into the opening slot.

**HARU AERLEN vs LEON VAREK**

The mood in the hall flipped right away.

A buzz ran through the stands. Several students laughed without trying to hide it. Others traded knowing looks, already betting how many seconds I would last.

Leon laid one hand over his chest in fake worry.

Leon: "Sir, I'm not sure this is fair."

Instructor Dren: "For who?"

That drew louder laughs.

Leon: "A Blue Circle against a first-day Green transfer might leave the wrong idea."

Instructor Dren: "It will. Step into the ring, not into your ego."

Aira turned toward me at once, real worry clear on her face rather than pity.

Aira: "You don't have to prove anything."

Haru: "I don't plan to."

Aira: "Leon loves making people look foolish in front of a crowd."

Haru: "I noticed."

Aira: "He's fast, but he shows off. Stay calm and force him to pick tight angles—you might hold on longer than he expects."

Hold on longer. A fair guess, given what she thought I was.

Haru: "Thank you."

Surprise flickered across her face—a quick lift of her brows followed by a small nod, as if plain gratitude wasn't the answer she expected from me either.

Leon dropped from the platform into the ring with practiced ease. Blue mana curled around his wrists like water ribbons catching sunlight. He worked the crowd without shouting—just standing like someone used to owning every eye in the room.

I walked down to the stone floor across from him.

A shield dome sealed overhead with a low hum. Right away, I felt the eyes of the stands pressing down. The Greens watched quietly. The Blues leaned forward. The Reds watched with cool boredom. Aira leaned over the rail with tight focus.

Instructor Dren stepped outside the barrier line and raised one hand. Three balance pads hummed alight on my half of the floor, and three more across the ring behind Leon.

Instructor Dren: "The match starts on my mark. Score three hits on your opponent's side pads, force them to quit, or knock them out to win. Follow the rules."

Leon smiled across the ring.

Leon: "You can tap out before we begin. Nobody will blame you."

Haru: "I'm fine."

His smile sharpened.

Dren dropped his hand.

Instructor Dren: "Begin."

Leon moved first.

A tight jet of water mana shot straight at me—fast enough to force a dodge, light enough to avoid real harm. I stepped half a pace left. The jet sliced past my ribs and splashed into mist against the far wall.

Leon didn't slow down. A second jet followed right behind it, then a third curving from my blind side to box in my feet. Clean timing. Better than most boys his age.

I stepped back twice and turned. The second strike grazed my sleeve. The third missed my shoulder by inches.

Low murmurs rolled through the stands. They had expected me to panic. Leon's eyes narrowed.

He snapped both wrists upward, and six thin water pillars shot out of the floor tiles around me in a tight ring. All six leaned inward together, closing off my sides to force a clumsy jump.

Smart move against someone trying to muscle out.

My shoulders tightened as the walls closed in. Half a heartbeat before they snapped shut, I slipped sideways through the narrowest gap.

Startled gasps broke out from the benches. Leon clicked his tongue.

Leon: "Not bad. For a Green."

A few chuckles answered him. He pulled in more mana.

Five sharp, ice-clear water spears formed above his shoulders, spinning slowly with heavy pressure. Far sharper than his opening shots—hard enough to bruise or crack bone. He wanted me flat on the floor in front of everyone.

Leon: "Last chance. Quit gracefully."

I looked at him across the ring. Behind the polish stood a boy checking whether the crowd still belonged to him. That alone would make him careless.

Haru: "I'd rather keep going."

The spears fired.

I raised one hand and threw up a thin, uneven mana shield—the exact kind of shaky wall a Green Circle student might summon under stress. The first spear broke across its front. The second slid off the angled edge. The third tore past my collar as I shifted my weight, letting the last two smash harmlessly into the barrier behind me.

Gasps rose across the stands. No ordinary Green Circle could read a trained Blue that cleanly.

Leon heard the shift in the room and hated it. His smile vanished.

Leon: "Fine."

The air inside the barrier turned chilly as he pulled moisture straight out of the room around him. Water gathered around his arms in spinning bands, thicker and colder than before.

Instructor Dren called from outside the ward line.

Instructor Dren: "Varek. Watch your output."

Leon set his jaw and kept pushing anyway.

Three whip-like water lashes snapped toward me from three sides: left, right, overhead. I threw myself back across the tiles. One lash carved a shallow groove right through the stone where my boot had just stood. Another clipped my left forearm before I cleared the swing.

Sharp pain shot up my arm—real enough to make my fingers clench.

I landed near one of the lit balance pads defending my half of the floor. Leon spotted it immediately and switched angles mid-step, slamming a focused pulse of water straight through my guard into the stone beneath my boots.

I pushed off a split second too late. The tile cracked under impact and the pad lit up bright blue.

**POINT: VAREK**

The overhead board flashed.

Cheers burst from a cluster of Blue Circle students. One Red near the front clapped twice, bored and pleased. Down on the lower benches, the Greens stared at the glowing score instead of Leon, as if that single point proved a rule they had been taught for years.

Leon: "There it is."

He thought the fight had snapped back to how it was supposed to go. I let him keep believing it.

We circled each other. He pressed harder now, dropping neat form for pure speed. Flat blades of water zipped across the ring while low mist crept across the stone to hide my footing. He wanted to force a mistake before anyone finished thinking about the first exchange.

I blocked one cut with a tight rush of shield magic, sidestepped the next, and let the third shove me backward.

I could have ended the match in three seconds. Keeping it close took way more effort. Pretending to be weak took far more focus than showing strength.

Pain was simple. Pain showed up, stung, and faded. Hiding it smoothly was the real work—letting my shoulders hunch just enough to look cornered, letting small laughs travel across the hall without snapping back, keeping my breath tight so nobody mistook calm for danger.

It never felt natural. Only practiced.

Underneath the habit, something older lifted its head. Not the Black Circle. Me. The part that wanted, just once, to stop tiptoeing for people who treated caution like proof I belonged under their boots. The part that wanted the stands to go dead silent for the right reason.

I swallowed that feeling down until my throat hurt.

Leon rushed inside close range, shaping water along his forearm into a hard blade. He swung cleanly.

I blocked a beat late and let the hit skid me back across the stone.

Student: "There!"

Student: "Now he's done."

Leon had leaned too far forward. His weight hung past his front foot, leaving his guard open for a split second while his next spell was already building before he got his balance back. Small flaws. Common ones.

I moved—not fast enough to look strange, just faster than his pride expected.

I stepped inside his swing, caught his wrist, and tapped two fingers against the magic spot beside his elbow. One tiny pulse. Just enough to cut the flow running down his main arm.

His water blade burst into spray. Leon's eyes went wide.

I spun past his shoulder onto his half of the floor and pressed my palm flat on the nearest of his balance pads while he was still stumbling. The pad flashed bright.

**POINT: AERLEN**

The entire hall drew one sharp breath and forgot to let it out.

A Green Circle had scored on a Blue through pure timing—no luck, no wild burst of magic to blame it on.

Leon whipped around, stunned. Voices broke across the stands in jagged pieces.

Student: "What?"

Student: "How did he—"

Student: "Did Leon just lose a point?"

Student: "No, he cut off the spell flow—"

Student: "Can Greens even do that?"

On the lower bench, a Green Circle boy smiled before remembering to hide it. A Blue beside him glared on Leon's behalf.

Up in the seats, Aira was already standing, all her worry gone, replaced by bright, serious focus.

Leon's face burned dark red with shame.

Leon: "Fluke."

I stayed quiet. That only made it worse.

Water roared around him in a wild spiral and crashed forward in a wide wave across the floor. Heavy mist blew upward, blinding half the ring as students leaned back from the humming shield wall.

Instructor Dren: "Varek! Drop your power!"

Leon pushed harder. He wasn't fighting for points anymore. He was fighting to drown out the sound of the room.

Through the fog, I tracked him easily by his leaking mana. His breath had gone ragged, his spell rhythm broken by anger. Six water blades formed around him and fired in uneven waves: two high, two chest-level, two low.

I slowed my breath and stopped pretending to stumble.

Step left. Pivot. Drop. Turn.

My palm brushed the first blade and knocked it off-line. The second hissed past my ear. The third shattered on the stone. I broke the fourth with a sharp tap hidden inside a clumsy-looking block, and slipped straight past as the last two missed completely.

Noise in the hall thinned out until only water hissing against the barrier wall remained.

Leon saw me walk out of the mist untouched, and for the first time fear flickered behind his anger. Fear of not knowing what was standing across from him.

He pulled magic way too fast after that. Too much power, too quickly, forced through too tight a spin.

The knot of pressure above his right wrist swelled, stopped acting like an attack, and locked into a dangerous inward spin.

I felt the backfire coming a breath before anyone else did. So did Dren.

Instructor Dren: "Stop casting!"

Leon tried to force the spell back into shape anyway. A tight, violently shaking ball of wild water mana bucked above his wrist. If it blew inward that close, his own Blue Circle would shred the magic channels in his arm.

The shield dome buzzed tight. Students jerked back from the rail.

I felt the Black Circle stir behind my ribs. Three bad choices, none of them clean: show too much power and save him effortlessly, hold back and let his pride ruin his arm, or step in and take the smaller hit myself.

Mercy was never gentle in moments like that. It was just ugly math with no clean out.

I stepped straight into the edge of the collapsing spell.

Leon's eyes snapped to mine, anger cracking wide open into plain terror.

The spinning edge tore my left sleeve open above the wrist and sent a bright, stinging shock straight up my arm. While every eye in the stands flinched at the hit, I raised my hand and slipped a razor-thin canceling tap inside plain shield magic. Nowhere near real Black Circle strength—just clean enough to keep the spell from exploding.

The unstable ball of water folded inward with a dull, airless thump.

No blast. No shockwave. No broken bones. The trapped water simply dropped around us like warm rain.

My fingers went numb to the knuckles. Beneath my torn left sleeve, my fake Green mark flickered half a beat behind my pulse. I lowered my arm before anyone caught the thin black gleam fading under my skin.

High on the upper balcony, a tall student in an elite cloak went completely still. Pale-gold hair caught the arena lights, a bright **Yellow Circle** glowing at her collarbone as she started to stand before the rest of the hall even realized Leon was safe.

Outside the barrier, Dren's eyes narrowed. He had seen enough to know that ending didn't come from any ordinary Green transfer.

Leon stared at his unharmed hand as if his own magic had become a stranger.

Voices crashed back into the room all at once.

Student: "What just happened?"

Student: "Did Varek cancel his own spell?"

Student: "No, the transfer touched it—"

Student: "A first-day Green?"

Student: "That kind of control makes no sense—"

Leon stumbled back a step, wet hair plastered to his forehead.

Dren walked straight through the dropping shield wall and tapped his slate to lock the ring's damaged stone and tripped ward sensors.

Instructor Dren: "Match over. Remaining matches move to Thursday once the safety log and ward reset clear."

The board overhead flashed white.

**ASSESSMENT PAUSED — WARD CHECK**

Complaints rolled down from the benches.

Student: "What? Today's whole block?"

Student: "Sir, that's it?"

Student: "He was about to lose!"

Student: "No, Leon would've won—"

Student: "Would he?"

Dren ignored all of them. He stopped in front of Leon first.

Instructor Dren: "You lost your temper, passed safe power limits, and ignored a direct order."

Leon opened his mouth, then shut it.

Dren turned to me.

Instructor Dren: "And you showed sharp control and timing."

Haru: "Thank you, sir."

His eyes stayed on me a beat longer than praise needed, glancing briefly down at my torn sleeve before he faced the crowd.

Instructor Dren: "Remember this lesson clearly. Rank matters. It does not decide everything. Any student who treats natural talent as an excuse to skip discipline will get embarrassed sooner or later."

Several Greens sat up a touch taller. Several Blues looked sour. Above us, the Yellow Circle student on the balcony had already turned toward the exit stairs.

For the class, the match was over. For me, a real problem had just begun. Attention had weight, and I could already feel it settling across my shoulders.

As the class filed out onto the floor, Leon slammed his shoulder into mine hard enough to make sure I knew he meant it.

Leon: "You planned that."

Haru: "No. You rushed."

He stopped and half-turned, anger and disbelief colliding across his face for one second before he stormed out.

Aira reached me a moment later.

Aira: "That wasn't normal."

Haru: "For a Green Circle?"

Aira: "For anyone on day one."

There was no anger in her voice—only surprise mixed with the start of real trust. I had no idea what to do with either one.

Haru: "It was just timing."

She held my eyes, testing the edge of the answer I wouldn't give.

Aira: "Maybe. I still think there's a lot more to you than timing, Haru."

Before I could answer, someone new stepped onto the upper balcony.

The whole hall changed without anyone saying why. Talk cut off mid-word. Students stood straighter. Even Leon froze near the door.

At the top of the stairs stood a girl in the formal dark cloak reserved for top student council officers. Her posture was razor-clean, her face unreadable, and the magic around her carried a bright, heavy pressure you could feel against your skin from across the floor.

A high-grade **Red Circle** crest glowed at her collarbone.

She looked down at Dren, then Leon, then locked her eyes squarely onto mine. Not disgust. Pure calculation.

Instructor Dren: "Student Council President."

So that was who ran the student council. I hadn't seen her face before, though even my transfer papers and hallway whispers warned that Aurelis's council didn't hang party banners—they handled rule punishments, crisis hearings, duel passes, and enough real power to make noble heirs mind their manners in public.

She walked down the stairs without hurrying.

Council President: "Excuse the interruption. An emergency ward alert reached the council desk from this hall."

Her voice was calm, smooth, and completely guarded.

Instructor Dren: "The matter is settled."

Council President: "Settled, then. Still, it looks like this year's transfer student already triggers safety alarms on day one."

Nearby students traded quick glances. I kept my face blank.

She studied me one beat longer, then offered a thin smile—the look of someone who had just found a loose thread and planned to keep pulling until the seam ripped open.

Council President: "I look forward to seeing more."

She turned and left at the same steady pace, leaving half the hall pretending not to watch her climb back up the stairs.

Aira: "That's bad."

Haru: "Who is she?"

Aira stared at me as if I had just asked the name of a hurricane everyone else knew to board windows for.

Aira: "Rhea Valmont. Red Circle. Once the council president takes an interest in you, even school paperwork grows teeth."

I looked back toward the empty steps where Rhea had gone. My first day at Aurelis wasn't even over yet, and keeping a low profile had already turned into a live show.

## Interlude: The Wrong Kindness

The west hallway outside the training hall emptied out in waves.

First the loudest students poured past, carrying the match away in messy pieces.

*A Green scored on Leon. Leon lost control. Dren looked furious. Elira Vale stood up. No, she didn't. Yes, she did. The transfer did something weird at the end.*

By the time I reached the side door, the afternoon had already split into four different stories. Confusion was helpful. Confusion made hiding easier.

I stopped under a narrow stone arch where late sun fell across the floor in pale gold bars. My left sleeve hung ripped above the wrist from Leon's collapsing spell, showing more of the fake Green Circle than I liked. I tried to fold the torn cuff down. Badly.

My fingers were still numb from keeping the blast off Leon's arm. That bothered me more than the sting.

Haru: "You can come out."

The air behind the pillar shifted, and the Yellow Circle girl from the upper balcony stepped into the sunlight. The one passing students had called Elira Vale.

Up close, her calm grace felt quieter than her rank, and she didn't even look embarrassed to be caught waiting. Embarrassment would have made this easier.

Elira: "I was trying to decide if meeting you here would feel like an ambush."

Haru: "And?"

Elira: "I decided it probably would."

I looked at her.

Elira: "Then I waited anyway."

That pulled a short breath out of me, closer to a laugh than I wanted.

In one hand she held a folded strip of white healer cloth—clean and soft enough that most people would save it for a real cut instead of a ripped sleeve. She followed my eyes.

Elira: "Your arm."

Haru: "It's fine."

Elira: "Your sleeve disagrees."

Haru: "Cloth is dramatic."

Her mouth softened just a fraction.

Elira: "May I?"

That single polite question was the trap. If she had stepped right in, I could have backed up. If she had ordered me to hold still, I could have disliked her cleanly. Instead she asked, making saying no feel heavier than it should have.

Haru: "It doesn't need a bandage."

Elira: "I know."

Haru: "Then why offer?"

Some warmth faded from her face.

Elira: "Because something can be unnecessary and still kind."

I should have said thank you and walked away. Instead I misread the motive behind her words. A Yellow Circle star offering help to a Green transfer right after a flashy fight—generous, noble, the exact kind of gesture people praised when stories spread. The kind of kindness that could turn me into a prop in someone else's good name.

Haru: "You don't have to prove anything."

Her hands stilled.

Elira: "Prove?"

I knew right away I had used the wrong word. Knowing didn't take it back.

Haru: "People watch."

Elira glanced down the empty hall, then back at me.

Elira: "No one is here."

Haru: "People don't need to be in the room to twist what happened."

Her expression quieted even more.

Elira: "Is that what you think I'm doing?"

Haru: "I don't know what you're doing."

Honest in the worst possible direction.

Elira folded the white cloth once between her fingers.

Elira: "I was trying to hand you something clean before you walked back into a school full of people deciding what your ripped sleeve means."

I had no answer for that.

She held the cloth out anyway, neither stepping closer nor pulling back.

Elira: "You can take it without agreeing to anything else."

That sentence had no business working on me. I reached out and took the cloth.

Our fingers didn't brush. She made sure of it. Somehow the care in keeping that space unsettled me more than the gift itself.

Haru: "Thank you."

Elira nodded. For half a second I thought that might smooth over the scrape.

Elira: "You're very careful about not owing anyone."

I looked up too fast. Her golden eyes were steady, without a hint of smugness. She hadn't said it to win a point.

Haru: "Owing people gets messy."

Elira: "So does turning down every kindness before it gets a chance to be one."

No blame sat in her voice, which was why the line stuck right under my ribs. She turned toward the hall.

I had three polite, safe things I could have said. I used none of them. I stood there clutching the white cloth in my numb fingers and watched her walk back toward the sunlit hall.

At the corner, she paused without looking back.

Elira: "I didn't stand up because you scored on Leon."

My fingers tightened around the fabric.

Elira: "I stood up because you looked hurt and pretended you weren't."

Then she was gone.

Quiet settled back under the arch. After she turned the corner, I tied the cloth loosely around my ripped sleeve for the walk back to the dorm. Unnecessary. Still helpful. That made it worse.

---

The rumors beat the morning bell on my second day.

That was the problem with elite schools: nothing stayed quiet overnight. By the time I crossed the main courtyard of **Aurelis Arcane Academy**, students were already retelling yesterday's test as if it had been a tournament duel instead of a class exercise.

Some stories were just stretched. Others turned straight into fiction.

Student: "He broke Leon's spell using Green Circle power."

Student: "No, Leon lost control on his own."

Student: "I heard Dren stopped it because the transfer was hiding a magic tool."

Student: "Someone said his mana moved strangely."

Student: "Strangely how?"

Student: "No clue. That's what makes it weird."

I walked past them with my bag over one shoulder at a steady pace. Starving gossip was the easiest way to let it die, though rumors had an annoying habit of feeding themselves.

Near the center stairs, two Blue Circle boys went quiet as I walked up. One stared straight at the pale green mark under my left cuff, then up at my face.

Student 1: "That's him."

Student 2: "He still looks normal."

Student 1: "That's the creepy part."

I kept walking.

Silver-leaf trees along the paths glittered in early light while practice runes spun high above the towers. Yesterday I had been invisible. Today I was a mystery—and mysteries drew trouble faster than insults did.

Inside the main hall, Aira stood near a glowing board holding two paper cups from the school cafe. She caught sight of me before I could slip down another hallway and held one up.

Aira: "I guessed you'd walk this way."

Haru: "You planned a lucky meeting?"

Aira smiled.

Aira: "I call it 'efficient friendliness.'"

She held the cup out.

Aira: "Tea. I didn't know what you drink, so I picked the lowest-risk option."

I looked from the cup to her face.

Haru: "Thank you."

Aira: "You're welcome."

She let a beat pass before falling into step beside me.

Aira: "Also, before the day gets worse, half the school has decided you're either secretly a genius or deeply suspicious."

Haru: "Only half?"

That made her laugh.

Aira: "You're getting better at this."

Haru: "At making trouble?"

Aira: "At sounding like you're joking."

Walking beside her toward the east wing drew fresh stares. Aira brushed them off easily. I wasn't sure I ever would. She took a sip of tea and lowered her voice.

Aira: "Leon is furious, by the way."

Haru: "I figured."

Aira: "He's pretending he doesn't care, which on Leon is somehow louder than regular anger."

Haru: "Inconvenient."

Aira: "For you, yeah. For me, it's Tuesday."

We turned into the east hallway just as another wave of whispers rolled through the crowd. This time the tone shifted—softer, sharper, mixed with real awe.

I felt her mana before I saw her. Warm, bright, steady as sunlight pressing through tall glass.

Students ahead stepped sideways almost without thinking, clearing the center of the stone walkway. Blue Circles stood straighter. Red Circles turned with polite nods. Even two teachers paused.

Then she walked into view.

In the quiet west hallway yesterday, she had simply been a tall girl with pale-gold hair and a strip of white healer cloth. Here, with fifty students parting around her, I saw what Aurelis had made of her. Her uniform looked almost ceremonial under her dark officer cloak, and at her collarbone shone an unmistakable **Yellow Circle**. Open. Unhidden. Unsoftened for anyone's comfort.

The hallway went hush as she passed.

Student 3: "It's her."

Student 4: "Lady Elira."

Student 5: "Did she really stand up during Section Three's match yesterday?"

Aira followed my gaze and leaned in closer.

Aira: "That's **Elira Vale**."

I kept yesterday's hallway—and Elira's cloth, neatly refolded in my coat pocket after I stitched my cuff back together last night—to myself.

Haru: "I guessed she was a big deal."

Aira shot me a flat look.

Aira: "That might be the biggest understatement anyone has ever made about Elira Vale."

Haru: "Should I know more?"

Aira: "Yellow Circle. One of the youngest high-rank light mages in the country. First in spell theory, first in control, and somehow still nice enough to make people feel guilty for acting awful in front of her."

Haru: "So everyone looks up to her."

Aira: "Looks up to her is putting it lightly."

Elira walked down the hall surrounded not by followers, just empty space. Nobody crowded her. Unlike the Reds who wore status like a shiny badge, she looked faintly tired of all the room people gave her.

Then her eyes moved across the crowd and landed straight on me.

Just for a second. Not cold disgust or idle staring—just quiet recognition, the calm look of someone remembering an unfinished talk. Her eyes flicked once toward my mended left sleeve, narrowed by a hair, and then she walked on.

The hallway seemed to breathe out all at once.

Aira turned to me with mock seriousness.

Aira: "If you say you missed that, I'll feel insulted on behalf of human eyes."

Haru: "She looked over."

Aira: "Yes."

She pointed her tea cup at me like a witness showing proof.

Haru: "People do that here."

Aira stared at me.

Aira: "You still don't get how this school works."

Haru: "I'm learning."

Aira: "Rule one: Yellow Circle students don't pick Green Circle transfers out of a packed hallway."

Haru: "Maybe she was looking past me."

Aira: "Haru."

Haru: "Fine. She looked at me."

Aira crossed one arm over her waist, balancing her cup.

Aira: "Good. Progress."

Homeroom felt tighter when we walked in a minute later. Yesterday's curiosity had hardened overnight into sharp staring.

Leon was already in his seat, talking to two Blue Circle boys in a voice low enough to sound important and loud enough for others to hear. When I walked in with Aira, the corner of his mouth pulled tight.

Leon: "Impressive. One lucky test and you already have an escort."

Aira: "Morning to you too, Leon."

Leon: "I'm just being polite."

Aira: "Then I'd hate to hear you rude."

I sat down without answering, which bothered him twice as much.

Instructor Dren walked in moments later and launched straight into multi-layer shield lessons without bringing up yesterday once. That didn't stop half the class from sneaking looks at the back row.

Partway through class, a faint prickle brushed the edge of my senses. Someone watching—not from inside the room, from somewhere higher up. When I glanced out the tall windows, nothing stood outside except bright morning clouds. Still, the feeling hung there until the bell rang.

---

Lunch at Aurelis felt less like a meal and more like a ranking contest dressed up with silverware.

The dining hall rose in wide steps under tall windows looking down over the gardens. Students sat grouped by circle rank, family name, and confidence. Even which corner you picked told the room where you thought you belonged.

Aira and I had just set our trays down at a quiet side table when the hall fell hush again. People shifted their chairs without even needing to be asked.

I didn't need to look up to know who had just walked in.

Elira Vale moved between the tables with easy calm, sunlight catching the warm gold edges of her hair near the glass. Up close, Yellow Circle magic carried a steady weight that made weaker streams flicker around it like candle flames near an open window.

She stopped right beside our table. Half the dining hall forgot to chew.

Her hand paused on the empty chair back for half a breath while her eyes swept the watching crowd, measuring every stare before turning back to us.

Elira: "May I sit here?"

Aira nearly dropped her fork.

Aira: "You're asking us?"

Elira's mouth curved faintly.

Elira: "Seemed polite."

Aira looked at me, looked at Elira, and clearly decided to save her panic for after class.

Aira: "Yes. Of course. Please."

Elira took the seat across from us. Around us, fifty talks started back up at half volume while everyone pretended not to listen.

Aira found her voice first.

Aira: "I'm Aira Solen."

Elira: "I know."

Aira blinked.

Elira turned her calm golden eyes onto me.

Elira: "And you're Haru Aerlen."

Haru: "Yes."

She gave no hint in front of Aira that we had already talked in the west hallway, yet her steady eyes still carried the quiet weight of yesterday's challenge.

Elira: "You asked an interesting question in class yesterday."

Aira stared at me.

Aira: "Your homeroom question reached the Yellow Circle lunch crowd?"

Haru: "Looks like it."

Elira's expression softened slightly.

Elira: "Questions are rarely the problem. Fear of them usually is."

Not exactly normal academy talk.

Haru: "Do you really think history left chapters out?" I asked quietly under the clatter of trays.

Even at half-voice, two students at the next table nearly choked leaning in to catch every word.

Elira glanced down at her cup, then back up at me.

Elira: "I think magic history is missing chapters. And I think most people are happy calling anything fake if it threatens a system that works for them."

Aira's fork froze halfway to her tray.

Aira: "Not the answer I expected."

Elira: "Most people expect Yellow Circles to defend the system."

Aira: "Don't you?"

Elira smiled, though a quiet shadow sat behind it.

Elira: "The system defends itself well enough."

For a short moment none of us spoke, and the clatter of trays and forks faded until our table felt like its own quiet island.

Elira folded her hands lightly beside her tray.

Elira: "I also wanted to check on your arm after yesterday's assessment."

Aira spun toward me so fast her chair scraped the stone.

Aira: "Hold on—why did Elira Vale notice your arm?"

Across the hall, several tables leaned closer. I kept my cuff steady on the table edge.

Haru: "Just a torn sleeve from Leon's spell."

Elira: "You held your ground cleaner than most second-years would."

Haru: "Luck and timing."

This time her smile was real—not grand or polite, just warm. It changed her whole face, and for one awkward second I forgot what to say back.

Aira caught it right away, glancing between us like someone realizing her peaceful semester had just sprung a leak.

Elira set her cup down.

Elira: "There's an advanced practice showcase this afternoon in the upper training gardens. My group was asked to help."

Aira: "Your group?"

Elira: "High-tier elemental control."

Aira made a tiny sound that clearly meant *that is way above our level*.

Elira looked straight at me.

Elira: "You should come watch, Haru."

No pressure in her voice. Just plain honesty, which made saying no harder.

Haru: "Why?"

Elira: "Because I think you notice things other people miss."

Aira propped her chin on one hand, fully taking over her new job as commentator on my bad choices.

Aira: "He does that."

Haru: "Aira."

Aira: "I'm helping."

Amusement lit Elira's eyes.

Elira: "Good. Then maybe both of you should come."

Aira: "Now I definitely have to."

---

The upper training gardens climbed up wide stone decks looking out over the western cliffs. Neat trees lined the paths, and practice courts sat surrounded by flowerbeds, crystal pillars, and carved rings built to hold heavy high-rank magic.

Older Red and Blue students already lined the viewing rail when Aira and I got there, with a few teachers standing along the edges in case a spell got out of hand.

At the center of the deck, four top students took their spots: two high-grade Reds, one skilled Blue, and Elira.

Even standing still, she held the whole field steady. Afternoon sun turned the gold of her crest bright white at the edges. When she raised one hand, the air across the deck answered—a quiet rise in pressure, then a smooth hum from the stone pillars.

Instructor Maren spoke to the crowd from the side.

Instructor Maren: "Today's drill tests layering different elements together under uneven magic pressure. Watch their balance, hand shifts, and control limits."

The Reds went first, braiding fire and wind into crossing rings while the Blue raised water walls to catch any spillover. Sharp, well-trained casting.

Then Elira stepped into the center spot.

Light bloomed—never harsh, just bright and clear. Golden-white threads spun out from her fingertips like silk, weaving between the other three spells without shoving them aside. She didn't crush the setup; she tuned it, steadying weak spots, smoothing out shakes, and making everyone else's work look stronger just by touching the edges.

The entire deck went dead silent.

Aira whispered without looking away.

Aira: "Every time I watch her cast, it feels unfair."

Haru: "She has amazing control."

Aira shot a sideways look at me.

Aira: "Only you could watch something that pretty and call it 'good control.'"

I kept my eyes on the ring.

Haru: "It's beautiful."

Aira blinked.

Aira: "Oh. Good. You actually have feelings."

The teachers pushed the load higher for stage two. One of the Red Circle students slipped half a beat off rhythm, tilting the ring until the outer runes buzzed with a sharp metallic hum.

Before Maren could even raise a hand, Elira rebuilt the whole shape midair.

Her left hand opened. Her right turned. Gold threads multiplied, crossed, and lifted the shaking ring back into smooth balance so seamlessly that most of the crowd would walk away thinking nothing had ever gone wrong.

I noticed the split second where it almost snapped—and how calmly she refused to let someone else's mistake hurt anyone.

Quiet clapping rolled across the deck. Elira let out a breath, lowered her arms, and scanned the crowd until her eyes landed on me.

She smiled openly—not for the audience.

Aira turned her head slowly toward me.

Aira: "Oh no."

Haru: "What?"

Aira: "Nothing. Just the start of my headaches."

Haru: "You say that a lot."

Aira: "Because you keep making it true."

When the dismissal bell rang, students rushed forward to praise the casters. I would have slipped out quietly, except a classmate grabbed Aira's sleeve and dragged her into a loud group near the stairs.

That left me standing alone by the cliff rail for about seven seconds before Elira walked over.

With the formal show behind her, she felt both more ordinary and much harder to guard against.

Elira: "You came."

Haru: "You invited me."

Elira: "Lots of people show up for the wrong reasons."

Haru: "And what reasons did you expect from me?"

She looked at me with the late afternoon sun resting softly across her shoulders.

Elira: "Curiosity, maybe."

Haru: "Harmless, then."

Elira: "It can be. It can also change everything."

She turned and rested one hand on the stone rail overlooking the western cliffs. Below us, mist drifted through the deep valleys, catching early lantern sparks from the city far below.

Behind us, voices praised the showcase while stray gold sparks faded out of the practice rings. Around Elira, noise always seemed to take one step back.

Elira: "You weren't impressed the usual way."

Haru: "There's a usual way?"

Her lips curved.

Elira: "Most people watching a Yellow Circle either cheer loudly or stay bitter quietly."

Haru: "And I did neither?"

Elira: "You watched how it worked."

Too sharp.

Haru: "You noticed that while holding together a four-person spell?"

She turned back to face me.

Elira: "I notice when someone watches the frame instead of just the fireworks."

No accusation sat in her voice, which put me even more on guard, not less.

Haru: "Your control was excellent."

Elira: "That sounds like a teacher's report."

Haru: "Would you rather hear something less formal?"

Her smile came right away, warm and quick enough to vanish before I could brace for it.

Elira: "I'd rather hear what you really think."

I looked back at the empty circles where the last gold wisps were fading into cooling afternoon air.

Haru: "It was beautiful."

I meant the spell. The quiet pause that followed left way too much room for other meanings.

Elira's expression softened by a degree too small to name.

Elira: "Thank you."

Behind me, I could feel Aira staring from across the deck. I didn't turn around.

Elira folded her hands lightly in front of her.

Elira: "You really did stop Leon's backfire yesterday."

There it was, laid straight on the table with no guessing.

Haru: "I kept a mistake from getting worse."

Elira: "That isn't a no."

Haru: "It isn't a yes either."

For a second I thought she might push. Instead she looked back out over the cliff gardens.

Elira: "People assume power has to be loud."

Haru: "Usually it wants a crowd."

Elira: "And yours doesn't?"

Every muscle in my shoulders locked tight inside, though my face stayed still.

Haru: "My what?"

She didn't look at me when she answered.

Elira: "Your mana feels wrong for your rank."

Far more dangerous than Leon's hurt pride or classroom rumors. Elira wasn't guessing. She was sensing it.

Haru: "I'm a Green Circle transfer. There isn't much mystery to that."

She turned back to meet my eyes.

Elira: "That excuse would work better if you actually believed it."

For the first time since walking onto campus, I didn't have a quick comeback ready. She saw that too, and her voice gentled.

Elira: "You don't have to explain anything to me."

Haru: "Then why bring it up?"

Elira: "Because sometimes being seen isn't the same as being exposed."

That hit harder than any spell Leon had thrown. Most people who noticed something odd leaned closer wanting a label, leverage, or a secret to sell. Elira sounded like she thought understanding someone could be a kind of protection.

Haru: "You're strangely kind for someone this school treats like royalty."

She let out a soft, amused breath.

Elira: "That might be the worst compliment I've ever gotten."

Haru: "I meant it nicely."

Elira: "I know."

She glanced past my shoulder toward the deck, where Aira was clearly pretending not to watch us while staring straight at us.

Elira: "Your friend looks worried."

Haru: "Aira expects trouble."

Elira: "Is she usually right?"

Haru: "More often than I'd like."

Elira laughed softly—easy and open—and in one second I understood why people trusted her. Behind the gold crest and the fame, she actually listened.

Elira: "Can I ask you something?"

Haru: "You already have."

Elira: "One more thing, then."

Haru: "Go ahead."

Her face turned thoughtful.

Elira: "Why did you ask about Black Circle magic in class?"

Playing curious would have been easy. Giving a textbook answer would have been safe. For reasons I hadn't figured out yet, I gave her neither.

Haru: "Because I wanted to see how people react when something doesn't fit their chart."

She held my eyes.

Elira: "And what did you learn?"

Haru: "Most people laugh first."

Elira: "And after that?"

Haru: "If it threatens what they lean on, they get scared."

Elira stayed quiet for a few seconds, then nodded once—settling something for herself rather than just being polite.

Elira: "That sounds lonely."

I shouldn't have felt that line as deeply as I did.

Haru: "Not always."

Elira: "No?"

Haru: "Sometimes there are people who make it less lonely."

For the first time, surprise caught her completely off guard—not just the words, just the fact that I actually said them. Faint color touched her cheeks before she looked down the garden path.

Elira: "You're quieter than most people here."

Haru: "Usually helps."

Elira: "Maybe. Being quiet also makes people underestimate how much you really mean."

Behind us, the terrace was clearing out as teachers ushered remaining students—Aira included, after throwing one helpless glance my way—down toward the garden stairs, and the glowing circles clicked off one by one. Cool canyon air drifted up from the cliffs as shadows stretched toward sunset.

Elira stepped away from the rail.

Elira: "There's a quiet archive hall in the north library wing. Almost nobody goes up there after class."

Haru: "Should I ask where this invite is going?"

Her eyes brightened.

Elira: "Only if you hate old books, quiet rooms, and honest talk."

Haru: "Very specific."

Elira: "I thought you liked specific."

She was right, which was the entire problem.

Haru: "You're asking me to meet at the library."

For half a beat, her calm confidence paused.

Elira: "I don't usually ask people up there. Yes. I am."

Haru: "People will get the wrong idea."

Elira: "People get the wrong idea about most things."

Haru: "Not very reassuring."

Elira: "Wasn't trying to be."

I almost smiled before quick footsteps broke in from the side path.

An annoyed Blue mana signature arrived half a second before Leon did.

Leon: "Of course."

Aira walked one step behind him, looking deeply annoyed.

Aira: "He caught me right outside the lower garden gate. I told him coming back up here was a terrible idea."

Leon stopped three steps away, looking from Elira to me with stiff disbelief.

Leon: "Lady Elira, I didn't know you spent time listening to second-year rumors."

All the warmth vanished from the terrace in a second. Elira's posture cooled by one clear degree.

Elira: "And I didn't know my conversations needed your permission, Leon."

Aira's mouth twitched, clearly enjoying every second of this more than she meant to show.

Leon stiffened.

Leon: "That isn't what I meant."

Elira: "Then pick your words more carefully."

His eyes snapped back to me.

Leon: "With respect, reputations matter here. Especially when someone starts enjoying attention he didn't earn."

Aira stepped straight into his view.

Aira: "He earned enough to embarrass you in front of a whole arena."

Leon: "Aira."

Aira: "Go on. If you're going to be petty, at least be good at it."

Leon's jaw locked as yesterday's shame flared right back up, made ten times worse by Elira standing right beside us.

Elira didn't look angry. She looked disappointed, which rattled him worse.

Elira: "If you care about status, keep it to yourself. If you care about truth, pay closer attention."

Aira let out a quiet sound somewhere between shock and a cheer. Leon went pale, then flushed red.

Leon: "You actually think there's something special about him?"

Elira glanced at me for half a second before answering.

Elira: "I think writing someone off just because their circle is green says a lot about whoever does it."

Not quite the same as saying no. Leon caught that gap right away. His eyes narrowed.

Leon: "Then I'll prove yesterday was just luck."

Haru: "You don't owe me proof."

Leon gave a dry, humorless laugh.

Leon: "Big words from someone hiding behind cheap mystery."

Aira: "Cheap mystery is a terrible line."

Leon ignored her.

Leon: "There's an interclass combat tournament in three days. Ranked matches, official judges, no teachers stepping in unless someone breaks. If you're really that calm, sign up."

Haru: "I transferred in yesterday."

Leon: "Then say no. In front of everyone, if you want. Everyone can decide what that means."

Aira: "That's a trap."

Leon: "That's a fair offer."

Elira's voice stayed even.

Elira: "It's a cheap trap, and everyone can see it."

Leon spun toward her quickly, desperate to save face.

Leon: "Then he can turn it down without shame, can't he?"

The dare hung in the cooling evening air.

I had zero interest in attention, zero interest in a school tournament, and definitely no desire to give Leon a stage. Yet I could already hear the talk if I walked away: *the Green transfer who got lucky once against a Blue and hid behind Elira Vale.*

Even worse, I felt something else.

Eyes watching from farther up the garden path. Not Leon's friends or random students. Cold, careful watching, studying the four of us from behind the trees. Before I could lock onto it, the presence vanished cleanly.

Elira caught the tiny shift in my gaze.

Elira: "Haru?"

I brought my eyes back to the terrace. Leon waited. Aira frowned. Elira watched closely.

Haru: "Fine."

Aira whipped around.

Aira: "Fine?"

Bitter relief lit up Leon's face.

Leon: "You'll fight?"

Haru: "I'll sign up."

Aira: "Wrong answer."

Elira didn't look surprised—just thoughtful.

Leon stood straighter, clearly relieved the world had clicked back into a contest he understood.

Leon: "Good. Now we can stop pretending."

He walked away before anyone could spoil his exit.

Aira stared after him, then turned on me like she couldn't decide whether to scold me or sigh herself into an early grave.

Aira: "So you just enjoy making your life harder."

Haru: "Not really."

Aira: "Then why say yes?"

Haru: "Because he won't stop pushing until he hits the wall once."

Aira: "He'll keep pushing anyway."

Haru: "Then I'll deal with that when he tries."

Aira shut her eyes for a second.

Aira: "I hate how calm you stay when you do this."

Elira took half a step closer, keeping her voice low.

Elira: "You felt something just now."

Aira glanced sharply between us.

Aira: "Felt what?"

Haru: "I'm not sure."

True—and nowhere near enough.

Elira held my eyes steadily.

Elira: "Then be careful."

Haru: "I usually am."

Elira: "No," she said softly. "Usually you're controlled. Those aren't always the same thing."

Again, she dropped a line right where I couldn't easily shake it off.

Aira looked from Elira to me and threw up her hands.

Aira: "My peaceful school year is officially dead."

Elira smiled at her.

Elira: "I'll try not to make it worse."

Aira: "Not very comforting coming from a Yellow Circle standing three feet away from my very suspicious friend."

Haru: "I'm suspicious?"

Aira: "Very."

Elira's soft laugh loosened the tight knot in the air for a brief second.

Sunset washed the terrace in warm orange, carrying the faint smell of spell-scorched stone and garden flowers up from the cliffs. Then the evening bells rang across Aurelis, calling students down toward dinner and the study halls.

Elira turned toward the upper walkway.

Elira: "The library invite still stands."

Haru: "You don't give up easily."

Elira: "Only when something matters."

Her eyes met mine for one quiet second, then she nodded to Aira and slipped into the lantern-lit trees.

Aira waited until Elira was out of earshot before crossing her arms.

Aira: "Well."

Haru: "Well what?"

Aira: "A Yellow Circle just invited you to a private library hall, shut Leon down in public, and looked at you like a puzzle she wants to figure out gently."

Haru: "Very specific guess."

Aira: "Accurate one. Honestly, I don't know whether to worry about you or the whole school."

Haru: "That sounds dramatic."

Aira: "You asked about Black Circle magic on day one, survived Leon's meltdown, signed up for the tournament, and caught Elira Vale's eye. Drama is already here."

She started down the terrace stairs.

Aira: "Come on. If you're going to ruin your quiet life, eat dinner first."

I followed her down into the lantern-lit halls.

## Coda: The Gate Remembered

After dinner, Aira turned toward the east dorm stairs, leaving me to take the quieter stone path back past Aurelis's old outer gate arch.

Twilight settled deep purple over the towers. My mind kept drifting back to the upper garden path—to that cold stare vanishing into the trees before I could trace it, and to Elira saying being seen wasn't the same as being exposed.

Then, as I walked under the ancient stone arch, the gate behind me let out one soft chime.

No student walking across the lower yard noticed. The sound was too quiet for show and too exact to be the wind.

I stopped and looked back.

A thin line of light ran across the weathered crest carved high over the arch. Green first. Then Blue. Then Red. Then Yellow. Four obedient colors, the approved ladder of the world, glowing softly in the dusk.

Below them, where solid stone should have stayed dark, a fifth ring pressed once from inside the rock—ink-black, razor-sharp, older than the towers around it—and vanished before the fake Green mark on my wrist could even flicker.

The cold pressure I'd felt stepping through this gate yesterday morning finally made sense. It hadn't checked my pass.

It had counted me.

Two first-years hurried past under the arch, arguing loudly about tournament odds. One glanced down at the pale green glow peeking under my left cuff, wrote me off without slowing down, and kept walking. Normal cruelty. Useful cover.

Inside my coat pocket, my fingers brushed the folded edge of Elira's healer cloth, still kept from yesterday's hallway.

Up on the garden deck, someone had watched me closely. Inside Aurelis's old stone, ancient magic had seen straight through the lie on my wrist. And somewhere across the yard, a girl with gold eyes had looked straight past it too.

I stood beneath the darkening arch and realized I wasn't sure anymore which of those three scared me more.

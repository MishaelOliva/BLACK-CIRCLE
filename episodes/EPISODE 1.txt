Aira: "That wasn't normal."

Haru: "For a Green Circle?"

Aira: "For anyone on their first day."

There was no accusation in her voice. Only amazement.

And something more dangerous than suspicion. Interest mixed with trust.

I did not know what to do with either.

Haru: "It was timing."

She held my gaze, searching for the edge of the answer I would not give.

Aira: "Maybe. But I think there's more to you than timing, Haru."

Before I could answer, a new presence entered the arena from the upper observation platform.

The arena changed without anyone announcing why.

Conversations broke off mid-word. Students straightened. Even Leon looked up.

At the top of the stairs stood a girl in the formal elite mantle reserved for special-ranking students. Her posture was elegant, her expression composed, and the mana around her felt bright, heavy, and refined enough to press lightly against the skin from a distance.

A **Red Circle** crest glowed at her collarbone. Not just any Red. A high-grade one.

She looked down at the arena.
At Dren.
At Leon.
And then at me. Her gaze lingered. Not with contempt. With calculation.

Instructor Dren: "Student Council President." So this was her.

The one reputation alone had reached even transfer paperwork and hallway whispers.

Aurelis student council did not run dances and decorate notice boards. It controlled student disciplinary referrals, emergency witnesses, club and duel permits, and enough access privileges to make most heirs behave in public.

She descended the steps slowly, each movement controlled.

Rhea: "I apologize for interrupting. I heard there was unusual activity in the assessment hall."

Her voice was calm, polished, and impossible to read.

Instructor Dren: "The matter is handled."

Rhea: "Handled, then. Still, it seems this year's transfer student has already become interesting."

A few students exchanged glances. I said nothing.

She studied me for one second longer, then smiled very slightly.

It was not a warm smile.

It was the smile of someone who had noticed a thread and intended to keep pulling until the whole pattern revealed itself.

Rhea: "I'll look forward to seeing more."

Then she turned and left with the same controlled pace, leaving half the room pretending not to watch her go.

Aira: "That's not good."

Haru: "Who was that?"

She looked at me as if I had asked the name of a storm everyone else had learned to fear.

Aira: "Rhea Valmont. Student Council President. Red Circle. If she notices you, paperwork starts growing teeth."

I looked back toward the stairs where Rhea had vanished.

My first day at Aurelis was not even over.

Already, hiding had become a group activity.

---

The rumors started before morning classes did.

It was the problem with elite schools. Nothing stayed small.

By the time I entered the main courtyard of **Aurelis Arcane Academy**, students were already talking about the previous day's assessment as if it had been a championship duel instead of a classroom exercise.

Some of the versions were merely exaggerated.

Others had already become nonsense.

Student: "He broke Leon's spell with a Green Circle output."

Student: "No, Leon lost control on his own."

Student: "I heard the instructor ended it because the transfer was secretly using a relic."

Student: "Someone said his mana moved strangely."

Student: "Strangely how?"

Student: "No idea. That's what makes it strange."

I walked past them with my bag over one shoulder, expression neutral, pace unhurried.

The safest way to survive attention was to refuse to feed it.

Unfortunately, attention had a way of feeding itself.

A pair of Blue Circle students near the central staircase went quiet as I passed.

One of them looked directly at my concealed crest, then at my face. Student 1: "That's him."

Student 2: "He still looks ordinary."

Student 1: "That's the unsettling part." I kept walking.

The silver-leaf trees lining the courtyard shimmered in the early light. Overhead, practice glyphs rotated above the upper towers, marking today's training schedules. Students moved through the paths in clusters of polished uniforms, family insignias, and carefully managed confidence.

And somewhere in all of that, I could already feel the shift.

Yesterday I had been invisible.

Today I was a question. It was more dangerous.

As I reached the entrance hall, I spotted Aira standing near one of the floating class directories, holding two cups from the academy cafe. She noticed me before I could decide whether to change direction.

She raised one of the cups.

Aira: "I guessed you'd come through this way."

Haru: "You planned for coincidence?" Aira smiled.

Aira: "I prefer 'efficient friendliness.'"

She stepped closer and offered me the drink.

Aira: "It's tea. I didn't know what you liked, so I picked the least risky option."

I looked at the cup, then at her.

Haru: "Thank you."

Aira: "You're welcome."

There was a small pause.

Aira: "Also, before the day gets any worse, I should warn you that half the academy has decided you're either secretly brilliant or deeply suspicious."

Haru: "Only half?" That made her laugh.

Aira: "You're getting better at this."

Haru: "At attracting trouble?"

Aira: "At sounding like you're joking."

We started walking together toward the east wing.

That alone was enough to draw glances.

Aira seemed used to them.

I was not sure I ever would be.

Aira took a sip from her own cup and lowered her voice.

Aira: "Leon is furious, by the way."

Haru: "I assumed."

Aira: "He's pretending not to care, which for Leon is somehow louder than normal anger."

Haru: "That seems inconvenient."

Aira: "For you, yes. For me, it's Tuesday."

We entered the corridor just as a new wave of conversation moved through the hall.

Only this time, the tone was different. It was softer. Sharper. More reverent.

I felt the mana before I saw the source.

It was not merely strong. It was radiant.

A presence like warm sunlight pressing through glass.

The students ahead began moving aside almost instinctively, clearing a path down the center of the corridor. Blue Circles straightened. Red Circles turned with measured interest. Even some instructors paused. Then she appeared.

She was tall-taller than most of the girls in our year, and tall enough that the elegance of her posture became part of her presence rather than an afterthought. Her hair fell in long waves the color of pale gold touched with amber, catching the morning light as if it belonged to it. Her eyes were clear and luminous, somewhere between honey and sunlight, and when she moved, there was a calm to it that made everyone around her seem hurried by comparison.

Her uniform was the same as everyone else's in design, but on her it looked almost ceremonial. The academy mantle rested neatly over her shoulders, marked with the insignia reserved for the highest-ranked students.

And at her collarbone, unmistakable and brilliant, glowed a **Yellow Circle**. Not hidden.

No one had softened it for the comfort of the hallway.

A true Yellow Circle crest carried itself like a law of nature.

The corridor fell into a respectful hush. Student 3: "It's her." Student 4: "Lady Elira."

Student 5: "She's back on campus today?"

Aira noticed the direction of my attention and quietly leaned closer.

Aira: "That's **Elira Vale**."

Haru: "I gathered she was important."

Aira gave me a look.

Aira: "That might be the most restrained sentence anyone has ever said about Elira Vale."

Haru: "Should I know more?"

Aira: "She's a Yellow Circle. One of the youngest high-grade light-affinity magicians in the country. Top-ranked in magical theory, top-ranked in practical control, and somehow still kind enough to make people feel guilty for being awful around her."

Haru: "So everyone admires her."

Aira: "Admire is a gentle word for it."

Elira continued down the corridor, surrounded not by attendants but by open space. No one crowded her. No one dared. And yet unlike the Reds who often wore status like a polished blade, she did not seem to enjoy the way people moved for her.

If anything, she looked faintly uncomfortable with the distance.

Then, to my surprise, her gaze shifted. And stopped on me. Only for a second.

But long enough that I felt it.

Neither contempt nor idle curiosity. Recognition of anomaly.

Her eyes narrowed very slightly, almost imperceptibly. Then she passed.

The corridor's breathing seemed to return all at once.

Aira turned toward me with exaggerated seriousness.

Aira: "If you tell me you didn't notice that, I'll be offended on behalf of all observational skill."

Haru: "She looked at me."

Aira: "Yes." Her pen hovered above the margin.

Haru: "People do that here."

Aira stared at me for a moment.

Aira: "You really don't understand how this school works yet."

Haru: "I'm learning."

Aira: "Then lesson one: Yellow Circle students do not casually notice Green Circle transfers."

Haru: "Maybe she was looking past me."

Aira: "Haru."

Haru: "Fine. She was looking at me." Aira folded her arms.

Aira: "Good. Growth."

We entered homeroom a minute later, and the room's atmosphere sharpened immediately.

Yesterday's curiosity had matured overnight into watchfulness.

Leon Varek was already in his seat, speaking to two other Blue Circle boys in a voice low enough to signal strategy and loud enough to invite overhearing. When he saw me enter beside Aira, the corner of his mouth tightened.

Leon: "Impressive. One lucky assessment and now you travel with company."

Aira: "Good morning to you too, Leon."

Leon: "I'm being civil."

Aira: "Then I'd hate to hear you hostile."

I took my seat without responding.

That seemed to irritate him more.

Instructor Dren arrived soon after and began the lesson on multi-layered reinforcement theory. He said nothing about yesterday's assessment, which was somehow more effective than mentioning it would have been. The class kept glancing at me anyway.

During the lecture, I felt it again.

A strange sensation at the edge of awareness. Someone watching. Not from the room. From above.

When I looked toward the tall windows, there was nothing there but light. Still, the feeling remained.

---

Lunch at Aurelis was less a meal and more a social sorting ritual disguised as one.

The academy dining hall was vast, divided into elegant tiers and alcoves with panoramic windows overlooking the lower gardens. Students clustered by rank, influence, and confidence. Even table placement carried meaning.

Aira and I had just taken our trays toward a side table that offered relative quiet when the room shifted again.

The same hush as before, though softer now. People made room.

I did not need to turn to know who it was.

Elira Vale crossed the dining hall with composed steps, sunlight gathering at the edges of her hair where it struck the glass. Up close, the force of her presence was even stranger. Yellow Circle mana was not merely heavy. It was refined to a degree that made lesser currents around it feel unsteady, like candle flames in a bright room.

But there was no arrogance in her expression. Only attentiveness.

She stopped beside our table.

Half the hall forgot how to breathe.

Her hand hovered near the chair back for half a second. Her gaze moved once across the crowded hall, measuring every eye that had followed her, before returning to us.

Elira: "May I sit here?"

Aira nearly dropped her fork.

Aira: "You're asking us?" Elira's lips curved faintly.

Elira: "That seemed polite."

Aira looked at me, then back at Elira, then visibly decided she would process this emotional damage later.

Aira: "Yes. Naturally. Please."

Elira sat gracefully across from us.

The room had become painfully attentive while pretending not to be.

Aira, to her credit, recovered first.

Aira: "I'm Aira Solen."

Elira: "I know." Aira blinked. Elira turned to me.

Elira: "And you're Haru Aerlen."

Haru: "Yes." The cold pulse behind my ribs remained quiet.

Her golden eyes held mine with a calm steadiness that felt more dangerous than open scrutiny.

Elira: "You asked an interesting question in class yesterday." Aira stared at me.

Aira: "You asked a question that reached the Yellow Circle lunch network?"

Haru: "Apparently."

Elira's expression softened very slightly.

Elira: "Questions are rarely the problem. The fear of them usually is."

It was not a typical academy answer.

Haru: "Do you believe Black Circle theory has merit?"

A few nearby students almost choked trying to listen discreetly.

Elira lowered her gaze briefly to her tea, then looked back up.

Elira: "I believe the history of magic is incomplete. And I believe most people are comfortable dismissing anything that unsettles the order they benefit from." Aira's pen stopped moving.

Aira: "That is not the answer I expected."

Elira: "Most people expect Yellow Circle students to defend the system."

Aira: "Do you?"

Elira smiled, but there was something sad in it.

Elira: "The system defends itself quite well."

Briefly, none of us spoke.

The noise of the dining hall returned at the edges, but distant, as though our table had become its own smaller world.

Elira folded her hands neatly.

Elira: "I also wanted to thank you."

Haru: "For what?"

Elira: "Yesterday. You prevented Leon's spell from collapsing badly enough to injure him."

Aira turned to me so quickly her chair made a sound against the floor.

Aira: "You did what?"

Across the hall, several people were now very openly pretending not to listen.

Haru: "I reacted."

Elira: "With extremely precise timing."

Haru: "So I've been told."

For the first time, Elira's smile became genuine.

It was neither grand nor dazzling, just warm.

It changed her whole face.

And for one deeply inconvenient second, I forgot how to answer. Aira noticed. She noticed.

Aira looked between us with the expression of someone who had just realized the day might become entertaining in ways she had not planned for.

Elira set down her cup.

Elira: "There is an advanced practical demonstration this afternoon in the upper training gardens. My group has been asked to assist."

Aira: "Your group?"

Elira: "High-tier affinity control."

Aira made a soft noise that translated easily into *that is far above our schedule*.

Elira looked at me again.

Elira: "You should come watch, Haru."

There was no challenge in the invitation. Only sincerity.

Which somehow made it more dangerous.

Haru: "Why?"

Elira: "Because I think you notice things other people miss."

Aira, now fully committed to her role as witness to my future complications, rested her chin on one hand.

Aira: "He does that."

Haru: "Aira."

Aira: "I'm helping."

Elira's eyes brightened with amusement.

Elira: "Good. Then perhaps both of you can come."

Aira: "Now I definitely have to."

---

The upper training gardens were built on a series of broad terraces overlooking the academy's western cliffs. Ornamental trees lined the paths, and the practice fields were arranged between flowerbeds, crystal pylons, and rune-etched stone circles designed to stabilize high-level mana output.

Students gathered in the observation area well before the demonstration began. Most were upper-year Red and Blue Circles hoping to witness something impressive. A few instructors stood at the edges with the posture of people prepared to intervene if necessary.

Aira and I arrived just as the first display circle activated.

At the center of the terrace, several elite students took their positions. Two high-grade Reds. One specialized Blue. And Elira.

Even standing still, she drew the eye.

The afternoon light fell across her hair and shoulders, turning the gold in her crest almost white at its brightest points. When she lifted one hand, the mana around the terrace changed. It was subtle at first, a soft rise in pressure, then a harmonic resonance through the practice field as the stabilizing runes responded.

Instructor Maren, supervising the demonstration, addressed the crowd.

Instructor Maren: "Today's exercise will focus on synchronized affinity layering under unequal mana conditions. Observe the circle balance, hand transitions, and restraint thresholds."

The Reds began first, weaving complex fire and wind patterns into intersecting rings. The Blue maintained structural water barriers to contain overflow. It was skilled work. Precise. Elegant.

Then Elira stepped into the center node. Light bloomed. Not blinding. Beautiful.

Golden-white threads unfolded from her fingertips and spread across the air like woven silk, threading between the existing spells without disturbing them. She did not overpower the formation. She refined it. Strengthened weak points. Smoothed instability. Elevated everyone else's work simply by touching the structure. The terrace fell silent.

Aira whispered without taking her eyes off the field.

Aira: "Every time I see her cast, it feels unfair."

Haru: "She's very controlled."

Aira looked sideways at me.

Aira: "That is such a Haru thing to say about something that beautiful."

I kept my gaze on the field.

Haru: "It is beautiful." Aira blinked.

Aira: "Oh. Good. You do have a functioning heart."

On the field, the demonstration shifted into its second phase. The instructors increased the mana load. One of the Red Circle students lost alignment for a moment, causing the spell structure to tilt. The stabilizing rings vibrated dangerously.

Before anyone else could react, Elira changed the entire pattern.

Her left hand opened. Her right turned.

The golden threads multiplied, crossed, and lifted the failing structure into a new equilibrium so smoothly it looked effortless.

Most of the crowd would remember the spell as flawless.

I remembered the half-breath where it almost wasn't, and the exact calm with which she refused to let someone else's mistake become damage.

The crowd broke into quiet applause.

Elira exhaled and lowered her arms.

Then her gaze moved through the audience. And found me again.

This time, she smiled openly. Not to the crowd. To me.

Aira slowly turned her head.

Aira: "Oh no."

Haru: "What?"

Aira: "Nothing. Just the beginning of trouble."

Haru: "You say that a lot."

Aira: "Because you keep making it true."

After the demonstration ended, students surged forward to congratulate the participants. I would have preferred to leave before becoming part of that movement, but Aira had already been recognized by one of her friends and pulled into a side conversation.

Which left me standing near the terrace railing alone. For approximately seven seconds. Then Elira approached.

Up close, with the demonstration over, she somehow felt more human and more dangerous at the same time.

Her expression was calm, but there was a natural softness to it now that the formal distance of the performance was gone.

Elira: "You came."

Haru: "You invited me."

Elira: "Many people accept invitations for the wrong reasons."

Haru: "And what reasons were you expecting from me?"

Elira studied me for a moment, the late afternoon light resting across her hair and shoulders like something that belonged to her naturally.

Elira: "Curiosity, perhaps."

Haru: "Harmless, then."

Elira: "It can be. It can also change everything."

She turned slightly and rested one hand on the stone railing overlooking the western cliffs. Below us, clouds drifted through the lower ravines, and the light from the city far beneath reflected faintly upward through the mana mist.

For a few seconds, neither of us spoke.

The noise from the training terrace continued behind us-students praising the demonstration, instructors giving measured feedback, mana still fading from the practice circles. But around Elira, everything always seemed to narrow into focus.

She had a quiet gravity to her. Not like authority.

More like a star that did not need to prove it was burning.

Elira: "You weren't impressed in the usual way."

Haru: "There's a usual way?" Elira's lips curved faintly.

Elira: "Most people watching a Yellow Circle demonstration either admire it openly or resent it privately."

Haru: "And I did neither?"

Elira: "You observed." It was accurate. Too accurate.

Haru: "You noticed that?"

Elira turned to face me again.

Elira: "I notice when someone is watching the structure instead of the spectacle."

There was no accusation in her tone.

That somehow made me more cautious, not less.

Haru: "Your control was excellent."

Elira: "That sounds like an academic evaluation."

Haru: "Would you prefer a less academic one?"

This time her smile was immediate.

Warm. Bright. Brief enough to disappear before it became dangerous.

Elira: "I would prefer an honest one."

I looked back toward the training field, where the last traces of golden mana were dissolving into the air.

Haru: "It was beautiful."

When I said it, I meant the spell.

Unfortunately, the silence that followed suggested the sentence had room for interpretation.

Elira's expression softened by something almost too subtle to name.

Elira: "Thank you."

From behind us, I could feel Aira looking in our direction. I did not turn.

Because if I did, I was certain I would find an expression that would inconvenience me later.

Elira folded her hands lightly in front of her.

Elira: "You really did stop Leon's backlash yesterday." There it was.

The certainty had finally arrived without pretense.

Haru: "I prevented the situation from getting worse."

Elira: "That isn't a denial."

Haru: "It isn't a confession either."

Briefly, I thought she might press harder.

Instead, she looked out over the cliffside gardens.

Elira: "People assume power is loud."

Haru: "Usually it wants to be noticed."

Elira: "And yours doesn't?" My hands stopped moving. Only inwardly.

Outwardly, I kept my expression calm.

Haru: "My what?"

Elira did not look at me when she answered.

Elira: "Your presence feels inconsistent."

It was a dangerous sentence.

Far more dangerous than suspicion from Leon or curiosity from the class.

Because Elira was not guessing carelessly. She was perceiving.

Haru: "I'm a Green Circle transfer student. There isn't much room for inconsistency in that."

Elira finally turned back to me.

Elira: "That answer would work better if you believed it."

For the first time since meeting her, I did not have a response ready. She saw that too. Her tone gentled.

Elira: "You don't have to tell me anything."

Haru: "Then why say it?"

Elira: "Because sometimes being seen is not the same thing as being exposed."

That caught me off guard more effectively than any attack spell could have.

Most people, if they noticed something unusual, pushed toward it.
Wanted to define it.
Wanted control over it.
Wanted a secret they could use.

But Elira said it as if recognition itself could be an act of care.

I was not sure what to do with that.

Haru: "You're unusually kind for someone people seem to treat like royalty."

She gave a soft, almost amused exhale.

Elira: "That may be the least flattering compliment I've ever received."

Haru: "I meant it well."

Elira: "I know."

She glanced over my shoulder toward the terrace.

Aira was now definitely pretending not to watch us while very clearly watching us. Elira noticed and smiled.

Elira: "Your friend looks concerned."

Haru: "Aira tends to expect complications."

Elira: "Does she often turn out to be right?"

Haru: "More than I would prefer." Elira laughed quietly.

The sound was gentle and unguarded, and I understood immediately why people found themselves drawn to her. Not because she was a Yellow Circle. Not because she was beautiful. Those things were obvious.

It was because despite the aura, the status, the impossible rank, there was something deeply human in the way she listened.

Elira: "May I ask you something?"

Haru: "You already have."

Elira: "Another thing, then."

Haru: "All right."

Her expression became more thoughtful.

Elira: "Why did you ask about Black Circle magicians in class?"

The easier answer would have been curiosity.

The safer answer would have been theory.

For reasons I did not fully understand, I gave her neither.

Haru: "Because I wanted to know how people react to the idea of something beyond their system." She held my gaze.

Elira: "And what did you learn?"

Haru: "That most people laugh first."

Elira: "And after that?"

Haru: "If the idea threatens the order they trust, they become afraid."

Elira was quiet for a few moments.

Then she nodded once, slowly, as if confirming something to herself rather than agreeing with me.

Elira: "That sounds lonely."

I should not have felt that sentence as strongly as I did. But I did.

Haru: "Not always."

Elira: "No?"

Haru: "Sometimes there are people who make it less so."

For the first time, Elira looked surprised.

Not by the words themselves.

By the fact that I had said them.

A faint color touched her expression before she looked away toward the garden path.

Elira: "You're quieter than most people here."

Haru: "That usually benefits me."

Elira: "Maybe. But I think silence also makes people underestimate how much you're really saying." It was possible.

Or maybe she simply heard more than others.

Behind us, the terrace had begun to empty. Instructors were dismissing students. Mana circles were dimming one by one. The air felt cooler now, touched by evening.

Elira stepped back from the railing.

Elira: "There's a moonlit archive hall in the north library wing. Hardly anyone uses it after classes."

Haru: "Should I be concerned about where this invitation is going?" Her eyes brightened again.

Elira: "Only if you dislike old books, quiet places, and honest conversation."

Haru: "That is suspiciously specific."

Elira: "I thought you might appreciate specific." She was right. It was the problem.

Haru: "Are you inviting me to the library?"

For the first time since she had approached the table at lunch, her confidence did not arrive immediately.

Elira: "I do not usually invite people there."

Elira: "I am."

Haru: "People are going to misunderstand that."

Elira: "People misunderstand many things."

Haru: "That doesn't sound reassuring."

Elira: "It wasn't meant to be." I smiled faintly. Almost.

Then footsteps approached quickly from the side path.

A familiar, irritated mana signature arrived half a second before the voice.

Leon: "Naturally."

Aira appeared just behind him, looking deeply unimpressed.

Aira: "I told him this was a bad idea."

Leon stopped a few paces away from us, his eyes moving from Elira to me with visible disbelief.

Leon: "Lady Elira, I didn't realize you were entertaining lower-year speculation now."

The temperature of the moment changed immediately.

Elira's expression remained calm, but something in her posture cooled.

Elira: "And I didn't realize my conversations required your approval, Leon."

Aira's mouth twitched, clearly enjoying that more than she intended to admit. Leon stiffened.

Leon: "That isn't what I meant."

Elira: "Then say what you mean more carefully."

He glanced at me again.

Leon: "With respect, I only think reputations should be guarded. Especially when certain people are enjoying attention they haven't earned."

Aira stepped forward at once.

Aira: "He earned enough to embarrass you in front of an entire assessment hall."

Leon: "Aira."

Aira: "No, go ahead. If you're going to be petty, at least be efficient about it." Leon's jaw tightened.

I could feel the old humiliation from yesterday rising in him again, sharpened now by the fact that Elira herself was standing here.

Elira, however, did not seem angry. She seemed disappointed.

That somehow made Leon more uncomfortable than anger would have.

Elira: "If your concern is status, keep it. If your concern is truth, improve your eyesight."

Aira made a small sound that might have been shock or admiration.

Leon went pale, then flushed.

Leon: "You think there's something special about him?"

Elira looked at me for the briefest second before answering.

Elira: "I think dismissing people because their circle is green reflects poorly on everyone who does it."

It was not the same as saying no. Leon noticed. His eyes narrowed.

Leon: "Then I'll prove yesterday was luck."

Haru: "You don't need to prove anything to me."

Leon gave a short, humorless laugh.

Leon: "That's generous from someone who's enjoying borrowed mystery."

Aira: "Borrowed mystery is a terrible phrase." Leon ignored her.

Leon: "There's an interclass combat ladder event in three days. Restricted brackets, official observation, no instructor interference unless necessary. If you're really so composed, enter."

Haru: "I just transferred."

Leon: "Then decline. Publicly, if you want. People can decide for themselves what that means."

Aira: "That is manipulation."

Leon: "That is an invitation." Elira's voice remained calm.

Elira: "It's coercive and transparent."

Leon turned to her quickly, almost desperate to regain dignity.

Leon: "Then he can refuse without shame, can't he?"

The challenge hung in the evening air.

I didn't want attention.
I didn't want the combat ladder.
I definitely didn't want to give Leon a stage.

But I could already see the shape of the rumor if I declined.

The Green Circle transfer who surprised a Blue once and then hid.

More importantly, I could feel something else. Eyes.

Watching from farther up the garden path.

They were neither Leon's allies nor idle students. The attention felt more deliberate.

Before I could isolate the mana signature, it vanished.

Elira noticed the shift in my focus.

Elira: "Haru?"

I returned my attention to the group.

Leon was waiting.
Aira was worried.
Elira was unreadable.

Haru: "Fine." Aira turned sharply.

Aira: "Fine?"

Leon's expression brightened with vindictive satisfaction.

Leon: "You accept?"

Haru: "I'll enter."

Aira: "It was not the correct answer."

Elira, however, did not look surprised. She looked thoughtful.

Leon straightened, clearly relieved that events had returned to a shape he understood.

Leon: "Good. Then everyone can stop pretending."

He turned and walked away before anyone could take the moment from him.

Aira watched him go, then looked at me with the expression of someone deciding whether to lecture me, worry about me, or both.

Aira: "You enjoy difficult lives, apparently."

Haru: "Not especially."

Aira: "Then why agree?"

Haru: "Because he would keep pushing otherwise."

Aira: "He will keep pushing anyway."

Haru: "Yes." I kept my hands resting flat and open.

Aira closed her eyes for a second.

Aira: "I hate when you're calm like that."

Elira stepped a little closer, her voice lower now.

Elira: "You sensed something just now." Aira looked between us.

Aira: "What did he sense?"

Haru: "I'm not sure." It was true. And not true enough.

Elira's gaze lingered on me.

Elira: "Then be careful."

Haru: "I usually am."

Elira: "No," she said gently. "Usually you're controlled. That isn't always the same thing."

Again, she said things that stayed with me longer than they should have.

Aira looked from Elira to me and sighed in visible surrender.

Aira: "I can already tell my peaceful academic year is gone." Elira smiled at her.

Elira: "I'll try not to make it worse."

Aira: "That sentence is not comforting when spoken by a Yellow Circle girl standing this close to my extremely suspicious friend."

Haru: "I'm suspicious?"

Aira: "Profoundly."

Elira's laughter was soft and bright. Briefly, the tension eased.

The sunset washed the terrace in gold, and the wind from the western cliffs carried the fading scent of spell-burnt air and garden flowers.

Then the academy bells rang, low and resonant, calling students back toward the residence halls and evening study blocks.

Elira turned toward the upper path.

Elira: "The library invitation still stands."

Haru: "You're persistent."

Elira: "Only when something matters."

Her eyes met mine for one quiet second.

Then she inclined her head to Aira and began walking away.

Aira waited until Elira had disappeared into the upper garden lights before speaking.

Aira: "Well."

Haru: "Well what?" Aira folded her arms.

Aira: "A Yellow Circle just invited you to a secret quiet library hall, defended you in public, and looked at you like a riddle she wants to solve gently."

Haru: "That's a very specific interpretation."

Aira: "It's an accurate one."

Haru: "You sound confident."

Aira: "I am. Also, I don't know whether to be worried for you or for the entire academy."

Haru: "That seems dramatic."

Aira: "You literally asked about Black Circle magic on your first day, survived Leon's meltdown, and now have the attention of Elira Vale. Dramatic has already happened."

She started down the terrace path.

Aira: "Come on. If you're going to ruin your life, at least do it after dinner." I followed.

But as we descended toward the main halls, my thoughts were no longer on Leon.

Or even on the combat ladder. They were on Elira.

On the way she had looked at me without fear.
On the way she had noticed what others missed.
On the dangerous kindness in her voice when she said being seen was not the same as being exposed.

And beneath all of that, quieter but no less real, was the feeling I had sensed at the edge of the garden. Someone had been watching. Not with schoolyard curiosity. With intent.

The evening lights of Aurelis flickered on one by one below the cliffs.

Far above, hidden behind my false Green Circle crest, the sleeping darkness near my heart gave one slow, silent pulse.

As if reminding me that no matter how gentle this school life tried to become, something older and more dangerous was already moving.

## Interlude: The Wrong Kindness

The west corridor outside the training complex had gone quiet in stages.

First the excited students left, carrying the match with them in pieces.

A Green scored on Leon. Leon lost control. Dren looked angry. Elira Vale stood up. No, she did not. Yes, she did. Haru Aerlen did something strange.

By the time I reached the side exit, the story had already become several versions of itself. That was useful. Contradiction made hiding easier.

I stopped beneath a narrow arch where late sunlight cut across the floor in gold bars. My sleeve was still torn from Leon's lance. The fabric hung loose near my wrist, exposing the false Green Circle more than I liked. I adjusted it. Badly.

My fingers were still numb from correcting Leon's collapse.

That irritated me more than the pain.

Haru: "You can come out."

The air behind the pillar shifted.

Elira Vale stepped into view.

She did not look embarrassed.

That would have been easier.

Elira: "I was deciding whether approaching you here would feel like an ambush."

Haru: "And?"

Elira: "I decided it probably would." I looked at her.

Elira: "Then I did it anyway."

That surprised a breath out of me. Not laughter. Close enough to be dangerous.

She held a folded strip of white cloth in one hand. Healer fabric. Clean. Expensive enough that most students would have saved it for an official injury rather than a torn sleeve.

Elira noticed where I was looking.

Elira: "Your arm."

Haru: "It's fine."

Elira: "Your sleeve disagrees."

Haru: "Fabric is dramatic."

Her mouth softened. Only slightly.

Elira: "May I?"

The question was the problem.

If she had simply stepped forward, I could have retreated. If she had ordered me to hold still, I could have disliked her for it. If she had acted like my answer was irrelevant, the world would have stayed simple. She asked.

That made refusal feel larger than it should have.

Haru: "It doesn't need a bandage."

Elira: "I know."

Haru: "Then why offer?"

The softness left her face.

Elira: "Because something can be unnecessary and still kind."

I should have said thank you.

Instead, I heard the shape beneath her words wrong. Yellow Circle prodigy offering aid to Green Circle transfer after a public match. A beautiful gesture. A generous one. The sort of thing people might admire if they saw.

The sort of thing that could turn me into another detail in someone else's reputation.

Haru: "You don't have to prove anything." Elira's hands quieted.

Elira: "Prove?"

I knew immediately that I had chosen the wrong word.

Knowing did not undo it.

Haru: "People are watching."

Elira looked down the empty corridor. Then back at me.

Elira: "No one is here."

Haru: "People don't need to be present to use what happened."

Her expression changed again, quieter this time.

Elira: "Is that what you think I'm doing?"

Haru: "I don't know what you're doing." The answer was honest.

Too honest in the wrong direction.

Elira folded the healer cloth once.

Elira: "I was trying to give you something clean before you walked back into a school full of people deciding what your torn sleeve means." I said nothing.

She held the cloth out anyway.

She neither stepped closer nor withdrew it.

Elira: "You can take it without accepting anything else."

That sentence should not have worked on me. It did. I took the cloth.

Our fingers did not touch.

She made sure of that.

The care in the distance was worse than the care in the gift.

Haru: "Thank you." Elira nodded.

For a moment, I thought that might repair it. Then she said:

Elira: "You are very careful about not owing anyone."

I looked at her too quickly.

Her eyes were steady, but not triumphant. She had not said it to win.

That made it more dangerous.

Haru: "Owing people is complicated."

Elira: "So is refusing every kindness before it becomes one."

There was no accusation in her voice.

That was the part that stayed. She turned to leave.

I should have said something better.

I had several options. Tactical, clean, harmless.

Instead, I held the healer cloth in my numb fingers and watched her walk back toward the gold-lit hall.

She paused once at the corner. Not looking back.

Elira: "I did not stand up because you scored on Leon."

My grip tightened around the cloth.

Elira: "I stood up because you looked hurt and pretended not to be." Then she left.

The corridor became quiet again.

I wrapped the cloth around my arm after she was gone. It was unnecessary. It helped. That made it worse.

## Coda: The Gate Remembered

After Elira left, the academy gate behind me gave one quiet chime. No student nearby reacted. The sound was too small for ceremony and too deliberate for wind. I turned.

A hairline of light ran through the old crest above the arch. Green first. Then Blue. Then Red. Then Yellow. Four obedient colors, the approved ladder of the world, bright enough to be harmless. Beneath them, where stone should have stayed stone, a fifth ring pressed once from inside the wall and vanished before my false crest could answer.

The gate had not welcomed me. It had counted me.

A pair of first-years hurried past, laughing too loudly about dorm assignments and tournament rumors. One of them glanced at my Green Circle, dismissed me, and kept walking. Ordinary cruelty. Useful camouflage.

I unwound Elira's healer cloth, folded it into my pocket, and took more care than I meant to show.

Somewhere inside Aurelis, old magic had noticed the lie I wore.

Somewhere else, a girl with gold eyes had noticed the person under it.

I was not sure which discovery frightened me more.

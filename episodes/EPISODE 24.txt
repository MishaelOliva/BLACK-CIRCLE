# Episode 24: **When Restraint Breaks the Floor**

The floor opened because I asked it to. I had to keep that truth instead of calling it an accident, an instinct, or the Black Circle acting on its own.

I asked Aurelis for a door, and the old academy answered.

Stone did not split right away. Aurelis heard me, and the lower channel answered, with twelve heartbeats of danger caught between them.

At the first heartbeat, the flagstones remembered the old observatory.

At the third, the false Green Circle veil at my wrist slipped out of rhythm.

At the sixth, the black ring beneath my boots turned visible to everyone close enough to see the floor.

At the ninth, pain drove through my sternum so sharply I nearly lost the question I was trying to ask.

Open below, without consuming, punishing, or claiming. Open below.

Stone split beneath my feet in a perfect black ring. Down, straight past the rescued children, Maelin's healer cots, and Rhea's witness station, dropping straight into the old observatory's lower channels where older magic from before the colored Circles still remembered what a safeguard had been before institutions tried to own one.

Dren grabbed Benn Arlow by the shoulder and hauled the bandaged first-year back from the crumbling rim.

Halden swore with quiet, academic precision.

On the tenth heartbeat, black-silver pressure rose behind my shoulders. Dren saw it and went very still, counting the seconds.

Instructor Dren: "Aerlen. Release or anchor."

I could not release. The moving hall had sealed with Elira inside.

The eleventh heartbeat broke blood under one of my fingernails.

The twelfth widened the ring. Rhea shouted three orders, and none of them reached me clearly over the roar of shifting stone. The moving hall had closed with Elira inside, and that single fact tried to swallow the rest of the world.

Then the pendant at her throat flashed once through the broken connection. Open rings. A question.

It carried neither command nor permission. It only asked what I was doing.

The Black Circle stopped widening, held back by Elira's question before my panic could disguise itself as mercy.

Haru: "I am not entering the hall."

My voice sounded too quiet for the pressure humming beneath the stones.

Haru: "I am opening the lower channel."

Halden stepped up to the edge of the ring, keeping both hands where Dren could see them.

Halden: "For what purpose?"

The question was not about rules. It was a lifeline back to being a person, and I caught hold of it.

Haru: "To give them exits the hall did not design."

Rhea: "Recorded. Dren, perimeter. Maelin, students. Valen, with me."

The rescued children were crying, coughing, and clinging to blankets and one another. Sela would not let go of empty air where Mira's hand had been, whispering her name as if calling could turn into a rope.

Iven sat upright on his cot, eyes fixed on the black opening.

Iven: "The senior is Aric."

He swallowed hard as Halden turned toward him.

Iven: "Aric Dain. Yellow Circle. Third year. He teaches first-years how not to cry when instructors watch."

Darian's jaw tightened beside Halden.

Iven: "He said volunteering is the only way to choose what Noctis takes."

Cael's shadow hung over the court like a warning. Noctis taught children that their only choice was picking how they got used, and Elira was trapped inside with that doctrine.

I lowered one hand into the black ring. Cold pressure bit my palm, tasting of ozone and old metal. Old Aurelis answered without sound.

**SAFEGUARD RESPONSE REQUESTED**

Haru: "Not alone."

The ring pulsed under my knuckles.

**DEFINE WITNESS**

I looked across the practice court: at Rhea and Senna's remote seal, at Halden's ink-stained fingers, at Valen's pale face, at Dren standing between the rescued children and the drop, at Maelin wrapping Sela in a blanket while Nera and Darian steadied the trembling younger children behind her, and at Iven, who had survived being taken and still gave us a name.

Haru: "Everyone who can say no."

Sela lifted her head. Her face was wet and pale, and she trembled harder as she found her voice.

Sela: "No."

The Black Circle paused. Every adult in the court turned toward her.

Sela: "Don't take us."

The words struck deeper than any Council motion. I turned completely toward her, away from the opening.

Haru: "I won't."

Sela did not relax, and I was glad of it. Trust handed over too quickly after rooms like Noctis would have been another wound.

Haru: "If the Black Circle moves toward you without someone you trust saying why, you can say no again."

Sela's eyes moved to Rhea.

Rhea: "Recognized."

She looked to Maelin.

Maelin: "Very recognized."

Then, after a beat, she looked to Dren. The grizzled instructor placed one calloused hand over his chest with dry solemnity.

Instructor Dren: "No one crosses this line without getting through me first."

Sela let out a shaky breath. The Black Circle steadied, accepting the boundary.

The descent opened into a spiral stone stair. No one cheered, and we went down.

Maelin stayed upstairs with the rescued children because a rescue that abandoned the rescued was only spectacle. Dren stayed at the rim to guard the living door. Rhea held the upper command perimeter while sending her council seal down ahead of us, with Senna's remote crystal hovering beside it. Halden, Valen, Nera, Darian, Iven, and I descended.

Iven should not have come, and Maelin told him so twelve times before we reached the third turn. He came because the moving hall had already marked him as returned, making him less useful as bait, because Aric had taught him how not to cry, and because he wanted Aric to learn something better.

We reached the lower channel beneath the observatory. The space below was no chamber, only a wound the old stonework had learned to keep open.

Seven bridges crossed an impossible vertical dark. Along each span, old witness sigils glowed in broken sequences: some belonging to Aurelis, some to schools that no longer existed, and some to Circle Zero doctrine so old my body recognized them before my mind caught up.

At the center hung the moving hall, suspended like a tangled knot of black glass, gold light, green sparks, blue water, red flame, and white Noctis thread.

Through fractured windows inside, I saw Elira standing before a Yellow Circle student older than the rescued children. Aric Dain had dark hair, hollow cheeks, and the composed posture of someone praised for disappearing. His Yellow crest burned cleanly at his wrist. Lucien stood just behind him without touching him; he did not need to, because Aric had learned to stand where the hand would be.

Elira's voice carried down the lower channel.

Elira: "Volunteering under a doctrine that punishes refusal is not consent."

Aric: "It is the only consent Noctis recognizes."

Mira and Finn stood to Elira's left, while Aira and Cassian held the line to her right, Cassian keeping his blistered right palm wrapped in a strip of torn sleeve. Leon's water mirror carried their voices outward until the lower channel aligned enough for us to hear without turning the hall into proof of my intrusion.

Lucien looked through the fracture straight at me.

Lucien: "You opened the floor."

Haru: "I did."

The cold air bit my lungs, steadying my pulse.

Lucien: "So much for restraint."

Haru: "Restraint is not paralysis."

The bridges answered, seven old sigils brightening along the stone. Halden leaned over the rail, his voice barely above a whisper.

Halden: "That phrase is in the record."

Valen: "Before the erasure."

The moving hall shuddered. Lucien's eyes narrowed; he had expected rage and planned for rescue as an invasion, never expecting the old system to recognize a distinction he did not believe in.

Iven stepped to the edge of the nearest bridge, and Valen caught her breath beside him.

Iven: "Aric!"

Inside the hall, Aric flinched, just enough to prove a person still lived under the posture.

Aric: "You should be safe."

Iven: "I am not safe if you are spent."

Aric's Yellow crest flickered.

Lucien: "First-years are emotionally imprecise."

Darian stepped forward to the bridge rail beside me, facing Lucien across the fracture.

Darian: "You hide behind children and call them imprecise because adults keep leaving you fewer places to stand."

Lucien looked at Darian as if he had forgotten his fellow Noctis student existed and resented the reminder. Every crack in his poise mattered.

Elira stepped closer to Aric. Maelin would have screamed if she had seen how close, and perhaps she did through the upper relay.

Elira: "You can leave with us."

Aric smiled faintly. I had not seen an expression that sad since Cael's funeral.

Aric: "If I leave, the hall follows the others. It needs a final witness to close."

Nera's voice cut across the lower channel from the bridge beside us, raw and immediate.

Nera: "No. That is the lie. Final witness means final lock, not final death."

Aric blinked, staring at her.

Nera: "They changed the lesson. They always change the lesson after making sure only frightened students remember the old words."

Valen gripped the bridge rail beside me.

Valen: "She's right."

Halden: "How do you close it?"

Valen: "Not from inside."

Every eye turned toward me, and the Black Circle waited. Lucien smiled again because he saw the trap: if I closed the moving hall, he would claim Black Circle ownership over every witness inside; if I did nothing, he would spend Aric or seal the others in.

Elira looked through the fracture at me, asking rather than pleading. I remembered her warning from the morning about practice, and looked away from her toward Iven.

Haru: "Can you describe the hall's closing lesson?"

Iven wiped his face with his sleeve.

Iven: "We say: a loyal hall closes when the final witness kneels."

Beside me on the bridge, Nera shook her head sharply.

Nera: "No. The old form."

Iven squeezed his eyes shut, forcing the memory past years of drills.

Iven: "A lawful hall closes when the final witness is counted."

Finn shouted from inside the hall.

Finn: "Counted by whom?"

Silence held for a beat before Mira answered.

Mira: "By everyone who would have used them."

The green sparks inside the hall flared.

Aira: "Mira, that is either brilliant or about to get us all killed."

Mira: "Both?"

Cassian: "Respectable answer."

We built the count across the bridges and through the water mirror. Rhea counted Aric as a protected student. Senna counted him as a Council witness. Nera counted him as not property. Darian counted him as no proof of Noctis honor. Iven counted him as the boy who had taught him how not to cry and now deserved to learn how. Mira counted him as a person with a name, Finn counted the absence around him as filled, Aira counted every threat disguised as advice used to make him volunteer, Leon and Cassian counted the cold and danger that made saying no unsafe, and Elira counted his choice as forced—but still his to take back.

Then they all looked at me, with Lucien watching and hungry for the Black Circle to claim final authority.

I stepped back from the bridge rail.

Haru: "I do not count him last."

The hall froze, and Lucien's smile vanished.

Haru: "He counts himself."

Aric stared across the fracture as the Yellow crest at his wrist shook.

Lucien: "He has already chosen."

Elira: "Then he can choose again."

Aric's knees buckled. He did not kneel, though he nearly fell. Elira caught him by the sleeve rather than the wrist, giving him just enough support to refuse. Leon pushed his water mirror harder to carry the whisper across the void.

Aric: "I am Aric Dain."

The moving hall cracked from foundation to ceiling.

Aric: "I am not the final witness."

Lucien moved without casting, his bare hand snapping out fast enough to remind us he was never only doctrine. He reached straight for Aric's crest.

Elira struck him across the face with the palm of her hand. The sound cracked across the lower channel, and every witness heard it. Lucien stood motionless, shock breaking across his face for the first time while Elira's hand trembled.

Elira: "Do not touch him."

The moving hall shattered.

Shards of black glass tore across the room, one slicing open Elira's palm as she dragged Aric clear of the collapse. Elira, Mira, Finn, Aira, Leon, Cassian, and Aric spilled outward through the shattered Noctis spells toward the drop.

I threw the Black Circle out beneath them as a catching net, never closing over them, holding a boundary that caught without claiming. All seven landed across the seven bridges in gasping heaps, alive and unowned.

Lucien did not fall. He stood on a suspended shard of black glass above the dark, one hand pressed against his struck cheek, and let an angry smile pull at his mouth.

Lucien: "There she is."

The shard vanished. In the empty space where the moving hall had hung, the lower channel revealed a door marked with a symbol no modern Circle system used: a black ring, split open by a crown.

No one touched the door.

The first victory looked like that: exhausted students, broken doctrine, rescued witnesses, and an ancient symbol waiting like a drawn knife while nobody rushed forward. We stood on the seven bridges beneath Aurelis and breathed. Some of us cried, and some pretended not to.

Aric Dain sat on the third bridge with a blanket around his shoulders and Elira beside him. Her slap still burned in my memory, not out of helplessness, but refusal. No spell could have been cleaner than a human hand raised to stop a boy from being reclaimed.

Twelve minutes later, the lower-channel lift rattled down to the bridge. Maelin stepped off with three healer aides, arms full of blankets, and Rhea right behind her after leaving Dren at the upper rim. Sela and Pella Orthis hurried off beside them, along with the rest of the rescued Noctis children; Krail's Council clerks had already swarmed the upper court to separate the minors for statements, and Maelin had flatly refused to let bureaucrats near her patients while Sela refused to leave Mira's sight.

Maelin took one look at the cracked black ring and immediately began scolding everyone still breathing, which was how I knew we might survive the hour.

Maelin: "You opened a floor."

Haru: "I did."

I did not look away from her.

Maelin: "You went down into an ancient lower channel with a barely recovered patient, a crowd of witnesses, and an active spell breach."

Haru: "I did not bring the minors."

Maelin gave me a flat stare.

Haru: "That was not a defense."

Elira let out a small, real laugh, and Maelin pointed straight at her.

Maelin: "You. Sitting. And give me that cut hand before you bleed on my floor."

Elira was already sitting.

Maelin: "More obediently."

Elira held out her bleeding palm with grave dignity while Maelin wound gauze tightly around it, and even Aric smiled faintly.

The door waited beyond them, black ring and split crown gleaming faintly in the dark. Valen and Halden studied it from three paces away with the restraint of scholars smelling catastrophe and itching to poke it with ink. Rhea locked her perimeter seal across the central span while Senna's remote crystal recorded from above; Krail had already tried twice to force his way down through legal relay and been denied twice while the rescue was still underway.

Aira sat on the flagstones sorting damp pages into stacks. Leon brought water flasks around to the rescued students. Cassian warmed the bridges without open flame because several Noctis children flinched at sudden fire. Mira and Finn sat with Sela, who kept a tight grip on Mira's sleeve, while Nera and Darian spoke quietly with Aric, offering water and names before any questions.

The crown inside the black ring watched all of it.

Halden: "This predates the Circle Zero Observatory."

Valen: "Or sits under it as root doctrine."

Halden: "The crown suggests authority."

Valen: "Or the failure of authority."

Rhea: "Translate faster."

Valen brushed her fingers through the air near the symbol without touching the stone. Gold-white script surfaced around the frame, and I felt the vibration behind my sternum before I read it: cold, ozone, and metal.

**REGALIA PROTOCOL**

The words meant nothing to me, yet my body hated them on sight. Valen went pale, her whisper barely carrying over the stone.

Valen: "No."

Halden: "You know it?"

Valen looked at me as if she had just found my name written in a book that was supposed to have burned.

Valen: "Regalia was an old blueprint behind how Circles were first sorted. Before colors became a ladder, before Yellow was named peak, before Black was erased."

Elira pushed to her feet despite Maelin's warning hand.

Elira: "What did it do?"

Valen: "It did not rank power."

The door brightened.

Valen: "It assigned authority over crisis response."

My mouth went dry.

Haru: "Safeguards."

Valen nodded slowly.

Valen: "And sovereigns."

The word moved through the lower channel like a draft from an unsealed tomb. Neither I nor the Black Circle wanted anything to do with it. Senna's remote seal sharpened overhead.

Senna: "Define sovereign."

Valen: "Most references were destroyed or redacted into myth. The surviving fragments suggest that when Circle Zero safeguards failed, certain institutions tried to build a command rank above witnesses—a crisis crown with the power to order safeguards around during a disaster."

Krail would love that. Lucien would become it if the world let him, or force the world to accuse me of wanting it.

The door eased open by one inch. Nobody moved. Then a voice came through the crack, young, female, and terrified.

Voice: "Please tell me someone is there."

The rescued Noctis students froze. Iven stood up.

Iven: "That is Pella."

Pella Orthis, Reserve Eight, one of the younger children we had pulled from the moving hall, sat beside Maelin under a blanket and stared at the door in horror.

Pella: "That is me."

The voice behind the door sobbed.

Voice: "I do not want to be copied anymore."

Silence dropped over the lower channel. Finn stared at the opening.

Finn: "Not a door."

Mira's face drained of color, and Halden swore under his breath.

Mira: "Archive."

The Regalia door opened into stored student impressions: not souls, not living minds, but forced copies carved out of fear, obedience drills, and conduit strain. Noctis had not only moved children; it had made rehearsals of them, practice bodies that could be spent in argument or ritual without technically killing anyone.

The pendant at Elira's throat warmed so fiercely light bled between her fingers.

Elira: "We are opening it."

Maelin: "No."

Elira: "Not alone, and not right now. But we are opening it."

I stared at the door, the Black Circle sitting completely still inside me.

Haru: "If Regalia assigns crisis authority, opening it may trigger a claim."

Rhea: "Over whom?"

Valen: "Everyone tied to the old protocol."

Halden looked at me, then at Elira, then across at the rescued students.

Halden: "Potentially all of them."

Senna: "Then we seal the site and petition the full Council."

Krail's voice forced its way through a side relay at last.

Krail: "Agreed. And Haru Aerlen is to be removed from proximity to the site immediately."

Rhea: "No."

Krail: "President Valmont, this is beyond student council authority."

Rhea: "Then be grateful I am not relying on it."

She turned her seal toward Senna's crystal.

Rhea: "Aurelis requests shared emergency custody of the Regalia site under witness protection, with no single institution, official, or extra-classification individual granted solitary access."

Senna: "Granted provisionally."

Krail: "You cannot grant custody of an ancient pre-Circle command system to children and compromised faculty."

Senna's voice cooled to frost.

Senna: "I can grant temporary protection of endangered students and evidence during an ongoing Noctis breach. I just did."

Krail's relay cut out. Aira leaned toward Rhea, whispering.

Aira: "I am naming my next notebook Senna."

Leon: "Please don't."

The door remained open by one inch. Behind it, the copied Pella wept, while the real Pella shook under her blanket with her face buried in Maelin's sleeve. Maelin stared at the door with a healer's quiet hatred.

Maelin: "Can we quiet it without entering?"

Valen: "Maybe."

Elira unclasped the chain at her throat.

Elira: "Use my pendant."

Haru: "Elira."

She looked straight at me.

Elira: "Not as bait, as a consent filter. Those copies were made without their consent. The crystal asks before it carries. Let it ask."

The idea was reckless, and completely right.

We built the temporary quieting array at the center bridge. Elira placed her pendant above Rhea's open council seal while Valen anchored old restoration grammar around it and Halden added safety limits. Aira drafted the questions in plain language first, muttering that ancient systems kept getting poetic right before people died.

Do you know you are a copy? Do you want to continue speaking, or do you want quiet? Do you want your living source notified? Do you refuse use?

One by one, the voices behind the door answered. Some knew what they were, and some did not; some asked for quiet, while others wanted to keep speaking until someone wrote their names down. Mira and Finn wrote until their fingers cramped, and Pella asked to hold the transcript of her own copy's answers. No one denied her.

The lower-channel lift rattled against the bridge again as a healer aide hauled down extra cots, water flasks, and warming wraps. Krail's relay forced itself back into Senna's channel behind the aide. Rhea angled his image away from the rescued children, leaving it open only as a recorded witness line, while Maelin glanced over her shoulder from Pella's side.

Maelin: "If you issue one order that makes my patients stand longer, I will enter it into the medical record with adjectives."

Near the fourth bridge, Aric sat with Iven and Sela, staring at the door like someone recognizing a chapter from a book he had never been allowed to finish. Elira left Maelin's side before Maelin could object, crossed to him, and sat beside him rather than standing over him.

Elira: "You said Noctis has a crown lesson."

Aric's throat worked.

Aric: "Senior conduit track. They called it command mercy."

The phrase was ugly enough that even Krail's projection lost its appetite for a moment.

Aric: "They taught that witnesses are needed in ordinary crises because ordinary people want comfort. In a final crisis, comfort becomes delay, and delay becomes death. So one authority has to command the safeguard."

Haru: "And the conduit?"

He looked at me with an apology on his face for a lesson he had never written.

Aric: "The conduit proves the command was obeyed."

Nera closed her eyes, and Darian turned away. Cael's absence pressed down on every bridge; Votive Return had not been a separate horror, only practice for a crown.

Elira: "Did Lucien teach it?"

Aric shook his head.

Aric: "No. He corrected it. He said the old lesson was cowardly because it waited for a final crisis. He said the superior student creates the crisis where command becomes obvious."

There he was: Lucien Voss, not merely cruel, but a boy so convinced rank revealed truth that he would manufacture a crisis just to prove someone deserved to stand above it.

Krail's projection had gone very still, and Senna caught it immediately.

Senna: "Councilor?"

Krail: "Do not mistake my concern about Aerlen for sympathy with Voss."

Senna: "Then stop using arguments his doctrine can open."

The sentence landed hard enough that the relay light bent, and Aira's pen gave a small, triumphant scratch.

The Regalia door widened another fraction in answer. This time, many voices whispered together, asking questions instead of pleading: Am I used? Am I counted? Am I still a child if I am only a copy of fear? Does my living body know I am here? Can a copy refuse?

The rescued students heard versions of themselves close enough to cut. Pella began sobbing, Sela pressed both hands over her ears, and Iven's face went blank until Aric laid a hand on his shoulder, then froze.

Aric: "May I?"

Iven nodded. Late consent, still repaired.

Maelin ordered the younger students moved farther back along the bridges, and nobody argued; rescue did not mean forcing children to listen to copies of their own terror. Elira's pendant lay in the center of the quieting array, asking one question at a time. When one voice asked for food, Mira broke down crying while writing the answer, and Finn took over the line until her hand steadied.

The Black Circle remained quiet through all of it, less like control than grief learning not to interrupt.

Aric asked quietly if copies could be guilty.

Nera: "No."

Darian: "Institutions can."

The door did not shut, though the crying softened. Then one final voice came through: male, older, and amused. Not Lucien.

Voice: "So the Black Circle returns before the crown is claimed. How inconvenient."

Valen went rigid.

Halden: "Identify yourself."

The voice laughed.

Voice: "No. Identification is a door, and I have lived too long by refusing doors."

The black crown symbol brightened.

Voice: "Tell Haru Aerlen this: Lucien Voss is not the first student to mistake Dominion for sovereignty. He is only the most recent child to find the handle."

My skin went cold.

Haru: "Who built Regalia?"

The voice paused, and when it answered, every bridge light dimmed.

Voice: "The people who feared what witnesses could do when safeguards listened to them."

The door slammed shut. Across the lower channel, every open ring went dark, and a single word burned above the crown.

**ASCENDANT**

No public crystal answered yet. Rhea sealed the word inside the local record before anyone could broadcast it.

Rhea: "Local result only. Shared custody remains. No one enters alone, and no one announces a throne while rescued children are still waiting for beds."

Senna: "Provisionally sustained. Move the patients."

The Regalia door stayed closed under shared guard as we brought the living back up toward the infirmary. Elira came to stand beside me with her cut hand wrapped, the pendant returned to her throat, and her eyes bright with exhaustion and anger.

Elira: "Then tomorrow we refuse it publicly."

Haru: "Tomorrow?"

She looked across the bridges, the rescued students, the closed door, and the people who had not run from me even when every ancient symbol in the room wanted to turn my name into a crown.

Elira: "Tonight we take care of the living."

She scared systems more than I did for exactly that reason.

Taking care of the living turned out to be far messier than refusing a crown. It meant mismatched blankets, forty-two healer cots and mattresses commandeered across three departments, student council runners carrying soup through warded corridors, and Cassian learning quickly that terrified children rarely wanted heroic speeches but usually accepted warm bread without questions.

Maelin refused to stack the rescued Noctis students into a single ward.

Maelin: "Grouping every recovered conduit together is how lazy institutions make one target and call it efficiency."

Rhea updated the room assignments before anyone argued. The children were placed in adjoining rooms with open doors, visible exits, and witness rotations chosen by comfort rather than rank. Sela asked for Mira. Tovin asked for someone quiet, so Finn volunteered and sat in the corner so still he looked like furniture with a pulse. Pella Orthis asked for Maelin and held onto the healer's sleeve for twenty minutes. Iven asked for Nera near the door, panicked when she first stepped across the sill, then asked her not to leave.

Nera stayed right in the doorway until Iven spoke.

Iven: "Inside is okay."

He still refused to release his academy scarf. Tunnel dust had turned it gray, one end was half-frozen, and the knot was tangled badly enough that Cassian regarded it with professional respect. When Maelin lifted a clean warming wrap, Iven flinched back, and she stopped at once.

Maelin: "Keep the scarf. May I put the wrap over it?"

Iven studied her face, the open doorway, and Nera, his fingers clamped around the ruined knot, before finally nodding. Maelin settled the wrap over his shoulders without brushing the scarf. Across the room, Leon looked away, then set his jaw and forced himself to look back.

Elira paused in the doorway with frost drying along the hem of her coat.

Elira: "At Noctis, does a new yes erase the old one?"

Iven: "Usually."

Elira: "Here it can sit beside it."

Iven pinched one fold of the clean wrap between two fingers while his other hand kept hold of the scarf. Then Elira stepped quietly inside, and Nera took the chair closest to the doorway, careful never to sit between Iven and the exit.

The night moved like that: doors, questions, and small permissions that never made heroic summaries and mattered all the more for it.

I stayed in the central corridor under Halden's observation because Regalia had written **ASCENDANT** beside my name and nobody with sense wanted me left alone with that word. Dren guarded the far end of the hall. Rhea worked at a table by the nurse station while Senna's remote crystal hovered dimmed above the files. Aira sat on the floor with her back against the wall, sorting notes into four neat stacks:

**Living Students**

**Copied Impressions**

**Regalia Door**

**Lucien Being The Worst**

Rhea spotted the last label.

Rhea: "Rename it."

Aira: "It is accurate."

Rhea: "Rename it accurately in court language."

Aira sighed and crossed it out. The new label read:

**Lucien Voss: Hostile Doctrine Author / Active Cowardice**

Rhea stared at it.

Aira: "Compromise."

Rhea let it stand, which said enough about the night.

Elira kept checking room after room until Maelin caught her by the shoulder and turned her around like an uncooperative compass.

Maelin: "Your room."

Elira: "I am not..."

Maelin: "Your room."

Elira glanced toward me, and I stayed put.

Haru: "She's right."

Elira's eyes narrowed.

Elira: "I dislike when you practice against me."

Maelin: "Good. You can dislike it horizontally."

Elira was escorted to a side room with a couch, a cot, and window wards checked by three separate witnesses. I followed only after she asked, and after Halden noted the move with such solemnity that Elira threatened to make him log the emotional texture of his own discomfort. He looked briefly tempted, then stayed in the hall.

Inside the side room, the infirmary clamor softened to human scale: a child asking for water, Leon arguing quietly with a heater charm, Mira telling Sela that freckles were not a symptom, Cassian being banned from cooking unsupervised, and Aira sneezing ink dust.

Elira sank onto the couch and finally let the exhaustion reach her face. I stayed by the door until she looked up at me.

Elira: "If you stand there like a punishment, I will throw a pillow."

There were no pillows within reach, so I sat beside her anyway. She leaned into me slowly, testing her bruised shoulder, her bandaged hand, and the weight of the day.

Haru: "Too much?"

Elira: "Everything is too much."

My hands stopped moving, and she caught my sleeve.

Elira: "That was not a no."

I let my shoulder relax, and she rested her head against it.

Elira: "When I said we take care of the living, I meant you too."

Haru: "I am being observed."

Elira: "That is not the same as being cared for."

Outside the door, Halden cleared his throat once. Elira raised her voice without lifting her head.

Elira: "That was not criticism, Professor."

Halden's voice came from the hallway.

Halden: "Accurate, though."

I almost smiled, and Elira did, before the smile faded.

Elira: "You heard the copied voices."

Haru: "Every word."

The cold pulse behind my ribs stayed quiet.

Elira: "Did any of them feel like Cael?"

The question found the exact bruise it meant to test. I closed my eyes.

Haru: "No."

Her fingers tightened on mine.

Haru: "I kept listening for him. Which makes no sense. Regalia pulled copied fear from living students. Cael is dead."

The word still refused to fit in my mouth, and Elira did not try to rescue me from saying it.

Haru: "But I listened anyway."

She leaned closer.

Elira: "So did I."

For a long while, neither of us spoke. The infirmary breathed around us: footsteps, water cups, soft crying, and people who had not been saved completely yet, only kept out of reach of the hands trying to take them back.

At midnight, Aira knocked on the doorframe, her eyes red though she pretended they were not.

Aira: "We have rotations through dawn. Rhea is threatening paperwork at anyone who calls them guard shifts."

Elira: "What are they called?"

Aira checked her clipboard.

Aira: "Presence intervals."

Haru: "That sounds like Rhea."

Aira: "It sounds like Rhea lost an argument with Maelin and named the bruise."

Elira's quiet laugh sounded almost painful. Aira glanced at both of us, then down at our joined hands, and for once skipped the joke.

Aira: "Good."

Then she left, letting the word stay behind her.

When Elira finally slept, I stopped counting her breaths and listened to the hallway instead: footsteps, water cups, Maelin's low murmur, Rhea's pen scratching paper, Nera asking quietly if she could sit by the doorway, and Iven letting her. Under Aurelis, Regalia waited, while up here no throne formed, only chairs pulled beside beds.

That should have been too small for an ancient protocol to register, yet the floor beneath us seemed to listen.

Just after midnight, when the presence intervals had settled into something close to rest, every chair in the east infirmary turned one inch toward the floor. Nobody touched them. Wood scraped stone across a dozen rooms at once.

The rescued students woke faster than the adults; Noctis training recognized shifting rooms as danger long before ordinary sleep caught up. Sela sat upright reaching for Mira's sleeve, caught herself halfway, and waited until Mira woke too.

Mira: "You can."

Sela took the sleeve lightly, enough to ground herself. Across the hall, Iven's thermal charm flared amber against his chest. Nera, dozing in the far chair near the doorway because Tovin had not invited her closer, opened her eyes and lifted both hands where everyone could see them.

Nera: "No binding."

Tovin's breathing slowed by a fraction. Maelin marched out of the supply alcove with her hair half-unpinned and murder in her eyes.

Maelin: "If this is another Council inquiry, I am resigning from politeness."

The floor answered in arrangement rather than words. Chairs pulled away from the beds and slid into a circle down the corridor: plain chairs, patient chairs, companion chairs. The repaired wooden chair from Cael's recovery room dragged itself last, leaving a thin track in the corridor dust.

Elira stood before I could worry over whether she should, and I rose beside her without trying to stop her.

Haru: "Do you want my arm?"

Elira: "Please."

She took my arm, leaning her weight against me for a heartbeat until her balance held, and the chairs stopped moving.

Aira appeared in a doorway with a blanket around her shoulders and a pencil tucked behind one ear.

Aira: "Everyone please note that asking before support appears to be structurally significant and also personally irritating to ancient furniture."

Rhea arrived three seconds later, fully dressed as though she slept like a legal objection waiting to happen.

Rhea: "Status."

Senna's remote crystal flickered above the hall.

Senna: "Regalia resonance through care furniture. No breach yet."

Valen hurried through the archive-side door with crooked glasses and a pale face.

Valen: "To the protocol, this isn't furniture. It's a map of who stands beside whom."

Leon blinked from the doorway.

Leon: "It is chairs."

Valen: "Yes, and that is the point."

The floor pulse deepened, throwing the chairs' shadows upward. Above them, light gathered into the outline of a throne and failed three times in a row. Each time, one chair refused to align by a few degrees: the repaired companion chair would not twist into an armrest, Sela's bedside chair would not rise above the rest, Iven's far chair stayed apart because Tovin had chosen that distance, and Elira's bench refused to pull free of the wall while she leaned on me.

Regalia had no idea what to do with people choosing their own distance, and it hated it.

The air above the chairs split open into copied impressions: Sela's fear, Tovin's cold, Iven's apology, Mira's trembling courage, Nera's guilt, Darian's defection, and Elira's death-breath stolen from a memory neither of us had consented to share.

My Black Circle surged so violently the corridor lamps went black. Elira's fingers dug into my arm.

Elira: "Haru."

I stopped, barely holding the edge back. The copied breath vanished before it could solidify into an image.

The pendant at Elira's throat burned black-gold, acting as a filter instead of a wall. Rather than blocking every memory, the crystal asked the question Dominion Severance had tried to erase: who consented to this being used?

The copied voices faltered. Awake and shaking, Sela whispered into the dark.

Sela: "Mine can stay if it helps show what they did."

Tovin looked up from his knees.

Tovin: "Not mine."

His fear dissolved on the air. Iven clutched Cael's charm in both hands.

Iven: "Mine can say I was scared. Not that I owed pain."

The impression shifted from apology to bare fear, terrible and clean and useless as debt. Nera stared at the filtering light as if Elira had opened a door in a language she thought had been lost.

Nera: "Consent filter."

Elira's breath shook.

Elira: "Trying."

Maelin slid a chair behind her, and Elira sat without arguing. Aira scribbled that down too, possibly for medical history, possibly for future leverage.

The half-formed throne thinned, so Regalia tried another route. The remaining impressions gathered into a faceless child-shaped figure standing inside the circle of chairs, speaking with dozens of overlapping voices.

Regalia: "AUTHORITY REQUIRED TO PREVENT RECURRENCE."

The words vibrated inside the bones of the corridor. Rhea stepped forward.

Rhea: "Define authority."

Regalia: "COMMAND CAPACITY. SUCCESSION CAPACITY. FINAL OVERRIDE."

Krail's relay projection flared to life at the far end of the corridor, bright, official, and disastrously eager.

Krail: "Magistra Voss, President Valmont, do not engage. This may be an opportunity to define the Black Circle's official role under Council supervision."

Aira stared at the glowing image.

Aira: "You heard final override and thought opportunity."

Krail: "Miss Solen..."

Senna's crystal sliced across his transmission.

Senna: "Councilor, you are intruding on an active resonance event in a medical ward."

Krail refused to back down.

Krail: "Regalia Protocol may be the first real chain of command strong enough to bind an extra-classification risk."

Every chair in the corridor turned toward him. For one dizzying second, I thought the protocol agreed. Then the repaired companion chair scraped backward, away from Krail's projection, and Sela's, Iven's, Tovin's, and Mira's chairs followed it. They were not fleeing; they were refusing to face him.

Valen watched the chairs lock into place.

Valen: "It is comparing authority models."

Regalia spoke again.

Regalia: "COUNCIL COMMAND PROPOSED."

The corridor filled with ghostly images of seals, record rails, emergency motions, containment orders, and proximity reviews, words polished until cruelty could pass through them dry. Krail's jaw tightened as he recognized himself in the mirror the old system held up.

Regalia: "NOCTIS COMMAND PROPOSED."

The light went bitter cold, flashing rows, bells, winter fields, binding rings, and children kneeling because warmth had been rationed. Nera made a choked sound, and Darian stepped close enough for his sleeve to brush hers, waiting until she leaned into the contact.

Regalia: "BLACK CIRCLE COMMAND PROPOSED."

Every eye turned to me, and the corridor vanished.

For a heartbeat, I stood alone inside a throne room built of eclipse light, with no walls, no witnesses, no Council, no Noctis, and nobody close enough to be hurt if I decided everything by myself. The temptation waiting there was not power, only simplicity: an end to arguments, an end to asking, an end to watching Elira bleed because other people kept building rooms that reached for her.

My hands shook as the throne waited. The worst part was how quiet and merciful it looked.

Elira's voice cut across the darkness.

Elira: "Haru Aerlen."

A name, not a myth. The throne flickered. Aira's voice followed right behind hers.

Aira: "If you sit down, I am throwing every notebook I own at your head."

Rhea: "For the record, that is not an officially sanctioned deterrent."

Cassian: "Morally strong, though."

Leon: "Focus."

The throne cracked along its edges because their voices were ordinary, badly timed, and stubbornly human. I turned my back on it.

Haru: "No."

Regalia: "REFUSAL WITHOUT SUCCESSOR CREATES INSTABILITY."

Haru: "Then be unstable."

The throne room shattered. I stumbled back into the infirmary corridor, breathing hard with Elira's hand gripping my arm and every witness crowded around me.

Regalia's faceless figure tilted its head.

Regalia: "SUCCESSOR REQUIRED."

The corridor did what frightened rooms always did first: everyone turned toward Rhea, toward Krail's relay, and toward me, pulled by the old lie that when a room is scared enough, someone large enough should answer for everyone else. The Black Circle stirred under my skin, and Elira's hand tightened on my sleeve, reminding me I was not the only answer in the hall.

Rhea raised her seal, yet before she could speak, Sela stood up. Her infirmary blanket slipped from one shoulder, and when Mira reached to pull it back up, Sela shook her head. She stared straight at the Regalia figure, her voice small enough that the corridor fell silent to catch it.

Sela: "No."

Regalia gave no answer. Sela swallowed, then kept speaking.

Sela: "At Noctis, they ask who is strongest when they already know who they want us to obey."

Her eyes widened, not from fear, but because the sentence had left her mouth and the ceiling had not collapsed.

Sela: "If you need a successor, you are asking the wrong question."

Regalia brightened.

Regalia: "AUTHORITY REQUIRED TO PREVENT RECURRENCE."

Sela flinched, and Mira stepped up shoulder-to-shoulder beside her. Sela held her ground.

Sela: "Authority did not prevent us. It arranged us."

The sentence cut through the corridor like a dropped blade.

Aric, who had been resting under Maelin's orders in the second ward, appeared at the doorway barefoot with a blanket wrapped around him and his Yellow crest flickering weakly at his wrist.

Aric: "No."

His voice was hoarse and scared, yet it mattered because he had once believed someone had to close the door by becoming final.

Aric: "Do not ask me."

Regalia: "FINAL WITNESS CANDIDATE."

Aric stepped back, and Iven rose slowly beside him, his healer wrap showing beneath his sleeve.

Iven: "Aric taught us how not to cry when instructors watched. That was not authority. That was survival with witnesses missing."

Aric pressed a hand over his mouth, holding himself together, while Maelin's eyes shone fiercely from the healer line. Regalia pulsed again.

Regalia: "SUCCESSION PRESSURE CONTINUES UNTIL STRUCTURE STABLE."

Mira lifted her notebook, her grip tight on the binding.

Mira: "Public witness correction. The children hurt by absolute command reject absolute command as the cure."

Aira's pencil scratched across her page without adding a joke. Rhea waited until Mira's line had been copied three times before speaking.

Rhea: "Emergency witness authority recognizes testimony from those directly harmed."

Senna's remote crystal flared.

Senna: "Council record accepts their testimony as a formal block."

Elira stood despite Maelin's sharp hiss.

Elira: "Then the structure is wrong."

Her gold light flared, clean and sharp enough to sever copied doctrine from living children. The pendant filter expanded through the corridor, asking every lingering impression the same question: do you consent to becoming succession evidence?

Sela: "No."

Tovin: "No."

Iven: "No."

Mira stepped beside Sela anyway.

Mira: "No."

Nera: "No."

Darian: "No."

Aira: "Absolutely not."

Leon: "No."

Cassian: "No, with fire later if required."

Rhea: "No."

Senna: "No."

Valen: "No."

Maelin: "No, and sit down."

That last one was aimed at Elira, who sat with a faint smile.

Regalia failed to raise the throne again, not because I refused it, but because the people it claimed to protect spoke before anyone could protect them into silence.

The broadcast began anyway, pouring out from every relay crystal in Aurelis, from the courtyard fountain, from dormant tournament mirrors, and from Council notice panes that never asked permission once an old system woke. At first it showed no letters, only an empty throne against an eclipse and a thousand chairs refusing to form steps.

Then the words surfaced in universal glyphs every Circle could read without translation:

**THE BLACK CIRCLE HAS A THRONE.**

The whole academy woke. Noctis woke. The Council woke. Standing in the east infirmary among children who had just said no, I understood that refusal had not ended Regalia. It had only made Regalia announce the question to the world.

When the fountain light died, it left something solid in the basin: a fingernail-sized shard of black glass split by a single vein of gold. Halden reached for it, checked himself, and waited for Rhea to raise her seal before Valen lifted it under old light. Three lines moved along the gold vein:

**REFUSAL RECORDED.**

**SUCCESSION UNRESOLVED.**

**ASCENDANT CANDIDATE: UNSEATED.**

Haru: "I am not a candidate."

The shard ignored me with ancient professionalism.

Aira: "I dislike objects with committee instincts."

Rhea sealed it inside a warded case under distributed campus custody while Senna logged where Aurelis authority ended, where Council observation began, and that neither boundary granted anyone solitary access. As the case clicked shut, the fountain caught the reflections of Aurelis's four towers, each fracturing around the shard. Between the west and north reflections, a fifth patch of darkness returned no sky at all. Valen saw it, and said nothing.

The announcement did not fade when the glyphs cleared. Worse, it settled. A loud proclamation could be argued down, condemned, or boxed into rules, but a settled idea changed people's posture. It changed how students glanced at me when they thought I wasn't looking, how faculty hovered near exits, and how Council clerks held their pens.

The Black Circle has a throne. The sentence wanted to feel inevitable.

By second bell, three versions of it were already circulating through the courtyard: the frightened version, claiming someone had to sit before the power chose badly; the ambitious version, insisting the Council must control access before Noctis could; and the reverent version, murmuring that Haru Aerlen had simply been denying the shape of his destiny.

I hated the reverent version most. Fear at least admitted it was afraid, while reverence tried to paint refusal as modesty waiting to be corrected.

Elira found me in the east corridor after Maelin finally downgraded her from direct observation to indirect harassment. She walked slowly without apology, the dark crystal pendant resting openly outside her uniform against the pale gold of her tie.

Haru: "You are showing it."

Elira: "I am."

There was no apology in how she held my eyes.

Haru: "Because of Regalia?"

Elira: "Because people keep staring at where they think it is. I prefer honest staring."

Very Elira. It made me want to kiss her, and made me remember Lucien turning private memory into a weapon. The want and the fear collided, and she must have read it on my face because she stopped right in front of me.

Elira: "Haru."

Haru: "I am here."

Elira: "Are you?"

Her voice was soft enough to reach the part of me still standing inside that quiet throne room after I said no. I looked out toward the infirmary windows.

Haru: "Part of me liked the quiet."

She did not flinch, so I kept going.

Haru: "No arguments. No children waiting for us to be careful enough. No Council turning care into procedure. No Lucien using every wound as proof. Just a place where I could decide and the world would stop making anyone else pay for my restraint."

Elira listened without trying to spare me the ugliness of it.

Haru: "That is why I cannot sit there."

Elira: "Because it tempted you?"

Haru: "Because it tempted me by sounding merciful."

Her hand rose toward my face, stopping an inch short.

Elira: "May I?"

Haru: "Always."

I kept my hands open at my sides as her palm rested against my cheek, warm and alive and chosen.

Elira: "Then we name that too. The throne is dangerous not because you secretly want domination. It is dangerous because it offers relief from being answerable."

Her words settled cleanly into my chest.

Haru: "You make terrifying things precise."

Elira: "You fall in love with historians and then act surprised."

Haru: "You are not a historian."

Elira: "No, but Professor Valen is contagious."

I let out a quiet laugh, and for a second it was enough.

Down the corridor, Aira hurried toward us with three notebooks tucked under her chin.

Aira: "I hate to interrupt what looks like emotionally responsible flirting, but Rhea needs everyone. Krail filed a minority motion."

Elira did not pull her hand back immediately, letting the order stand.

Elira: "What kind?"

Aira's mouth flattened.

Aira: "Provisional succession custody."

The air in the corridor chilled.

Haru: "Custody of what?"

Aira: "That is the problem. The motion is phrased like custody of the Regalia question, when the real effect would be custody of who gets to answer it."

Elira lowered her hand, her eyes hardening.

Elira: "He wants to choose the room."

Aira: "That is the whole trick of it."

She uncapped her pen and poised it over a fresh sheet. I looked down the corridor at the mismatched infirmary chairs.

Haru: "Then we choose it first."

Aira's expression sharpened into pure calculation.

Aira: "Courtyard?"

Elira: "Courtyard."

No enclosed chamber, no polished wall for Lucien, no rigged seating count, and no private custody route. If Regalia wanted a throne, if Krail wanted a trial, and if the world wanted a spectacle, we would hold it in a space too wide for anyone to center one person without the whole academy watching them try.

Word moved through Aurelis before the ink on Krail's motion finished drying. Rhea sent notices, Mira gathered Green witnesses, Leon mapped water lines, Cassian checked heat routes, Nera and Darian prepared Noctis phrase objections, and Maelin started organizing breakfast trays first because she understood governance better than most governments.

Elira walked beside me out into the waking academy with the crystal visible at her throat and her hand in mine, showing every staring student that intimacy did not become shame just because a throne wanted evidence.

Ahead, benches scraped across the courtyard flagstones. Someone had already removed the dais before Krail could claim it.

No one yet knew where to put me. Good.

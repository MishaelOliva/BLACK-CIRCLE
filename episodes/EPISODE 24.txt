# Episode 24: **When Restraint Breaks the Floor**

The floor opened because I asked it to. I had to keep that truth instead of calling it accident, instinct, or the Black Circle acting without me.

I asked Aurelis for a door, and the old academy answered.

Stone did not split immediately.

Aurelis heard me. The lower channel answered. Between those two truths came twelve heartbeats of danger.

At the first heartbeat, the stone remembered the old observatory.

At the third, my Green Circle disguise slipped out of rhythm.

At the sixth, the black ring beneath my feet became visible to everyone close enough to see the floor.

At the ninth, pain drove through my sternum so sharply I almost lost the question I was trying to ask.

Open below—without consuming, punishing, or claiming. Open below.

Stone split beneath my feet in a perfect black ring. Not outward toward the rescued students. Not under Maelin's healer cot. Not through Rhea's witness station. Down. A vertical descent into the old observatory's lower channels, where pre-standardization magic still remembered what a safeguard had been before people tried to own one.

Dren grabbed Leon and shoved him back from the edge.

Halden swore with academic precision.

On the tenth heartbeat, black-silver pressure rose behind my shoulders.

Dren saw it and went very still. Not afraid. Counting.

Instructor Dren: "Aerlen. Release or anchor."

I could not release. The moving hall had closed with Elira inside.

The eleventh heartbeat broke blood under one fingernail.

The twelfth widened the ring. Rhea shouted three orders.

I heard none of them clearly.

The moving hall had closed with Elira inside.

That fact tried to become the whole world.

Then the pendant at her throat flashed once through the broken connection. Open rings. Question.

It carried neither command nor permission. It asked a question. What are you doing?

The Black Circle stopped widening. Not because I calmed.

Because Elira's question found me before my fear could name itself mercy.

Haru: "I am not entering the hall." My voice sounded wrong.

Too quiet for the force under it.

Haru: "I am opening the lower channel."

Halden appeared at the edge of the ring.

Halden: "For what purpose?"

The question was not procedural.

It was a hook into personhood. I held it.

Haru: "To give them exits the hall did not design."

Rhea: "Recorded. Dren, perimeter. Maelin, students. Valen, with me."

The rescued children were crying, coughing, clinging to blankets and each other. Sela would not release Mira's hand even though Mira was still trapped inside the hall; she held empty air where the connection had been and whispered Mira's name as if calling could become rope.

Iven sat upright on the cot, eyes fixed on the black ring.

Iven: "The senior is Aric." Nera turned. Iven swallowed.

Iven: "Aric Dain. Yellow Circle. Third year. He teaches first-years how not to cry when instructors watch." Darian's mouth tightened.

Iven: "He said volunteering is the only way to choose what Noctis takes."

Cael's shadow moved through the room. Not as a ghost. As a pattern.

Noctis taught children to find agency only in choosing the shape of their own use.

Elira was inside with that pattern.

I lowered one hand into the black ring.

Cold pressure bit my palm. Ozone. Metal.

Old Aurelis spoke without words.

**SAFEGUARD RESPONSE REQUESTED**

Haru: "Not alone." The ring pulsed.

**DEFINE WITNESS**

I looked at the practice court.

At Rhea and Senna's remote seal. At Halden's ink-stained hands. At Valen's pale face.

At Dren standing between rescued children and the edge.

At Maelin wrapping Sela in a blanket while the girl cried for someone still inside.

At Iven, who had been taken and still gave us a name.

Haru: "Everyone who can say no."

Then Sela lifted her head.

Her face was wet and white.

Sela: "No." The Black Circle paused.

Every adult looked at her.

She shook harder, but her voice came again.

Sela: "Don't take us."

The sentence struck deeper than any Council motion.

I turned fully toward her, away from the opening.

Haru: "I won't."

Sela did not relax. Good.

Trust given too quickly after rooms like that would have been another wound.

Haru: "If the Black Circle moves toward you without someone you trust saying why, you can say no again."

Sela's eyes moved to Rhea. Rhea nodded once.

Rhea: "Recognized." Then to Maelin.

Maelin: "Very recognized." Then, surprisingly, to Cassian.

Cassian placed one hand over his heart with unusual solemnity.

Cassian: "I will be offensively loud about it." Sela breathed. The Black Circle steadied. The ring accepted that.

The descent opened into a spiral stair. No one cheered. Good. We went down.

Not all of us. Maelin stayed with the children because rescue that abandoned the rescued was only spectacle. Dren stayed because someone had to guard the living door. Rhea split herself between command and descent by sending her council seal with us. Senna's remote crystal hovered beside it. Halden, Valen, Leon, Cassian, Nera, Darian, Iven, and I descended.

Iven should not have come.

Maelin said that twelve times.

He came because the moving hall recognized him as already returned and therefore less interesting as bait.

He came because Aric had taught him not to cry.

He came because he wanted Aric to learn something else.

We reached the lower channel beneath the observatory.

It was not a chamber.

It was a wound architecture had learned to keep open.

Seven bridges crossed an impossible vertical dark. On each bridge, old witness sigils glowed in broken sequences. Some belonged to Aurelis. Some to schools that no longer existed. Some to Circle Zero doctrine so old my body understood them before my mind did.

At the center hung the moving hall. Not as a room.

As a knot of black glass, gold light, green sparks, blue water, red flame, and white Noctis thread.

Inside, through fractured windows, I saw Elira.

She stood before a Yellow Circle student older than the rescued children. Aric Dain had dark hair, hollow cheeks, and the composed posture of someone who had been praised for disappearing. His Yellow crest burned cleanly at his wrist.

Lucien stood behind him. Not touching.

He did not need to.

Aric had learned to stand where the hand would be.

Elira's voice came through the lower channel.

Elira: "Volunteering under a doctrine that punishes refusal is not consent."

Aric: "It is the only consent Noctis recognizes."

Mira and Finn stood to Elira's left. Aira to her right. Leon's water mirror carried their voices until the lower channel aligned enough for us to hear without turning the hall into proof of my intrusion.

Lucien looked out through the fracture. At me.

Lucien: "You opened the floor."

Haru: "Yes." My breath steadied against the cold air.

Lucien: "So much for restraint."

Haru: "Restraint is not paralysis." The bridges answered. Seven old sigils brightened.

Halden whispered, "That phrase is in the record."

Valen: "Before the erasure." The moving hall shuddered. Lucien's eyes narrowed. He had expected rage.

He had planned for rescue as invasion.

He had not planned for the old system to recognize a distinction he did not believe in.

Iven stepped to the edge of the nearest bridge. Nera caught her breath.

Iven: "Aric!"

Inside the hall, Aric's face changed. Not much.

Enough to prove a person was still there.

Aric: "You should be safe."

Iven: "I am not safe if you are spent." Aric's Yellow crest flickered.

Lucien: "First-years are emotionally imprecise."

Darian: "You hide behind children and call them imprecise because adults keep leaving you fewer places to stand."

Lucien looked at him like he had forgotten Darian existed and disliked being reminded. Good. Every crack mattered.

Elira stepped closer to Aric.

Maelin would have screamed if she had seen how close.

Maybe she did through the channel.

Elira: "You can leave with us." Aric smiled faintly.

It was the saddest expression I had seen since Cael's funeral.

Aric: "If I leave, the hall follows the others. It needs a final witness to close."

Nera: "No."

Her voice broke through the channel, raw and immediate.

Nera: "That is the lie. Final witness means final lock, not final death." Aric blinked.

Nera: "They changed the lesson. They always change the lesson after making sure only frightened students remember the old words."

Valen gripped the bridge rail.

Valen: "She's right."

Halden: "How do you close it?"

Valen: "Not from inside."

All eyes turned to me. The Black Circle waited.

Lucien smiled again, because he saw the same thing.

If I closed the moving hall, he would claim Black Circle ownership of every witness inside.

If I did nothing, he would spend Aric or trap the others.

Every cruel system had that shape: make every option evidence against the person trying to stop harm.

Elira looked through the fracture at me. Not afraid. Asking. I remembered the morning.

Do not make my certainty into something you must earn every morning. Practice.

I looked away from her. Toward Iven.

Haru: "Can you describe the hall's closing lesson?" Iven wiped his face.

Iven: "We say: A loyal hall closes when the final witness kneels."

Nera shook her head sharply.

Nera: "No. The old form."

Iven squeezed his eyes shut.

Iven: "A lawful hall closes when the final witness is counted."

Finn shouted from inside, "Counted by whom?" Silence. Then Mira answered.

Mira: "By everyone who would have used them."

The green sparks inside the hall flared.

Aira: "Mira, that is either brilliant or about to get us all killed."

Mira: "Both?"

Cassian: "Respectable answer."

We built the count across the bridges.

Rhea counted Aric as protected student.

Senna counted him as Council witness.

Nera counted him as not property.

Darian counted him as not proof of Noctis honor.

Iven counted him as the boy who taught him how not to cry and now deserved to learn how.

Mira counted him from inside the hall as a person with a name.

Finn counted the absence around him as filled.

Aira counted every coercive phrase used to make him volunteer.

Leon and Cassian counted the environmental pressures that made refusal unsafe.

Elira counted his choice as currently compromised but recoverable.

Then they all looked at me. Lucien did too. Waiting.

Hungry for the Black Circle to become the final authority.

I stepped back from the bridge rail.

Haru: "I do not count him last." The hall froze. Lucien's smile vanished.

Haru: "He counts himself." Aric stared.

The Yellow crest at his wrist shook.

Lucien: "He has already chosen."

Elira: "Then he can choose again." Aric's knees buckled. He did not kneel. He almost fell.

Elira caught him by the sleeve, not the hand, giving him enough support to refuse. Aric whispered something.

Leon pushed the water mirror harder. His voice came through.

Aric: "I am Aric Dain."

The moving hall cracked from foundation to ceiling.

Aric: "I am not the final witness."

Lucien moved. Not a spell. A hand.

Fast enough to remind us he was not only doctrine.

He reached for Aric's crest.

Elira struck him. Not with Yellow Circle force.

With the palm of her hand.

The sound carried across the lower channel. Every witness heard it. Lucien stood perfectly still.

For the first time, his face showed open shock. Elira's hand shook.

Elira: "Do not touch him." The moving hall shattered.

Witnesses, students, light, and broken Noctis grammar fell toward the bridges.

I opened the Black Circle as a net. Not around them. Under them.

A boundary that caught without claiming.

They landed on seven bridges in groups, gasping, alive, unowned. Lucien did not fall.

He stood on a shard of black glass suspended above the dark, one hand against his struck cheek.

Slowly, he smiled. Not calmly. Angrily.

Lucien: "There she is." Then the shard vanished.

And in the space where the moving hall had been, the old lower channel revealed a door marked with a symbol no modern Circle system used. A black ring.

Split open by a crown.

---

No one touched the door.

The first victory looked like that.

In a room full of exhausted students, broken doctrine, rescued children, angry witnesses, and a newly visible symbol that looked like history had been waiting with a knife, no one rushed forward.

We stood on the seven bridges beneath Aurelis and breathed. Some of us cried.

Some of us pretended not to.

Aric Dain sat on the third bridge with a blanket around his shoulders and Elira beside him. The mark of her slap still glowed red on Lucien's absence in my memory. It should not have mattered as much as it did.

It mattered because she had struck not out of helplessness, but refusal.

A human hand against a boy being reclaimed. No spell could have been more precise.

Maelin arrived twelve minutes later and began criticizing everyone still alive.

This was how I knew we might survive the hour.

Maelin: "You opened a floor."

Haru: "Yes." I met their gaze without looking away.

Maelin: "You entered an ancient lower channel with a recently recovered trauma patient, multiple rescued minors, and a hostile doctrine breach."

Haru: "I did not bring the minors." Maelin looked at me.

Haru: "That was not a defense."

Elira laughed. Small. Exhausted. Real. Maelin pointed at her.

Maelin: "You. Sitting. Now." Elira was already sitting.

Maelin: "More obediently."

Elira adjusted her posture with grave dignity. Even Aric smiled faintly. The door waited. Black ring, split crown.

Valen and Halden examined it from three paces away with the restraint of scholars who could smell catastrophe and wanted very badly to poke it with ink.

Rhea had established a perimeter seal. Senna's remote crystal recorded from above the center bridge. Krail had tried to enter the channel twice through legal relay and been denied twice under the highly satisfying category of "ongoing rescue conditions."

Aira sat on the floor sorting pages into piles.

Leon brought water to the rescued students.

Cassian warmed the bridges without making fire visible because several Noctis children flinched at sudden light.

Mira and Finn sat with Sela, who refused to release Mira's sleeve.

Nera and Darian spoke quietly with Aric. Not interrogation. Not yet. Names first. Water first. Proof later.

The crown inside the black ring watched all of it.

Halden: "This predates the Circle Zero Observatory."

Valen: "Or sits under it as root doctrine."

Halden: "The crown suggests authority."

Valen: "Or the failure of authority."

Rhea: "Translate faster."

Valen touched the air near the symbol, not the symbol itself.

Gold-white script surfaced around the door.

I felt it behind my sternum before I read it. Cold. Ozone. Metal.

**REGALIA PROTOCOL**

The words meant nothing to me.

My body hated them anyway. Valen whispered, "No."

Halden: "You know it?"

Valen looked at me. Not like a professor.

Like someone who had found my name written in a book that should have burned.

Valen: "Regalia was a theoretical root of Circle classification. Before colors became hierarchy, before Yellow was named peak, before Black was erased."

Elira stood despite Maelin's hand.

Elira: "What did it do?"

Valen: "It did not rank power." The door brightened.

Valen: "It assigned authority over crisis response." My mouth went dry.

Haru: "Safeguards." Valen nodded slowly.

Valen: "And sovereigns."

That word moved through the lower channel like a draft from a sealed tomb. Sovereigns.

I did not like it.

Neither did the Black Circle. Senna's remote seal sharpened.

Senna: "Define sovereign."

Valen: "I cannot. Not fully. Most references were destroyed or redacted into myth. Some fragments imply that when Circle Zero safeguards failed, certain institutions tried to create a command class above witnesses. A crisis crown. A person or office authorized to order safeguards during catastrophe." Krail would love that.

Lucien would become that if the world let him.

Or he would make the world accuse me of wanting it.

The door opened one inch. No one moved.

A voice came through. Not Lucien's. Not old record echo. Human. Female. Young. Terrified.

Voice: "Please tell me someone is there."

The rescued Noctis students froze. Iven stood.

Iven: "That is Pella."

Pella Orthis, Reserve Eight, one of the children we had pulled from the moving hall, sat beside Maelin under a blanket.

She stared at the door in horror.

Pella: "That is me."

The voice from behind the door sobbed.

Voice: "I do not want to be copied anymore."

The lower channel went silent.

Finn whispered, "Not a door." Mira's face drained.

Mira: "Archive." Halden cursed.

The Regalia door was not opening into a room.

It was opening into stored student impressions. Not souls, not fully living minds, but coercive copies made from fear, obedience drills, and conduit pressure. Noctis had not only moved children. It had made rehearsals of them. Practice bodies for doctrine.

Lucien had shown us the reserves because he had more than the living students.

He had copies that could be spent in argument, training, or ritual without technically killing anyone.

Elira's ward warmed so hard the light showed through her fingers.

Elira: "We are opening it."

Maelin: "No."

Elira: "Not alone. Not now. But we are opening it."

I looked at the door.

The Black Circle stood very still inside me.

Haru: "If Regalia assigns crisis authority, opening it may trigger a claim."

Rhea: "Over whom?"

Valen: "Everyone tied to the old protocol." Halden looked at me. Then at Elira.

Then at the rescued students.

Halden: "Potentially all of them."

Senna: "Then we seal the site and petition the full Council."

Krail's voice finally forced itself through a side relay.

Krail: "Agreed. And Haru Aerlen is to be removed from proximity to the site immediately."

Rhea: "No."

Krail: "President Valmont, this is beyond student council authority."

Rhea: "Then be grateful I am not relying on it."

She turned her seal toward Senna.

Rhea: "Aurelis requests distributed emergency custody of the Regalia site under witness protection, with no single institution, official, or extra-classification individual granted access alone."

Senna: "Granted provisionally."

Krail: "You cannot grant custody of a pre-Circle authority protocol to children and compromised faculty." Senna's voice cooled.

Senna: "I can grant temporary protection of endangered students and evidence during an ongoing Noctis breach. I just did." Krail's seal cut out.

Aira whispered, "I am naming my next notebook Senna."

Leon: "Please don't."

The door remained open one inch.

The copied Pella cried behind it.

The real Pella shook under her blanket, face buried in Maelin's sleeve.

Maelin looked at the door with a healer's hatred.

Maelin: "Can we quiet it without entering?"

Valen: "Maybe."

Elira: "Use my pendant." I turned to her.

Haru: "Elira." She met my eyes.

Elira: "Not as bait. As consent filter. The copied impressions were made without ongoing consent. The crystal asks before it carries. Let it ask." The idea was dangerous. It was also right.

I hated how often those had become the same shape.

We built the temporary quieting array at the bridge center.

Elira removed the pendant and placed it above Rhea's open council seal. Valen anchored old restoration grammar around it. Halden added observation constraints. Aira wrote the questions in plain language first, because, as she said, "Ancient systems keep getting poetic and then people die." The questions were simple.

Do you know you are a copy?

Do you want to continue speaking? Do you want quiet?

Do you want your living source notified? Do you refuse use?

One by one, the voices behind the door answered. Some knew. Some did not. Some wanted quiet.

Some wanted to speak until someone wrote their names down.

Mira and Finn wrote until their fingers cramped.

Pella asked to hold the transcript of her own copy's answers. No one denied her.

The lower-channel lift rattled against the bridge. Maelin arrived with three healer aides, six blankets, and the moral certainty of a woman prepared to fight architecture if it delayed triage.

Krail's relay forced itself back into Senna's channel behind her. Rhea angled his image away from the rescued children and left it open only as a recorded witness line. Maelin glanced at him.

Maelin: "If you issue one order that makes my patients stand longer, I will enter it into the medical record with adjectives."

Aric sat with Iven and Sela near the fourth bridge. He stared at the door like someone recognizing a lesson from a book he had never been allowed to finish. Elira saw.

She left Maelin's side before Maelin could stop her, crossed to him, and sat rather than stood above him.

Elira: "You said Noctis has a crown lesson." Aric's throat moved.

Aric: "Senior conduit track. They called it command mercy."

The phrase was so ugly that even Krail's image lost its appetite for one second. Aric continued, voice careful.

Aric: "They taught that witnesses are necessary in ordinary crisis because ordinary people need comfort. But in final crisis, comfort becomes delay. Delay becomes death. So one authority must command the safeguard."

Haru: "And the conduit?" He looked at me.

There was apology in his face for a lesson he had not written.

Aric: "The conduit proves the command was obeyed." Nera closed her eyes. Darian turned away.

Cael's absence entered every bridge.

Votive Return had not been a separate horror.

It had been practice for a crown. Elira's voice stayed level.

Elira: "Did Lucien teach it?" Aric shook his head.

Aric: "No. He corrected it."

Valen looked sharply at him.

Aric: "He said the old lesson was cowardly because it waited for final crisis. He said the superior student creates the crisis where command becomes obvious."

There he was. Lucien Voss. Not merely controlled. Not merely cruel.

A boy so convinced hierarchy revealed truth that he would manufacture catastrophe to prove someone deserved to stand above it.

Krail's image had gone very still. Senna saw that too.

Senna: "Councilor?"

Krail: "Do not mistake my concern about Aerlen for sympathy with Voss."

Senna: "Then stop using arguments his doctrine can open."

The sentence struck hard enough that the relay light bent.

Aira's pen made a small triumphant scratch.

The Regalia door widened another fraction in answer.

The copied Pella's voice did not return.

This time, many voices whispered at once. Not pleas. Questions. Am I used? Am I counted?

Am I still a child if I am only a copy of fear?

Does my living body know I am here? Can a copy refuse?

The rescued students heard themselves, or versions near enough to hurt.

Pella began sobbing. Sela pressed both hands over her ears. Iven's face went blank until Aric put a hand on his shoulder, then froze and asked, "May I?" after the fact.

Iven nodded. Late consent. Still repaired.

Maelin ordered the younger students moved farther from the door. This time no one argued. Rescue did not require making children listen to every copy of their fear.

Elira's ward lay in the center of the temporary quieting array, asking one question at a time. The copies answered unevenly. Some wanted silence. Some wanted names.

Some wanted their living source notified.

One voice asked for food. That undid Mira.

She cried while writing the answer down.

Finn took over the line until her hand steadied again.

The Black Circle remained quiet through all of it, which felt less like control than grief learning not to interrupt.

Aric asked if copies could be guilty. Nera said, "No." Darian said, "Institutions can."

The door did not close. But the crying softened.

Then one final voice came through.

Male. Older. Amused. Not Lucien.

Voice: "So the Black Circle returns before the crown is claimed. How inconvenient." Valen went rigid.

Halden: "Identify yourself." The voice laughed.

Voice: "No. Identification is a door, and I have lived too long by refusing doors."

The black crown symbol brightened. The voice continued.

Voice: "Tell Haru Aerlen this: Lucien Voss is not the first student to mistake Dominion for sovereignty. He is only the most recent child to find the handle." My skin went cold.

Haru: "Who built Regalia?" The voice paused.

When it answered, every bridge light dimmed.

Voice: "The people who feared what witnesses could do when safeguards listened to them." The door slammed shut.

Across the lower channel, every open ring went dark.

Then a single word appeared above the crown.

**ASCENDANT**

No public crystal answered. Not yet.

Rhea sealed the word inside the local record before anyone could turn it into an announcement.

Rhea: "Local result only. Distributed custody remains. No one enters alone, and no one broadcasts a throne while rescued children are still waiting for beds."

Senna: "Provisionally sustained. Move the patients."

The Regalia door stayed closed. The bridges stayed under shared guard. We took the living back toward the infirmary.

Elira rose and came to stand beside me, injured hand wrapped, pendant returned to her throat, eyes bright with exhaustion and anger.

Elira: "Then tomorrow we refuse it publicly."

Haru: "Tomorrow?"

She looked at the bridge, the rescued students, the door, the relay, the records, the people who had not run from me even when every symbol in the room wanted to make a crown out of my name.

Elira: "Tonight we take care of the living." The order was correct.

She scared systems more than I did for exactly that reason.

Taking care of the living was less graceful than refusing a crown.

It involved blankets that did not match, healer cots borrowed from three departments, student council runners carrying soup through warded corridors, and Cassian discovering that terrified children did not always want heroic speeches but often accepted warm bread without questions. He adapted quickly.

Cassian had better traits, and that was one of them.

The rescued Noctis students were moved to the east infirmary wing under distributed protection. Not one room. Maelin refused that immediately.

Maelin: "Grouping every recovered conduit together is how lazy institutions make one target and call it efficiency."

Rhea changed the plan before anyone argued.

The students were placed in adjoining rooms with open doors, visible exits, and witness rotations chosen by comfort rather than rank. Sela asked for Mira. Tovin asked for nobody, then amended it to "someone quiet." Finn volunteered and then sat in the corner so still he became furniture with a pulse. Pella asked for Maelin and refused to let go of the healer's sleeve for twenty minutes. Iven asked for Nera, then panicked when she entered, then asked her not to leave.

Nera stood in the doorway until he said, "Inside is okay."

Iven would not let go of the academy scarf. Tunnel dust had turned it gray, one end was half-frozen, and the knot was bad enough that Cassian regarded it with professional respect.

Maelin lifted a clean warming wrap. Iven recoiled before it came near him. She stopped.

Maelin: "Keep the scarf. May I put the wrap over it?"

Iven studied her face, then the open doorway, then Nera. His fingers never left the ruined knot. Finally, he nodded.

Maelin settled the wrap over his shoulders without touching the scarf.

Across the room, Leon looked away. Then, jaw tight, he made himself look back.

Elira stood at the doorway with frost drying along the hem of her coat.

Elira: "At Noctis, does a new yes erase the old one?"

Iven: "Usually."

Elira: "Here it can sit beside it."

Iven pinched one fold of the clean wrap between two fingers. His other hand kept hold of the scarf. He kept both. Then she entered.

The night went like that. Doors. Questions. Corrections.

Small permissions that would never appear in heroic summaries and therefore mattered more.

I stayed in the central corridor under Halden's observation because Regalia had written **ASCENDANT** near my name and because no one with sense wanted me alone with that word. Dren stood at the far end of the hall. Rhea worked at a table beside the nurse station. Senna's remote crystal hovered above the files, dimmed unless addressed. Aira sat on the floor with her back against the wall, sorting notes into piles labeled:

**Living Students**

**Copied Impressions**

**Regalia Door**

**Lucien Being The Worst**

Rhea saw the last label.

Rhea: "Rename it."

Aira: "It is accurate."

Rhea: "Rename it accurately in court language."

Aira sighed and crossed it out. The new label read:

**Lucien Voss: Hostile Doctrine Author / Active Cowardice**

Rhea stared.

Aira: "Compromise."

Rhea let it stand, which said something about the night.

Elira moved from room to room until Maelin caught her by the shoulder and turned her around like a misbehaving patient-shaped compass.

Maelin: "Your room."

Elira: "I am not-"

Maelin: "Your room." Elira looked toward me.

I did not step forward. Practice had become irritatingly relevant.

Haru: "She's right." Elira's eyes narrowed.

Elira: "I dislike when you practice against me."

Maelin: "Good. You can dislike it horizontally."

Elira was escorted to a side room with a couch, a cot, and window wards that had been checked by three separate people. I followed only after she asked, and only after Halden noted the movement with such solemnity that Elira threatened to make him record the emotional texture of his own discomfort. He did not. But he looked tempted.

Inside the side room, the noise of the infirmary softened. Not vanished. Nothing vanished anymore. But the voices became human scale: a child asking for water, Leon arguing quietly with a heater charm, Mira telling Sela that freckles were not a medical symptom, Cassian being ordered not to cook unsupervised, Aira sneezing ink dust.

Elira sat on the couch and finally let the exhaustion reach her face.

I remained near the door. She looked at me.

Elira: "If you stand there like a punishment, I will throw a pillow."

There were no pillows within reach.

I sat beside her anyway.

She leaned into me with careful slowness, testing her body, her hand, the crystal, the day.

Haru: "Too much?"

Elira: "Everything is too much." My hands stopped moving. She caught my sleeve.

Elira: "That was not a no."

The correction moved through me more gently than I deserved.

I let my shoulder relax.

She rested her head there.

Elira: "When I said we take care of the living, I meant you too."

Haru: "I am being observed."

Elira: "That is not the same as being cared for."

Outside the room, Halden coughed once.

Elira raised her voice without lifting her head.

Elira: "That was not criticism, Professor."

Halden, from the hallway: "It was accurate."

I almost smiled. Elira did. Then the smile faded.

Elira: "You heard the copied voices."

Haru: "Yes." The cold pulse behind my ribs remained quiet.

Elira: "Did any of them feel like Cael?"

The question entered quietly and found the bruise it wanted. I closed my eyes.

Haru: "No." Her hand tightened.

Haru: "I kept listening for him. That is not rational. Regalia used copied fear impressions from living students. Cael is dead."

The word still did not fit in my mouth.

Elira did not rescue me from it.

Part of how she loved me lived there.

Haru: "But I listened anyway." She leaned closer.

Elira: "So did I."

The living, being cared for imperfectly and in public enough that no one could turn care into ownership without being challenged. Under Aurelis, Regalia waited.

Maelin: "If this is another Council inquiry, I am resigning from politeness."

Rhea: "Status."

Senna: "Regalia resonance through care furniture. No breach yet."

Valen: "It is not furniture to the protocol. It is adjacency architecture." Leon blinked.

Leon: "It is chairs."

Valen: "Yes. That is the point." The floor pulse deepened.

The repaired companion chair would not become an armrest. Sela's bedside chair would not rise higher than the others. Iven's far chair remained far because Tovin had chosen that distance. Elira's bench refused to detach from the wall while she leaned on me.

Regalia did not know what to do with consent distances. It hated that.

Who consents to this being used? The copied voices faltered.
